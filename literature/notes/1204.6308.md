## Main claims and evidence

[P] The protocol compares randomized benchmarking (RB) of each subsystem with
its neighbor undriven against RB of both subsystems simultaneously. The change
measures the context dependence of average gate errors (pp. 1-3).

- [P] The three settings are Clifford operations on subsystem 1 with subsystem 2 idle, the reverse setting, and local Clifford operations on both subsystems.
- [P] Single-subsystem decay estimates the individual error rate; simultaneous decay estimates the rate while the other subsystem is driven. The protocol checks the quality of the single-exponential approximation rather than assuming every subsystem decay has that form (Eqs. 11-13).
- [P] Eq. 13 defines the addressability metric as the absolute difference `|r_k - r_k|k'|`. The PDF's absolute-value bars matter; the text extraction drops them.
- [P] A second indicator compares the joint decay parameter with the product of the marginal parameters: `delta_alpha = alpha_12 - alpha_1|2 * alpha_2|1`. Nonzero values witness some correlated errors. Footnote 30 explicitly gives correlated errors that the indicator misses.
- [P] Experiments use two superconducting samples, each comprising two transmons coupled through a coplanar-waveguide resonator. The samples have identical layouts and different qubit detunings (p. 3).
- [P] Table I reports sample-a individual and simultaneous error rates of `0.0039 +/- 0.0001` and `0.0086 +/- 0.0003` for the first qubit, and `0.0067 +/- 0.0002` and `0.0120 +/- 0.0005` for the second. Reported uncertainties are one standard deviation.
- [P] Sample b has smaller added errors: the absolute differences in Table I are `0.0003 +/- 0.0003` and `0.0006 +/- 0.0003`. These are observations on the two samples, rather than a universal separation-distance rule.
- [P] The authors compare the observations with a simple model containing control leakage, cross-resonance terms, and residual coupling; the model estimates have the same order as measured values, while the paper acknowledges missing addressability effects (p. 4).

## Mapping to this repository

[I] [packing.py](../../gororoba_quantum/packing.py) creates disjoint placements
and keeps replicas' classical registers separate. Those properties establish
circuit separation. Hardware independence requires additional evidence; shared
control, readout, and environmental effects can couple outcome statistics.

[I] The direct methodological transfer is a comparison of the same target
circuit on the same physical qubits with and without companion activity. The
repository's [adjudicate.py](../../gororoba_quantum/adjudicate.py) already reads
ordered register outcomes, estimates shot-wise correlations, and compares packed
and reference schedules. Those functions provide useful observables; they do
not implement simultaneous RB or establish its assumptions.

[I] Two outcome questions need distinct estimands: a target circuit's endpoint
change under packing, and dependence between streams in a packed circuit.
Either question can expose an important failure even when the other comparison
has limited sensitivity. A directional `packed minus reference` endpoint change
also differs from the paper's absolute-value addressability metric.

[I] Label: known protocol available for adaptation. Applying the comparison to
folded QPE, an associator-Walsh circuit, or an N21 witness is a proposed
application measurement. The paper supplies neither those circuits nor a
quantitative prediction for their behavior on Heron hardware.

## Relevance

- [I] Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: high. Simultaneous activity is a context that isolated calibration can miss; sample-specific error rates remain confined to the paper's devices.
- [I] Cayley-Dickson structure: none. The paper establishes an addressability method rather than an algebraic classification.
- [I] Factoring resource estimates: low. Addressability affects physical execution quality; the source supplies neither an asymptotic factoring speedup nor a full factoring resource estimate.

[I] Diagnostic applicability is broader than the structural and resource ratings.
Exact algebra supplies target distributions, and addressability controls can
test whether concurrent execution changes Walsh or small QPE endpoints.

## Action items and acceptance gates

1. Define the target endpoint and a permissible packed-versus-reference change
   before selecting companion circuits. Acceptance: the experiment plan names
   the endpoint, signed effect, uncertainty method, and practical tolerance.
2. Compile target-only and target-with-companions circuits on identical target
   qubits. Acceptance: the target instruction sequence, physical mapping,
   measurement settings, and schedule differences are retained and inspected.
3. Analyze endpoint degradation and cross-stream dependence separately.
   Acceptance: each conclusion names its test family, sample count, uncertainty,
   and detectable effect; a failed rejection carries a sensitivity limit.
4. Use [Rudinger et al.](1810.05651.md) for arbitrary-circuit context tests and
   timing controls, and [Sarovar et al.](1908.09855.md) for varied settings and
   joint outcome tests. Acceptance: the design explains which mechanisms each
   control can reveal and which remain outside its coverage.
5. If simultaneous RB is added, validate fits and sequence construction against
   the source before hardware use. Acceptance: an offline fixture reproduces
   the chosen statistic and catches injected context effects. A written,
   separately approved QPU case is required before submission.

## Boundaries

[I] Disjoint qubits and a small observed correlation coefficient do not prove
independence. An insensitive detector can miss correlated errors, as the paper
explicitly demonstrates. A throughput claim additionally needs charged usage,
schedule duration, and endpoint precision; those measurements are outside this
paper's reported objective. The [parallel-experiment synthesis](quantum_experiment_parallelism.md)
keeps those resource and inference questions together.
