## Main claims

[P] Hidden shifts of quadratic Boolean functions, and of functions whose Gowers U^3 norm is close to 1, are found with O(n) quantum queries, and an unknown quadratic form is identified with O(n) quantum queries against Omega(n^2) classical queries.

- [P] Sec. 1: the hidden shift problem is: given a finite group G, a finite set R, and maps f, g: G -> R with g(x) = f(x + s) for some s in G, find s.
- [P] Sec. 2, Theorem 1 (Dickson): for a symplectic matrix B = Q + Q^t over Z_2 there is R in GL(n, Z_2) and h <= n/2 with R B R^t = (1_h tensor sigma_x) direct sum 0_(n-2h); the quadratic becomes ip_h(x_1, ..., x_2h) + L'(x). A quadratic with rank(B) = 2h = n is bent, affine transforms of bent functions are bent, and the dual of a quadratic bent function is quadratic.
- [P] Algorithm 3.1 (standard hidden shift algorithm): prepare |0>, apply H^n, apply the phase oracle of g, apply H^n, apply the phase oracle of the dual ftilde, apply H^n, measure s. It uses one query to g and one query to ftilde, runs in O(n) quantum time, and is exact. Step (iii) needs a flat spectrum.
- [P] Sec. 3, identification of an unknown quadratic f(x) = xQx^t + Lx^t (not necessarily bent): the phase state |psi> = 2^(-n/2) sum_x (-1)^(xQx^t + Lx^t + b) |x> (Eq. 3) and the unitary T: |x, y> -> 2^(-n/2) sum_z (-1)^(z.y) |x + y, z> (a CNOT layer plus Hadamards) give T(|psi> tensor |psi>) = 2^(-n/2) sum_u (-1)^p(u) |u, u(Q + Q^t)>. About k = O(n) samples (u_i, v_i) give U^t M = V^t with M = Q + Q^t, solved by Gaussian elimination with constant success probability; L then follows from a constant number of Fourier samplings. A coherent version applies T^(tensor k) to |psi>^(tensor 2k).
- [P] Abstract and Sec. 3: the classical identification cost of a quadratic is Theta(n^2); the text calls the Omega(n^2) lower bound "straightforward" and does not prove it.
- [P] Sec. 3: for a known quadratic the classical hidden shift costs Theta(n) queries (queries at 0 and at e_i give equations s.u_i = b_i; Gaussian elimination). For functions only close to quadratic the equations carry errors; the author conjectures that the classical cost then grows exponentially.
- [P] Theorem 2: if g is a quadratic and |<f, g>| > 1 - epsilon for the +/-1-valued functions, Find-Close-Quadratic returns g with probability at least c(1 - n epsilon). The proof gives p_success >= p_0 - 8 k epsilon for k copies.
- [P] Sec. 4: the Gowers norm is ||f||_(U^k) = (E_(x, h_1..h_k) Delta_(h_1)...Delta_(h_k) f(x))^(1/2^k) with Delta_h f(x) = f(x + h) - f(x). The text prints "degree k polynomials are characterized precisely by the vanishing of Delta_(h_1)...Delta_(h_k) f(x) for all h_i" and, in the next sentence, "||f||_(U^k) = 1 if and only if f is a phase polynomial of degree less than k".
- [P] Theorem 3 (Green and Tao, inverse theorem for U^3): ||f||_(U^k) >= 1 - epsilon implies a phase polynomial g of degree less than k with ||f - g|| = o(1). Algorithm 4.1 (Shifted-Large-U3) and Theorem 5: if ||f||_(U^3) >= 1 - epsilon, the hidden shift is found with probability above c(1 - epsilon) using O(n) queries and a SWAP test (Lemma 4) to verify the candidate.
- [P] Remark 6: for rank 2h < n the function has 2^(2h) nonzero Fourier coefficients, all of absolute value 2^(-h), supported on an affine shift of the dual space; the hidden shift is then determined only up to an affine subspace s + V of dimension n - 2h, and one shift plus a basis of V describes all shifts.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Repository claim: "At degree 2 the distribution is flat on an affine subspace of size 2^rank, where rank is that of the quadratic form's symplectic matrix: 4 outcomes for dim 16 pair (1,8)." (gororoba_quantum/FACTORING_NOTES.md, "Hidden quadratic and cubic forms").
  - Source: Theorem 1 (Dickson) and Remark 6 (rank 2h gives 2^(2h) nonzero Fourier coefficients of equal absolute value 2^(-h) on an affine shift of the dual space).
  - Model map: the measured outcome probability is the squared Walsh coefficient of (-1)^omega, so each supported outcome has probability 2^(-2h) and the support has 2^(2h) = 2^rank elements. For dim 16 the rank is 2, which gives 4 outcomes of probability 1/4.
  - Label: known theorem reproduced.
  - Not established by the source: any statement about degree 3 or higher, any hardware statement, and any claim that a shift exists to be found. The rank-2 rung is a quadratic of rank 2 < 4 on 4 variables, so Remark 6 gives a shift only up to a 2-dimensional affine space, and the classical cost with f known is Theta(n); no separation arises at this rung.
- [I] Statement under adjudication: the classical value-query cost of a degree-d Boolean phase oracle, as used to read the degree ladder of Design C (gororoba_quantum/DESIGN_NOTES.md, "Design C": phase oracles of degree up to n - 2).
  - Source: Sec. 4 (Gowers norm, derivative criterion) and Sec. 3 (Theta(n^2) classical identification of a quadratic).
  - Model map: the printed derivative criterion is off by one. A derivative lowers the degree by at least one, so f has degree at most d iff every (d+1)-fold derivative Delta_(h_1)...Delta_(h_(d+1)) f vanishes, which evaluates f on the 2^(d+1) points of an affine cube x + span-sums of h_1..h_(d+1). The paper's second sentence ("degree less than k" for k-fold derivatives) is the correct one. The displayed derivative Delta_h f(x) = f(x + h) - f(x) is additive; the standard Gowers norm of a complex function uses f(x + h) times conj f(x), which for a phase function (-1)^F equals (-1)^(Delta_h F), so the criterion above applies to the Boolean function F. The identification count for degree at most d is sum_(k<=d) C(n, k) = dim RM(d, n) (note elkies-2013-m256), the number of monomials; the Mobius transform on the points of Hamming weight at most d recovers every coefficient. At d = 2 this is 1 + n + n(n-1)/2, the Theta(n^2) of this paper's abstract.
  - Label: known theorem reproduced.
  - Not established by the source: any quantum-classical separation above degree 2. For d = 2 the identification gap is O(n) against Theta(n^2), polynomial. The conjectured exponential classical cost concerns shifts of functions that are close to a quadratic, with no proof.

## Action items

1. Quote the derivative criterion with k = d + 1 for degree at most d whenever the Gowers-norm text of this paper is cited; the printed k-for-degree-k sentence misdirects by one.
2. Do not cite Theorem 2 as noise tolerance for hardware runs: its hypothesis |<f, g>| > 1 - epsilon with epsilon of order 1/n is a correlation between +/-1 functions, and the measured total variation distances in the Design C record are distances between output distributions, a different quantity.
3. Before any hidden-shift statement about the rank-2 rung, state the shift group as translation of the index, state that f is known, and state the Theta(n) classical cost from Sec. 3.
