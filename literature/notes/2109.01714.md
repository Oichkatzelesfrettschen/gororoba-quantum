## Mechanism and evidence

- [P] Concurrent Quantum Circuit Sampling (CQCS) maps multiple instances of a
  program to disjoint physical resources and combines their samples for a
  variational optimization step (Sections 4.1 and 4.3).
- [P] For N requested samples and M instances, Section 4.3 assigns ceil(N/M)
  samples to each instance. The ideal M-fold gain requires equal sample quality
  and equal circuit duration, with exact division of N in the stated example.
- [P] Sections 4.4-4.5 identify two losses: additional instances occupy poorer
  qubits, and different routing overhead can make the slowest instance longer.
  Different coherent errors can produce different output distributions, making
  the pooled output harder to use. The relative per-iteration sampling latency
  is expressed as `(1/M) * (slowest_instance_time / baseline_sample_time)`.
- [P] Section 5 evaluates the approach with a Qiskit simulator driven from C++.
  The architectures include Melbourne, Rochester, Sycamore, and a hypothetical
  900-qubit square grid. QAOA examples use a 4-qubit or 12-qubit circuit.
- [P] Section 5.1 models Pauli, amplitude/phase damping, coherent rotations, and
  measurement errors. Section 5.1.1 scales gate and readout error rates linearly
  with utilization using isolated-versus-concurrent Sycamore characterization.
- [P] Sections 5.2-5.3 allocate subgraphs using reliability or depth heuristics
  and route each instance with SABRE. The instances can therefore have different
  SWAP counts and different physical circuit structures.
- [P] The abstract reports QAOA speedup up to 20x. Section 6.4 gives a particular
  simulated high-noise example with 60 instances, 10.9x speedup, and maximum
  approximation ratio 0.65 versus 0.677 for one instance. These are simulator
  results under the paper's model, rather than measured QPU usage reductions.
- [P] Section 7 also executes the 25 different compatible Pauli-measurement
  settings for a LiH VQE problem concurrently in simulation. The text reports
  25x quantum-sampling speedup when those circuits require no additional SWAPs.
  The paper therefore covers multiple required measurement tasks as well as
  repeated samples from one circuit.
- [I] The latency example in Section 4.1 prints 25 ms of sampling, less than
  150 ms of classical work, and a greater-than-99-percent sampling fraction.
  Those printed values do not derive that fraction. The repository should
  build its resource model from measured components, rather than copy the example.

[I] Diagnostic applicability is high: concurrent acquisition can amortize
repetitions while retaining per-layout recovery yields, controls, and separate
estimands for Walsh endpoints. Those experimental uses do not raise the direct
mathematical or factoring-resource ratings above none and low respectively.

- [I] `packing.candidate_replicas` preserves a compiled interaction pattern;
  CQCS routes each instance independently. Circuit identity can reduce one
  source of variation, while physical durations and noise still vary by patch.
- [I] `execution_pubs` supplies heterogeneous concurrent endpoints in addition
  to replica sampling. The LiH measurement-setting example establishes prior art
  for collecting different required outputs in one concurrent workload.
- [I] Logical separation permits independent ideal circuits; shared hardware
  can correlate observed samples. A replica count is an observation count,
  rather than a guaranteed multiplier of effective statistical sample size.
  Retain per-replica estimates and joint shot ordering before assessing pooling.
- [I] Several endpoints can benefit from concurrent acquisition even when a
  correlation-aware analysis grants little precision improvement to replicas.
  The [joint synthesis](quantum_experiment_parallelism.md) treats these objectives
  separately and links [palloq](2112.07091.md) and [QuMC](2102.05321.md).

## Prior art and action gates

1. Credit concurrent replica sampling and parallel measurement settings as prior
   art. Treat the retained Heron schedules, outcome distributions, and billing
   as candidate device-specific evidence that the simulator paper cannot supply.
2. Define the estimator for every endpoint before deciding which replicas can
   be pooled. Include patch effects, cross-stream covariance, and shot count in
   the uncertainty calculation; preserve every raw stream for reanalysis.
3. Compare complete acquisition cost at a fixed scientific criterion, including
   compilation, preparation, execution, repetitions, decoding, and any one-time
   costs amortized over a stated number of uses. Report charged QPU time and
   end-to-end elapsed time separately.
4. Preregister precision and degradation limits and compare packed results with
   suitable fixed-layout controls. Speedup under an assumed noise model is a
   planning input; hardware validation requires the existing QPU approval case.

[I] The paper accelerates a chosen quantum workload relative to its own serial
execution. That result supplies neither an algebraic classification theorem nor
a comparison against the strongest classical solver or sampler for our targets.
