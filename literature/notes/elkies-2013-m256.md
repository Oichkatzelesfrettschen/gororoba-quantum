## Main claims

[P] Reed-Muller codes RM(r, m) are the binary linear codes of polynomial functions of degree at most r on F^m with F = Z/2Z; their dimension, minimum weight, and dual are explicit, and RM(m-1, m) is the parity-check code.

- [P] Oct 31 entry: RM(r, m) has length n = 2^m and is the space of polynomial functions of degree at most r on F^m; it generalizes the zero, repetition, extended Hamming, parity-check, and full Hamming-space codes. RM(1, 3), the affine-linear functions on F^3, is the extended Hamming code.
- [P] Dimension: every function F^m -> F is a combination of the 2^m monomials that are linear in each variable (because x^2 = x); they span because the indicator of a point p is the product over i of (1 + x_i - p_i), hence they are independent; so dim RM(r, m) = sum_(j<=r) C(m, j).
- [P] Minimum weight: RM(r, m) has minimum weight 2^(m-r), attained precisely by affine subspaces of codimension r (induction on m, using that a degree-r polynomial vanishing on a hyperplane is the linear form times a degree r-1 polynomial, and a Fourier argument).
- [P] Duality: the dual of RM(r, m) is RM(m-1-r, m), proved by a dimension count and by orthogonality: the sum over F^m of any monomial of degree below m vanishes, because a monomial of degree d has weight exactly 2^(m-d). "This also proves that RM(r, m) is the parity-check code for r = m - 1, and thus that RM(r, m) is even for all r < m." RM(r, m) is self-orthogonal iff 2r <= m - 1 and self-dual iff 2r = m - 1.
- [P] Nov 5 entry: for a word c = sum over i in I of c_i, a sum of monomials, inclusion-exclusion for symmetric differences gives wt(c) = sum over nonempty J in I of (-2)^(|J|-1) wt(product of the c_j, j in J), used to show that all weights of RM(r, m) are divisible by 2^v with v = floor((m-1)/r).
- [P] Sep lectures: a binary word is in the one-checksum (parity-check) code iff its weight is even; its weight enumerator is ((X+Y)^n + (X-Y)^n)/2. Adding a checksum coordinate to a binary linear code gives a code of the same dimension with all words of even weight, and an [n, k, d] code of odd d becomes [n+1, k, d+1]. The subspace of an [n, k, d] code supported on n' coordinates is an [n', k', d'] code with k' >= k - (n - n') and d' >= d. Concatenation of arbitrary words of C_1 and C_2 gives an [n_1 + n_2, k_1 + k_2] code with weight enumerator W_(C_1) W_(C_2); the page prints its distance as min(k_1, k_2), a typographical slip for min(d_1, d_2).

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Repository claim: "Equivalently, H_n is the monomial code spanned by the monomials x^S with S a proper subset of {0, ..., n-2}, together with the single monomial x_{n-1}: the Reed-Muller code RM(n-2, n-1) on the low bits, direct sum the top coordinate. The two descriptions agree because a function of m variables has even weight exactly when its ANF omits the full product x_0 ... x_{m-1}." (literature/notes/left_mult_sign_rank.md).
  - Source: Oct 31 entry, "RM(r, m) is the parity-check code for r = m - 1", with the monomial weight 2^(m-d) and the dimension count; Nov 5 entry for the parity statement.
  - Model map: m = n - 1; a Boolean function of the low bits is a word of length 2^(n-1) indexed by x'; "even weight" is even Hamming weight of that word. RM(m-1, m) has dimension 2^m - 1 (the sum of C(m, j) over j < m), every word is even because a monomial of degree d < m has weight 2^(m-d), and the parity-check code has dimension 2^m - 1, so the two coincide. The ANF criterion follows from the Nov 5 formula: terms with |J| >= 2 carry the even factor (-2)^(|J|-1), the terms with |J| = 1 contribute 2^(m-d), odd only for d = m, so wt(f) is congruent mod 2 to the coefficient of the full product (note author's derivation from the page's formula).
  - Label: known theorem reproduced. The second description is a translation of the first by this identification and adds no content beyond it; as a statement it is a reformulation of the repository's own characterization, and the identification it rests on is the known theorem.
  - Not established by the source: the group-theoretic content, that H_n is the span of the Schreier sign vectors of the Cayley-Dickson left-multiplication group. The page contains no Cayley-Dickson algebra.
- [I] Repository claim: "C_n = H_n cap ker(ev_0) ... dim C_n = 2^(n-1) - 1, with the explicit ANF basis { x^S : S a nonempty proper subset of {0..n-2} } together with { x_{n-1} }" (literature/notes/open_gororoba_handoff.md, sign kernel section; checked by scripts/left_mult_sign_rank.py, _check_normalized_code).
  - Source: the dimension formula and the independence of the 2^m monomials (Oct 31 entry).
  - Model map: write a word of H_n as v(x', xi) = g(x') + c xi with g an even-weight word of length N = 2^(n-1). In the truth-table layout with the top bit selecting the half, v = (g | g + c 1), the (u | u + w) construction with u = g in RM(n-2, n-1) and w = c 1 in RM(0, n-1), the repetition code. For two linear codes of length N the (u | u + w) code has length 2N, dimension k_1 + k_2, and minimum distance min(2 d_1, d_2); here that is length 2^n, dimension (2^(n-1) - 1) + 1 = 2^(n-1), minimum distance min(4, 2^(n-1)) = 4 for n >= 3. The identity and the distance formula are standard textbook facts (MacWilliams and Sloane, cited as reference [MS77] in note 0811.3208) that are not on this page and were not read here. The evaluation at the origin of v is g(0), the constant coefficient of the ANF of g, so ker(ev_0) removes the empty monomial; because the all-ones word lies in H_n, ev_0 is nonzero on H_n, and C_n is H_n shortened at the origin coordinate with a zero coordinate retained: dimension 2^(n-1) - 1, and H_n = C_n direct sum <1>. The monomial basis of the handoff note is the nonconstant monomials of RM(n-2, n-1) together with x_(n-1). Puncturing H_n at the origin coordinate deletes a coordinate and, because the minimum distance 4 exceeds 1, keeps the dimension 2^(n-1); the dimension drop from H_n to C_n comes from the shortening, not from puncturing. The dual of RM(n-2, n-1) is RM(0, n-1) = {0, 1}, which is the statement that evenness is orthogonality to the all-ones word. An independent enumeration by the note author (not a repository gate) confirms, for n = 3 to 6, that the span of { (g | g) : g even } and (0 | 1) equals the span of the monomials listed above, with dimension 2^(n-1), and that the image of H_n under v -> v + v(0) 1 has dimension 2^(n-1) - 1; the minimum weight 4 of H_n was confirmed for n = 3 to 5.
  - Label: known theorem reproduced (the code-level dimension count and monomial basis).
  - Not established by the source: that P(H_n) = C_n for the sign kernel of the algebra, or any statement on controlled implementations.
- [I] Repository claim: "Every element of H_n has ANF degree at most n - 2, and degree n - 2 is attained", and "x_0 ... x_{n-3}, of weight 2 on F_2^(n-1), lies in V_n and has degree n - 2" (literature/notes/left_mult_sign_rank.md, Degree paragraph).
  - Source: minimum-weight statement of the Oct 31 entry: RM(r, m) has minimum weight 2^(m-r), attained precisely by affine subspaces of codimension r.
  - Model map: with m = n - 1 and r = n - 2, the minimum weight is 2, and x_0 ... x_{n-3} is the indicator of the line {x_0 = ... = x_{n-3} = 1} in F_2^(n-1), an affine subspace of codimension n - 2. It is a minimum-weight word of RM(n-2, n-1).
  - Label: known theorem reproduced.
  - Not established by the source: membership of this word in the repository's V_n.
- [I] Consistency check at n = 3. H_3 has dimension 2^(n-1) = 4 and degree at most n - 2 = 1, so H_3 is contained in the affine functions on F_2^3, which have dimension 4, and H_3 = RM(1, 3), the extended Hamming code [8, 4, 4] (the page states RM(1, 3) is the extended Hamming code). This matches the repository table row n = 3 (rank 4) and the statement in literature/notes/open_gororoba_handoff.md that the n = 3 code is linear.
  - Label: known theorem reproduced.
  - Not established by the source: the order |G_3| = 2^7.
- [I] Notation hazard. The page writes RM(r, m) with the degree first; note 0811.3208 writes R(n, r) with the number of variables first. The repository's RM(n-2, n-1) follows the page.
