### F4 — The `cons()` extraction is exact; the squarefreeness scare closes (verified-OK; records the load-bearing check that holds)
The Pfaffians are heavily **non-squarefree**: my sympy replication of the emitted `CZ` (verified: every upper entry is a single variable, `ph = i^popcount(k+1)` matching the `_phase` docstring of `weight_slice_fiber_relations.py:150-152`) shows every sampled 10-Pfaffian carrying 170–270 repeated-variable monomials (e.g. `z(1)^4 z(11)`, `z(1)^5`), and the boost derivatives carry 388 repeated-variable monomials of 434. So the worry that `cons()`'s `coef(n, zall)` against the squarefree monomial `zall` might drop or mix conditions (`derivation_stability.py:149`) was live. The Singular 4.4.1 manual (5.1.11) settles the semantics: `coef(f, m)` develops `f` as `f = M[1,1]·M[2,1] + … + M[1,n]·M[2,n]` where the first row holds **the monomials of f in the variables of m** — the manual's own example keeps the non-divisor monomial `x20` in the first row — and the second row holds the coefficients in the *remaining* variables. With `m = z(1)…z(15)`, the remaining variables are exactly the `c(1..225)` block: every occurring monomial of the normal form contributes its own c-linear coefficient. The constraints are precisely "the normal form vanishes coefficient-by-coefficient" — nothing dropped, nothing spuriously mixed. Empirical agreement: all 194+194 `CONSTRAINT` lines in `alg-p32009.log` are linear forms in `c(i)` alone (zero occurrences of `z(`), and the w8 and iso lists are **byte-identical** — consistent with the "same decomposition for both" reading of the pair stabilizer 31.

### F5 — The `degBound=5` membership test is exact, not approximate (verified-OK)
Every input is homogeneous: the Pfaffians are degree 5; the emitted closure equations (checked in the regenerated `.sing`) are the quadric in degree 2 (including the `mu^2` term), `delta` and the 3×3 minors in degree 4, the W8 generators in degrees 1, 2, 2, the Iso generators in 1, 2, 2, 2. Every boost derivative is homogeneous of degree 5. Division of a degree-5 element against a `degBound=5`-truncated standard basis cannot leave degree ≤ 5 (each cancellation produces terms of degree at most the cancelled term), so it coincides with division against the full basis restricted to its degree-≤5 elements — which is exactly what `std` with `degBound` returns per the manual (5.3.1: the computation stops when total degree exceeds the bound; the inhomogeneous-input warning does not apply). Hence `reduce(DB, sPY)==0` ⟺ genuine membership `D(P_k) ∈ (P) + I(Y)`, and `size(sP)=2647` is the true degree-5 span dimension (3003 generators, 356 linear relations). The same argument covers the `cons(Pk, sPY)` conditions in ring r2 (the `c`-variables ride along as coefficients; the ideal has c-degree 0). A5-602's membership statements are exact.

## 2. E8-bar: mechanisms that keep the fiber constant despite instability, and the cheapest separating computation

- **M1 — the radicalized-germ mismatch (live, built into the records).** The recorded fiber cones are of the pair with the radical germ; the instability is of the raw pair. Since the boost stabilizes `I(E8)` and hence its radical, the radical pair may be D-stable while the raw pair is not. Cheapest decisive test: once a radical (or hull) is available, rerun the modulo line with `I_E8` replaced by it — the same 3 min 45 s class at p=32009. Interim probabilistic probe: evaluate a sample of the 1470 remainders at random F_32009-points of `V(P + I_E8)` (the A5-601 flint-sampling pattern); vanishing on the sampled support is evidence of radical membership, minutes.
- **M2 — local vs global (live).** The 1470 remainders are outside the ambient homogeneous pair ideal; the fiber cone at a basepoint sees the local pair ideal, and localization **can** add homogeneous elements (counterexample I checked: `J = (z1 z2)`, `f = z1`, `z0 = (1,1)` — `f ∈ J` localized but not globally). So the global failure does not yet decide the local question at the orbit basepoints. Cheapest decisive test: a local-ordering (`ds`) standard basis of the pair shifted to the retained VB basepoint (translate `z0` to the origin; the 10 selected Pfaffians plus the E8 closure germ; reduce the D-images of the local generators) — minutes-class over Q(i), and it directly decides whether the local pair is D-stable, in which case the E8→VB constancy follows by the A5-603 argument applied locally with no new symmetry. This reading is absent from the README's two.
- **M3 — another linear field.** Decided by the already-started 4 h E8 pair-stabilizer rerun. My prediction (hypothesis): exactly 15 — see F10.
- **M4 — a nonlinear symmetry.** Expensive in general (quadratic-field `cons()`); cheaper: test the explicit three-unipotent-product G_2 elements of A5-414 as group elements carrying `z0` along the boost orbit (finite membership checks per element).
- **M5 — abstract isomorphism with no symmetry at all.** Separated by degree-3 relation counts at more `w` values: A5-428 has 140 of 220 at a single boost and A5-600 covers only degree 2 at its twelve values; a few more degree-3 point runs either hold 140 or break constancy (11 s a value at p=32029).

## 3. Novel connections (each a hypothesis with falsifier and cheapest test)

- **N1 — the boost is one ray of a 2-parameter torus.** `D(u+v) = (u+v)` and `D(u−v) = −(u−v)`; with the Euler scaling the abelian 2-dim algebra integrates to `(C*)²` acting by independent scalings `(λ_+, λ_-)` on `(u+v, u−v)`; the boost is the `(1, −1)` ray, the A5-414 scaling the `(1, 1)` ray. The W8 closure stabilizer 59 = 15 + 2×22 already exhibits the `co(7)` on each eigenspace separately. Hypothesis: the eight incidence fibers are constant along the whole 2-parameter family, not just the boost ray. Falsifier: a fiber-cone computation at an independently scaled basepoint (e.g. `(λ_+, λ_-) = (2, 3)`) whose quadric ideal leaves the retained normal form. Cheapest test: one A5-600-style point run (11 s).
- **N2 — 31 = 15 + 16 as orbit geometry.** The 15 mu-fields vanish at every `mu = 0` basepoint, so the effective group on each boundary stratum is 16-dimensional (`G_2` + scaling + boost). A5-414's tangent span falls one short exactly at V, VB and E8-bar; the pair computation now says the boost is the **only** linear supply of the missing direction on W8 and Iso. On E8-bar the boost still moves the source closure (`boost_moves 0`) and only the Pfaffian half fails — so basepoint independence *of the source closure* holds along the boost there too; what is undecided is the pair, i.e. exactly the fiber-cone object. Falsifier: a linear field outside the 16-dim span moving an E8-bar basepoint while stabilizing the closure. Cheapest test: the pending E8 stabilizer rerun.
- **N3 — the ambient Klein group links the sign-conjugate rows.** The ideal table is invariant under `u ↔ v` (each closure ideal fixed; the 3×3 minors flip sign) and under `v → −v`, which **exchanges xp and xm** and conjugates `D` to `−D` — matching `torus_generators`' sign parameter (+1 at N+, −1 at N−, `weight_slice_fiber_relations.py:176-182`) and the status doc's sign-conjugate incidences. Hypothesis: this ambient coordinate Klein group is the transport of the producer-level diagonal sign group of A5-032 (the "every pair carries a diagonal Klein four-group" row), making the X+/X− pairing a geometric identification rather than a coincidence. Falsifier: a retained sign symmetry of a pair that does not come from an ambient coordinate involution. Cheapest test: match A5-032's four sign elements against the four coordinate involutions on one retained header (a table lookup against the existing sweep output).
- **N4** — as stated in F6: the identity control is vacuous; do not count it as discriminating power.
