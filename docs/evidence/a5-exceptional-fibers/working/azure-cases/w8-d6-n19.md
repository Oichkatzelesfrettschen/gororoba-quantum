# Case: W8 -> N- degree 6 at N = 19 on an Azure FX12mds_v2

**Approval.** This case waits for the owner's approval of this file. An earlier approval (2026-11-08) of an FX4 case
priced at 6 to 10 h and 40 to 70 GiB does not carry over: that forecast scaled the product count only and ignored N.

**What it measures.** h_6 of the special fiber F(I) of the Pfaffian kernel-coordinate ideal on the W8 closure at N-
(7 slice variables, 24 generators), as the rank r_19 of the receipt-bound closure space at N = 19, against the target
HF(k[y]/K2, 6) = 8,624. The N follows the law that h_d is reached at N = 3d + 1, measured on ten cells (Iso and W8,
d = 1..5; `../../forecasts/trends-2026-11-06.md` item 8). r_19 = 8,624 extends F(I) = k[y]/K2 for W8 to degree 6;
r_19 below 8,624 either falsifies the N = 3d + 1 law at degree 6 or leaves h_6 open below the count.

**Why no free host answers it.** The laptop probe measured the preflight at 375.62 GiB and forecasts a peak near
160 GiB, up to about 195 GiB (`../../w8-d6-n19-probe/README.md`); the laptop holds 54 GiB, a Kaggle CPU session about
30 GB, the always-free cloud hosts 24 GiB or less. No fold reduces the job: the W8 twisted product factors the base,
not the fiber ring (synthesis, "Folding: what it reaches and what it does not").

**Resource.** A new VM in westus3, Standard_FX12mds_v2 (12 vCPU, 252 GB), Ubuntu with passagemath Singular 4.4.1 and
the engine's pinned Python, retail 1.641 USD/h (prices.azure.com, Linux consumption, read 2026-11-08). Alternative:
Standard_E48ads_v5 (384 GiB), 3.144 USD/h, which clears the preflight without overriding it.

**Run.** One job under `systemd-run --scope -p MemoryMax=240G -p MemorySwapMax=0`, `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2`,
`GOROROBA_WORKERS=4`, `--quotient --k2-receipt receipt-d2.json --export-chunks 5 --dense-tables-gb 0 --max-gb 380`
(the override of the 375.62 GiB preflight rests on the measured degree-5 ratio and the D^2 scaling above), timeout
24 h. The laptop's standard-basis files are copied and hash-checked first, saving about 46 minutes; the tables,
products and closure (the longest stage) run on the VM. RSS is sampled every 60 s; a guard stops the job and starts
the copy-back when RSS passes 225 GiB, so a wrong forecast costs hours, not the run. The runner exits with the
engine's status (#793). Auto-shutdown moves past the deadline (it recurs daily); a watcher copies `out/` and the
export, verifies hashes, checks for live processes and deallocates.

**Expected cost.** 17 to 20 h x 1.641 = 28 to 33 USD, capped at 24 h = 39 USD (E48ads_v5: 53 to 63 USD).

**Falsifiers.** r_19 < 8,624 (the 3d + 1 law or the receipt path at degree 6); a failed nonstandard-product check
(the receipt is prime-specific); blocks 2 and 3 unequal (every W8 run so far has them equal); the top-stratum law
169 = (2 x 6 + 1)^2 for the last jump, if the N = 18 value is also measured.

**Order.** Before this run: the Iso degree-6 pilot on the laptop (free, 2 to 4 h, tests the same N = 3d + 1 law) and
the bounded C30 check (15 min, 8 GiB), which would make this run unnecessary if it proves C30 lies in the fiber. That check ran on
2026-11-08 and finds C30 in the fiber at both primes ([c30-bounded](../../structure/c30-bounded/README.md), ledger row
A5-066, `proved` after the Codex and Grok adversarial reviews repaired two steps), so F(I) = k[y]/K2 and h_6 = 8,624
at both primes without this run. The run stays worth its cost only as an independent test of the rank engine and of
the convergence rule N = 3d + 1 against a known answer.

**Records.** `out/` (json, err, time, events, RSS samples), the runner, `export.json` and the table hashes copy to
`docs/evidence/a5-exceptional-fibers/w8-d6-n19/` before deallocation.

## Reassessment, 2026-11-08: what survives of this case

Recommendation: do not run this case as written. Each premise, tested against the records of 2026-11-08:

- **It decides h_6.** Refuted. The C30 proof (`../../structure/c30-bounded/README.md`, ledger A5-066, `proved`) gives
  F(I) = k[y]/K2 in every degree at both primes, so h_6 = 8,624 (A5-143, A5-149) and, by the t9 sign isomorphism,
  W8 -> N+ (A5-131, A5-137). A rank run reads an answer that is already proved.
- **It tests the N = 3d + 1 law at degree 6.** Superseded. Iso at degree 6 reads r_18 = 2,253 below and r_19 = 2,422 at
  the K2 count, at p = 32009, and r_19 = 2,422 at 32029 (`../../iso-d6-n19-laptop/README.md`), so the law already holds
  at degree 6 on the fiber with the same generator orders (2 and 3). W8 would add a twelfth cell.
- **It tests engine v11 at scale against a known answer.** Survives, and it is the only thing that survives. Iso d = 6
  ran at quotient dimension 59,983; W8 d = 6 runs at 235,543, four times larger, and its answer is fixed in advance:
  r_19 = 8,624 with blocks 2,198 / 2,170 / 2,128 / 2,128 (the character-block counts of the standard monomials of
  k[y]/K2 in degree 6, computed with the engine's own `standard_monomials` and block rule; the same computation gives
  the measured blocks at d = 3 and d = 5), and, under the last-jump law A5-244, r_18 = 8,455 with blocks
  2,149 / 2,128 / 2,089 / 2,089. Any other value is an engine defect, which is what the run would find.
- **It needs 17 to 20 h on an FX12.** Weakened. The engine map (`docs/local/w8-engine-map.md`, not tracked; a call
  graph of 61 functions and measured peaks at d = 3 and d = 4 that the memory model reproduces within 7 %) puts the
  products stage at 129 GiB (the dense products matrix L alone is 103 GiB) and the closure at 132 GiB for the measured
  echelon fill 0.67, 192 GiB at full fill, all within 252 GB; it puts the wall time at 18 to 27 h on FX-class cores
  (closure 14 to 19 h, products 4 to 8 h, hypothesis from the d = 4 and d = 5 timings). The 24 h timeout of the run
  line above leaves no margin.
- **A smaller host can run it one character block at a time.** Refuted for this engine. Five of the seven W8
  variables carry nonzero characters, so the closure feeds rows of every block into every other block, and the
  products matrix couples the blocks too; a probe that dropped the other blocks lost rank (d = 4, block 0: 5,023 of
  5,036). A valid streaming schedule would keep one echelon basis resident and read the incoming rows from disk, but
  the 103 GiB products stage is unchanged, so no host below about 130 GiB runs the case.

Engine defects the map found, each a precondition for any future run of this size: the preflight overstates the
closure peak 2.8-fold (375.6 GiB against 132 GiB; the walk temporaries are 0.10 L, not 0.5 L, and the echelon term
assumes full rank with two copies); the Python marshaling of an insert (`Echelon.insert`, about 121 B per entry of chunk
x free columns) reaches 26.5 GiB at chunk 4,000 and no preflight term covers it; the block update holds four basis-sized
arrays and the first closure round copies every basis; L is rebuilt for each of the 24 generators (45 % of the d = 4
products stage); a failed receipt check exits before printing the per-block ranks. If the owner wants the engine test,
the configuration that answers it is: fix the preflight and the marshaling term, pass `--chunk 500`, set the timeout
to 30 h, FX12mds_v2 with `--max-gb 200` and the RSS guard at 225 GiB; expected 30 to 44 USD at 1.641 USD/h.
Cheaper engine checks at the same N rule exist below degree 6 (W8 d = 5 at both primes is already certified at N = 16
with blocks 1,017 / 1,005 / 1,047 / 1,047), so the run buys only the factor of four in scale.
