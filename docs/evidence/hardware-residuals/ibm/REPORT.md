# Residual structure in retained IBM Heron r2 acquisitions: first exploratory pass

Scope. Six offline analyses of the shot-ordered records under `results/live/` (64 acquisitions, 74 jobs, 2,232 PUB files, 107,631 classical bit columns on ibm_fez and ibm_kingston), the job-stack follow-up and `exact_laws.py`. No provider call, submission, credential read or random draw. Every null is named by its law. Exact conditional laws of a uniform shot arrangement: hypergeometric same-shot pair counts, the adjacency-of-ones and run-count laws, and the circular-shift distribution of the dispersion statistic; `exact_laws.py` checks each against enumeration of every arrangement at small sizes (`exact_laws_run.log`). Asymptotic laws of independent stationary shots: the exponential, Fisher, Gamma and F periodogram laws and t tests over replicate PUBs. Approximations with a stated direction of error: the Poisson tails of analysis 2 and of the off-diagonal ordered pairs of analysis 4, the normal law of the continuation test, and Chernoff bounds of two pooled run-count laws. Deterministic bin-enumeration, circular-shift and hash-reordering nulls complete the set. Scripts, JSON results and full p-value lists (`a*_pvalues.json.gz`) sit in `docs/evidence/hardware-residuals/ibm/`; `aggregate.py` applies one global correction. The files of `a1b`, `a2`, `a3`, `a4`, `a5` and `aggregate` come from reruns under Python 3.14.7, numpy 2.5.4 and scipy 1.18.1, run from the repository root with `GOROROBA_WORKERS` between 4 and 10 under a 12 GiB `systemd-run` scope; `a1_line_scan.json`, `a6_calibration_age.json` and `catalog.json` are the retained files of their original run, recorded under Python 3.14.9, numpy 2.5.4 and scipy 1.18.1.

Limits. IBM documents shot order as unguaranteed and the records keep chunk start and stop times only, so a null bounds structure that survives the service's ordering. Frequencies in Hz use the documented column-wise slot (docs/notes/ibm_quantum/repetition-rate-execution.md): consecutive shots of one PUB lie `sum over the job's PUBs of (rep_delay + circuit_duration x 4 ns)` apart. That sum predicts `circuits_execution_time_ns` to within 0.001 (ratio 1.0000006 to 1.00094) in all 74 jobs; every job holds one executor chunk and equal shots per PUB. The execution-time sum checks the per-circuit period, not the interleaving order. All 74 jobs run with twirling and dynamical decoupling disabled (`options.twirling`, `options.dynamical_decoupling`), so the runtime adds no designed shot grouping.

## Analysis 1. Shot-order line scan across all 64 acquisitions

Falsifier (`a1_line_scan.py`, fixed before the run). Per channel (each bit column, the parity of each multi-bit register, the sum of all bits of a PUB with 16 or more columns) the normalized periodogram of shot order is an exponential law under independent shots. Fisher's max-ordinate law gives a white-line p-value; the ratio of an ordinate to the mean of its 64 neighbors is `(1 + r/64)^-64` under a locally flat spectrum, giving a narrow-line p-value; the lowest eight bins are Gamma(8,1), giving a drift p-value. "No structure" means all three stay above 0.05/348,882 and the bulk follows the exponential law. A Hoeffding-bound variant guards the white statistic. The bulk fractions validate the marginal exponential law only; the tails of the maximum and of the local ratio are asymptotic under independent shots, and slow drift and short-lag dependence of the same records (analyses 4 and 5) color the spectrum. Channels with fewer than 30 minority shots are not tested.

Data. 64 acquisitions, 74 jobs, 2,232 PUB files; 116,294 channels tested (86,700 bit columns, 28,088 parities, 1,506 PUB sums); 21,417 skipped. Reader: `common.load_pub` with the index digest check; `reader_crosscheck.json` shows all 104 registers of four runs identical to `n21_exact_planning.retained_registers`.

Result.
- Bulk calibration over 384,624,572 ordinates: fractions above c = 3.0, 4.605, 6.908 are 0.04980, 0.01010, 0.00108 against 0.04979, 0.01000, 0.00100.
- Controls: a reordering of the 149-qubit prep0 PUB by the splitmix64 hash of the shot index reads 6.4 percent of 141 columns below 0.05 (line) and 5.7 percent (drift), against 14.2 and 20.6 percent unscrambled. A multiplicative index scramble is no control: a dilation only permutes periodogram bins, which the first attempt showed. A pulse comb of added probability 1.6 and 0.4 percent is recovered at a multiple of its fundamental at 10^-139 and 10^-21.
- Narrow lines (122 pass the analysis line, 118 the global line): 69 at 59.98-60.05 Hz in seven jobs, 37 at 29.70-29.72 Hz in six jobs, 16 elsewhere: 52.8-53.2 Hz (two jobs), 42.21 Hz, 127.0 and 139.8 Hz (one job each), and bins at exactly 0.125 and 0.25 cycles per shot (period 8 and 4 shots) in one register of one pathion-map job, a list-position periodicity that twirling does not explain (all jobs untwirled; inferred circuit or replica packing).
- White lines: 332 pass the analysis line (5.59 percent of channels below 0.05, 0.71 percent below 0.001), mostly the slow Ramsey-phase oscillations of n21-orbit runs at 1 to 2 Hz.
- Drift: 1,232 pass the analysis line; 375 of the strongest 500 on ibm_kingston, 125 on ibm_fez.
- Detectable sinusoidal modulation of P(1) at the Bonferroni line (50 percent power): median 0.049, 10th to 90th percentile 0.019 to 0.083, which bounds amplitudes of the null columns.

Job stack (`a1b_job_stack.py`). All PUBs of a job share N and the slot, so one bin is one physical frequency for every column. Stacking normalized ordinates (Gamma(C,1) white; F(2C,128C) neighbor ratio) gives one test per job and family (222 tests). Seven jobs show 60 Hz (a targeted window of +-0.15 Hz, post hoc, counted) and six show 29.70 Hz. Stacked columns of one PUB can share a noise source; positive column correlation raises the variance and upper tail of the stacked sum above the Gamma(C, 1) and F laws, so every stack p-value is anti-conservative and descriptive. Three calibrations that need no column independence sit in `a1b_job_stack.json` (`calibrated`). Bin enumeration: the window statistic is the maximum neighbor-normalized ordinate within 0.15 Hz of 60 Hz (physical prior) or of 29.70 Hz (found in these records, post hoc), compared with the same statistic on every other non-overlapping tile of the same width of the same spectrum (329 to 330 tiles at 4,096 shots; resolution 1/tiles). Cross-unit counts: the number of jobs, designs (slot, shots and PUB count) and PUBs whose window ranks at 0.01 or 0.05 or better, against the Poisson-binomial law of the attainable thresholds (items 1 and 2). Job-level Bonferroni minimum: the smallest column p-value of a job from the column scan times the job's number of columns, valid under any dependence. At the PUB level 59 of 1,763 PUBs (84 of 1,877 at 29.70 Hz) rank at 0.01 or better against 15.4 (15.5) expected, and 845 of 81,548 columns against 719 expected (962 of 84,213 against 713 at 29.70 Hz); the columns of a PUB are dependent, so those counts are descriptive. The 60 Hz stack passes the global line in five jobs by the Gamma and F laws and ranks first to third of its tiles in all seven.

Verdict: **structure found** (items 1 to 3; minor lines at 53 Hz and above).

## Analysis 2. Burst multiplicity and temporal clustering

Falsifier (`a2_burst.py`). Per PUB with at least 24 ground-prepared columns, block-local (512-shot) marginals set a Poisson-binomial null. Five tests: dispersion of the error count; upper-tail counts at two thresholds fixed from the marginals (expected 5 and 0.5 shots; Poisson approximation); adjacency at lag 1 and within lags 1 to 16 of the 1 percent highest-scoring shots against a circular-shift null that keeps each column's own time structure (four golden-ratio shifts estimate a mean that takes a Poisson tail; an approximation). The dispersion p-value is the circular-shift distribution of the same statistic over the full group of shifts: column i rotates by i s mod N shots for every s = 1 ... N-1, the z statistic is recomputed with block-local marginals of the rotated columns, and p = min(1, 2 min(upper, lower)) over the N-1 shifts and the observation. The rank p-value is exact when shots are exchangeable inside independent columns; under drift the rotation moves each column's time structure, and the law serves as a reference that keeps it. The resolution 1/N floors the p-value at 10^-3.91 for a 16,384-shot PUB, 10^-4.21 for a 32,768-shot PUB, 10^-3.31 and 10^-2.71 for the pilot PUBs of 4,096 and 1,024 shots. A sensitivity over the shifts that keep all column offsets at least 16 shots apart (the per-qubit dependence of analysis 4 falls by 0.55 to 0.7 per lag) sits beside it; the 1,024-shot PUBs have none. 180 tests.

Data. The 153 sensors of 24 ibm_fez actor PUBs (73,728 shots, error = outcome xor prepared bit, all sensors prepared in |0>) and the ground-prepared first read of 12 wide ibm_kingston state-prep PUBs. Not run: the 156-bit and 58-bit PUBs of `results/live/kingston-wide-control-result` (512 shots, no record of the prepared state); the footprint-connectivity statistic (no PUB shows a tail above the independence law, so no footprint exists to test).

Result.
- ibm_fez pilot (named target): pooled dispersion ratio 1.0077 +- 0.0057 (95 percent upper bound 1.019); the 24 PUBs span 0.88 to 1.07 with 2 below 0.05 (1.2 expected); pooled upper-tail count 107 against 90.6; adjacency 0.85 times the shift null. The descriptive excess at five or more errors in `docs/evidence/ibm-fez-actor-pilot/findings.md` disappears under block-local marginals.
- ibm_kingston extension: dispersion ratio 1.014 to 1.074 (median 1.046, per-PUB SE 0.009 to 0.013, z 1.5 to 6.7); 11 of 12 below 0.05 under the shift law; 8 pass the analysis line at the law's resolution floor and none passes the global line. Pooled upper-tail count 2.18 times the Poisson-binomial value; no PUB passes at the lower threshold. Adjacency is 3.79 times the permutation mean but 1.43 times the circular-shift null; no PUB passes, so per-qubit temporal dependence explains the adjacency.

Verdict: pilot **null (bound: dispersion ratio below 1.019; no clustering of high-multiplicity shots beyond per-qubit temporal dependence)**; ibm_kingston extension **structure found** (same-shot cross-qubit covariance, about 4.6 percent of register variance).

## Analysis 4. Cross-qubit lag-1 temporal dependence

Falsifier (`a4_lag1_memory.py`). The count C_ji of (error of j at shot n, error of i at shot n+1) has exact mean (K_j K_i - J_ji)/N under a uniform reordering of whole shot rows (J_ji the same-shot joint count). One two-sided test per ordered pair, diagonal included. On the diagonal the adjacent-ones count has the exact law P(R = r) = C(K-1, r-1) C(N-K+1, r) / C(N, K) for R runs of ones, convolved over the pilot's 24 PUBs. Off the diagonal a Poisson law at the exact mean stands in; the exact variance of C_ji pooled over a dataset's off-diagonal pairs reads 0.85 to 0.96 of the pooled mean (median pair 0.97 to 0.98, no pair above 1), so the Poisson tail is conservative there (an inference from the variance, not a tail computation). Survivors are re-evaluated at lags 2 and 3 (drift decays slowly, a dependence of one shot at once); a block-local variant of the diagonal test permutes shots only inside 512-shot blocks (boundary pairs excluded); a chain prediction (same-shot correlation times per-qubit dependence) separates a directed cross-qubit effect from two known effects. 182,967 tests.

Data. ibm_fez actor pilot pooled per ordered pair over 24 PUBs (23,409 pairs; list neighbors lie one 8-circuit slot, 308 to 468 us, apart). Extension: 12 ibm_kingston state-prep PUBs, one PUB per job, so list neighbors are consecutive shots 31 to 261 us apart.

Positive control. Three static PUBs in one job, qubit 147: P(prep0 at s+1 = 1 | remeasure first read at s = 1) = 0.0825 against 0.0200 after a 0 (count 187 against an exchangeable mean of 55.0, z = 17.8; the repository's README gives 0.0825, 0.020, z = 18.7); the earlier-step arm reads 55 against 55.0.

Result.
- ibm_fez pilot (named target): 0 of 153 qubits pass; diagonal z mean -0.001, SD 0.99; median conditional-to-marginal ratio 0.90; coupled pairs read 0.996 times the mean. One ordered pair (123 to 86, 8 against 0.71, 10^-5.8, flat at lags 2 and 3) sits at the analysis line (-5.67) and below the global line. A qubit with the median pooled event count (13 expected) would need a ratio of 2.6 to pass.
- ibm_kingston extension: significant per-qubit adjacency in 27, 7, 32, 18, 15, 16 qubits (prep0 PUBs) and 6, 55, 106, 41, 43, 43 qubits (x_history PUBs); 0 negative. The block-local null keeps 26, 7, 31, 16, 13, 15 and 6, 55, 106, 41, 42, 42 of them. The median ratio is 1.3 to 1.9 (prep0) and 4.3 to 13.8 (x_history) at 20 to 50 us repetition delay, 0.65 to 0.79 at 250 us. Among significant qubits the lag-1 excess over the mean is 5.6 to 10.1 times, lag 2 is 1.0 to 7.2 and lag 3 is 0.7 to 5.1 (lags 2 and 3 are free of the lag-1 selection); the decay per lag is about 0.55 at 61 us and 0.7 at 31 us spacing, a lifetime near 90 to 110 us. Verdict: pilot **null (bound: per-qubit dependence ratio below about 2.6 to 4 at 300 us spacing)**; ibm_kingston extension **structure found** (the q147 effect generalizes to 6 to 106 qubits per PUB, beyond 512-shot nonstationarity). The mechanism is open (hypothesis: a 90 to 110 us decay lifetime is comparable to T1; untested).

## Analysis 5. Run-length structure of ground-prepared reads

Falsifier (`a5_run_length.py`). For a column of N reads with K ones the count of runs of at least L ones has an exact law: the N-K zeros cut the arrangement into N-K+1 gaps filled by the K ones as a uniform weak composition, so the binomial moments are closed forms and P(count >= m) is an alternating sum evaluated in Python integers. Its mean is `E_L = (N-L+1) F_L - (N-L) F_{L+1}`, with F_L a ratio of falling factorials. One-sided exact tests per column (L = 2, 3, 5), per group (L = 2, 3, 4, 5; the pooled law is the convolution of the columns' laws, or for state-prep run lengths of 2, whose convolution exceeds the exact budget, the Chernoff upper bound of the exact generating function), and a memory-free continuation test per group (runs of 3 or more against sum R2^2/R1, binomial variance, a normal approximation). 4,397 tests. Each column's pmf stops where its upper tail lies below 1e-300 (bisection on the exact tail), so a pooled p-value written as -300 states p below about 10^-297. Reads are two-level (`meas_type` classified), so the analysis measures run lengths of 1 outcomes and cannot name a hidden state.

Data. 1,459 columns: the 153 pilot sensors pooled over 24 PUBs (3,672 columns), and the ground-prepared first read of every qubit of 14 state-prep PUBs (653 prep0, 647 x_history, 6 remeasure columns).

## Analysis 6. Calibration-age dependence of readout excess

Falsifier (`a6_calibration_age.py`). Excess y = f - e10 per qubit, f the excited fraction of the ground-prepared read, e10 = `prob_meas1_prep0` of the plan snapshot (a multiple of 1/4096). Because f carries initialization excitation and readout error, y is the ground-read excess. Regress y on plan-snapshot age in hours with qubit and (arm x repetition delay) fixed effects, cluster-robust over plan-snapshot stamps (t, dof = clusters - 1). Skill test: a fresher snapshot must predict f better. "No age dependence" means the slope interval contains 0 at the global correction.

Data. 13 state-prep runs (14 PUBs, 1,306 qubit-run rows): plan ages 0.30 to 3.70 h, five distinct plan stamps (ages 0.38, 0.41, 0.46, 0.84, 3.70 h, all ibm_kingston). Not usable: the two runs with 18 h old snapshots (2026-11-07 pathion maps) hold no ground-prepared column; idle_ground PUBs have no recorded bit-to-qubit map (candidate maps correlate with e10 at -0.23 and +0.08); baseline_ground is one 6-qubit run; the actor pilot keeps calibration medians only, at snapshot age 0.23 to 0.24 h.

Result.
- e10 of the plan snapshot equals the execution snapshot to the last digit in all 12 runs that carry both (maximum difference 0.0), including pairs 0.6 h and 3.3 h apart. The skill test is **not run** (no distinct reference), and a snapshot stamp is not the age of its readout numbers.
- Slope of ground-read excess: +0.00159 per hour, analysis-level 95 percent interval [0.00107, 0.00210], t = 8.49, 4 degrees of freedom, p = 10^-3.0. At the global alpha the interval is [-0.0158, +0.0190] per hour (t critical 93.1). Leave-one-stamp-out slopes are +0.00135, +0.00139, +0.00542 (dropping the 3.70 h stamp), +0.00175, +0.00159 per hour, all positive.
- Excess is near 0 at 50 and 250 us repetition delay (-0.0007 to +0.0011) and +0.0018 to +0.0096 at 20 us; the 3.70 h stamp is also a 69-qubit session at 20 us, so age is confounded with date and repetition delay.

Verdict: **null (bound: at the global correction the slope lies within -0.016 to +0.019 per hour over 0.3 to 3.7 h; no bound exists for older snapshots because no retained record pairs one with a ground-prepared read)**. The nominal positive slope with a stable sign in all leave-one-out fits is a lead to test on a design that varies age within one session.

## Observed versus inferred

Observed: the exponential-law calibration of the periodogram bulk; lines at 60.00 Hz (three slots) and 29.70 Hz (three slots, weakly outside one); the same-shot dispersion and distance gradient; the per-qubit lag-1 temporal dependence with lag-2 and lag-3 decay; the run-length excess and its concentration; the e10 equality between snapshots; the q147 positive control. Inferred: that 60.00 Hz is line-frequency pickup into the qubit frequency (US mains frequency; phase-sensitive circuits only); that the 29.70 Hz source is environmental; that the run-length excess has a hidden-state origin (it equally fits slow drift); that the decay timescale relates to T1.

## Checks not run

A nonstationary-Markov or block-local null for the run-length law; phase-preserving or moving-block surrogates of single-column periodograms (the bin enumeration calibrates only the 60 Hz and 29.70 Hz windows); the exact joint law of the cross-lag count C_ji (the exact variance only bounds the direction of the Poisson error); a calibration-snapshot-level test of the pilot (one session); direct two-excited-level (|2>) population or IQ data (`meas_type` is classified in every record); footprint connectivity of bursts; the readout-feedline attribution of one-hop pairs; the wide-control PUBs (no prepared state); phase coherence of the lines across PUBs against wall-clock offsets (Ramsey response phases are unknown); the fresh-versus-stale skill test; age data from idle_ground and baseline_ground; any family outside the ground-prepared sets beyond analysis 1; replication of the lag and run-length structure on another backend (all wide ground-prepared PUBs except the pilot are ibm_kingston).
