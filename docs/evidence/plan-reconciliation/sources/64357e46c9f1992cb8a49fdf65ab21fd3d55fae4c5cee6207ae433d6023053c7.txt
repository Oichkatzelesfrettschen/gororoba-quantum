# Tuna-17 run design: platform configuration and the packed box-kite follow series

A Tuna-17 batch costs the account about three minutes of wall time no
matter what it holds: 120 s of enforced pause, a few seconds of fixed cost per
job, collection and checks. The scarce resource is circuits per batch (three)
and batches per hour, not shots. This note records what the platform
documentation, the API and the retained runs establish about that cost, and
how the box-kite follow series (`scripts/box_kite_cases.py`, `FOLLOW_PLAN`)
spends it. `docs/evidence/box_kite_follow_design/design.json` recomputes every
measured number below from retained records (`scripts/box_kite_follow_design.py`,
gates core-439 and core-440).

## Platform configuration

| Fact | Consequence for a case | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tuna-17's default compiler passes are SWAP2CZDecomposer then CZDecomposer; mapping, routing and optimization are empty | the gate list runs in the order and on the qubits written; adjacent self-inverse pairs execute | catalogue `default_compiler_config`; `docs/evidence/quantum_inspire/toolchain_check.json` preserves the gate and control sequence of every follow and twin circuit |
| The control stack schedules as late as possible after decomposition; `barrier` and `wait(n)` (20 ns cycles) are the only timing controls | one-qubit gates slide late and line up across qubits; a barrier run fixes an order | [Tuna page](site/kbase_tuna-operational-specifics.md); `scripts/qi_toolchain_check.py` docstring |
| Native: Rx, Ry, Rz, X90, Y90, mX90, mY90, X, Y, Z, I, S, Sdag, T, Tdag, CZ; H, CNOT, SWAP, CR, CRk are decomposed | H costs more than one instruction; its decomposition is not published | Tuna page |
| A job submits `number_of_shots` and `raw_data_enabled` only; `compile_stage` labels a file and changes nothing | no repetition-delay, initialization-time or active-reset option exists; `reset` is measure and flip | `docs/notes/quantum_inspire/api` OpenAPI `JobIn`, `FileIn` |
| `raw_data_enabled` returns one bit string per shot; `qi_platform.py` always requests it and retains `raw/` | per-shot data are available; whether their order is temporal is untested | catalogue `supports_raw_data`; `scripts/qi_platform.py` |
| At most 3 jobs queued on a hardware backend, abuse removes the account; "avoid unnecessary loss of processor hours" | the repository submits 3 circuits per batch, one batch at a time, 120 s apart | [FAQ](site/faq.md); terms |
| The platform publishes no calibration data | the account's own readout pairs, coupler-ZZ map and T0 ladder are the device model | `docs/notes/quantum_inspire/tuna-17.md` |

## Measured constraints

**Shot cost.** Over the 159 completed Tuna-17 jobs retained (4096, 8192,
16384 and 131072 shots), execution time fits 2.62 +- 0.22 s per job plus
0.534 +- 0.003 ms per shot (residual 1.5 s): the 500 us initialization
dominates, so a 16384-shot job takes about 11 s, the 21-job follow series
about 240 s and a 131072-shot job about 72 s. The fit also carries 0.42 +-
0.30 us per CZ line per shot, against the documented 60 ns: a 1.4-sigma term
with no known mechanism, which may be control-stack time rather than qubit
time. The fit over the 105 jobs before the follow series predicted 233.6 s
for it; its 21 jobs executed in 243.3 s, 4% more, about two residual
standard deviations of a 21-job sum. The 126-job fit predicted 1462 s for
the ladder series; its 21 jobs at 131072 shots executed in 1536 s, 5% more,
70.7 to 77.3 s each.

**Readout drift.** Across the three T0 batches (15:41Z to 15:48Z), e01 and
e10 of the six LCU qubits moved by at most 0.0071 (q13, |1> misassignment);
most per-qubit homogeneity chi-squares sit at their two degrees of freedom.
A readout pair in every batch therefore buys little: readout pairs at the
start, middle and end of the series bracket every witness within one batch,
and `BRACKET_DRIFT_MAX` = 0.02, about three times the observed spread, stops
the series. Differences of two replicas on one qubit in one batch, such as
the twist test, carry no readout correction to first order.

**Simultaneous pairs.** The admitted coupling graph (TUNA17 without q0, q5,
q6, q7, q9 and q15) has 10 couplers; its largest induced matching has 3
couplers and there are 5 of them, `LCU_PAIRS` among them. Re-admitting q0,
q9 and q15, whose readout recovered, raises the count of maximum matchings
but not their size, so three LCU replicas per circuit is the device's limit
under the no-shared-coupler rule.

**Walk placement.** A star walk (control plus three data) and a path walk
(control plus two) fit in one circuit without a shared qubit or coupler in 12
ways, all of them star 14 with path 4. Under the coherent ZZ model of the
design record, the data order (11, 12, 16) on star 14 keeps |<X_c>| at 0.947
with a 0.8 us CZ window, against 0.751 to 0.889 for the other orders; the
60 ns window gives at least 0.995 for every order. Path 4 with data (1, 2)
keeps 0.978. Static ZZ costs under one percent at the documented CZ time, so
placement matters only if the CZ window is long.

**Phase scale.** With the targets' Ramsey detunings of 2026-10-16
(coupler-ZZ map), the walk control's phase about omega is -7 to -8 degrees
for a 60 ns CZ window and -54 (star) to -63 (path) degrees for a 0.8 us
window. Detunings drift between days and each CZ adds its residual local
phase on top, so these numbers set the scale the phase readouts resolve
(about 0.5 degree at 16384 shots), not a prediction, and a measured phase
alone does not identify the CZ window.

## The follow series

Seven batches, 21 jobs, 16384 shots each:

| Batch | Circuits | What they measure |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | readout-0, readout-1, walk-omit | readout bracket; both omit-last controls |
| 1 | lcu-bright-x-1, lcu-bright-x-2, depth-9-x | bright crossover in X; bright edge at CZ depth 9 on all pairs, X |
| 2 | lcu-bright-y-1, lcu-bright-y-2, depth-9-y | the same in Y |
| 3 | readout-0, readout-1, walk-x | readout bracket; both walk signs (star 14: 6 CZ, path 4: 4 CZ) |
| 4 | walk-y, lcu-dark-x-1, lcu-dark-x-2 | both walks read in Y; dark crossover and strut in X |
| 5 | lcu-dark-y-1, lcu-dark-y-2, depth-5-x | dark crossover and strut in Y; depth 5, X |
| 6 | depth-5-y, readout-0, readout-1 | depth 5, Y; readout bracket |

The X and Y circuits that one Bloch vector combines run in one batch or in
consecutive ones, and walk-x and walk-y run as consecutive jobs, so a phase
drift between the two quadratures has one pause, about two minutes, to act
in; `validate_series` refuses a plan whose pair lies more than
`QUADRATURE_SPAN` = 1 batch apart. The ladder slopes rest on depths 1 and 9
(an unweighted fit on 1, 5, 9 gives depth 5 zero weight), and those two
share a batch in each setting.

- **Crossover.** In each lcu circuit pair, the edge (1,10)(4,15) of kite 3
  at y_sign y and its twist (4,10)(1,15) of kite 6 at -y trade the pairs
  (1, 4) and (10, 13). The average of the two same-pair differences cancels
  both the pair offset and the circuit offset, so twist equality is tested
  at shot noise, without the 0.1007 pair allowance T0 imposed. The edge and
  the twist differ only in local Z, S and Sdag gates, which Tuna executes
  as written, so a nonzero difference measures a gate-dependent error.
- **Two observables per replica.** A setting on the data qubit leaves the
  selector marginal unchanged, so every lcu replica gives the T1
  classification statistic and a conditional coherence. Bright rows populate
  the a = 0 branch (census E_0 = -1 in X), dark rows the a = 1 branch
  (E_1 = +1), the strut both (0); Y reads the imaginary part, 0 for every row.
- **Coherence depth ladder.** The bright edge at CZ depths 1, 5 and 9 in X
  and Y on all three pairs gives magnitude and phase against depth beside the
  population of the same replicas. The joint coherence Pc(n) m(n) and the
  population contrast 2 Pc(n) - 1 both decay as lambda^n under a global
  depolarizing channel, while the conditional magnitude m(n) alone decays as
  lambda^n / Pc(n); their rate ratio r_coh / r_pop separates dephasing
  (below 1) from depolarizing error. The phase slope per CZ is the data
  qubit's detuning phase over one CZ window plus the CZ's residual local
  phase; the fit unwraps the phase along depth, so it identifies a slope
  below pi / 4 rad per CZ in magnitude, since adjacent depths lie four CZs
  apart.
- **Walks.** walk-x reads the associator signs, walk-omit the branch-marked
  controls, walk-y the control's imaginary part; with walk-x it gives the
  control's phase and the coherence magnitude that T0's r cross-predicts.

Against the plan it replaces (T1, coherence and walk stages: 7 batches of one
witness and a readout pair, 7 witnesses with 18 replicas at 4096 shots), the
follow series runs in the same 7 batches with 15 witnesses and 42 replicas at
16384 shots. It adds the allowance-free twist test, both-branch Bloch
vectors, the coherence ladder and the walk phase, and cost 243 s of
execution.

## The ladder series

The pause guard of `qi_platform.py submit` refuses a hardware batch until
every hardware job of the account has been finished for 120 s, so the pause
falls per batch. The three jobs inside a batch run back to back. Shots are
priced by the timing fit and capped at 131072 per job and 300 s per job.
Shots inside one job are therefore the cheap resource.

That only pays where the comparison sits inside a job:
- The follow series' X and Y replicas of one depth-9 edge, a batch apart,
  scatter by about 0.03 beyond shot noise, which no shot count removes.
- The shot record (claim `qi-tuna17-shot-record-50hz-line`) adds a second
  within-job resource: every shot carries its phase of the 50 Hz line, so a
  longer job gives more line cycles and finer phase bins.

The ladder series (`LADDER_PLAN`) moves every comparison it makes into one
job or one batch:

| Batch | Circuits | What they measure |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | readout-0, readout-1, readout-iso-x | bracket; q1, q13, q14 excited alone against all 17 excited |
| 1 | latin-x-1, latin-x-2, latin-x-3 | every pair at CZ depths 1, 5 and 9 in X, inside one batch |
| 2 | latin-y-1, latin-y-2, latin-y-3 | the same in Y |
| 3 | readout-0, readout-1, readout-iso-hzh | bracket; q1, q13, q14 prepared by H Z H, alone |
| 4 | latin-x-2, latin-x-3, latin-x-1 | batch 1 repeated, circuit order rotated |
| 5 | latin-y-2, latin-y-3, latin-y-1 | batch 2 repeated, circuit order rotated |
| 6 | readout-0, readout-1, readout-iso-x | bracket; the context readout again |

- **Latin square.** Circuit c places the bright edge at depth
  LADDER_DEPTHS[(p + c - 1) % 3] on pair p. Every job therefore holds depths
  1, 5 and 9 at once, and every pair reads all three depths inside one
  batch. The ladder of one pair never crosses a batch boundary, and the
  depth-9 pair of every job gives the line's phase reference for the
  shallower pairs.
- **Replication.** Batches 4 and 5 repeat the square with the circuit
  order rotated. The difference of rho = r_coh / r_pop between batches 1 and
  4 measures between-batch scatter at the ladder level, and the rotation
  separates a circuit from its position in the batch.
- **Readout context.** LADDER_ISO_QUBITS = (1, 13, 14) takes one qubit of
  each pair, none coupled to another, so in readout-iso-x every excited
  qubit has each coupled neighbor in |0>. That is the context q13 reads in
  on the bright branch of a witness. readout-iso-hzh reaches |1> by H Z H,
  the gate path of a witness's data qubit. e10 from readout-1 against
  readout-iso-x separates context-dependent readout; readout-iso-hzh
  against readout-iso-x separates the preparation path. LADDER_CONTEXT_MIN =
  0.02 is about three times the largest e10 move between follow brackets.
- **Line.** Each Latin job is scanned and locked in at its peak. The
  preregistered falsifier is a job without the line, or a job whose
  depth-9 amplitude does not exceed its depth-1 amplitude by 5 standard
  errors. Each pair's contrast is also reported in eight bins of the line's
  phase.

Budget at LADDER_SHOTS = 131072, the platform cap, from the timing fit:
- 2.92 s + 0.509 ms x 131072 = 69.6 s per job from the 126-job fit, against
  the 300 s limit, and 1462 s for the 21 jobs.
- With the seven pauses and collection, about 50 minutes of wall time.
- About 3 MB of raw shots per job, and 2621 cycles of the 50 Hz line.
The QX twins of the six Latin circuits run in two emulator batches first.
All 27 circuits pass their 117 checks on local qxelarator.

## Libraries

- scipy.signal.zoom_fft scans a job's shot record in a narrow band and a
  numpy least-squares lock-in reads every bit's amplitude and phase at the
  peak, all bits of a job in one call (`scripts/qi_shot_record_line.py`).
- numpy carries the design model (state vectors of at most four qubits under
  diagonal ZZ and detuning phases), the timing least squares and the
  analysis bootstraps, batched over draws.
- Qiskit builds and state-vector-checks every emitted circuit; libqasm and
  OpenSquirrel replay the platform's parse and compile passes
  (`qi_toolchain_check.py`); qxelarator runs the identical bytes locally
  before any QX or Tuna submission.
- Enumeration with itertools is exact and immediate at this graph size (11
  admitted qubits, 10 couplers), so no graph library enters the design; the
  placement scores run through `parallel.ordered_map`.
- stim models Pauli channels and cannot carry the coherent ZZ and detuning
  phases the design turns on. mthree's independent calibration equals the
  per-qubit tensor inversion at two qubits per replica. The follow run shows
  a context-dependent readout error on q13 (Results below), which a
  calibration taken in the all-excited readout-1 state cannot carry, whatever
  the inversion. quimb and cotengra pay off far above seven active
  qubits. pyzx and other optimizers would cancel the local-phase pairs whose
  cost the twist test measures. galois has no GF(2) synthesis to do: the
  walks are fixed CNOT sequences from the census.

## Results, 2026-10-17

The series ran from 23:08Z to 23:29Z, after its five QX twin batches read
every deterministic replica exactly; every batch passed its checks, the
brackets moved at most 0.0076, and the verdict is continue with no stop and
no refutation (`results/quantum_inspire/box_kite_tuna_17_follow_analysis.json`,
gate core-425; claims `qi-tuna17-boxkite-follow-crossover` and
`qi-tuna17-boxkite-follow-ladder-drift`).

- Classification: all six rows, |D| 0.916 to 0.967, the strut D = 0.001.
- Twist equality in all four block x setting crossovers: |Delta| at most
  0.0040, |z| at most 2.44, with no pair allowance.
- Coherence: every depth-1 X sign as the census states; Y components at most
  0.052 in magnitude; strut branches inside 0.1 + 3 sigma, the largest
  +0.135 against 0.136.
- Walks: <X_c> = -0.784 (star) and -0.937 (path) with omega's sign,
  magnitudes 0.819 and 0.938 inside 0.10 of r^n_CZ, phases 0.297 and 0.052
  rad about omega; the path omit control reads -0.119 against a limit of
  0.126.
- Every depth-5 and depth-9 job carries the 50 Hz line of
  [tuna-17.md](tuna-17.md) on its selectors, at amplitude 0.09 to 0.26 and
  in phase across the three pairs; the depth-1 replicas carry none.
- The X and Y replicas of a depth-9 bright edge, which sit in consecutive
  batches and share the selector marginal, differ by 0.024 to 0.033 on all
  three pairs, 4 to 7 shot-noise sigma: deep-circuit populations scatter by
  about 0.03 between batches. The ladder's reported intervals carry shot
  noise only, so r_coh / r_pop = 1.040 and 1.047 on (1,4) and (10,13) and
  the (12,14) depth-9 population 0.483 are not established; depths 1 and 9
  in X share batch-01, which a within-batch ladder can use.
- Against T0, seven and a half hours earlier, the
  bright-edge populations fell by 0.209 on (12,14) at depth 9 and by 0.106
  on (10,13) at depth 5.
- q13: the readout-1 bracket gives e10 = 0.091, and the bright (10,13)
  coherence's raw -0.911 corrects to -1.105; on the dark branch, where e01
  enters, raw 0.942 corrects to 0.990. The all-qubit bracket overstates
  q13's |1> misassignment in the witness context, through neighbor-dependent
  readout or through readout-1's own X on q13.

## Ladder results, 2026-10-18

The series ran from 01:55Z to 03:02Z at 131072 shots per job. Both QX twin
batches read every replica exactly, every batch passed its stop rules, and
the brackets moved e01 and e10 by at most 0.009
(`results/quantum_inspire/box_kite_tuna_17_ladder_analysis.json`, gate
core-425; claims `qi-tuna17-boxkite-ladder-readout-context`,
`qi-tuna17-boxkite-ladder-within-batch` and
`qi-tuna17-boxkite-ladder-drift-harmonics`).

- **q13 readout.** With all 17 qubits excited, q13's e10 reads 0.090 and
  0.086; with every coupled neighbor in |0> it reads 0.044 and 0.040. The
  difference of 0.045 is about 45 standard errors, and the preregistered
  rule establishes context on q13. q1 and q14 show none. The two circuits
  differ in the neighbors' state at measurement and in the X pulses on the
  neighbors, so the rule does not name which one moves q13. The H Z H
  preparation moves q13's isolated e10 by +0.016, below the 0.02 threshold.
  Corrected with the isolated e10, the depth-1 coherence on (10,13) reads
  -0.997 and -0.955, against -1.104 and -1.060 with readout-1. The follow
  series' -1.105 is therefore an artifact of the all-qubit readout-1
  bracket.
- **rho.** r_coh / r_pop lies above 1 on all three pairs in both X batches:
  1.020 to 1.022 on (1,4) and (10,13), and 1.005 to 1.006 on (12,14).
  Population contrast decays faster than joint coherence, which neither
  global depolarizing nor dephasing gives. Only (12,14) scatters between the
  replicates beyond 3 SE. With every iso qubit's e10 taken from
  readout-iso-x, rho moves by at most 0.0004 and every 99% interval still
  excludes 1, so the decision holds under both corrections.
- **Line.**
  - The line is detected in all 12 Latin jobs.
  - The depth-9 over depth-1 margin holds in 10 of them. It fails where the
    depth-9 pair is (12,14) in batches 4 and 5: that selector's first
    harmonic drops from 0.025 to 0.003 while its second harmonic stays at
    0.020 to 0.022.
  - None of the 9 readout circuits carries the line at 131072 shots.
- **Drift.** Against the follow series, the depth-9 bright populations rose
  by 0.07, 0.09 and 0.39, with (12,14) moving from 0.483 to 0.877.

### Ladder line-phase bins (exploratory)

`scripts/qi_ladder_line_bins.py` re-reads the 12 Latin jobs' raw shots with the
ladder estimators after cutting each job into 8 line-phase bins, bin 0 at the
depth-9 selector's P1 peak, and tests the max(A1, A2) line statistic
(`results/quantum_inspire/box_kite_tuna_17_ladder_line_bins.json`, gates
core-433 and core-550). Both rules were chosen after the ladder data were
read, so the record is exploratory and binds a future run only. Its bootstrap
stream, numpy seed [20261017, 2], differs from the ladder's.

- **rho by phase.** A peak and a trough pooled into one cell restore the
  covariance of the modulation with the decay, so the stationary estimate
  averages ln rho over bin 0 and bin 4, each fitted on its own shots. Against
  the job-averaged value, rho reads:

  | pair | job average | mean over 8 bins | stationary bins 0 and 4 | bin 0 | bin 4 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | (1,4) | 1.0208 | 1.0259 | 1.0300 | 1.058 | 1.0026 |
  | (10,13) | 1.0214 | 1.0274 | 1.0312 | 1.063 | 1.0004 |
  | (12,14) | 1.0056 | 1.0058 | 1.0086 | 1.011 | 1.0059 |

  Bins 0 and 4 are extrema of the line only where the depth-9 selector's
  first harmonic supplies the phase, so the stationary verdict combines
  batch 1 alone, the only X batch whose jobs all carry a detected line and a
  significant depth-9 first harmonic: 1.0326, 1.0303 and 1.0075, with 99%
  intervals 1.0297 to 1.0358, 1.0272 to 1.0332 and 1.0059 to 1.0092, all
  excluding 1. Both X batches together (the table) give 1.0280 to 1.0322,
  1.0291 to 1.0334 and 1.0074 to 1.0098, and the readout-iso-x correction
  moves those by at most 0.0003. The ln-mean over all 8 bins, which carries no choice of bin, also reads at or above the job
  average, so job-averaging does not raise rho here and the modulation
  covariance does not explain the excess.
- **Phase profile.** The excess follows the line. On (1,4) and (10,13) rho
  is 1.05 to 1.07 in bins 7, 0 and 1, around the depth-9 selector's lowest
  population contrast, and 1.000 to 1.004 in bins 3 to 5, where the 99%
  interval of (10,13) includes 1. A coherent selector-population error
  that is itself modulated by the line fits this profile; a depth-only
  error would leave rho flat across bins.
- **Reference.** In batch 4 the depth-9 selector of latin-x-1 has a first
  harmonic of 0.0027, so that job's bins take their phase from noise and every
  pair's depth cell from it averages over phases; the stationary verdict
  and the job average it is read against leave that job's batch out, and
  batch 1 alone gives job averages 1.0213, 1.0206 and 1.0048, each 99%
  interval above 1, as both X batches do. A job whose line scan missed the detection
  threshold would leave its harmonic rules undetermined and its batch out of
  the verdict; all 12 detect the line. A selector at an extremum of its
  response follows the line at 2 f alone, so each job also scans the doubled
  band, both bands at half the detection p; all 12 detect in the band itself.
  The other depths' selectors share the reference phase where every
  significant harmonic is extremal within a quarter bin (pi/16) of bins 0
  and 4, up to 5 phase standard errors. On the clean jobs the depth-5 first
  harmonics sit -0.024 to +0.120 rad from the reference and the depth-9
  second harmonics -0.252 to -0.049 rad, so every selector aligns and no
  batch leaves the verdict on that count.
- **Harmonics at 2 f.** max(A1, A2) at depth 9 exceeds depth 1 by 0.0174 to
  0.137 in all 12 jobs, against a margin error of 0.0014 to 0.0018 from a
  bootstrap that draws the depth-9 and depth-1 selectors' eight lock-in
  coefficients jointly, since both read the job's shots, and repeats the
  maximum, where the first-harmonic rule holds in 10. In the two failing jobs (batch 4
  latin-x-1 and batch 5 latin-y-1, both with (12,14) at depth 9) A1 is 0.0027
  and A2 0.0203 and 0.0220, and the max rule's depth differences are 12 and
  14 margin errors. A depth-9 second
  harmonic of 0.009 to 0.030 appears in every job, so the selector responses
  are not pure first harmonics.

The intervals carry shot noise and readout-count noise only, conditional on
each job's fitted line frequency and phase, which set the bins and are not
refitted per draw; the batch-to-batch scatter of deep populations that the
follow series measured is outside them.

## Open questions

- Readout context: resolved by the Walsh exemplar series
  ([tuna17-walsh-exemplars.md](tuna17-walsh-exemplars.md)): q13's e10
  doubles beside an excited q11 and moves by less than 0.01 beside an
  excited q10 or after X X on both, so the dependence is q11's state at
  measurement. The other used qubits' contexts remain unmeasured.
- rho above 1: the stationary line-phase bins keep rho above 1 on all three
  pairs, so the job-averaged modulation does not explain it, and the excess
  tracks the line phase. A run that separates a line-modulated selector
  population from a depth-dependent one needs the population measured per
  phase bin at each depth in one job, so every depth shares the bin
  reference; the Latin square puts each pair's depths in different jobs.
- The line at 2 f: max(A1, A2) holds in all 12 jobs where the first-harmonic
  test fails in two. The max rule is exploratory; a preregistered line test
  for a future run adopts it with its bootstrap margin and the Rice bias of
  a noisy amplitude stated.
- The per-CZ time term and the decomposition of H: an A/B of H against
  Y90 then X on one replica would price the decomposed gate, and a ladder of
  `wait` cycles beside the CZ ladder would separate the detuning phase from
  the CZ's residual phase.
- Measuring a subset of the register: every retained Tuna-17 circuit
  measures all qubits.
- The MCP server lacks the queue read restricted to the account, batch-level
  status and completed-job logs (`scripts/qi_mcp_server.py`).
