# n21-rep-delay: state-preparation control and a shorter repetition delay for the n21-feedback-encoded run

Owner session: ibmquantum-12. Branch (planned): live-run/repetition-delay-state-prep-control. Gates: core-523 to core-526 (second range 520-539).

## Finding

The n21-feedback-encoded plan (PR #195, `N21_USAGE_BUDGET.md`) spends 78 percent
of each shot pair on the backend's default 250 us `rep_delay`: 657.8 us per
pair at 250 us, 357.8 at 100, 257.8 at 50. At 122,880 shots per PUB and R = 2
the plan charges 83.1 s; the same plan at 100 us is about 45 s and at 50 us
about 33 s (planner seconds scale with the per-pair column). IBM's
repetition-rate guide (docs/notes/ibm_quantum/repetition-rate-execution.md)
states that a lower `rep_delay` raises state-preparation error; no retained
record measures by how much on Kingston, so the exact model, which starts
every qubit in |0>, cannot size the run at a shorter delay.

`live_run` always uses `qpu_planning.repetition_delay(backend)` (the default),
in cost planning and in `sampler.options.execution.rep_delay` (live_run.py
around 3917); no option or family sets or measures it.

## Why IBM, and what the QI devices can and cannot do

The question is a property of the Heron r2 device: how much residual
excitation the n21 qubits on Kingston carry after `init_qubits` at a given
`rep_delay`. AGENTS.md admits a device question on IBM.

| Backend | Qubits / couplings | Shots per job (default / max) | Job limit | Batches | Relevant physics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QX emulator | 10, all-to-all (45) | 1024 / 2048 | 3600 s | 5 jobs per batch, 5 batches queued | noiseless statevector: no preparation error to measure |
| Tuna-17 | 17, 24 couplings, bipartite | 8192 / 131072 | 300 s | repository: at most 3 circuits per batch, one queued batch, 120 s between batches | transmons; 0.60 to 0.94 ms per shot (the platform's 500 or 200 us initialization dominates), so 131072 shots take about 80 to 120 s and fit the 300 s limit; initialization time is set by the platform scheduler, not by the program |
| Tuna-9, Tuna-5 | 9 / 12 couplings; 5 / 4 (star) | 8192 / 131072 | 300 s | as Tuna-17 | same initialization rule |
| Ry emulator | 9, all-to-all (36) | 1024 / 1024 | 3600 s | 5 / 5 | no mid-circuit measurement; only an X-only program has run (box_kite_ry_ladder settles the gate set) |

None of them can vary a repetition delay, and none measures Kingston, so the
control runs on IBM. Tuna and QX stay the place for logic and toolchain
questions (the n21 QX conformance check, core-499).

## Design

1. **`--rep-delay SECONDS`** in `live_run`: validated against
   `backend.configuration().rep_delay_range` at planning and at each batch,
   recorded in `policy` so the approval binds it, used by cost planning
   (`qpu_planning` charge per pair) and set on the Sampler. Default unchanged.
2. **Family `state-prep-control`** on a named layout (the n21 R = 2 placement
   from the approved-record candidate): PUB A `init -> measure` on every
   qubit (excited fraction = preparation plus readout error), PUB B
   `init -> reset -> measure` (an active reset, which helps only where the
   residual excitation exceeds the reset's readout misassignment), PUB C
   `x -> measure` (the |1> column of the assignment matrix). Every qubit is
   measured in each shot, so 14 qubits share the shots.
3. **Exact model:** an initial per-qubit excitation epsilon as a channel
   before the circuit in `dynamic_density` (bit-flip with probability
   epsilon on |0>), so the n21 planner can size power with the measured
   epsilon at the chosen delay; the n21 cross-check (core-487) extends to it.
4. **Sizing:** 8192 shots per PUB resolves a per-qubit excess of 0.4
   percentage points at 3 sigma (SE about 0.0011 at epsilon near 1
   percent). Two jobs, `rep_delay` 50 us and 250 us (the baseline, which
   also checks the calibration's `prob_meas1_prep0`): about 2 s overhead per
   job plus 3 x 8192 shots x (rep_delay + about 10 us), about 3.5 s and
   8.4 s, about 12 s in total. Preparation error falls monotonically with
   delay (the guide's figure), so 50 us passing admits 100 us without a
   third job.
5. **Decision rule, stated before measuring:** choose the shortest delay
   whose excess epsilon keeps the n21 plan's power at 0.8 within the 95 s
   ceiling once the exact model carries it; add the initial reset only where
   PUB B beats PUB A on that qubit. Expected saving on the n21 run: 83 s to
   about 45 s at 100 us, for about 12 s of control.

## Acceptance and falsifier

- Contracts (qpu_planning_contracts): `--rep-delay` outside the range refuses;
  the policy and approval comparison bind it; the charge model uses it.
- `dynamic_density` with epsilon equals Aer with an initial `x` error of the
  same probability (cross-check oracle).
- Submit-free dry runs of `state-prep-control` and of n21 at 100 and 50 us
  with an epsilon placeholder; the cases stay held for owner approval.
- Falsifier: Kingston's excess epsilon at 50 us large enough that no delay
  below 250 us keeps n21 within its ceiling, in which case the plan keeps
  250 us and the control cost (about 12 s) is the price of knowing.
