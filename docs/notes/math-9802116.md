## Main claims

[P] The octonions are a twisting of the group algebra of Z_2 x Z_2 x Z_2 by a 2-cochain F, living as an algebra in the quasitensor category of representations of a quasi-Hopf algebra built on the group 3-cocycle phi = dF; the 2^n-onion Cayley algebras are the same construction iterated.

- [P] Sec. 2: for a dual quasi-Hopf algebra and a 2-cochain F, twisting gives a new product, associator, and braiding (Eq. 8; Proposition 2.2 for module-comodule quasialgebras). For H = kG with a pointwise-invertible group 3-cocycle phi (Eq. 9), the coboundary case is phi(x,y,z) = F(x,y) F(xy,z) / (F(x,yz) F(y,z)) (Eq. 11). Definition 2.3: a G-graded quasialgebra satisfies (a.b).c = a.(b.c) phi(|a|,|b|,|c|). Corollary 2.4: k_F G, the group algebra with product x .F y = xy F(x,y), is a coboundary G-graded quasialgebra for any 2-cochain F. Proposition 2.5: it is braided-commutative with Psi(a x b) = b x a F(|a|,|b|)/F(|b|,|a|). The twisting "does not change the category of comodules up to monoidal equivalence".
- [P] Sec. 3 (G abelian): Proposition 3.1 (a quadratic character or composition norm forces F^2 = 1 up to a coboundary, or exactly F^2 = 1 for the Euclidean norm). Definition 3.2: k_F G is altercommutative if R(x,y) = F(x,y)/F(y,x) equals 1 when x = e, y = e, or x = y, and -1 otherwise. Proposition 3.3: k_F G is alternative iff two identities in phi and R hold, which force phi(x,x,y) = phi(x,y,y) = phi(x,y,x) = 1. Lemma 3.4: a diagonal involution exists iff R = ds for a 1-cochain s with s^2 = 1, and then phi(x,y,z) = phi(z,y,x)^(-1). Proposition 3.5: a diagonal strong involution exists iff G = Z_2^n, sigma(e) = e, sigma(x) = -x for x != e, and k_F G is altercommutative. Proposition 3.7: for G = Z_2^n and the Euclidean norm, k_F G is a composition algebra iff F^2 = 1 and F(x,xz) F(y,yz) + F(x,yz) F(y,xz) = 0 for all x != y and all z; Corollary 3.8: alternative iff composition under a strong involution with F^2 = 1.
- [P] Sec. 4: the Cayley-Dickson process (a + vb)(c + vd) = (ac + alpha d sigma(b)) + v(sigma(a) d + c b), with sigma-bar(a + vb) = sigma(a) - vb. Proposition 4.1 is its cochain form on G-bar = G x Z_2: F-bar(x,y) = F(x,y), F-bar(x,vy) = s(x) F(x,y), F-bar(vx,y) = F(y,x), F-bar(vx,vy) = alpha s(x) F(y,x), with s-bar(x) = s(x) and s-bar(vx) = -1. Proposition 4.2 (standard process: alpha = -1, s(x) = F(x,x) = -1 for x != e): F-bar(x,x) = -1 persists, F^2 = 1 persists, and altercommutativity persists. Corollary 4.3: for G = Z_2^n and F = (-1)^f, f-bar((x,xi),(y,eta)) = f(x,y)(1 - xi) + f(y,x) xi + eta f(x,x) + xi eta.
- [P] Proposition 4.4: f for the complex numbers (xy), the quaternions (x1 y1 + (x1 + x2) y2), the octonions (sum over i <= j of x_i y_j, plus y1 x2 x3 + x1 y2 x3 + x2 x2 y3 as printed), and the 16-onions (a sum of bilinear, cubic, and quartic terms as printed). The text states that the bilinear part makes F a bicharacter for C and H (phi = 1), that the octonion f has an added cubic term contributing to phi, and that the 16-onion has additional cubic and quartic terms.
- [P] Proposition 4.5 (for the standard process with a diagonal involution): phi-bar(x,y,z) = phi(x,y,z); phi-bar(vx,y,z) = R(y,z) phi(x,y,z); phi-bar(x,vy,z) = R(y,z) R(xy,z) phi(x,y,z); phi-bar(x,y,vz) = R(x,y) phi(x,y,z); phi-bar(vx,vy,z) = R(xy,z) phi(x,y,z); phi-bar(vx,y,vz) = R(y,z) R(x,y) phi(x,y,z); phi-bar(x,vy,vz) = R(x,yz) phi(x,y,z); phi-bar(vx,vy,vz) = R(xy,z) R(x,y) phi(x,y,z). Corollary 4.6: k_F-bar G-bar is associative iff k_F G is associative and commutative.
- [P] Sec. 5: further quasialgebras from normalized Hadamard matrices (Propositions 5.1 to 5.5). Sec. 6: a comeasuring (automorphism) dual quasi-Hopf algebra M_1(A) with diagonal quotients (Proposition 6.4, Corollary 6.6) and the examples of C and H; for the octonions M_0 projects onto k[G_2] "on general grounds", with no computation. Sec. 7: Definition 7.1, a representation of a G-graded quasialgebra satisfies (ab) |> v = phi(|a|,|b|,|v|) a |> (b |> v); Propositions 7.2 and 7.3 give quasimatrices M_{n,phi} with a phi-twisted product, and the left regular representation of the octonions is an 8 x 8 quasimatrix representation.

## Mapping to this repository

Convention map.

- [I] The paper's process with alpha = -1 and sigma(x) = x* is (a + vb)(c + vd) = (ac - d b*) + v(a* d + c b), the product P3 of note 1602.02843, which is the transpose of the repository's P3T doubling (ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c)). The new generator v sits on top, G-bar = G x Z_2 with vx = (x, nu), which is the cd_core index convention (bit n-1 is the last doubling). Measured: iterating Corollary 4.3 from f(x,y) = xy gives F_AM(x,y) = cd_sign(2^n, y, x) for n = 2 to 6 with 0 mismatches, against 6, 42, 210, 930, and 3906 mismatches for cd_sign(2^n, x, y). The associator transposes harmlessly: phi_AM(x,y,z) = phi_repo(z,y,x) by definition, and phi(x,y,z) = phi(z,y,x) by Lemma 3.4, so phi_AM = phi_repo; measured identical on all triples for n <= 5, and the repository's triple_sign (equal signs iff the triple associates) agrees with phi = +1 on all triples n <= 5. The altercommutator R is the same function under transposition. The DESIGN_NOTES prior-art sentence therefore holds verbatim for the associator; the cochain F itself is transposed.
