## Main claims

[P] Left multiplication by a Cayley-Dickson element is governed by one positive semidefinite operator whose spectrum controls zero divisors, cancellation, and the failure of alternativity.

- [P] Conventions (Section 2). A_0 = R, A_n = A_(n-1) x A_(n-1), (a,b)(c,d) = (ac - d*b, da + bc*), (a,b)* = (a*, -b). The standard basis (Definition 2.10 of [DDD]) is orthonormal for <a,b> = Re(ab*). i_n = (0,1), and C_n = span(1, i_n).
- [P] Definition 3.1: for nonzero a, M_a = (1/|a|^2) L_(a*) L_a, and the eigenvalues of a are the eigenvalues of M_a. M_(ra) = M_a for nonzero real r. Lemma 2.3: the adjoint of L_a is L_(a*) for the real inner product.
- [P] Lemma 3.8: ker M_a = ker L_a, so a is a zero divisor exactly when 0 is an eigenvalue. Proposition 3.9: M_a is diagonalizable with nonnegative eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenspaces. Proposition 3.17: the eigenvalues of a, counted with multiplicity, sum to 2^n (from tr(L_(x*) L_y) = 2^n <x,y>, Lemma 3.16).
- [P] Proposition 3.13: 1 is an eigenvalue of every nonzero a, with <<a, i_n>> inside Eig_1(a). Proposition 3.20 (n >= 2): every eigenspace has real dimension a multiple of 4. Proposition 3.21: x lies in Eig_lambda(a) exactly when |ax| = sqrt(lambda) |a||x| and |M_a x| = lambda |x|.
- [P] Lemma 3.5 and Proposition 3.10: for unit a in C_n^perp and unit beta in C_n, M_(a cos t + beta sin t) = I sin^2 t + M_a cos^2 t, so Eig_lambda(a) = Eig_(sin^2 t + lambda cos^2 t) of the mixture. Remark 3.12: zero divisors are orthogonal to C_n.
- [P] Proposition 4.4: sqrt(lambda^-) |a||x| <= |ax| <= sqrt(lambda^+) |a||x|, with equality exactly on the extreme eigenspaces. Proposition 4.7: lambda^+ of (b,c) is at most 2 max(lambda^+_b, lambda^+_c). Corollary 4.8: for n >= 3 every eigenvalue lies in [0, 2^(n-3)]. Theorem 8.3: for n >= 4 every real number in that interval occurs.
- [P] Proposition 6.2 and Corollary 6.5: for a in C_(n-1)^perp, alpha and beta in C_(n-1), |a| = 1, |alpha|^2 + |beta|^2 = 1, and cross-product norm |alpha cross beta| = 1/2, the eigenvalues of the doubled unit element (alpha a, beta a) are 0 (multiplicity 2^(n-1) - 4 + dim Eig_0(a)), 1 (8 + dim Eig_(1/2)(a)), 2 (dim Eig_1(a) - 4), and 2 lambda with the multiplicity of lambda in a otherwise. Proposition 6.6: linearly dependent alpha, beta double the multiplicities of a.
- [P] Theorem 7.2: for linearly independent imaginary a, b in A_3 with |a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1 and c = (a cross b) / |a cross b|, the 1-eigenspace of (a,b) contains <<a,b>> times <<a,b>> (Cartesian product of the quaternion subalgebra with itself), the (1 + 2|a cross b|)-eigenspace contains {(x, -cx)}, and the (1 - 2|a cross b|)-eigenspace contains {(x, cx)}, for x in <<a,b>>^perp. Corollary 7.3: the eigenvalues are 1, and 1 +- 2|a||b| sin(theta) / (|a|^2 + |b|^2), with multiplicities 8, 4, 4.
- [P] Proposition 7.4: for orthogonal imaginary nonzero a, b in A_3 with |a| = |b|, which are exactly the zero divisors (a,b) of A_4 (a characterization quoted from [M1] and [DDD, Prop. 12.1]), the eigenvalues of (a,b) are 0, 1, 2 with multiplicities 4, 8, 4. Eig_0 = {(x, -ab x/|ab|) : x in <<a,b>>^perp}, Eig_1 = <<a,b>> times <<a,b>>, Eig_2 = {(x, ab x/|ab|) : x in <<a,b>>^perp}.
- [P] Theorem 8.2 (n >= 3): a top-dimensional zero divisor of A_n (Eig_0 of dimension 2^n - 4n + 4, the sharp bound of [DDD, Prop. 9.10]) has eigenvalues 0 and 2^k for 0 <= k <= n - 3, with multiplicity 2^n - 4n + 4 for 0, 8 for 1, and 4 for each other eigenvalue.
- [P] Section 9 questions. Question 9.2: dim Eig_1(a) is conjectured never to equal 2^n - 12 or 2^n - 4, and every other multiple of 4 is conjectured to occur, on computer evidence; an A_5 element with a 4-dimensional 1-eigenspace is given. Questions 9.3 and 9.4: the spaces of spectra of elements and of zero divisors are open. Questions 9.1 (minimum eigenvalue of (b,c)) and 9.5 (characteristic polynomial) are questions in this paper. [I] The later trace/Newton construction in the linked source audit gives an invariant characteristic-polynomial presentation; it leaves the geometric space of attainable spectra unclassified.
- [P] Introduction. Writing x as a sum of eigenvectors x_i of M_a, a*(ax) = |a|^2 sum lambda_i x_i, so ax = b is solvable by dividing each component by lambda_i whenever no lambda_i is zero; a zero eigenvalue marks the failure of cancellation.

Convention map. [I]
- Algebra and basis: the doubling (a,b)(c,d) = (ac - d*b, da + bc*) is the one in the repository's `cd_sign` (see note 1207.4230, which checks `cd_sign` against the same rules); the standard basis is the repository's e_m with m the bit index, i_n = e_(2^(n-1)).
- Operator normalization: M_a = L_(a*) L_a / |a|^2 is the unit-input form; its eigenvalue lambda is |ax|^2 / (|a|^2 |x|^2) on an eigenvector. The repository's Design D operator for a = e_i + t e_j, i != j and t = +-1, is B = (L_(e_i) + t L_(e_j)) / 2 = L_a / 2, which is L_a scaled by the inverse l1 norm of the coefficients. With |a|^2 = 2 and L_a^T = L_(a*) (Lemma 2.3), B^T B = L_(a*) L_a / 4 = (|a|^2 / 4) M_a = M_a / 2. The scaling between the two normalizations is |a|^2 / ||c||_1^2 with c the coefficient vector, and equals 1/2 for the equal-magnitude signed two-term family. For a = alpha e_i + beta e_j with arbitrary nonzero real coefficients, the factor is (alpha^2 + beta^2) / (|alpha| + |beta|)^2; e_1 + 2 e_2 gives 5/9.

By Proposition 7.4, for the zero divisor (a,b) with c = ab/|ab|, the partners are the Eig_0 vectors (x, -c x) and the sign-flipped partners are the Eig_2 vectors (x, +c x); in the repository's notation the partner e_k + s e_l is one such (x, -c x) with x a basis unit and c x a basis unit up to sign. Design B's bright control (the flipped sign of the partner) therefore has B^T B = 1 and P(anc = 0) = 1, and the dark partner has P(anc = 0) = 0. The Design D fringe P(anc = 0 | phi) = (1 - s cos phi) / 2 is the weight on Eig_2 of the input (e_k + e^(i phi) e_l) / sqrt(2) in span{e_k, e_l}, which is the span of one Eig_0 vector and one Eig_2 vector and has no Eig_1 component. For the Design D signed corpus and B = L_a/2, any basis input e_p has B^T B expectation exactly 1/2, the Eig_1 value, because |L_a e_p|^2 = |a|^2 = 2. Label: new proof or formalization. - [I] Normalization boundary of the handoff. The handoff table says unit-input normalization differs from block-encoding normalization. In this paper the unit-input form is Definition 3.1, and the block-encoding form is M_a times |a|^2 / ||c||_1^2 as derived above. For a general a, Corollary 4.8 bounds lambda^+ by 2^(n-3), so a block encoding of L_a / ||c||_1 has largest squared amplitude lambda^+ |a|^2 / ||c||_1^2. For a = e_i + t e_j with i != j and t = +-1, this is lambda^+ / 2: B = L_a / 2 is an equal-weight average of two signed permutation matrices and has operator norm at most 1. Arbitrary nonzero real coefficients alpha, beta give a weighted average after division by |alpha| + |beta|, so lambda^+ <= (|alpha| + |beta|)^2 / (alpha^2 + beta^2) <= 2, while the squared-amplitude factor retains the coefficient-dependent value above. ## Gaps and cautions

- [P] The paper relies on [DDD] for most foundations (Section 2 "reviews the main points"); Proposition 7.4 inherits the zero-divisor characterization of [DDD, Prop. 12.1] and [M1, Cor. 2.14] without proof.
- [I] Proposition 7.4 is stated for a unit normalization implicitly (Theorem 7.2 uses |a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1); the invariance M_(ra) = M_a removes the dependence, and Definition 3.1 divides by |a|^2.
- [I] The mapping uses the doubling formula as printed in the PDF; the repository's `cd_sign` agrees with that formula by the comparison in note 1207.4230. A paper with a different doubling formula would need its own convention map.
