## Main claims

[P] A scalable version of Shor's algorithm replaces the n-qubit control register by one qubit recycled n times, and a two-photon compiled instance of it factors N = 21.

- [P] Resource claim (p. 2): for full-scale implementations, with n between log(N^2) and log(2N^2), qubit recycling cuts the qubit count from ceil(3 log2 N) to ceil(log2 N) + 1. The only penalty is a polynomial increase in computation time, so the exponential speedup remains.
- [P] Attribution (p. 2, ref. 12): the iterative order-finding algorithm is credited to Parker and Plenio (ref. 10) and Mosca and Ekert (ref. 11), and the footnote says the control-qubit recycling in ref. 11 had not been widely appreciated as a resource reduction. The paper calls the iterative version "closely related to the semi-classical picture of the quantum Fourier transform" (ref. 18, Griffiths and Niu).
- [P] Order-finding structure (p. 2): measurement of the control register gives k 2^n / r, with k between 0 and r-1; the single-qubit version measures from the least to the greatest significant bit and chooses the phase of the next measurement from the previous result.
- [P] Power-of-two orders (p. 2, footnote 19): when r = 2^p the peaks correspond exactly to the logical states of p qubits, so the output equals an incoherent mixture of p qubits, and any additional control qubits stay unentangled and encode trailing zeros in the uniform distribution. The same holds for qudits and orders d^p. For N = 15 every coprime has order 2 or 4, so independent verification of entanglement is required; the authors choose N = 21 with x = 4, order r = 3.
- [P] Ideal outcome (Fig. 1b): to two bits of precision the outcomes 00, 01, 10, 11 occur with probabilities 3/8, 1/4, 1/8, 1/4. Interference is constructive for the 00 term, which reaches three times the probability of the 10 term, and phase instability in the Fourier-transform step drives the output toward the uniform distribution.
- [P] Compilation (p. 3, Appendix A): the unitaries for N = 21, x = 4 are computed by hand, redundant elements are omitted, and because x^a mod 21 only visits {1, 4, 16}, the work register is one qutrit on those levels instead of five qubits (3 of 2^5 levels). The first controlled unitary is a CNOT on work modes 0 and 2; the final controlled unitary is a CNOT on modes 0 and 1 followed by an uncontrolled swap on modes 0 and 2.
- [P] Implementation (pp. 3-5): the experiment uses post-selection in place of measurement and feed-forward, two consecutive photonic CNOT gates (an entanglement-driven CNOT followed by a post-selected CNOT), and a Jamin-Lebedeff calcite beam-displacer interferometer. Photons come from a polarization-entangled down-conversion source of state fidelity 96.9 +/- 0.2%; the post-selected CNOT violates CHSH at 2.67 +/- 0.01.
- [P] Measured quality: fidelity (one minus trace distance) of the two-bit distribution is 99 +/- 4%; the first-iteration distribution is uniform at 99 +/- 5%; the eight distributions between two settings of the Fourier-transform phase agree with the ideal at 91.6 +/- 0.6% (Fig. 3). Integrating the distribution over the phase range 0 to pi simulates phase instability and gives a near-uniform output (Fig. 3e).
- [P] Footnote 20: for squares x = 2^(2j), a numerical study over the first 4851 odd composites N that are products of two primes finds odd order for about 58% of this class, and about 36% of those lead to nontrivial factors by the classical gcd step. The N = 21, x = 4 case is one such success.
- [P] Scope: two bits of precision, one compiled instance, no real-time feed-forward, no scaling data beyond the qubit-count argument.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Recycled control qubit and qubit count. Repository claim: HYBRID_SHOR.md, "Exact order-finding decomposition" -- one recycled control qubit, a work register of n = bit_length(N) qubits, m = 2n phase bits; the N = 15 circuit uses 5 qubits. Source item: Fig. 1a and the resource claim on p. 2. Model map: the paper's work register of ceil(log2 N) qubits is `plan.work_qubits`, and its single control qubit is `ctrl`; ceil(log2 15) + 1 = 5. The N = 21 target-flip circuit uses 7 qubits, which is the paper's 5 + 1 plus one clean workspace qubit for multi-controlled gate synthesis. Label: "known theorem reproduced". Not established by the source: reset and reuse of a superconducting qubit, mid-circuit measurement latency, any synthesis of the controlled multipliers for a full register.
- [I] Leading-identity pruning, principle. Repository claim: HYBRID_SHOR.md, "Leading-identity pruning" -- when the first k repeated-square multipliers equal one, the first k semiclassical rounds output zero and their feedback history is zero, so the circuit omits them (six of eight rounds for N = 15, a = 2). Source item: p. 2 and footnote 19 (additional control qubits stay unentangled and encode trailing zeros) together with the "redundant elements of these unitaries are omitted" step of the compilation (p. 3, Appendix A). Model map: the six omitted rounds are the paper's trailing zeros, and the omission is the paper's compilation class. The pruning fires exactly when a^(2^(m-1)) = 1 modulo N, that is when the order of a is a power of two, 2^p, and then m - p rounds are omitted and p run (N = 15, a = 2: p = 2, six omitted). Label: "known theorem reproduced". Not established by the source: the omission rule stated for arbitrary N, a, or m; the paper's compile uses the known orbit of x.
- [I] Leading-identity pruning, host-derived rule. Repository claim: the proof depends on the computed multiplier values rather than a known order, and `order_finding_plan` finds `leading_identity_rounds` from `scheduled_multipliers`. Source item: Appendix A, which derives the omitted elements by hand for one instance. Model map: the repository computes a^(2^e) mod N for every round on the host and stops at the first value other than one; a host that computes 2^4 mod 15 = 1 certifies that the order of 2 divides 4, so the omission uses information equivalent to a bound on the order. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: any automatic rule, and any statement about orders that are not powers of two (the rule never fires for them: N = 21, a = 2 has order 6 and no leading identity).
- [I] Power-of-two order makes the feed-forward phase invisible in the ideal distribution. Repository claim: HYBRID_SHOR.md, "Small-case interpretation" -- N = 15 is a structured Mersenne case and a successful run proves the pipeline and hardware control path. Source item: p. 2 and footnote 19 (r = 2^p gives an incoherent mixture of p qubits; N = 15 needs independent verification of entanglement). Model map: for N = 15, a = 2 the executed rounds are multiplier 4 then multiplier 2. Round 6 leaves the work register in (|1> +/- |4>)/sqrt2 and its bit is uniform. Round 7 entangles the control with (|1> +/- |4>)/sqrt2 on one branch and (|2> +/- |8>)/sqrt2 on the other; these states are orthogonal, so the reduced control state is maximally mixed and the second bit is uniform whatever phase the feed-forward applies. An exact state-vector calculation by the note author gives {0: 0.25, 1: 0.25, 2: 0.25, 3: 0.25} for y = b0 + 2 b1 with the conditional -pi/2 phase and without it. Label: "known theorem reproduced". Not established by the source: any hardware statement about the repository circuit, and any quantitative bound on what a partially dephased control yields.
- [I] Consequence for the repository's hardware claim. Repository claim: the first hardware record reads factor yield 0.4971 [0.4726, 0.5216] against the exact uniform null 0.2969 and an identity-null yield 0.0042, and HYBRID_SHOR.md concludes that conformance of the semiclassical inverse QFT holds at the factor-yield level. Source item: the same p. 2 statement. Model map: the ideal yield 0.5 equals the yield of two fair coin flips on the two executed bits, and the identity null differs from the primary only in whether the controlled arithmetic randomizes the control. A fully dephased control reproduces the ideal exactly, so the admission rule (primary lower bound above the uniform null, identity-null upper bound below the primary lower bound) cannot separate a working feed-forward from an absent or wrong one. It does show that the controlled Fredkin sequence acts on the control. The only hardware test of the phase is the folded-QPE conformance test (0.715 against 0.899), where odd eigenstate labels require the phase. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: anything about the repository's hardware data; the paper has no superconducting data and no mid-circuit measurement.
- [I] N = 21 compiled instance versus the repository's full-register N = 21. Repository claim: MODULAR_SYNTHESIS.md and HYBRID_SHOR.md -- N = 21, a = 2, ten rounds, seven logical qubits, 5,492 CZ and ISA depth 13,355 at the reference seed, compile-only status, with an identity-padded permutation on all 32 register states and a compiler that consumes only public N and the multiplier. - [I] Phase-instability scan (Fig. 3c-e). ## Action items

1. Run the paper's N = 21, x = 4, two-bit case on a Heron device after a compile and noise-simulation admission. The observable is the four-outcome distribution against the ideal (3/8, 1/4, 1/8, 1/4) and the control prediction with the feed-forward removed, (3/8, 1/8, 1/8, 3/8); the two differ by total-variation distance 1/8, about ten times the expected sampling TVD at 4096 shots of about 0.01 (binomial mean-absolute-deviation estimate by the note author). The host factor decoder uses gcd(a^(r/2) +/- 1, N) and needs an even order, so the observable is the four-outcome distribution, not factor yield. Order recovery is identically zero at two bits: the measured values y/4 lie in {0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4}, whose continued-fraction denominators are 1, 2, and 4, and 4, 16, and 4^4 = 4 modulo 21 are all different from one, so no convergent passes the order check. Two rounds need 34 controlled cubes (17 per multiplier network); the five-bit row of MODULAR_SYNTHESIS.md gives about 550 CZ per round, so about 1.1e3 CZ is a rough expectation and must be measured by compilation first. Source: Fig. 1b, Fig. 3a.
2. Report the folded-QPE test, not the factor yield, as the hardware evidence for the semiclassical feed-forward in HYBRID_SHOR.md, and add the power-of-two argument above next to the "Small-case interpretation" paragraph. Source: p. 2, footnote 19.
3. Map the paper's phase scan to the ladder: on folded QPE with an odd label, replace the correction by a random phase drawn per shot (the integration of Fig. 3e) as a companion to rung c, and compare with the absent correction. Source: Fig. 3c-e.
4. State in the N = 21 sections that the paper's orbit-encoded compile consumes the order and that the repository's identity-padded circuit does not; cite the paper as the N = 21 hardware factoring demonstration that this note records. Source: Appendix A.
5. Read Parker and Plenio (ref. 10) and Mosca and Ekert (ref. 11) before the repository attributes the recycled-control construction to them; this paper only reports the attribution.
