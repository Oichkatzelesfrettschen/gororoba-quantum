## Main claims

[P] For the doubling product P2 numbered over the shuffle basis, the sign function omega_2 has a closed form, and with it the product of any two basis vectors of a Cayley-Dickson algebra.

- [P] Setup (Sec. 1 and 2): the complex doubling (a,b)(c,d) = (ac - bd, ad + bc); eight doubling products P0 to P3 and P0T to P3T (the list of note 1602.02843), of which "only P3 and P3T have been investigated"; "the eight algebras resulting from these products are isomorphic [4]" with the same elements and unit basis vectors; for basis vectors P(e_p, e_q) = P^T(e_q, e_p). The shuffle basis is e_0 = 1, e_{2k} = (e_k, 0), e_{2k+1} = (0, e_k), with (a,b)* = (a*, -b); the index p xor q is the same for all eight products and the sign differs.
- [P] Properties common to all eight (Eq. 3 to 7): omega(p,0) = omega(0,p) = 1, omega(p,p) = -1 for p > 0, omega(p,q) = -omega(q,p) for 0 != p != q != 0.
- [P] Properties of omega_2 (Eq. 8 and 9): if 2^N <= p < q < 2^(N+1) then omega_2(p,q) = 1; if 2^N <= p < 2^(N+1) <= q then omega_2(p,q) = (-1)^floor(q / 2^N). The proof traverses the omega_2 tree of Figure 2.
- [P] Theorem 5.1: with those two cases, e_0 = 1, e_p^2 = -1 for p > 0, and e_p e_q = -e_q e_p for 0 != p != q != 0, the product of any two basis vectors is fixed; for P2 only the numbering by (W, xor) makes "a natural inverse fractal pattern" appear.
- [P] Worked examples: e3 e14 = -e13; e35 e55 = e20; e87 e340 = -e259; e51 e12 = -e53. Figures 3 to 5 plot omega_2 on Z_2^5 x Z_2^5, omega_2 on Z_2^10 x Z_2^10, and the "inscrutable" omega_3 on the same grid.
- [P] The conclusion attributes the historical opacity of omega to the use of only P3 and P3T together with non-shuffle numbering.

## Mapping to this repository

Convention map.

- [I] The repository's doubling is P3T (note 1602.02843, convention map), numbered with the last doubling at index bit n-1; the paper's formula is for P2 numbered with the last doubling at index bit 0. The two sign tables differ in algebra and in numbering, so Theorem 5.1 does not give cd_sign under any relabeling tested.
- [I] Measured: a recursion on the printed P2 reproduces Theorem 5.1 for every p, q below 256 (0 mismatches), and a nested-pair implementation of the printed P2 reproduces that recursion at dimension 8 through the bit reversal (0 mismatches). Theorem 5.1 is therefore the correct sign table of the printed P2. Of the four worked examples, three agree (e3 e14, e35 e55, e87 e340); the fourth misreads 12 as 000110B (that is 6) and gives the index 53, while 12 = 001100B gives 51 xor 12 = 63 and the sign -1 agrees with the recursion.
- [I] The printed P2 is not the Cayley-Dickson algebra of the repository, and not an octonion algebra at dimension 8: with the nested-pair implementation and the repository's top-bit-last index, x = e_1 + e_4 and y = e_2 + e_7 give |xy|^2 = 8 against |x|^2 |y|^2 = 4, and A(e_1,e_4,e_2) + A(e_4,e_1,e_2) = 2 e_7, which an alternative algebra forbids; P3T gives no violation in either test. Analytically, on quaternion halves a, b, c, d the cross terms of |(a,b)(c,d)|^2 under P2 cancel iff Re(a c d* b) = Re(c a d* b), which fails when a and c do not commute. Alternativity is an isomorphism invariant, so the claim "the eight algebras are isomorphic" does not hold for the printed P2 (and P1, P1T, and P2T, which fail the norm test); reference [4] was not read, so a misprint in both papers cannot be separated from a difference in [4]. No GL(n,2) index relabeling with a diagonal sign gauge maps the P2 table onto cd_sign for n = 3 or 4.

Overlapping repository claims.

- [I] Claim: e_p e_q = cd_sign(dim,p,q) e_{p xor q}, the twisted-group structure of the Cayley-Dickson basis (gororoba_quantum/cd_core.py docstring). Source: Eq. 3 and Eq. 4 to 7. Convention map: shuffle index = rev_n of the cd_core index; the repository's product is P3T and the paper's closed form is for P2. Label: known theorem reproduced. Not established by the source: the repository's product (P3T) and numbering, and any table for P3T.
- [I] Claim (DESIGN_NOTES, "Phase degree along the ladder"): the sign function f_m has degree log2(dim) - 1 for every m != 0. Source: Theorem 5.1 gives closed forms only for the printed P2. Convention map: P2 in the shuffle index against cd_sign in the cd_core index; no GL(n,2) relabeling with a diagonal gauge relates the tables at n = 3 or 4. Measured: from the P2 table in the shuffle index, the degrees of f_m(x) = [omega_2(m,x) = -1] over m != 0 are {1, 2}, {1, 2, 3}, {1, ..., 4}, {1, ..., 5} at n = 3, 4, 5, 6, against {n-1} for cd_sign; the highest generator row (shuffle index 1) is linear, since omega_2(1,q) = (-1)^q. Label: candidate new result. Not established by the source: any degree, and any statement about P3T; the contrast shows that the closed form of the printed product, which is not a Cayley-Dickson algebra, cannot be transplanted to cd_sign.
- [I] Observation: the repository gains nothing from a closed form for cd_sign, because the bit loop of cd_sign is already linear in n (note 1602.02843). The paper calls omega_3 "inscrutable" (Figure 5) and offers no closed form for P3 or P3T.
