## Statements and boundaries

- [P] Graph vertices are projective lines `[a]=R*a` through nonzero zero
  divisors. The zero-divisor graph directs `[a]` to `[b]` when `ab=0`;
  the orthogonality graph requires both `ab=0` and `ba=0`
  (Section 2.1). Algebraic orthogonality differs from Euclidean
  inner-product orthogonality.
- [P] The paper studies arbitrary real Cayley-Dickson algebras subject to
  component norm and strong-alternation hypotheses. Under the conditions
  carried by Remark 3.4 and the preceding lemmas, Corollary 3.7 constructs
  a directed six-cycle. Corollary 3.16 adds explicit pairwise strong
  alternation and purity conditions to obtain an undirected chordless cycle.
- [P] In the main sequence, whose doubling parameters all equal `-1`, the
  construction extends to a double hexagon consisting of six copies of
  `K_(2,2)` glued together (Description 4.8). Theorem 4.11 supplies the
  multiplication table for the constructed elements and auxiliary products.
  The introduction identifies earlier sedenion constructions by de Marrais
  and explains the wider scope of its proof.
- [P] Section 6 treats pairs of signed standard basis elements `(e_i,+/-e_j)`.
  Notation 6.1 defines their restricted projective graph. Theorem 6.16
  classifies membership in the zero-divisor set for this support class,
  including explicit exceptions depending on dimension, signs, and norms.
  The support restriction is part of the theorem.

## Reuse in the repository

[I] The paper provides an independent finite oracle for
[the signed sparse census](topics/cayley_dickson_sparse_census.md), after the
multiplication convention, projective identification, and index split agree.
A robust comparison records both the vertex map and the adjacency map; matching
only the number of vertices leaves sign and orientation errors undetected.
The table's algebraic identities also supply exact circuit test vectors before
any noisy execution.

[I] The graph quotient discards scale and sign orientation. The normalized
zero-product pair space retains ordered unit representatives, continuous
coefficient directions, and all partners in each kernel. Graph cycles alone
do not provide its cells, attaching maps, or Betti numbers. In particular,
a graph cycle does not by itself define a noncontractible loop in that space.

[I] A useful extension starts with a graph-derived witness, introduces real
coefficient parameters, and verifies the rank equations and kernel chart on
the whole resulting family. An arbitrary basis change need not preserve
Cayley-Dickson multiplication. Any symmetry used to merge families requires
an explicit algebra-automorphism certificate and coverage proof.
[The kernel chart implementation](../../gororoba_quantum/rank_charts.py)
and [attachment analysis](../../gororoba_quantum/rank_attachments.py) supply
such exact family tools; a finite graph supplies starting points.

[I] The paper's graph classification is prior work, rather than a novelty
claim for an 84-element phase sweep. Hardware value requires an additional
measurable decision, such as discriminating competing noise models or choosing
a compilation change at fixed accuracy and charge. The
[Part II note](2608.28163.md) describes the recursive support graph, while
[the anisotropic-norm paper](2608.26893.md) develops the broader annihilator
linear algebra relevant to arbitrary-real rank equations.
