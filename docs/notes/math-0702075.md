## Main claims

[P] A splitting of A_(n+1) into a codimension-4 piece H_(n+1)^perp, where multiplication takes a simple form, reduces annihilator dimensions of {a,b} to those of a and b, and shows that the zero divisors with near-maximal annihilators are stable in n.

- [P] Notation (Definition 3.1, Lemma 3.2). For a, b in C_n^perp, {a,b} = (1/sqrt 2)(a + b, i_n(-a + b)) in A_(n+1). H_n = span(1, i_n, i_(n-1), i_(n-1) i_n) = C_(n-1) x C_(n-1) is a quaternion subalgebra (Definition 2.8), and H_(n+1)^perp is exactly the set of {a,b} (so (x,y) = {(x + i_n y)/sqrt 2, (x - i_n y)/sqrt 2}). Lemma 3.5: |{a,b}|^2 = |a|^2 + |b|^2. Corollary 4.10: <{a,b},{x,y}> = <a,x> + <b,y>.
- [P] Proposition 1.2 (4.1): if a, b are C-orthogonal to x, y (all in C_n^perp), {a,b}{x,y} = sqrt 2 {ax, by}. Theorem 4.5 gives the product of arbitrary {a,b}, {x,y} as sqrt 2 (pi(ax), pi(by)) plus a C_(n+1) term pi(xa + by) plus a (0,i_n) term pi(ay - xb), with pi the projections of Lemma 2.15.
- [P] Theorem 1.3 (5.10): for n >= 3 and nonzero a, b in C_n^perp, dim Ann{a,b} is dim Ann a + dim Ann b or that plus 4. Definition 1.4 (6.1), the D-locus: a and b are C-orthogonal, a is orthogonal to Ann(b), and b is orthogonal to Ann(a). Theorem 1.5 (Theorems 6.7 and 6.12): outside the D-locus the dimension is dim Ann a + dim Ann b; in the D-locus it is that plus 4, and Ann{a,b} is the C-orthogonal sum of Ann(a) x Ann(b), the C-line through {|b|^2 a, -|a|^2 b}, and the C-line through a second explicit vector.
- [P] Proposition 5.11 (Theorem 10.2 of [DDD]): for n >= 4 and nonzero a in C_(n-1)^perp, {a,0} and {0,a} are zero divisors of A_n with annihilator of dimension dim Ann(a) + 2^(n-1) - 4. Remark 4.2: {a,0}{0,y} = 0 whenever a and y are C-orthogonal in C_n^perp.
- [P] Section 7 (A_5). For a, b in A_4 the D-locus is explicit when a or b is not a zero divisor; when both are zero divisors it is Corollary 7.4: {a,b} lies in the D-locus exactly when b lies in the span of (a_1, -a_2), (a_2, a_1), and Eig_2(a), where a = (a_1, a_2). Theorem 7.5: the set X of {a,b} in the D-locus of A_5 with a and b unit-length zero divisors of A_4 is diffeomorphic to the unit sphere bundle of xi (+) 2 over V_2(R^7), where xi is the 4-plane bundle whose sphere bundle is G_2; Remark 7.6: an S^5-bundle over V_2(R^7).
- [P] Theorem 8.1: for n >= 3, dim Ann(a) >= 2^(n-1) forces a into H_n^perp. Proposition 8.2: for a nonzero alternative unit a in C_(n-1)^perp, Ann(i_(n-1), a) = {(x, a i_(n-1) x) : x C-orthogonal to 1 and a} has dimension 2^(n-1) - 4, outside H_n^perp (Question 8.3 asks for all such elements). Proposition 8.6: dim Ann(a) >= 2^n - 8n + 24 forces a = {b,0} or {0,b}; Lemma 8.9 gives elements of dimension 2^(n+1) - 8n + 12, so the bound is sharp.
- [P] Definition 8.4: T_n^c is the set of unit elements of A_n with annihilator dimension at least 2^n - 4n + 4 - c (c a multiple of 4, 0 <= c <= 2^n - 4n; the definition differs from the one in [DDD]). Theorem 1.7 (8.12): for n >= 4, T_n^c is stable, meaning T_(n+1)^c is the set of {a,0} and {0,a} with a in T_n^c, exactly when n >= c/4 + 4. Corollary 8.13: ZD_16(A_5) is two copies of V_2(R^7). Corollary 8.14: the zero divisors of A_6 with annihilators of dimension at least 40 are two copies of the zero divisors of A_5 with annihilators of dimension at least 12.
- [P] Corollary 4.13: C_n^perp contains 2^(n-3) elements with pairwise zero products (Question 4.14: is 1 + 2^(n-3) the largest degenerate subalgebra). Section 4.11: in A_4 the 4-dimensional subalgebras generated by C-orthogonal a, b with a(ab) = -lambda b have lambda in {1, 2} (from [CD, Theorem 7.1]).
- [P] Limits (Section 1): the formulas treat only elements of a codimension-4 subspace of A_n, and the dimension is given up to two options differing by 4; the authors note that one codimension-2 slice is easy, so the restriction is "really only codimension 2".

## The published smooth family through the nullity-twelve center

The additional primary reading covers Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.5
(pp. 7-8), Definition 6.1 (p. 14), Theorem 6.12 (p. 16), and Section 7's
Corollary 7.4, Theorem 7.5 and Remark 7.6 (pp. 18-19). Page images 18-19
confirm the sphere-bundle statement and its proof. The exact-version
abstract-page license remains the arXiv distribution grant already
recorded; the source PDF remains external-only.

[P] The bracket coordinates are

```text
{a,b}=(a+b, i4*(-a+b))/sqrt(2),
||{a,b}||^2=||a||^2+||b||^2,
a,b in C4-perp.
```

[P] Definition 6.1 requires complex orthogonality of `a,b`, orthogonality
of `a` to `Ann(b)`, and orthogonality of `b` to `Ann(a)`. For nonzero
entries on that D-locus, Theorem 6.12 gives
`dim Ann{a,b}=dim Ann(a)+dim Ann(b)+4`. When both entries are sedenion
zero divisors their annihilator dimensions are four, so the resulting
dimension-32 nullity is twelve.

[P] Theorem 7.5 fixes `||a||=||b||=1` and identifies the resulting space
`X` with the unit sphere bundle of `xi direct_sum R^2` over `V2(R7)`,
where `xi` is a four-plane bundle. Remark 7.6 explicitly identifies the
fiber as `S5`. The proof constructs the relevant subbundles through
`Eig2(a)`, its isomorphism with `Ann(a)`, and two explicit trivial line
directions. [I] Since `dim V2(R7)=11`, the smooth total space `X` has
dimension sixteen. These dimensions concern the base family of algebra
elements; they are different from annihilator dimension twelve.

[I] Independent positive scalings preserve all D-locus conditions and
both zero-divisor properties. The fixed-norm, positive-length-ratio family
is therefore parametrized by

```text
F(ahat,bhat,theta)={sqrt(2)*cos(theta)*ahat,
                   sqrt(2)*sin(theta)*bhat},
(ahat,bhat) in X, 0<theta<pi/2.
```

The image has norm squared two and nullity twelve. The inverse bracket
map recovers `a,b`; their positive norms recover `theta`, and normalization
recovers `ahat,bhat`. The bundle construction and these smooth inverse
maps give a smooth embedded seventeen-dimensional family. One can replace
the trigonometric parameter by positive ratio `rho` and the algebraic
scalings `sqrt(2/(1+rho^2))`, `rho*sqrt(2/(1+rho^2))`. The open interval
excludes the bracket axes, whose nullities follow a different formula.
The length ratio is thus an explicit elementary extension of the published
family, rather than an unknown extra parameter or a new classification.
The [D-locus certificate](../evidence/d_locus_nullity/README.md) checks 200
exact members of the family at |b|/|a| from 0.18 to 508, each of nullity 12.

[I] In the repository basis, `x0=e1+e18=(e1,e2)` and `i4*e2=-e10` give

```text
a0=(e1-e10)/sqrt(2),
b0=(e1+e10)/sqrt(2),
x0={a0,b0}.
```

Both bracket entries are unit sedenion zero divisors. Their octonion halves
have equal lengths and are imaginary and orthogonal. Moreover,
`b0=(a1,-a2)` when `a0=(a1,a2)`, so Corollary 7.4 places the center in
the D-locus. The center corresponds to `theta=pi/4` and belongs to the
known seventeen-dimensional family.
