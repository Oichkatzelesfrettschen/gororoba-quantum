## Main claims

[P] RSA-2048 factors in under one week on under one million noisy physical qubits, at the cost of about 6.5 billion Toffoli gates and about 1,400 logical qubits.

- [P] Headline: a 2048 bit RSA integer factors in less than a week with fewer than a million noisy qubits. This replaces the 2019 estimate (Gidney and Ekera) of 8 hours on 20 million qubits.
- [P] Physical assumptions are unchanged from 2019: square grid, nearest-neighbor connections, uniform depolarizing gate error 0.1% (1 error per 1000 gates), surface code cycle 1 us, control-system reaction time 10 us.
- [P] The qubit reduction comes from three ingredients: approximate residue arithmetic (Chevignard, Fouque, Schrottenloher 2024), idle logical qubits stored in yoked surface codes, and magic state cultivation in place of the first distillation stage.
- [P] The runtime grows because the algorithm uses more Toffolis and fewer magic-state factories than the 2019 design. The Toffoli count falls by over 100x against Chevignard et al.
- [P] Prior points on the space-time trade-off: Gidney-Ekera 2021 used 3 billion Toffolis and a bit more than 3n logical qubits; Chevignard et al. used 2 trillion Toffolis and a bit more than 0.5n logical qubits (Introduction).
- [P] Table 5, expected cost per complete factoring (not per shot):

  | n | s | l | w1 | w3 | w4 | f | m | P_deviant | E(shots) | Toffolis | logical qubits |
  |---|---|---|----|----|----|---|---|-----------|----------|----------|----------------|
  | 1024 | 8 | 18 | 6 | 3 | 6 | 28 | 640 | 2.87% | 9.4 | 1.1e9 | 742 |
  | 2048 | 8 | 21 | 6 | 3 | 5 | 33 | 1280 | 1.25% | 9.2 | 6.5e9 | 1399 |
  | 4096 | 8 | 24 | 6 | 3 | 5 | 36 | 2560 | 0.80% | 9.2 | 4.0e10 | 2692 |
  | 8192 | 8 | 24 | 6 | 3 | 5 | 40 | 5120 | 0.40% | 9.1 | 2.7e11 | 5261 |

  The paper also lists n = 1536, 3072 and 6144; E(shots) = (s+1)/(1 - P_deviant)/0.99.
- [I] Across Table 5 the Toffoli count grows with local exponent 2.56 (1024 to 2048), 2.62 (2048 to 4096) and 2.75 (4096 to 8192), approaching the n^3 of loop1 in Table 3 from below. Logical qubits stay linear, 0.72 n at n = 1024 falling to 0.64 n at n = 8192.
- [P] Physical layout for n = 2048 (Sec. 3.2): code distance 25; hot patches use 2(d+1)^2 = 1352 physical qubits per logical qubit; cold (yoked, 2D parity check) storage uses 430 physical qubits per logical qubit.
- [P] Footprint: 1280 cold input qubits = 550,400; 131 hot qubits = 177,112; compute region of 7 x 18 hot patches (six magic-state factories of 3 x 4 patches plus three workspace columns) = 170,352; total 897,864. The paper reports one million to leave 100k of slack for streaming cold qubits to hot storage during loop1.
- [I] Footprint shares: cold storage 61.3%, hot storage 19.7%, compute region 19.0%. The factories alone cover 72 patches, about 97,000 physical qubits, 10.8% of the total.
- [P] Runtime: 12.07 hours per shot; 9.2 expected shots gives 4.63 days; dividing by the 93.3% chance of a shot without logical error gives 4.96 days, rounded to a week.
- [P] Error budget: target logical error rate 1e-15 per logical qubit per round; about 6.9e13 logical-qubit rounds per shot (1600 qubits x 12 h at 1 us rounds).
- [P] Magic states: cultivation yields a T state at logical error 1e-7 for about 30,000 physical-qubit-rounds; 8T-to-CCZ distillation suppresses error as 28 p^2, giving CCZ error below 1e-12; 114.7 rounds per CCZ are rounded to 150; six factories give one CCZ per 25 us, equal to the d = 25 lattice-surgery period.
- [P] Operation latencies used: 33-qubit addition 2 ms, 6-address-bit lookup 2 ms, phaseup 1 ms.
- [P] Conclusion: under the same physical assumptions the author sees no way to cut the qubit count by another order of magnitude and does not claim 100 thousand qubits suffice.

## Methods

[P] The algorithm replaces one n-qubit modular exponentiation by an approximate sum over small-prime residues and a short accumulator.

- [P] Target function: V = g^e mod N = product over k of M_k^(e_k), M_k = g^(2^k) mod N (Eq. 5-7). Everything decomposes into controlled multiplications by classical constants.
- [P] Residue system: L is a product of l-bit primes with L >= N^m (Eq. 8) so that no wraparound occurs. The result is V = (sum_j r_j u_j mod L) mod N with r_j the residue modulo prime p_j (Eq. 11).
- [P] Truncation: keep f bits, add (s mod N) >> t with t = len N - f, accumulate modulo N >> t. Each truncated addition adds deviation O(2^-f); total deviation is O(|P| l 2^-f) (Eq. 22), so f = O(log log N) (Eq. 23).
- [P] L must satisfy Delta_N(L) < 2^-f. The paper finds such L by random search over prime sets (Assumption 1, conjectured). Example: 25,000 primes of 22 bits with deviation below 2^-32 against RSA-2048, found in ten seconds on 128 cores.
- [P] The residue system is chosen per shot, after g, because no prime may divide any multiplier (Eq. 49).
- [P] Approximate period finding with superposition masking (Sec. 2.2): measure f~(x) + s with s uniform over [0, ceil(S N)). The shot-failure probability is bounded by S + eps/S; the choice S = sqrt(eps) gives P_deviant <= 2 sqrt(eps) (Eq. 43-45). Assumption 2 treats a mask of proportion S as multiplying expected shots by 1/(1 - S).
- [P] Ekera-Hastad period finding (Sec. 2.3): x register of n/2 + ceil(n/(2s)) qubits and y register of ceil(n/(2s)) qubits, so m = ceil(n/2 + n/s). Each shot measures g^x h^y with h = g^(N-1) mod N; s+1 shots recover d = p + q - 2, then p(d - p + 2) = N gives the factors. Larger s shrinks m, which shrinks the residue system through L >= N^m.
- [P] Discrete-log trick (from Chevignard et al.): precompute D_{p,k} with g_p^(D_{p,k}) = M_k mod p, so the controlled product becomes a controlled sum S_p = sum_k D_{p,k} e_k (Eq. 48-50); reduce S_p modulo p - 1 and exponentiate with len p multiplications instead of thousands.
- [P] Windowing: lookups addressed by w1 exponent qubits (loop1), by 2 w3 qubits (loop3), by w4 residue qubits (loop4) cut the iteration counts by those factors.
- [P] Merge compute of S_{p_j} with uncompute of S_{p_{j-1}} through precomputed differences D'_{j,k} (Eq. 52-53); defer and merge phase corrections from measurement-based uncomputation; flip additions to subtractions so underflow is a single qubit. The modular adder costs 2.5 n Toffolis (2 n when later uncomputed) against 3.5 n in the prior state of the art.
- [P] Quantum Fourier transform: a frequency-basis measurement uses the deferred-measurement form with n - 1 adaptively phased gates, each implemented by addition into a phase-gradient state (Appendix A.5). The gradient state needs 159 T gates for its first 11 qubits at 5e-6 infidelity; the paper states the cost is negligible next to the arithmetic.
- [P] Primitive costs: n-qubit addition n - 1 Toffolis; lookup 2^n - n - 1 Toffolis; phaseup about 2 sqrt(2^n) (Sec. 3.1). Table 3 marks loop1 as Theta-tilde(n^3) and every other loop as Theta-tilde(n^2).
- [P] Parameter scan: s from 2 to 14, l from 18 to 25, w1 from 2 to 8, w3 from 2 to 6, w4 from 2 to 8, f from 24 to 59; Table 5 highlights the choices that minimize q^3 t (qubits cubed times Toffolis).
- [P] Layout: hot storage in ordinary surface-code patches, cold storage in yoked codes, compute region with lattice-surgery workspace, 8T-to-CCZ factories fed by magic state cultivation. Appendix A gives lattice-surgery mock-ups of addition, phaseup and lookup.
- [P] Reference Python (Appendix A.1) implements loop1 to loop4, unloop2, unloop3 with a quint simulator that tracks a few classical trajectories to check classical output and phase kickback.

## Lower bounds a decomposition cannot beat

[I] The figures below are the costs of this construction at the stated assumptions, not proven bounds. [P] The paper proves no lower bound and states that it sees no route to another order of magnitude in qubits.

Numbers the paper commits to (n = 2048, s = 8):

- [P] Logical qubits: m = 1280 exponent-register qubits, plus working registers. Table 5 gives 1399; Sec. 3.2 text gives a peak of 1409 in loop4; the layout totals 1537 logical qubits (1280 + 131 + 7 x 18) and the paper promises fewer than 1600.
- [I] The exponent register is 91.5% of the 1399 logical qubits. At large s it falls toward n/2 = 1024: m = 2048, 1707, 1536, 1280, 1171 for s = 2, 3, 4, 8, 14, with s + 1 shots each.
- [P] Toffoli count: 6.5e9 expected per factoring, over an expected 9.2 shots.
- [I] That is 7.1e8 Toffolis per shot (6.5e9 / 9.2).
- [P] Physical qubits: 897,864 at distance 25, physical error 1e-3, reported as under one million.
- [P] Runtime: 12.07 hours per shot, 4.96 days expected per factoring.
- [I] The 550,400 physical qubits that store the exponent register alone are 61.3% of the footprint. Shrinking every working register to zero leaves more than half a million physical qubits.

The structural obstacle, stated for this construction (not for textbook Shor):

- [P] Textbook and 2021-style Shor keep an n-qubit work register that every controlled multiplication updates in place. Residue arithmetic removes that register: the live arithmetic registers here are the dlog accumulator, a roughly 22-qubit residue register and the 33-qubit output accumulator (Table 4).
- [P] The obstacle that remains is the exponent register. The introduction states that the Chevignard et al. method is incompatible with "qubit recycling", reviving an old bottleneck on the number of input qubits. All m exponent qubits therefore stay coherent from first lookup to the final frequency-basis measurement.
- [P] The exponent register addresses lookups in loop1 once per prime plus one uncompute pass, (|P| + 1) x W1 lookups per shot (Table 3). Sec. 3.2 streams copies of cold qubits to hot storage to serve as those addresses.
- [I] With |P| = n m / (l w1) = 20,805 and W1 = 214 this is about 4.45 million exponent-addressed lookups per shot. The register idles in cold storage for about 12 hours between them.
- [P] Every prime's residue contribution is added into one 33-qubit accumulator (loop4), initialized to a uniform superposition mask and measured once. The exponent register is then measured jointly in the frequency basis (Appendix A.5). The final measurement is a function of all m exponent qubits and all |P| residues together.
- [P] The residue system must satisfy L >= N^m (Eq. 8), so the number of primes, and with it the Theta-tilde(n^3) loop1 cost, grows with the number of exponent qubits m. The classical knobs s and w1 are the only levers on that growth.
- [I] Splitting e into independent chunks needs g^(e_lo) and g^(e_hi 2^(m/2)) recombined by a quantum-by-quantum modular multiplication of two superposed n-bit values. Every saving in the paper rests on each multiplier M_k being a classical constant (tables C_{j,k}, discrete logs, one quantum operand), so a split removes the property that makes the construction cheap. Uncertainty: moderate; the paper does not analyze a split, and a cheaper recombination is not excluded by anything shown here.
- [I] Contrast with textbook Shor: there the obstacle is that the exponent register controls a sequence of modular multiplications on one shared n-qubit work register, and the multiplications of a fixed g commute but all write the same register. Residue arithmetic replaces the n-qubit register by small ones and leaves the coherent m-qubit control in place.

What decomposes, and what each decomposition buys:

- [P] Independent repetitions: s + 1 Ekera-Hastad shots, 9 at s = 8 with 9.2 expected, combined by classical post-processing over all (s+1)-subsets of the shots taken (Sec. 2.3).
- [I] These shots can run on separate machines, so they cut wall-clock time and leave the per-shot qubit count and per-shot depth at the numbers above.
- [P] Residue-by-prime serialization: the outer loop visits primes one at a time to reuse a small workspace.
- [I] Running primes in parallel would trade qubits for time at an unchanged Toffoli count and would still funnel into the single 33-qubit accumulator. Uncertainty: moderate; no data in the paper.
- [P] Classical pieces: choosing g, the prime set, the discrete-log tables, and post-processing are classical per-shot tasks.

## Relevance

(a) Calibration-aware or noise-adaptive compilation on IBM hardware: none. [I]
- [I] The paper assumes uniform depolarizing noise and a fault-tolerant surface-code stack; it contains no compilation against per-qubit calibration data. Its one indirect use is a yardstick: the median CZ errors of 1.95e-3 to 3.37e-3 in note 2507.08088 sit 2x to 3.4x above its 1e-3 gate-error assumption.

(b) Cayley-Dickson, octonion, zero-divisor, nonassociative structure: none. [I]
- [P] No occurrence of octonion, sedenion, Cayley-Dickson, zero divisor or nonassociative in the text (grep).
- [I] Eq. 5 consumes only powers of one classical element g in Z/N, a commutative associative ring, plus the Chinese remainder theorem. No nonassociative structure enters, so nothing in the paper supports a Cayley-Dickson route to factoring.

(c) Shor's algorithm and factoring resource estimates: high. [I]
- [P] What dominates: modular exponentiation is the whole algorithm. The QFT is a semiclassical frequency measurement of n - 1 adaptive phase gates and is negligible.
- [I] Within the arithmetic, loop1 (exponent-addressed discrete-log lookups) is about half the Toffolis and runtime per the re-tally above; the unloop3 uncompute of the residue exponentiation is about 30%.
- [P] Table 3 marks loop1 as the only Theta-tilde(n^3) loop.
- [P] Footprint drivers: idle input qubits in yoked cold storage, hot storage, and a compute region holding six magic-state factories. Each factory spends 150 rounds per CCZ state.
- [I] Shares of the 897,864: cold storage 61.3%, hot storage 19.7%, compute region 19.0% (factories alone about 11%).
- [P] Decompositions used: approximate residue arithmetic; superposition-masked approximate period finding; Ekera-Hastad short exponents with s + 1 shots; windowed lookups; discrete-log precomputation; semiclassical QFT; yoked surface codes; magic state cultivation with 8T-to-CCZ distillation; merged phase corrections.
- [I] Regev's multidimensional algorithm appears only in the introduction's reference list, and the active-volume architecture only there and in the Figure 1 caption, so neither is used.
- [I] The answer to "can Shor on RSA-2048 split into smaller pieces" is: the classical parts and the repetitions split; the quantum core does not. The 1280 coherent exponent qubits and the 6.5e9-Toffoli arithmetic remain one computation per shot.

## Gaps and cautions

- [P] Assumptions 1 and 2 (existence of low-deviation prime sets; success-rate suppression 1/(1 - S)) are conjectures supported by numerics at small N.
- [P] Cold-storage behavior under the loop1 streaming workload is left to future analysis; the author states confidence that 100k physical qubits of slack suffice.
- [I] Table 5 starts at n = 1024; the paper gives no estimates for toy sizes, so extrapolating it to Heron-scale n has no support in the text.
