## Results needed by the project

1. [P] A confidence sequence satisfies
   `P(for every t >= 1, theta_t is in CI_t) >= 1-alpha`
   (Eq. 1, p. 1). Repeated pointwise intervals generally lack that joint
   guarantee (Fig. 1 and P1-P4, p. 2). Lemma 3 identifies the corresponding
   uniform-event guarantee with validity at arbitrary random times, including
   stopping times (p. 19; proof in Appendix C.4, p. 39).
2. [P] The general estimand is
   `mu_t = (1/t) * sum_i E[X_i | F_(i-1)]`, with observations adapted to a
   filtration. The centered sum is
   `S_t = sum_i (X_i - E[X_i | F_(i-1)])` (Sec. 2, pp. 4-5).
   Independence and a common mean are unnecessary for that general target.
   Definition 1 instead requires a nonnegative exponential process to be
   bounded by suitable supermartingales with bounded initial expectation.
   Table 3 assumes the centered sum is a martingale in its unstarred cases.
3. [P] Mixtures of those supermartingales produce uniform boundaries
   (Lemma 2, p. 9), subject to its product-measurability condition.
   Propositions 7 and 8 give two-sided and one-sided beta-binomial mixture
   boundaries under the stated sub-Bernoulli conditions (p. 29). For bounded
   observations in `[a,b]`, the fixed-mean inversion discussed in Sec. 5 uses
   `g(mu)=mu-a`, `h(mu)=b-mu`, and `V_t(mu)=g(mu)*h(mu)*t` (p. 18).
4. [P] Theorem 4 handles bounded adapted observations and a predictable
   bounded predictor `Xhat_i`. Its two-sided radius uses
   `u(sum_i (X_i-Xhat_i)^2)/t`, where `u` is a suitable sub-exponential
   uniform boundary. The displayed coverage is `1-2*alpha`, not `1-alpha`
   (Eq. 23, p. 14). Appendix A.8 proves the conditional exponential
   supermartingale property and uses a union bound over the two tails (p. 35).
5. [P] The paper supplies reproducibility code at
   https://github.com/gostevehoward/cspaper and identifies boundary
   implementations at https://github.com/gostevehoward/confseq
   (footnote 1, p. 18). Those implementations were not installed or validated
   for this repository during the source review.

## Bernoulli arms, dependence, and drift

[I] Terminal correctness is a bounded Bernoulli observation. A constant arm
success probability `p_a` needs a statement such as
`E[X_(a,k) | history before its k-th observation] = p_a`.
Independent stationary repetitions are one sufficient model. The filtration
must include information actually used to choose arms, batches, predictions,
or stopping. A result proved under a smaller history cannot silently be
applied to a policy using additional correlated information.

[I] For predictable arm selection, the retained arm-specific sequence can
support a constant-mean confidence sequence when its conditional-mean and
boundedness assumptions remain valid under the full sampling history.
Arbitrary within-shot dependence between different arms can be retained by
forming valid bounds for each stream and taking a union bound. The union
bound supplies no justification for any individual stream's assumptions.

[I] Howard et al. also allow changing conditional means for their general
average-conditional-mean target. That result does not establish a constant
hardware success probability, remove drift from an arm comparison, identify
an instantaneous endpoint, or prove a physical cause. If arms run during
different periods, subtracting their time-average targets can retain a time
confound. Choose the estimand and balance or model acquisition contexts
before interpreting a correction-policy effect.

[I] Predictable predictors may use prior outcomes; a predictor chosen with
its own future outcome would violate Theorem 4's premise. Likewise, changing
the scientific observable or selecting a model after inspecting validation
outcomes needs its own selection guarantee. Optional-stopping validity alone
supplies neither that guarantee nor a fresh holdout.

## Repository decision and a finite-look alternative

[I] `MODEL_DISCRIMINATION.md` proposes four directional fixed-sample
Clopper-Pearson bounds for F/U at labels s1 and s3. Its gain gate is
`lower_U(s) - upper_F(s) > 0.01` for both labels. Repeated use of those
same fixed-sample bounds at their original error levels is outside that
fixed-sample coverage argument.

[I] One prospective design can replace the four directional bounds with
four valid one-sided confidence sequences, allocate their total crossing
probabilities to at most 0.05, and retain the same gain gate. That design
requires frozen stream identities, a declared mean model, exact look/stopping
rules, and validated numerical boundaries. If Theorem 4's two-sided form is
used instead, account explicitly for its `2*alpha` failure probability.

[I] A simpler finite-look alternative follows directly from a union bound.
Choose K looks and deterministic cumulative sample sizes per arm before
acquisition. At each look, use four directional Clopper-Pearson bounds with
one-sided failure allocations `alpha_(arm,look)` satisfying
`sum_(arm,look) alpha_(arm,look) <= 0.05`. Equal allocation is `0.05/(4*K)`.
Under the binomial assumptions at every planned size, the entire finite
family is simultaneously valid. Choosing a stopping look from that family
preserves coverage. This is a repository deduction using the union-bound
mechanism, not a separately claimed Howard theorem about Clopper-Pearson.
Adaptive cumulative sample sizes outside the prespecified grid require
additional justification or a time-uniform construction.

[I] Confidence sequences permit flexible observation of a statistical
process; IBM jobs still execute their submitted batches. Adaptive spending
must therefore price complete additional jobs, their controls, overheads,
limits, and approvals. Earlier statistical stopping cannot refund shots
already executed inside an accepted job. Use the same scientific endpoints
when comparing expected charge with a fixed-sample plan.

## Acceptance gates and proof boundary

[I] A proposed implementation should name its theorem, filtration, target,
conditional-mean assumptions, per-tail and family error allocation, predictable
sampling policy, numerical method, stop/continue/futility rules, and maximum
budget. Fixtures should include optional stopping, boundary outcomes,
correlated streams, changing conditional means, and malformed identities.
A drifting fixture must be judged against the target actually covered by the
chosen theorem, rather than automatically labeled valid or invalid.

[I] A Rocq development can separate the supermartingale/coverage theorem
from the finite count and decision certificate. The smallest finite-look
chain is fixed-size coverage, a finite union bound, and an implication from
the simultaneous event to the stopped gain decision. A full confidence
sequence additionally needs its time-uniform boundary proof. Numeric root
finding or beta evaluations require certified outward enclosures for exact
machine-checked acceptance; floating-point output alone supplies a narrower
implementation result. Observations cannot prove the sampling assumptions.
