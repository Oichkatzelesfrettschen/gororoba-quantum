## Main claims

[P] For n >= 4, the annihilator of a nonzero element of the 2^n-dimensional Cayley-Dickson algebra has dimension a multiple of 4 and at most 2^n - 4n + 4; every such multiple occurs. The unit elements that reach the bound form 2^(n-4) copies of the Stiefel manifold V_2(R^7). The zero element has annihilator A_n and falls outside the bound.

- [P] Conventions (Section 2). A_0 = R, (a,b)(c,d) = (ac - d*b, da + bc*), (a,b)* = (a*, -b). The standard basis (Definition 2.10) is orthonormal for <x,y> = Re(xy*). i_n = (0,1), C_n = span(1, i_n), and A_n is a left C_n-vector space (Proposition 5.3). L_x has adjoint L_(x*) (Lemma 3.4). Standard basis elements are alternative (Lemma 4.4).
- [P] Theorem 1.2 (proved as Proposition 9.10 and Theorem 13.2): dim Ann(x) <= 2^n - 4n + 4 for every nonzero x in A_n with n >= 4, and every multiple d of 4 with 0 <= d <= 2^n - 4n + 4 is the annihilator dimension of some element. A_4 has dimensions 0 and 4; A_5 has 0, 4, 8, 12, 16; A_6 has 0, 4, ..., 44.
- [P] Theorem 9.8 (Moreno's [M1, Cor. 1.17] reproved): for n >= 2, dim Ann(x) is a multiple of 4. For a nonzero zero divisor x, Lemma 9.5 places x in C_n^perp, where L_x is C_n-conjugate-linear and anti-Hermitian (Lemmas 6.4, 6.5), and the C-codimension of its kernel is even (Lemma 6.7). Invertible elements have nullity 0, and x = 0 has nullity 2^n.
- [P] Lemma 9.2 and Corollary 9.3: xy = 0 exactly when x*y = 0 and exactly when xy* = 0, hence xy = 0 exactly when yx = 0, so left and right annihilators coincide. Lemmas 9.4 and 9.5: every zero divisor is imaginary and orthogonal to C_n. Lemma 9.9: for nonzero (a,b), dim Ann(a,b) is at most 2^(n-1) - 2 + dim(Ann a cap Ann b).
- [P] Theorem 10.1: for a in C_n^perp and alpha, beta in C_n with alpha^2 + beta^2 != 0, Ann(alpha a, beta a) = Ann(a) times Ann(a). Theorem 10.2: for nonzero a in C_n^perp, Ann(a, i_n a) = {(x, i_n x) : x in Ann a} (+) {(y, -i_n y) : y in <<a, i_n>>^perp}, of dimension 2^n - 4 + dim Ann(a), and likewise for -i_n.
- [P] Proposition 11.1: for a quaternionic pair (a,b) of alternative vectors, Ann(a,b) = {(x, (ax)b) : x in the anti-associator Ass'[a,b]}. Corollary 11.2: for orthogonal imaginary a, b in A_3 with |a| = |b| != 0, Ann(a,b) = {(x, -(ab)x/|ab|) : x in <<a,b>>^perp}.
- [P] Proposition 12.1: nonzero (a,b) in A_4 is a zero divisor exactly when a and b are orthogonal imaginary vectors of equal nonzero norm. The unit zero divisors of A_4 form V_2(R^7); the unit pairs (x,y) with xy = 0 form the 14-dimensional group G_2; every zero divisor of A_4 has a 4-dimensional annihilator. Section 7: Aut(A_3) = G_2, with the fibration S^3 -> G_2 -> V_2(R^7) (Theorem 7.1: automorphisms correspond to ordered orthogonal imaginary triples (x,y,z) with z orthogonal to xy).
- [P] Definition of ZD_k(A_n) = {x : |x| = 1, dim Ann x = k} and the stratification of ZD(A_n) (Section 14): the strata "are also complicated, and unknown even in the case of A_5" (Section 1.1). Theorem 14.6: for a, b in A_4 with Ann a cap Ann b != 0 and (a,b) != 0, dim Ann(a,b) is 16 if b = +-i_4 a, 12 if a is orthogonal to b, |a| = |b|, and b != +-i_4 a, and 8 otherwise. Proposition 14.4 and Corollary 14.7: T_5 = ZD_16(A_5) is V_2(R^7) disjoint union V_2(R^7).
- [P] Proposition 15.6 and Theorem 15.7 (also stated as Theorem 1.3): for n >= 4, ZD_(2^n - 4n + 4)(A_n) is homeomorphic to 2^(n-4) disjoint copies of V_2(R^7); a top-dimensional zero divisor of A_n (n >= 5) has the form (a, +-i a) with a top-dimensional in A_(n-1). Remark 15.8 asks whether ZD_(max - c)(A_n) is two copies of ZD_(max - c)(A_(n-1)) for large n.
- [P] Remark 4.5: dim Alt(x), the alternator of x, is a multiple of 4; in A_4 it is 8 or 16; computer calculations give 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 in A_5, and "we do not know if dimensions 20 and 28 can occur". Proposition 8.11 and Section 8: anti-associator and associator dimensions of imaginary standard-basis pairs, built by induction.

Convention map. [I]
- Algebra and basis: the doubling is the one in `cd_sign`, and the standard basis is the repository's e_m with m the bit index (see note 1207.4230, which compares `cd_sign` with the same doubling). A_4 elements are (a,b) with a the lower eight coordinates and b the upper eight at index offset 8. An element e_i + t e_j with i < 8 <= j is (e_i, t e_(j-8)).
- The 84 standard zero divisors: i and j - 8 are distinct imaginary octonion units, 7 * 6 ordered pairs, 2 signs t. Proposition 12.1 identifies exactly these zero divisors in the signed family e_i + t e_j with t = +-1: the pair is orthogonal, imaginary, and of equal norm. Indices both below 8 give (a,0) and indices both at least 8 give (0,b); neither is a zero divisor (Theorem 10.1 with Ann a = 0, as in the proof of Proposition 12.1). The case j - 8 = i gives parallel halves, which Proposition 12.1 excludes.
- V_2(R^7) points: ordered pairs of orthonormal imaginary octonions. The signed basis pairs (+-e_i, +-e_(j-8)) with i != j - 8 number 7 * 6 * 4 = 168, and identifying (a,b) with -(a,b) gives 84 chosen sign representatives. Their unit A_4 representatives are (a,b)/sqrt(2). This 168 is distinct from the 168 signed zero-divisor pairs of the corpus (the t = +1 half of 84 * 4 = 336 ordered pairs of a zero divisor with a basis partner) and from the survey's 168 = 42 * 4.

## Gaps and cautions

- [I] The introductory Theorem 1.2 omits the nonzero hypothesis stated in Proposition 9.10. The bound applies to nonzero elements; Ann(0) is the whole algebra. The existence proof following Theorem 13.2 cites Theorem 10.1 for the special lift, while Theorem 10.2 supplies its required dimension formula.
- [I] The statement of Proposition 15.6 writes the element as (a, i_n a) with a in A_(n-1), while its proof and Theorem 10.2 use the unit i_(n-1) of the factor algebra. The fixtures here use i_4 for A_5, which the nullity 16 confirms.
- [P] The case analysis of Theorem 14.6 (Case 2, subcases) is described by the authors as "rather unpleasant" and uses four quaternionic systems; Remark 4.5 rests on computer calculations. The note author did not re-derive either.
- [I] Theorem 1.3 holds for n >= 4 only. At n = 4 it is one copy, V_2(R^7), the unit zero divisors of A_4.
- [P] The definition of T_n^c in the sequel differs from the one used in this paper's Remark 15.8 (note math-0702075, Definition 8.4).
