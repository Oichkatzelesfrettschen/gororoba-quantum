## Main claims

The paper defines a quantum kernel for data that live in a group and shows a
27-qubit hardware run in which kernel alignment recovers the optimal fiducial
state and classifies with 100 percent test accuracy.

- Covariant feature map: for x in a group G with unitary representation D on n
  qubits and fiducial state |psi> = V|0^n>, the feature state is
  D_x|psi><psi|D_x^dagger and the kernel is
  K(x, x~) = |<psi| D_x^dagger D_x~ |psi>|^2 = |<0^n| V^dagger D_x^dagger D_x~ V
  |0^n>|^2. The circuit is U(x) = D_x V and K is the frequency of the all-zero
  outcome. [from-paper, Eqs. 1-2, Fig. 1c]
- The kernel is left-invariant, K(gx, gz) = K(x, z); a right-invariant version
  uses D_x^dagger. [from-paper, App. B, Eqs. B3-B4]
- The Liu-Arunachalam-Temme discrete-logarithm kernel on Z_p^* is a special
  case: D_x|z> = |x*z mod p> (regular representation, implemented by a modular
  multiplication circuit) and |psi> = 2^(-k/2) sum_{v<2^k} |g^v>. A
  computational-basis fiducial state gives the identity kernel
  K = delta_{x,x~}. [from-paper, App. B.1, Eqs. B13-B15]
- Benchmark problem "labeling cosets with error": G = SU(2)^(tensor n),
  S = the graph-stabilizer group of the device coupling graph, classes
  C+/- = c+/- S with c+/- drawn from [-pi/2, pi/2]^(2n), Euler angles
  (theta_1, theta_2, theta_3 = 0), Gaussian perturbation of variance 0.01.
  [from-paper, p. 3, App. C]
- Fiducial family |psi_lambda> = prod_{(k,t) in E} CZ_{kt} prod_k RY_k(lambda)
  |0^n>, with optimum lambda = pi/2 (the graph state that is invariant under
  S). [from-paper, Eq. 5, Fig. 2]
- Hardware: 27 qubits of ibmq_kolkata (heavy-hex), CZ entanglers matching the
  coupling map, 8,192 shots per circuit. [from-paper, Fig. 2, p. 4]
- Training: 21 SPSA trial steps from lambda = 0.1, 10 training points per
  label, 50 test points per label. The cost F(alpha, lambda) flattens after
  about 11 trial steps; mitigated runs approach lambda = pi/2 faster; both
  mitigated and unmitigated runs reach 100 percent test accuracy after SPSA
  step 13. [from-paper, Fig. 3, p. 4]
- Device characterization: median T1 132 us, median T2echo 148 us, median
  readout error 9.6e-3 (defined as [P(0|1) + P(1|0)]/2), median CNOT error
  7.33e-3 at stretch c = 1 and 9.27e-3 at c = 1.3, median single-qubit error
  2.97e-4 (randomized benchmarking). Median CNOT length 476.4 ns (c = 1) and
  618.6 ns (c = 1.3); single-qubit gate 35.6 ns and 46.2 ns; circuit length
  4.6615 us (c = 1) and 5.9580 us (c = 1.3) including a 340 ns measurement
  pulse that is not stretched. [from-paper, App. E.1]
- The four days of experiments share one calibration of all gates and
  readouts. [from-paper, App. E.1]
- No readout error mitigation was applied; the authors expect threshold
  functions of the kernel to tolerate small readout error. [from-paper,
  Conclusions, p. 5]
- The circuit is shallow enough for classical simulation that exploits the 2D
  device structure. [from-paper, p. 5]
- Quote: "We implement the learning algorithm with 27 qubits on a
  superconducting processor." [from-paper, abstract]

## Methods

Reuse: build D_x as a product of single-qubit rotations, prepare |psi_lambda>
with a CZ layer on device edges, run V^dagger D_x^dagger D_x~ V and read the
all-zero frequency.

1. Choose the group, representation D, and subgroup S. Pick a fiducial family
   that contains an S-invariant state, D_s|psi> = |psi> for all s in S
   (Eq. C3). [from-paper, App. C]
2. Evaluate the Gram matrix K_lambda on the training set with the quantum
   device, R shots per entry. [from-paper, Alg. 1 step 8]
3. Solve the dual SVM program over alpha with CVXOPT for the box constraint
   0 <= alpha_i <= C and sum_i y_i alpha_i = 0. [from-paper, App. D.2]
4. Update lambda with SPSA: two objective evaluations per step independent of
   the dimension of lambda, step sizes c_i = c/(i+1)^gamma and
   a_i = a/(i+1+A)^sigma. [from-paper, Alg. 1 steps 6-10]
5. After P steps, evaluate the aligned kernel and train the final SVM.
   [from-paper, Alg. 1 steps 12-13]
6. Mitigation protocol: dynamical decoupling on idle windows longer than twice
   the single-qubit gate length, with sequence tau/2 - Xp - tau - Xm - tau/2
   and tau = (T_idle - 2 T_X)/2; zero-noise extrapolation from pulse stretch
   factors c = 1 and c = 1.3 with a first-order extrapolation of each kernel
   entry. [from-paper, App. E.1]
7. Diagnostics: total variation distance between measured and noiseless output
   distributions, Hilbert-Schmidt distance between class centers of mass, and
   within-class variance, each against SPSA step. [from-paper, App. E.2]
8. The weighted alignment minimizes the SVM generalization bound
   P(y != f(z)) <= O~(F*(lambda)/m); unweighted and centered alignment are
   listed as alternatives. [from-paper, App. D.1, D.3]

## Relevance

(a) Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: medium. The appendix is a
worked noise-aware protocol (single frozen calibration, DD rule, two-point ZNE,
mitigated versus unmitigated reporting) on a 27-qubit device, but it uses
pulse-stretch ZNE and has no layout selection.

(b) Cayley-Dickson, octonion, zero-divisor structure: low. The covariant kernel
needs a representation with D_x D_y = D_xy, which exists only for associative
groups, so unit octonions and sedenion basis units (nonassociative loops) fall
outside it. [inference] The usable neighbor is an associative substructure such
as a cyclic subgroup or a quaternion subgroup inside the basis-unit loop.
[inference]

(c) Shor and factoring resource estimates: low. Appendix B.1 uses modular
multiplication circuits and a superposition over g^v, the primitives of
discrete-log and order-finding circuits, but gives no gate counts or factoring
estimates and the hardware run uses SU(2)^(tensor 27), not Z_p^*. [inference
for the primitive overlap; from-paper for the absence of counts]

(d) Action items for gororoba-quantum:

1. Log a calibration record per job with the fields of App. E.1: median T1,
   T2echo, readout error as [P(0|1) + P(1|0)]/2, single-qubit RB error,
   two-qubit error, gate and circuit durations, and the calibration timestamp.
   Because the paper froze one calibration over four days, store the timestamp
   so cross-day comparisons can be separated from calibration drift.
   [inference; fields from-paper, App. E.1]
2. Reproduce the DD rule through the runtime option. The installed
   qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.50.0 exposes `dynamical_decoupling.enable`,
   `sequence_type` in {XX, XpXm, XY4}, `extra_slack_distribution`
   {middle, edges}, and `scheduling_method`. The paper sequence matches
   `XpXm` with slack split in the middle. [inference; option names verified by
   introspecting the installed package, equivalence not run on hardware]
3. Replace pulse-stretch ZNE. Runtime 0.50.0 offers ZNE only on the Estimator
   (`resilience.zne_mitigation`, amplifier in {gate_folding,
   gate_folding_front, gate_folding_back, pea}, `noise_factors` as a float
   sequence) and has no pulse stretching and no Sampler ZNE. The kernel entry
   is the expectation of the projector |0^n><0^n|, which expands into 2^n Pauli
   terms, so use the Estimator only for small n and fold gates by hand for the
   Sampler path with factors such as (1, 1.3) and a linear fit. [inference;
   option inventory verified, fractional-factor acceptance not run]
4. Use the Gram-matrix diagonal as a free noise probe: K(x, x) = 1 ideally, so
   the measured diagonal is the all-zero return probability of a mirror-style
   circuit at the same width and depth. [inference; Fig. 3c subtracts the
   identity for display]
5. Run one control kernel on an associative substructure of the Cayley-Dickson
   basis: a cyclic subgroup, or the quaternion group Q8 (regular
   representation as a permutation of its 8 elements on 3 qubits, or left
   multiplication on the quaternion basis as a signed permutation on 2 qubits),
   with a subgroup-invariant fiducial state per App. C Eq. C3. This control
   satisfies the invariance property by construction, so its deviation from
   the ideal kernel measures hardware noise independent of nonassociativity.
   [inference]
6. Adopt SPSA for any noisy low-dimensional structure parameter: two
   evaluations per step regardless of dimension, 21 steps sufficed for a
   single parameter. [from-paper, App. D.2]
