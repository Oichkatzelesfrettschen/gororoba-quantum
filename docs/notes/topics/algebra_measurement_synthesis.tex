Prerequisites are finite-dimensional linear algebra, Hermitian spectral theory, probability on finite sets, basic quantum measurement theory, and versioned computational provenance. \section{Cayley--Dickson algebra from its convention}
\subsection{Recursive construction and basis products}
Start with $\mathbb A_0=\mathbb R$. Define conjugation recursively by $\overline{(a,b)}=(\bar a,-b)$ and multiplication by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cd}
(a,b)(c,d)=(ac-\bar d\,b,\;da+b\bar c).
\end{equation}
After four doublings, $\mathbb A_4$ is the real 16-dimensional sedenion algebra. The basis is $e_0=1,e_1,\ldots,e_{15}$ with repository-specific signs determined by \eqref{eq:cd}. The authoritative executable convention is \texttt{cd\_core}; external packages require a declared basis/sign map checked on all basis pairs.

For basis indices represented in binary, multiplication has the form
\begin{equation}
e_i e_j=\sigma(i,j)e_{i\oplus j},\qquad \sigma(i,j)\in\{-1,+1\}.
\end{equation}
The XOR index follows the doubling; the recursive sign follows conjugation and factor order. Neither sign can be guessed from a differently oriented multiplication table.

\begin{corollary}
No pair of basis units is a zero-divisor pair.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A basis product is one nonzero signed basis unit. Therefore $e_ie_j\ne0$ for every $i,j$. Zero divisors require cancellation among several basis contributions.
\end{proof}
A $16\times16$ image labelled ``zero-divisor adjacency'' is false if its vertices literally mean the 16 basis units and bright entries mean zero products. It might describe composite objects or another relation, but those objects and that relation must be supplied before the image has a mathematical interpretation.

\subsection{Linear multiplication operators}
For $a\in\mathbb A_4$, define $L_a y=ay$. In coordinates,
\begin{equation}
(L_a)_{rj}=\sum_{i=0}^{15}a_i\sigma(i,j)\,\mathbf1\{r=i\oplus j\}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $j$, distinct $i$ produce distinct $r$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparsity}
\operatorname{nnz}(L_a)=16\operatorname{nnz}(a).
\end{equation}
There are no within-column cancellations. A claimed complete sedenion left-multiplier matrix with 40 active entries cannot satisfy this formula. A mask, threshold, separately defined bilinear object, or mislabeled random matrix would be a different object requiring its own definition. ``Box-kite compression'' alone does not define such an operation.

Linearity gives $L_{\alpha a+\beta b}=\alpha L_a+\beta L_b$. Associativity would be needed to infer $L_aL_b=L_{ab}$, because
\begin{equation}
(L_aL_b-L_{ab})c=a(bc)-(ab)c.
\end{equation}
The right side is the associator with the indicated sign. Sedenions are nonassociative, so this encoding is not an algebra homomorphism. It is an ordinary real linear operator representation used within standard complex quantum mechanics.

Thus $E_0$ is a valid effect. An orthonormal basis of its four-dimensional kernel is

These signs are executable claims in the supplementary checks, not conventions transferable without verification to another package.

\subsection{A quantum realization and the meaning of a dark outcome}
Set $K=L_x/\sqrt2$, so $K^\dagger K=E_0$ and $\norm K\le1$. The map
\begin{equation}
V\ket\psi=\ket0\otimes K\ket\psi+
\ket1\otimes\sqrt{I-K^\dagger K}\ket\psi
\end{equation}
is an isometry: $V^\dagger V=K^\dagger K+(I-K^\dagger K)=I$. Extending an isometry to a unitary supplies a conventional ancilla implementation. The selected branch probability is $\bra\psi E_0\ket\psi$. It is zero for a kernel state while the full output remains normalized. The computational state $\ket{0000}$ represents $e_0=1$, not the zero vector.

One may compress the selected output to its rank-12 image. If $T:\mathbb C^{16}\to\mathbb C^{12}$ satisfies $T^\dagger T=E_0$, then its nonzero singular values are four ones and eight $1/\sqrt2$. \section{D14 projections: exact combinatorics before crystallographic analogy}
\subsection{The source set and its second moment}
Let $S$ be the orthogonal projector onto the 14 support coordinates $\{1,\ldots,7,9,\ldots,15\}$ of $\mathbb R^{16}$. The D14 roots are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R=\{\pm e_i\pm e_j:i<j,\ i,j\text{ in the support}\}.
\end{equation}
There are $4\binom{14}{2}=364$ vectors, each of squared length two, and $\mathcal R=-\mathcal R$. For general D$n$, each coordinate occurs in $4(n-1)$ roots and mixed signs cancel off-diagonal second moments. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
\sum_{v\in\mathcal R}vv^T=52S,\qquad \sum_vv=0.
\end{equation}
This exact isotropy on the support explains why PCA cannot select a scientifically distinguished three-dimensional frame. Any orthonormal basis of the degenerate support eigenspace is permissible; one axis-aligned PCA output is not an independent corroborating projection method.

\subsection{Coordinate projection multiplicities}
Keep $k$ support coordinates. Roots whose two indices are discarded contribute
$4\binom{n-k}{2}=2(n-k)(n-k-1)$ copies of the origin. A fixed kept signed axis receives $2(n-k)$ roots, because the second index and its sign are discarded. Roots with both indices kept remain distinct, giving $4\binom{k}{2}=2k(k-1)$ outer points. When all categories are present, there are $1+2k^2$ distinct points.

For $n=14,k=3$, the conservation check is
\begin{equation}
220+6(22)+12(1)=364.
\end{equation}
The distinct locations are the origin, six axes at radius one, and 12 pair-sum points at radius $\sqrt2$. These counts are mathematical results, not uncertain estimates. Marker multiplicity is therefore an information-loss channel, not a statistical uncertainty channel. Sampling error would require a sampling model.

Across all $\binom{16}{3}=560$ ambient coordinate triples, the number of retained support coordinates is three, two, or one. \subsection{What survives a generic projection}
Let $P\in\mathbb R^{16\times3}$ have orthonormal columns and project a source vector to $P^Tv$. A generic continuous random draw avoids the finite collection of coincidence conditions $P^T(v-w)=0$, so all 364 images are distinct with probability one. Nevertheless,
\begin{equation}
P^T(-v)=-P^Tv.
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Inversion cannot disappear under a linear projection]
Every linear image of a centrally symmetric set is centrally symmetric. For a spanning nonempty three-dimensional image, its full orthogonal point group contains $\{I,-I\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $y=P^Tv$, then $-y=P^T(-v)$ belongs to the same image. Both $I$ and $-I$ preserve the image; they are distinct orthogonal transformations in three dimensions.
\end{proof}
Consequently the captions' full-group assignment $C_1$ is inconsistent for the D14 generic image. A proper-rotation subgroup may be trivial; the full orthogonal group still contains inversion, ordinarily denoted $C_i$ when no further operations survive. An analyser error is missing output, not evidence of no symmetry. The 84 blade representatives use a sign gauge and need not be centrally symmetric, so the same argument must not be applied to them without checking their definition.

\end{equation}

\section{Probability objects and reporting discipline}
For a common, explicitly ordered output alphabet, define requested, returned numerical, and observed laws $p_{\rm req},p_{\rm ret},\hat p_{\rm obs}$. For normalized distributions,
\begin{equation}
\TV(p,q)=\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^m|p_k-q_k|
=\max_{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}}|p(A)-q(A)|.
\end{equation}
Missing modes must be distinguished from zero-count modes; eligibility, vacuum, multiphoton, timeout, and corrected outcomes must have explicit treatment before normalization.

The two axes are
\begin{equation}
d_{\rm model}=\TV(p_{\rm ret},p_{\rm req}),\qquad
d_{\rm observation}=\TV(\hat p_{\rm obs},p_{\rm ret}).
\end{equation}
They diagnose different arrows. Their average is not a fidelity. Triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities provide the valid relation
\begin{equation}
|d_{\rm observation}-d_{\rm model}|
\le\TV(\hat p_{\rm obs},p_{\rm req})
\le d_{\rm observation}+d_{\rm model}.
\end{equation}
A small returned/requested discrepancy and a nearly unit observed/requested discrepancy are compatible: the returned matrix may be correct while the associated observed law is very wrong. This still does not identify whether physical application or record association failed.

\begin{proposition}[Coarse graining contracts TV]
For any stochastic map $M$, $\TV(Mp,Mq)\le\TV(p,q)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Using nonnegative entries and column sums one,
$\sum_y|\sum_xM_{yx}(p_x-q_x)|\le\sum_{yx}M_{yx}|p_x-q_x|=\sum_x|p_x-q_x|$.
Divide by two.
\end{proof}
Projection-induced collisions and discarded output labels can therefore conceal distinctions. Conditioning, by contrast, changes the denominator and is not a fixed stochastic map on normalized retained probabilities; it need not contract TV. This is a precise connection between the figure audit and measurement qualification, not a claim that algebraic projections and physical losses have the same mechanism.

\section{One linear effect and the six-state identities}
For orthonormal $f,g$, define $\ket{\pm}=(\ket f\pm\ket g)/\sqrt2$ and $\ket{\pm i}=(\ket f\pm i\ket g)/\sqrt2$. With $\rho_s=\ket s\bra s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sixrho}
\rho_++\rho_-=\rho_f+\rho_g=\rho_{+i}+\rho_{-i}.
\end{equation}
Every off-diagonal term cancels in each pair. For a fixed effect $F$, taking $\tr(F\cdot)$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:six}
p_++p_-=p_f+p_g=p_{+i}+p_{-i}.
\end{equation}
The derivation requires the stated preparation relation and one common linear probability functional. A common trace-preserving preparation channel $\Lambda$ preserves \eqref{eq:sixrho} by linearity, equivalently replacing $F$ by $\Lambda^*(F)$. Thus violation rejects a conjunction of assumptions; it does not uniquely identify phase error, faulty preparation, or the measurement itself.

Put $F_{fg}=\bra fF\ket g=u+iv$, with $F_{ff}=a$ and $F_{gg}=b$. Direct expansion gives
\begin{align}
p_\pm&=(a+b)/2\pm u,\\
p_{+i}&=(a+b)/2-v,&p_{-i}&=(a+b)/2+v.
\end{align}
In this explicit phase convention, $d_X=(p_+-p_-)/2=\operatorname{Re}F_{fg}$ and $d_Y=(p_{+i}-p_{-i})/2=-\operatorname{Im}F_{fg}$. The minus sign is not optional. The even components $m_Z,m_X,m_Y$ should coincide. A common displacement of all four superposition probabilities changes even components and does not, by itself, isolate coherent phase rotation.

\subsection{Input-dependent acceptance breaks the conditional identity}
Let $D$ be the acceptance effect and $G$ the selected accepted effect, with $0\preceq G\preceq D\preceq I$. Define
\begin{equation}
t_s=\tr(D\rho_s),\quad q_s=\tr(G\rho_s),\quad p_s=q_s/t_s\quad(t_s>0).
\end{equation}
The unconditional quantities $q_s,t_s$ are linear; their ratio is generally not. For
\begin{equation}
D=\diag(0.06,0.04),\qquad G=\diag(0,0.04),
\end{equation}
one obtains $(p_f,p_g,p_+,p_-,p_{+i},p_{-i})=(0,1,0.4,0.4,0.4,0.4)$. The conditional pair sums are $1$ and $0.8$ despite perfect states and fixed effects. This exact counterexample falsifies the inference ``six-state deficit implies phase error''.

If $t_s$ are independently admitted probabilities on a common attempt population, then
\begin{align}
t_+p_++t_-p_-&=t_fp_f+t_gp_g,\\
t_++t_-&=t_f+t_g,
\end{align}
with analogous imaginary-quadrature relations. Joint uncertainty must account for shared estimates; multiplying point estimates is not a confidence construction.

Equal basis acceptance does not suffice. Let $D=dI+\epsilon X$ with $0<|\epsilon|<\min(d,1-d)$. Its diagonal entries are both $d$, yet $t_+=d+\epsilon$ and $t_-=d-\epsilon$. Coherent inputs expose the off-diagonal filter component invisible to basis probes.

When $D$ is invertible on its support, define
\begin{equation}
\widetilde F=D^{-1/2}GD^{-1/2},\qquad
\widetilde\rho_s=\frac{D^{1/2}\rho_sD^{1/2}}{t_s}.
\end{equation}
Then $p_s=\tr(\widetilde F\widetilde\rho_s)$. This is a legitimate effect on filtered states. It does not establish the intended effect on the original preparation frame, because $\rho_s\mapsto\widetilde\rho_s$ includes state-dependent normalization. If $D$ has a kernel, restrict inverse square roots to its support and explicitly lose information about never-accepted inputs.

\section{Phase, gauge, compilation, and identifiability}
\subsection{What an intensity measurement can see}
An output diagonal phase $\diag(e^{i\theta_k})$ leaves all direct output probabilities $|(U\psi)_k|^2$ unchanged. To see a relative phase, recombine paths. In a declared two-path experiment, an amplitude $\alpha+\beta e^{i\phi}$ yields
\begin{equation}
p(\phi)=|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2+
2\operatorname{Re}(\bar\alpha\beta)\cos\phi
-2\operatorname{Im}(\bar\alpha\beta)\sin\phi
=A+B\cos\phi+C\sin\phi.
\end{equation}
Several quadratures identify $B,C$ only under that model and a stable denominator. Amplitude imbalance, incoherent admixture, preparation error, drift, and filtering remain competing mechanisms. Fit on development quadratures and test held-out ones. A changed Hong--Ou--Mandel summary concerns a different two-photon interference observable and is not directly a single-photon circuit phase estimate.

\subsection{Gauge freedom and independently compiled experiments}
In a linear representation, circuit probabilities can be written $p=\langle E|G_k\cdots G_1|\rho\rangle$. Under an invertible coordinate change $M$,
\begin{equation}
|\rho\rangle\mapsto M|\rho\rangle,\quad
\langle E|\mapsto\langle E|M^{-1},\quad
G\mapsto MGM^{-1},
\end{equation}
all such probabilities remain unchanged. Physical constraints restrict admissible representatives, but observations alone do not generally identify every internal coordinate convention. This is the practical relevance of GST gauge analysis \cite{gst}; it is not an argument that every physical error is arbitrary.

Likewise, compiling a fresh complete transformation for each state may realize a family $F_s$ rather than one analyser $F$ acting on known states. Perfect requested output columns do not prove that a common physical measurement existed. Preparation/analyser separation needs an implementational contract or a supported self-consistent model.

\subsection{Randomized implementations and their kernels}
Suppose a seed $r$ is drawn with weights $\pi_r$ independently of input label and realizes $F_r$. Then
\begin{equation}
\bar F=\sum_r\pi_rF_r,\qquad \bar p_s=\tr(\bar F\rho_s)
\end{equation}
is an effect. With seed-dependent acceptance $t_{s,r}$, pooled conditional probabilities instead weight seed contributions by $\pi_rt_{s,r}/\sum_u\pi_ut_{s,u}$, which can depend on $s$. Label-independent requested randomization does not automatically yield label-independent effective averaging.

\begin{theorem}[Kernel of a positive ensemble]
For positive weights $\pi_r>0$ and positive semidefinite $F_r$,
$\kerop\bar F=\bigcap_r\kerop F_r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $v$ lies in every kernel, the conclusion follows by summation. Conversely $0=v^\dagger\bar Fv=\sum_r\pi_r\norm{F_r^{1/2}v}^2$ forces every nonnegative summand to vanish, so $F_rv=0$ for each $r$.
\end{proof}
This connection matters: randomization can average biases but can also destroy an exact common dark direction when individual kernels move. A certificate for an ensemble is not a certificate for every realization. For a contrast $\Delta=c^Tp_r$, seed variation is $\operatorname{Var}(\Delta)=c^T\Sigma_{\rm seed}c$. Dispersion among different state labels is not an estimate of repeated-seed variance for one state. Cross state, seed, block, and compilation identities in the design.

\section{The observation chain and the denominator census}
\subsection{Trial definitions precede likelihoods}

An absent requested policy field is not necessarily an explicitly empty policy, and requested policy is not evidence of effective processing.

For genuine independent Bernoulli trials $Y_j$ on a declared population, $K=\sum_{j=1}^nY_j$ has binomial likelihood. Correlations change its variance to
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(\bar Y)=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{j,k}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_j,Y_k).
\end{equation}
Under a stationary correlation model this is often expressed through an effective sample size $n/[1+2\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}(1-h/n)\rho_h]$. This expression is a model-dependent variance summary, not a license to infer raw provenance from a smooth histogram. \begin{proposition}[Fixed-order observational nonidentifiability]

\section{A rigorous target/complement certificate}
\subsection{Assumptions and block structure}
Let $P$ project onto the intended rank-four kernel $\mathcal K\subset\mathbb C^{16}$ and $Q=I-P$. For one admitted physical effect $F$, write
\begin{equation}
F=\begin{pmatrix}A&B^\dagger\\B&C\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{on }\mathcal K\oplus\mathcal K^\perp.
\end{equation}
The target statement requires a fixed effect, or a precisely declared valid ensemble effect, known preparation relations, qualified acceptance and processing, a supported likelihood, and controlled drift. A small response on four named inputs alone does not establish these premises or exclude an additional dark direction.

\begin{theorem}[Separated low subspace and principal angle]\label{thm:angle}
Suppose $F\succeq0$, $\lambda_{\max}(A)\le a$, $\lambda_{\min}(C)\ge\gamma> a\ge0$. Let $S$ project onto the four lowest eigenvectors of $F$. Then
\begin{equation}
\lambda_4(F)\le a<\gamma\le\lambda_5(F),\qquad
\norm{(I-S)P}^2\le\frac a\gamma.
\end{equation}
Consequently the largest principal angle between $\ran P$ and $\ran S$ obeys
$\theta_{\max}\le\arcsin\sqrt{a/\gamma}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Courant--Fischer applied to the four-dimensional trial space $\mathcal K$ gives \[\lambda_4(F)\le\max_{u\in\mathcal K,\norm u=1}u^\dagger Fu\le a\]. Its dual form applied to the twelve-dimensional space $\mathcal K^\perp$ gives \[\lambda_5(F)\ge\min_{v\in\mathcal K^\perp,\norm v=1}v^\dagger Fv\ge\gamma\]. The strict gap makes $S$ a uniquely specified spectral subspace. Positivity and the spectral decomposition give $F\succeq\gamma(I-S)$. For each unit $u\in\mathcal K$,
$a\ge u^\dagger Fu\ge\gamma\norm{(I-S)u}^2$.
Take the supremum. For equal-dimensional subspaces the singular values of $(I-S)P$ on $\ran P$ are the sines of principal angles, proving the conclusion.
\end{proof}
The off-diagonal block is not assumed zero. Positivity controls it implicitly. If $C\succ0$, the Schur complement gives $B^\dagger C^{-1}B\preceq A$, another view of the constrained coupling.

For simultaneous confidence bounds $a_U,\gamma_L$, the 10-degree program target requires
\begin{equation}
\gamma_L>0,\qquad \frac{a_U}{\gamma_L}\le\sin^2(10^\circ)
=0.0301536896\ldots,
\end{equation}
with joint coverage at least 95 percent under the admitted statistical premises. Two separate nominal 95-percent intervals do not automatically provide 95-percent joint coverage. Allocate a joint error budget or use a simultaneous construction. Passing this certificate is neither full-effect tomography nor evidence of nonstandard quantum mechanics.

\subsection{What a fifth-low-eigenvalue statement needs}
\begin{theorem}[A fifth-eigenvalue upper bound]\label{thm:fifth}
For the same positive block matrix, let $a=\lambda_{\max}(A)$ and $c=\lambda_{\min}(C)$. Then $\lambda_5(F)\le a+c$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a unit eigenvector $v$ of $C$ with eigenvalue $c$ and the five-dimensional trial space $W=\mathcal K\oplus\operatorname{span}\{v\}$. Positivity gives the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality $|u^\dagger Fv|^2\le(u^\dagger Fu)(v^\dagger Fv)\le a\norm u^2 c$ for $u\in\mathcal K$. For $w=u+tv$,
\begin{align*}
w^\dagger Fw&\le a\norm u^2+2|t|\sqrt{ac}\norm u+c|t|^2\\
&=(\sqrt a\norm u+\sqrt c|t|)^2
\le(a+c)(\norm u^2+|t|^2).
\end{align*}
Courant--Fischer over $W$ proves $\lambda_5(F)\le a+c$.
\end{proof}
A small complementary Rayleigh quotient gives an upper bound on $c$, but the fifth-eigenvalue conclusion additionally needs the target block bound $a$ and their simultaneous uncertainty. It is not enough to find one dark-looking complementary input while leaving the original four-dimensional block uncontrolled.

\subsection{Complex preparations and finite reconstruction}
For a Hermitian $d\times d$ block, there are $d^2$ real parameters. Basis preparations determine diagonal entries. For $j<k$, the states $(e_j+e_k)/\sqrt2$ and $(e_j+i e_k)/\sqrt2$ determine real and imaginary off-diagonal entries through the expansions above. This minimal bank has $d+2\binom d2=d^2$ settings. For the four- and twelve-dimensional blocks, the total is $4^2+12^2=160$. Complementary negative-phase preparations add consistency and drift checks, rather than new algebraic degrees of freedom.

Restricting to real states cannot determine imaginary antisymmetric components of a general complex Hermitian effect. A real-effect restriction must be independently justified. Reconstructing a Hermitian matrix from noisy point estimates is not itself a confidence bound. One conservative route bounds every admitted probability simultaneously, propagates interval errors to an operator-norm bound, then uses Weyl's inequality to enclose extreme eigenvalues. Tighter matrix concentration is possible only with its sampling premises and constants declared. Neither approach repairs unqualified physical trial provenance.


\subsection{An explicit conservative confidence construction}
This construction is intentionally simple enough to audit. Assume that all $N=160$ minimal-bank settings return genuine independent Bernoulli trials, with $n_s$ qualified trials at setting $s$. They need not have identical probabilities. For total error budget $\alpha$, define
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_s=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2N/\alpha)}{2n_s}}.
\end{equation}
Hoeffding and the union bound give $|\hat p_s-p_s|\le\epsilon_s$ for all settings simultaneously with probability at least $1-\alpha$. Independence across settings is not needed for this union step, although each individual bound still needs its admitted within-setting model. Let $\epsilon=\max_s\epsilon_s$. For the minimal reconstruction, diagonal errors are at most $\epsilon$. Each real or imaginary off-diagonal reconstruction subtracts half of two diagonal estimates from one superposition estimate, so its error is at most $2\epsilon$. A complex off-diagonal entry therefore has magnitude error at most $2\sqrt2\epsilon$.

For a Hermitian block of dimension $d$, its error matrix $H$ consequently obeys
\begin{equation}
\norm H_2\le\norm H_\infty
\le\delta_d:=\epsilon+2\sqrt2(d-1)\epsilon.
\end{equation}
The first inequality follows from $\norm H_2\le\sqrt{\norm H_1\norm H_\infty}$ and Hermiticity, which makes the absolute column and row norms equal. Weyl's variational eigenvalue bound gives the simultaneous valid choices
\begin{equation}
a_U=\lambda_{\max}(\widehat A)+\delta_4,\qquad
\gamma_L=\lambda_{\min}(\widehat C)-\delta_{12}.
\end{equation}
Physical effect constraints may cap a valid upper bound at one or a lower bound at zero. Ad hoc PSD projection of a point estimate does not automatically preserve these particular error bounds. Missing, regenerated, or correlated trials cannot simply be inserted into $n_s$.

This proves a complete, conservative route from admitted counts to the angle theorem. It also exposes cost. For equal $n$, a desired per-probability error $\epsilon$ needs
\begin{equation}
n\ge\frac{\log(320/\alpha)}{2\epsilon^2},\qquad n_{\rm total}=160n.
\end{equation}
The twelve-dimensional block's row-sum factor is $1+22\sqrt2\simeq32.11$, so a naive elementwise method can be expensive. Matrix-aware or contrast-specific designs may improve efficiency, but must prove their own simultaneous coverage. This is a mathematical reason to qualify the observation model before spending on precision.

\section{A unified framework: preservation obligations across observation maps}
The following synthesis is a proposed organizational framework, not a priority claim or a new physical law. Its purpose is to make the program's next decision explicit.

For each arrow in the pipeline, declare an object type, a map, and an invariant to preserve. Algebra-to-operator encoding preserves bilinearity in the second argument and a specified kernel, while not preserving multiplication composition. Projection preserves central inversion and pushforward mass, while losing source identity. A compiler contract preserves a requested numerical effect within tolerance. A claim is reviewable when its evidence graph supplies a path of justified preservation obligations from prediction to endpoint. An unresolved arrow remains a named premise. This viewpoint explains why visually attractive agreement can fail certification: a downstream statistic may be insensitive to precisely the missing invariant. It also explains why a negative result can be productive: an intervention can close one path while preserving valid earlier mathematics and later service observations.

Three useful derived connections follow.

First, information loss and conditioning must be distinguished. Linear coarse graining contracts distinguishability, whereas state-dependent normalization changes the model class. The first calls for conserved mass and labels; the second calls for acceptance qualification.

Second, averaging and kernel certification interact nontrivially. A legitimate positive ensemble's exact kernel is the intersection of individual kernels. Randomization can improve average calibration while making an exact common kernel smaller. Measure the target scientific contrast across seeds rather than treating randomization as an automatic cure.

Third, identity closure and causal identifiability are independent. Perfect hashes and job IDs can establish that the same wrong response recurs. Only a discriminating intervention can determine whether changing a predecessor changes that response. Conversely, a well-randomized intervention with an unqualified result-association chain still cannot identify the physical instrument. Both closures are needed.
