## Main claims

[P] Several circuits can run on one IBM device at once, with reliable disjoint partitions chosen from topology, calibration, and crosstalk data, at a fidelity loss the user bounds by a threshold.

- [P] Contributions (Sec. 1): a parallelism manager that selects how many circuits to co-run, two qubit-partition algorithms, a crosstalk-aware partition process that the authors call the first, an improved mapping-transition step, and a VQE use case that the authors call the first multi-programming demonstration on a NISQ algorithm.
- [P] Parallelism manager (Sec. 4, Fig. 3a): circuits are ordered by density, number of CNOTs divided by qubits; K circuits are picked so that the qubit counts sum to at most N hardware qubits; K = 1 means independent execution.
- [P] Crosstalk characterization (Sec. 5.1.2): simultaneous randomized benchmarking (SRB) on CNOT pairs at one-hop distance only, because the effect on IBM devices is significant only there; a pair counts as strong when E(gi|gj) > 3 E(gi); the authors run SRB on ibmq_toronto only, twice, and report that the strongly coupled pairs persist across days; the ibmq_manhattan results do not use crosstalk data (Sec. 7.1.4).
- [P] Greedy sub-graph partition (GSP, Alg. 1, Eq. 1): enumerate connected k-qubit subgraphs, score = graph diameter L + average CNOT error x CNOT count + sum of readout errors, with crosstalk-corrected CNOT errors for pairs next to already allocated partitions; cost O(k^3 n^k).
- [P] Heuristic partition (QHSP, Alg. 2, Eqs. 2-3): grow a partition from high-degree starting qubits by merging the neighbor with the highest fidelity degree; polynomial cost O(m k^2 + n log n + g). GSP and QHSP allocate the same partitions in all but one two-circuit experiment, where GSP gains 6% fidelity.
- [P] Post-partition admission (Sec. 5.2): the difference DeltaS between the partition scores for simultaneous and independent execution is compared with a user threshold delta, 0.1 in the main runs and 0.2 in the stress test; DeltaS above delta reduces the number of co-run circuits.
- [P] Mapping transition (Sec. 6, Alg. 3): SWAP or Bridge (four CNOTs, mapping unchanged) chosen by a cost function with first- and extended-layer terms, O(g n^2.5).
- [P] Metrics (Sec. 7.1.1): probability of a successful trial (PST) over 8192 trials, number of additional CNOTs, and trial reduction factor (TRF, from Das et al.).
- [P] Results: two small circuits on a 27-qubit device and three or four on a 65-qubit device run at TRF 2, 3, and 4. Against the best-partition independent baseline (PHA) fidelity falls by 5.4%, 1.5%, and 6.4%; against the unpartitioned baseline (HA) it rises by 31.8%, 5.3%, and 13.3%. Five small circuits at delta = 0.2 (DeltaS = 0.18) lose 9.5% (0.493 against 0.54). In the deuteron VQE, eight co-run circuits raise throughput from 0.03 to 0.25 and the energy error from 9% to 13.3% (Table 4; mean DeltaS 0.06).
- [P] Crosstalk example (Sec. 5.1.1, Figs. 4-5): the same circuit on two partitions loses 36.8% and 23.1% of PST when the partitions sit at crosstalk distance and loses essentially nothing when they do not.
- [P] Other observations: two copies of one circuit on the two best partitions of a 127-qubit device do not always favor the higher-scored partition, which the authors attribute to calibration error and to drift while the job waits in the queue (Sec. 8.1); an as-late-as-possible schedule leaves the fidelity of a short circuit unchanged beside a circuit up to four times deeper on a 7-qubit device (Sec. 8.2, Fig. 16).
- [P] Scope: CNOT-based devices of the ibmq generation, no dynamic circuits, no mid-circuit measurement or reset, one device characterized for crosstalk, and PST values reported without intervals or tests (a search of the text for error bars, confidence, standard deviation, statistic, p-value, and equivalence finds no such analysis).

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Several instances of one program on disjoint qubits. Repository claim: DESIGN_NOTES.md, "Packed replicas and variance decomposition", and HARDWARE_PLAN.md -- every group runs on several disjoint copies of its compiled layout, one shot yields one sample per replica, and replica-to-replica differences are hardware differences. Source item: Sec. 2.3, which cites concurrent quantum circuit sampling (ref. 34) as executing multiple instances of the same program simultaneously, and Sec. 8.1 with two copies of one circuit on the two best partitions. Model map: a replica is one instance of the same program on its own partition. Label: "known technique reproduced" (the paper treats concurrent instances as prior art and as a special case). Not established by QuMC alone: replicas that are the identical ISA circuit on an edge-preserving embedding or per-replica registers. The [CQCS reading note](2109.01714.md) now covers ref. 34: CQCS pools outputs for variational optimization and also runs distinct VQE measurement settings concurrently, in simulation.
- [I] Placement by calibration-scored subgraphs. Repository claim: packing.py -- `candidate_replicas` enumerates every subgraph-isomorphic embedding of the compiled pattern in the CZ coupling graph with VF2, ranks embeddings by layout score, and `place` takes disjoint ones down to a floor times the best score; `allocate_fusion_replicas` places all groups of a backend and kind jointly by backtracking. Source item: GSP (Alg. 1, Eq. 1) and QHSP (Alg. 2, Eqs. 2-3). Model map: QuMC partitions the device for circuits that are routed afterward inside their partition; the repository fixes the routed pattern first and moves it, so a replica uses the same gates on the same relative couplers. The floor (0.9, and 0.8 for hybrid Shor) governs how far below the best score a replica may sit, and is the repository's analog of the paper's delta, which bounds a predicted DeltaS rather than a measured difference. Label: "new implementation or measurement" for the identical-ISA embedding, the joint backtracking allocator, and the score floor only. Not established by the source: embedding of an already routed circuit, replica-0 references, or any allocator that optimizes several circuit kinds under one placement search.
- [I] Qubit and edge exclusion. Repository claim: HARDWARE_PLAN.md, "From calibration to circuits" -- the runner excludes every qubit and CZ edge outside the QUALITY policy or flagged faulty, before layout and routing, and placement avoids the excluded elements. Source item: Eqs. (1)-(3), which rank by error rate and do not exclude. Model map: QuMC lowers the score of a poor qubit; the repository removes it from the admitted graph. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: any threshold, any hard exclusion, or any treatment of faulty-flagged hardware.
- [I] Crosstalk-aware placement. Repository claim: DESIGN_NOTES.md and `live_run.allocate_fusion_replicas` -- replicas of one group exclude neighbors in the admitted CZ graph. `ReplicaPolicy.cross_group_buffer=True` also excludes the physical-CZ neighbors of qubits assigned to other groups in the same backend/kind cluster. The physical graph includes couplers excluded by quality filtering, because those couplers still connect the device. Source item: Sec. 5.1.2 and Fig. 6a, where the significant crosstalk geometry is a pair of CNOTs separated by one hop, such as CX(0,1) beside CX(2,3). Model map: the default cross-group buffer keeps different groups' used qubits nonadjacent in the physical coupling graph. The option `--no-cross-group-buffer` retains disjoint placement without that buffer. The [historical pilot summary](prior_work_synthesis.md#what-the-device-showed-in-the-units-it-reports) reports a cross-group neighbor in the earlier unbuffered placement; that adjacency remains a property of the recorded run. The repository has no SRB characterization and no crosstalk map; QuMC has one for a single 27-qubit device and ignores crosstalk on the second. Label: "repository implementation of prior-art spacing" for the explicit placement rules and their audited policy: [palloq](2112.07091.md), Secs. III-B and IV and Alg. 1, already reserves idle-qubit buffers between programs. Not established by QuMC or palloq: whether one-hop crosstalk of this form exists on Heron fixed-frequency CZ devices (the paper's data are for the earlier CNOT generation), and the cost of SRB there.
- [I] Throughput and fidelity evaluation. Repository claim: HYBRID_SHOR.md, "Deficiencies and falsifiers", and HARDWARE_PLAN.md, "Statistics that decide a result" -- the shared replica-0 reference PUB and `global_packing_crosstalk`, with the interval chosen by `variance.difference_interval`: Newcombe's continuity-corrected hybrid score interval for Bernoulli scores, and the distribution-free Hoeffding interval for other per-shot scores in [0,1]. `variance.equivalence` tests its 90 percent interval against the preregistered margin 0.03 and reports a 95 percent difference interval. A separate 90 percent Hoeffding sensitivity interval carries its own within-margin flag. The verdicts are `equivalent_within_0.03`, `packed_degradation_detected`, `packed_improvement_detected`, and `difference_not_resolved`; fewer than 64 shots per arm returns `insufficient_shots`. Collection requires both PUBs to run under one calibration, with a confirmed shot window clear of calibration updates, before reporting the calibrated comparison. Source item: Sec. 5.2 (DeltaS against delta), Sec. 7.1.1 (PST, TRF), Figs. 8-10, Table 4. Model map: QuMC compares a calibration-derived score before the run and PST point values across separate runs; the repository compares measured yields of a packed replica and a reference copy inside one job and one calibration window, with an interval and an equivalence margin of 0.03. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: any interval, any hypothesis test, any same-job reference, or any rule that withholds a comparison across a calibration change (Sec. 8.1 names the drift problem and offers no remedy).
- [I] Concurrent dynamic circuits. Repository claim: HYBRID_SHOR.md Roadmap item 9 and HARDWARE_PLAN.md "Risks" -- nine concurrent feed-forward streams against two cost 187 dt of schedule on every replica-0 qubit (23 dt initialization alignment, 44 dt broadcast width, 120 dt reset stagger), with a historical Bernoulli-score equivalence verdict on the shared layout under the 0.03 margin. The wider Hoeffding sensitivity interval supplies a separate distribution-free check; the Bernoulli verdict alone does not establish the same margin under that check. Source item: Sec. 2.2 and Sec. 8.2, the as-late-as-possible argument that execution-time differences between circuits can be ignored because queue waiting dominates and that unequal depths do not affect fidelity. Model map: the paper's argument concerns unequal unitary depth; it contains no measurement, reset, or feed-forward, so it is silent on controller serialization. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: any dynamic-circuit behavior, any Heron result, any scheduler timing.
- [I] Calibration drift. Repository claim: HARDWARE_PLAN.md and `live_run.py` -- calibration is fetched in the process that decides, compiles, simulates, and submits; `--submit` revalidates before each batch and stops the run on change. Source item: Sec. 5.1.3 (retrieve the latest calibration before each use) and Sec. 8.1 (the best partition at submission may not be the best at execution). Model map: the paper states the hazard qualitatively; the repository enforces it as a gate. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: any mechanism that detects or bounds drift between submission and execution.

## Action items

1. Cite the prior-art boundary in DESIGN_NOTES.md: multi-programming with calibration-scored partitions, crosstalk-aware placement, and several instances of one program are prior art (this paper and its refs. 10, 23, 29, 34). The repository-specific implementation surfaces are the identical-ISA embedding, joint allocator, physical-CZ policy record, replica-0 reference with score-appropriate intervals and the 0.03 margin, and dynamic-circuit measurements. Generic physical buffers also have prior art in palloq; these implementation details require comparison and evidence before any novelty claim.
2. Retain a record-by-record census of cross-group adjacent qubit pairs in the archived packed placements (qubits in different groups joined by a physical CZ coupler). A historical pilot check and the default buffered allocator do not certify every archived record. Acceptance: name each record, physical coupling snapshot, pair count, and PUB association; a nonzero count makes that historical packing comparison a statement about adjacency as well as scale. The broader archived-record census remains open. Source: Sec. 5.1.2, Fig. 6a.
3. State in HARDWARE_PLAN.md that the margin 0.03 bounds a measured difference between a packed replica and a reference copy, and that the paper's delta bounds a predicted score difference; the replica floor, not the margin, is the analog of delta. Source: Sec. 5.2.
4. Do not spend QPU time on an SRB map. The paper calls the characterization expensive (Sec. 5.1.2), the 600 s window is the binding budget, and the same-job reference PUB measures the aggregate effect directly.
5. The reference-reading gate is closed at the scope recorded in [CQCS, ref. 34](2109.01714.md) and [palloq, ref. 29](2112.07091.md). Credit replica sampling, parallel measurement tasks, and physical buffers as prior art. Read [QuCP](2112.00387.md) as the direct precedent for concurrent folded-circuit ZNE; retain its patch-dependence and ideal-informed extrapolator-selection limits when designing diagnostics.
