## Results that determine the interpretation

[P] Equation (2.1) defines the unsymmetrized spectrum with
`exp(+i*omega*t)<F(t)F(0)>`. For `V=A*F*sigma_x`, equations (2.6a,b) give
`Gamma_up=(A/hbar)^2*S_FF(-omega01)` and
`Gamma_down=(A/hbar)^2*S_FF(+omega01)`. The reservoir emits at the negative
frequency and absorbs at the positive frequency under that convention.
The harmonic-oscillator example, equation (2.4), gives positive-frequency
weight proportional to `n_B+1` and negative-frequency weight proportional
to `n_B`. At zero temperature the latter vanishes for the passive oscillator.

[P] Equation (2.7) uses thermal equilibrium to derive detailed balance.
Equation (2.8) defines a frequency-dependent effective temperature from a
spectral ratio even for a stationary nonequilibrium source. Such a parameter
need not be a thermodynamic temperature shared across frequencies or modes.
For positive rates the corresponding qubit expression is
`k_B*T_eff(omega01)=hbar*omega01/log(Gamma_down/Gamma_up)`.
Equal rates give the infinite-temperature limit; rate inversion gives a
negative effective temperature for the two-level probe. Zero-rate boundaries
require limits or bounds rather than an unqualified finite point estimate.

[P] Equations (2.11)-(2.16) distinguish symmetric heating and antisymmetric
damping in the harmonic-oscillator model. The symmetrized spectrum is the
half-sum `[S(+omega)+S(-omega)]/2`. Replacing the unsymmetrized spectrum by
that half-sum loses the excitation/relaxation asymmetry. A one-sided
engineering convention can introduce a separate factor of two and must be
declared explicitly.

[P] Appendix B.1 treats the bath as stationary, evaluates the correlation in
the absence of the probe coupling, and derives rates perturbatively.
Footnote 21, p. 57, requires `tau_c << t << 1/Gamma`; p. 58 explicitly
requires short bath correlation time and sufficiently weak coupling. A bath
can be stationary without being thermal. Neither stationarity nor equilibrium
follows merely from fitting an exponential curve.

## Repository mapping and alternatives

[I] The [paired-rate source note](cond-mat-0210247.md) gives an implementable
probability model and a separate assignment-error layer. Use that model to
test effective device dynamics before interpreting rates as bath spectra.
Possible outcomes include a fitted relaxation law, a failure of the stationary
model, and a controlled change under DD or a scheduling intervention. Each
outcome is useful with an explicit endpoint and uncertainty model.

[I] A native gate-level relaxation experiment, a deliberately engineered
noise-spectroscopy experiment, and an encoded field simulation have different
observables and controls. The native experiment sees the device's effective
environment. Engineering a spectrometer requires calibrated frequency/coupling
control. The encoded experiment measures a specified model after mapping,
truncation and compilation. Computational occupations or pair correlations
require their own model-to-observable validation before a radiation analogy.

To admit a temperature claim, state the energy gap, spectral convention,
stationarity evidence, thermal assumption, SPAM constraints, rate confidence
region and zero/inverted-rate handling. To admit a vacuum-specific claim,
separate alternative losses and specify the physical coupling/detector model.
The review gives no conversion from an uncalibrated qubit decay rate to a
Casimir force, emitted photon flux or absolute vacuum-energy density.
