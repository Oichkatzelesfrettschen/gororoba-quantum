## Source results

- [P] Theorem 1 assumes either Conjecture 2 or 3. A polynomial-time classical
  sampler for every IQP circuit within `1/192` error in the `l1` norm would
  then imply collapse of the polynomial hierarchy to its third level.
  The approximation quantity is `l1` distance; the conventional total
  variation distance is half that quantity.
- [P] Conjecture 3 concerns uniformly random degree-three Boolean polynomials.
  It asserts average-case hardness of approximating the squared normalized
  gap to multiplicative error `1/4 + o(1)` on a `1/24` fraction of instances.
  The conjecture is an assumption of the result.
- [P] Appendix B implements degree-one, degree-two, and degree-three monomials
  with Z, CZ, and CCZ gates. For the resulting diagonal phase circuit `D_f`,
  the zero-output amplitude of `H^n D_f H^n` is `gap(f) / 2^n`, where
  `gap(f) = count(f=0) - count(f=1)`.
- [P] The implementation discussion requires correctly distributed circuit
  instances, controlled approximation error as size increases, and a
  polynomial resource bound. It identifies nonlocal interactions as a
  physical implementation challenge.

## Repository mapping and claim boundary

- [I] The repository's Boolean phase oracle and Walsh readout have the same
  circuit form. Adding the linear phase `x dot y` identifies a general output
  amplitude with the corresponding Walsh coefficient divided by `2^n`.
  Label: known circuit identity reproduced. The source supports the circuit
  relation; the repository's structured associator family supplies different
  input instances from the random family in Conjecture 3.
- [I] The two dimension-32 cubic spectral classes remain finite algebraic
  evidence. Their degree and CCZ implementation establish neither average-case
  hardness nor an advantage over the efficient classical formulas identified
  in [CLAIM_BOUNDARIES.md](../../gororoba_quantum/CLAIM_BOUNDARIES.md).
  Label: candidate new classification result, with its present finite scope.
- [I] One Walsh sample, a distribution estimate, phase reconstruction, and a
  classification are distinct output tasks. A fair resource comparison must
  price the requested output, input preparation, synthesis, shots, decoding,
  and the strongest relevant classical method. Explicit reuse can amortize
  preparation; a resource ledger must retain that preparation cost.
- [I] Physical multiprogramming can reduce the charge for obtaining samples
  from easy instances. That result has an experimental-throughput boundary;
  it does not satisfy the theorem's family and approximation assumptions.

## Actions and falsifiers

1. Keep exact small-instance Fourier and labeled-probability checks as
   validation fixtures for any new phase family. Record the input family
   separately from the polynomial degree and native gate set.
2. Before a hardness claim, specify an instance distribution, output task,
   approximation metric, scaling parameter, complexity assumptions, and
   classical comparator. An efficient sampler for the selected family
   falsifies that family's proposed sampling advantage.
3. Reuse the existing [hidden-shift notes](0811.3208.md) and
   [claim ledger](prior_work_synthesis.md) when separating oracle-query results
   from complete physical resource comparisons.
