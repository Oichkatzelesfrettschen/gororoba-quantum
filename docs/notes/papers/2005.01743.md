## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page
indices. M_s is the mean wavepacket overlap of the single-photon component, M_tot that of the total
input state including the multi-photon component, M_sn that between the single photon and an
additional noise photon, and g2 = g(2)(0).

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): the paper explores "how the nature of unwanted multi-photon components of
  single photon sources affect the interference visibility", and finds "that the impact of
  multi-photon events has thus far been underestimated, and that the effect of pure dephasing is even
  milder than previously expected".
- [P] Prior result (p. 1): when the additional photons occupy the same spectral and temporal mode as
  the predominant ones, V_HOM = M_tot - g2.
- [P] Model (p. 2): an imperfect source is a true single photon mixed with separable noise at a beam
  splitter, restricted to small g2 (typically g2 < 0.3) so that the noise field carries at most one
  additional photon.
- [P] Eq. 1 (p. 2): V_HOM = M_s - [(1 + M_s)/(1 + M_sn)] g2, with 0 <= M_sn <= M_s.
- [P] Limiting cases (p. 2): for noise photons identical to the single photons (M_sn = M_s),
  V_HOM = M_s - g2, so M_s = M_tot; for noise with no overlap (M_sn = 0),
  V_HOM = M_s - (1 + M_s) g2. "The degree to which HOM interference is affected by a non-zero g2 is
  therefore dependent on the origin of the additional photons."
- [P] Emulation (pp. 2-3): with fully distinguishable added photons (a laser at a different
  wavelength) a fit of V_HOM = M_s - (1 + M_s) g2 gives M_s = 0.94 +- 0.02; with identical added
  photons (a delayed copy of the source's own photons) a fit of V_HOM = M_s - g2 gives
  M_s = 0.89 +- 0.01; these two values "represent the upper and lower bound of the intrinsic single
  photon indistinguishability" of the source used, and "it is necessary to know the origin of the
  unwanted photon emission".
- [P] Quantum-dot sources (pp. 3-4): for a neutral exciton the residual two-photon component is
  limited by imperfect excitation-laser suppression, for a trion by re-excitation during the pulse;
  fits to the distinguishable-noise form give M_s = 0.920 +- 0.003 (exciton) and M_s = 0.942 +- 0.004
  (trion).
- [P] Eq. 2 (p. 4): for both source types "the multi-photon component ... can be treated as
  separable distinguishable noise", so in the limit of low g2 the intrinsic indistinguishability is
  M_s = (V_HOM + g2)/(1 - g2), valid "for any QDSPS with a small g2 and fast excitation pulse".
- [P] Consequence (p. 5): "It is therefore likely that many of the values of single photon
  indistinguishability that have been quoted in the literature for QD sources are in fact an
  underestimate of the true value." The paper adds that its approach "is only valid for separable
  noise" and that the non-separable case needs further study.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Belenos acceptance and routing controls
  ([bdsc-qualification.md](../../design/bdsc-qualification.md)). The provider's processor records carry a
  Hong-Ou-Mandel visibility and a g(2)(0) among their performance fields; Eq. 1 separates a visibility deficit caused by
  multi-photon emission (a g2 term of slope between 1 and 1 + M_s) from a loss of single-photon
  indistinguishability M_s. A visibility change across a calibration, as in the HOM episode recorded
  in [2606.18408](2606.18408.md), is attributable to M_s only after the g2 term is subtracted, and the
  slope depends on the noise origin, so one reported pair (V_HOM, g2) fixes M_s only between the two
  limiting forms.
- [I] Ascella signed-coherence prediction
  ([ascella-signed-coherence-conformance.md](../../design/ascella-signed-coherence-conformance.md)).
  Treating the processor record's HOM field as a raw V_HOM, the pair quoted there (HOM 87.28 %,
  g2 1.9 %) gives through Eq. 2
  M_s = (0.8728 + 0.019)/(1 - 0.019), about 0.909 for distinguishable noise, and M_s = 0.8728 + 0.019,
  about 0.892, for identical noise; this arithmetic is derived here and the design note does not use
  it.
- [I] The multiphoton-to-single-photon separation also bears on any two-photon or multiphoton
  postselection the repository adds: a two-photon coincidence rate scales with M_s and g2 separately,
  and the relation holds only for separable noise at g2 below about 0.3.
