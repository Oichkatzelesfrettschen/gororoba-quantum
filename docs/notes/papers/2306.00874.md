## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the PDF page indices, which equal the printed page numbers
for the main text and continue through the supplementary pages.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): a "first user-ready general-purpose quantum computing prototype based on single
  photons"; "a high-efficiency quantum-dot single-photon source feeding a universal linear optical
  network on a reconfigurable chip for which hardware errors are compensated by a machine-learned
  transpilation process"; one-, two- and three-qubit gate fidelities of 99.6 +- 0.1%, 93.8 +- 0.6% and
  86 +- 1.2%.
- [P] Architecture (p. 3): a quantum-dot source operated at 80 MHz emits "with 55% probability into the
  collection lens"; an active demultiplexer with fibered delays sends up to 6 photons to the even
  inputs of a 12-mode Si3N4 rectangular interferometer with 126 voltage-controlled thermo-optic phase
  shifters and 132 directional couplers; the outputs go to superconducting nanowire detectors. Fig. 1
  caption (p. 2): the software stack "recalibrates hourly and readjusts all hardware control knobs".
- [P] Efficiency and rates (p. 3): the average total efficiency of the optical setup is "~8%";
  coincidence rates are 50 Hz for 5 photons and 4 Hz for 6 photons.
- [P] Source quality (p. 3): single-photon purity "> 99 %", indistinguishability "~94 %" independent
  of the delay between photons up to 1 microsecond, and a measured on-chip two-photon visibility of
  "91 - 94 %" for all 15 pairs; genuine 4-photon and 6-photon indistinguishability 0.85 +- 0.02 and
  0.76 +- 0.02. Supplementary S-III (p. 14) gives the source g(2)(0) = 0.0075 used to correct the
  pairwise matrix.
- [P] Chip control (p. 3): measured coupler reflectivities average (56.7 +- 0.6) % at the 928 nm
  operating wavelength against the ideal 50 %; the paper names fabrication error and wavelength
  dependence as the cause and states that such errors "reduce the range of implementable 12 x 12
  unitary matrices".
- [P] Phase-voltage model (pp. 3-4): the phase shifters follow phi = A V^(.2) + b, with V^(.2) the
  element-wise squared voltage and the off-diagonal elements of the 126 x 126 matrix A representing
  thermal crosstalk; a machine-learning process fits "more than 16000 free parameters", offers a
  "7-fold improvement on the transpilation" over interference-fringe characterization, and the full
  compilation and transpilation reach F = 99.7 +- 0.08 (printed without a unit symbol, p. 4).
- [P] Supplementary S-IV (pp. 15-16): the diagonal elements of A average (0.034 +- 0.001) rad/V^2, so
  a pi shift needs about 10 V; the elements of b are (0.1 +- 1.2) rad; coupler reflectivities are
  (56.8 +- 0.6) %. The paper adds that the long-range structure of A is an artifact of
  transformations that leave the output quantum state unchanged, and that mode 10 transmits 75% of
  mode 6, confirmed on two detection systems and attributed to the chip.
- [P] Methods, transpilation benchmark (p. 11): on 300 random phase configurations the measured
  12-output countrates differ from a simulation using the estimated reflectivities and output losses
  by a total variation distance of (3.0 +- 1.3) % at the 928 nm source wavelength, against
  (21 +- 11) % at 925 nm with a standard interference-fringe characterization; "the relative variation
  on the obtained average TVD between successive benchmarkings is of the order of 3 %, showing
  repeatability of the obtained value".
- [P] Table I (p. 4): average gate fidelities T-gate 99.6 +- 0.1 % (M = 4 configurations), CNOT
  93.8 +- 0.6 % (36), Toffoli 86 +- 1.2 % (340); the values "include errors related to state
  preparation and measurement" and are lower bounds on the gate fidelities.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Ascella is the 12-mode predecessor of Belenos. The model above (a quadratic phase-voltage map
  with a crosstalk matrix, per-coupler reflectivities, per-port losses, an hourly recalibration)
  names the static parameters whose per-job drift the Belenos acceptance and routing controls of
  [bdsc-qualification.md](../../design/bdsc-qualification.md) test and the drift series of
  [bdsc_drift_series.py](../../../scripts/bdsc_drift_series.py) samples.
- [I] The Ascella signed-coherence prediction of
  [ascella-signed-coherence-conformance.md](../../design/ascella-signed-coherence-conformance.md)
  takes a 3 % contrast deficit as the calibrated-chip scale it attributes to this paper; the matching
  statement is the (3.0 +- 1.3) % total variation distance on p. 11 above, and the 91-94 % pairwise
  visibilities on p. 3 are consistent with it. The survey in [literature.md](../quandela/literature.md)
  (source 1) carries the full reading.
- [I] The 56.7 % coupler reflectivity and the A matrix are the reason a compiled unitary differs per
  calibration. The paper reports the repeatability of its benchmark (about 3 % relative variation of the
  average distance between successive benchmarkings, p. 11) and no drift series, so drift magnitudes
  come from the repository's own measurements.
