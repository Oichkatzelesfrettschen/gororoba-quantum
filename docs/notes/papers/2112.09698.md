## Statements read

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): "bad" (non-Hausdorff) quotient spaces are studied through the groupoid C*-algebra of an equivalence relation; for relations that are reflexive and symmetric but not transitive (tolerance relations), the paper observes that such a relation leads to an operator system carrying a natural product that is in general non-associative, relates this product to truncations of (C*-algebras of) topological spaces, and discusses a connection with positive operator valued measures.
- [P] Remark 4 (p. 6): on a finite set a tolerance relation is an equivalence relation if and only if each connected component of its graph is a complete graph. For `X = {1, ..., n}` with the discrete topology, `A(R)` denotes the tolerance algebra of `R`, the algebra `C_c(R)` with the convolution product (8). If `R` is an equivalence relation with connected graph, `A(R)` is `*`-isomorphic to `M_n(C)`, and in general an equivalence relation gives a direct sum of matrix algebras.
- [P] Example 5 (p. 6): for the relation (9) on `{1, 2, 3}` with `1 ~ 2`, `2 ~ 3` and `1 not~ 3`, the tolerance algebra `A_3` consists of the matrices in `M_3(C)` with `a_13 = a_31 = 0` under the matrix product followed by the projection that kills the `(1,3)` and `(3,1)` entries; it is not power associative, because for `a` the matrix with rows `(0,1,0)`, `(1,0,1)`, `(0,1,0)` one has `(a * a) * a = [[0,1,0],[2,0,2],[0,1,0]]` and `a * (a * a) = [[0,2,0],[1,0,1],[0,2,0]]`, which differ.
- [P] Lemma 6 (pp. 6-7): a tolerance relation `R` on `{1, ..., n}` is an equivalence relation if and only if `A(R)` has no subalgebra isomorphic to `A_3`. Proposition 7 (p. 7): for such `R` the following are equivalent: (i) `A(R)` is associative; (ii) `A(R)` is power associative; (iii) `R` is an equivalence relation.
- [P] Statement after Proposition 7 (p. 7): "an algebra that is power associative but not associative cannot be obtained from the above construction. Thus, for example, algebras obtained from `R` by a iterated Cayley-Dickson construction with dimension `>= 8` (octonion, sedenions, etc.) are not tolerance algebras. The same is true for split octonions." Division algebras such as the quaternions also cannot arise: since `E_ii * E_jj = 0` for `i != j`, `A(R)` is a division algebra if and only if `X` is a singleton.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The authors state on p. 7 the corollary of Proposition 7 for the Cayley-Dickson tower: the real doubling algebras of dimension at least 8 are not finite tolerance algebras. It follows from Proposition 7 together with the power associativity of the Cayley-Dickson algebras (Schafer's theorem, not proved in this paper), and it gives a route, independent of the zero-divisor structure, to the fact that the non-associativity of the tower is intrinsic ([octonion structure checks](../topics/octonion_structure_checks.md)).
- [I] The paper's non-associative algebras arise from relations on finite sets and are unrelated to the doubling construction; the two meet only through the corollary above.
