## Definitions (pp. 1-3)

Notation: N is the dimension of the system, {|n>} an orthonormal input basis, E the experimental linear map with rho_out = E(rho_in), and U0 the intended unitary.

- [P] Eq. (1) (p. 1): U0|n> = |f_n>, and the |f_n> are again orthonormal. Eq. (2) (p. 1): the classical fidelity F_{n->f_n} = (1/N) sum_n <f_n|E(|n><n|)|f_n> = (1/N) sum_n p(f_n|n), the average probability of the correct output over the N inputs.
- [P] Eq. (6) (p. 2): the complementary input basis |k'> = N^(-1/2) sum_n exp[-i 2 pi k n / N] |n>, with |<n|k'>|^2 = 1/N. Eqs. (7) and (8) (p. 2): U0|k'> = |g'_k>, with |g'_k> the same Fourier combination of the |f_n>. Eq. (10) (p. 3): the complementary classical fidelity F_{k->g_k} = (1/N) sum_k p(g_k|k).
- [P] The two truth tables are the N input to output maps of Eq. (1) and the N maps of Eq. (7), so the estimate needs 2N measurement probabilities (p. 1 and the summary on p. 5).
- [P] Eq. (4) (p. 2): E(rho) = sum_{q,r} chi_{q,r} U_q rho U_r^dagger over N^2 orthogonal unitary operators, the first N given by Eq. (3), U_q|n> = exp[-i 2 pi q n / N]|f_n>, with U_0 the ideal operation. Orthogonality of operators is Tr{A^dagger B} = 0 (footnote 18, p. 5). The process fidelity is F_process = chi_{0,0}, the probability of obtaining (U0 (x) 1)|phi>_{A,R} when E acts on the A half of a maximally entangled state (p. 2). Footnote 16 (p. 5) identifies it with the entanglement fidelity.
- [P] Eq. (5) (p. 2): F_{n->f_n} = chi_{0,0} + sum_{q=1}^{N-1} chi_{q,q}. Eq. (13) (p. 3) builds N-1 further orthogonal unitaries U_q, N <= q <= 2(N-1), and Eq. (14) (p. 3): F_{k->g_k} = chi_{0,0} + sum_{q=N}^{2(N-1)} chi_{q,q}. The two index sets share only q = 0 (Eqs. (11) and (12), p. 3).
- [P] CNOT example, Eqs. (18) to (20) (p. 4): the tables are the computational (Z) basis and the Hadamard-transformed (X) basis of both qubits, with F_Z and F_X each the mean of four probabilities, eight probabilities in all.

## The bound and its stated conditions (pp. 2-4)

- [P] Eq. (15) (p. 3): F_{n->f_n} + F_{k->g_k} - 1 <= F_process. The text derives it from Eqs. (5) and (14): each classical fidelity is a sum of process fidelities of N orthogonal processes, and adding the two counts only U0 twice, so the excess over one lower-bounds F_process.
- [P] Eq. (16) (p. 4): F_process <= Min{F_{n->f_n}, F_{k->g_k}}. The text justifies it by the sum of N process fidelities being equal to or greater than each individual fidelity, with a general derivation attributed to reference [20]. Eq. (5) with a remark on p. 2 states that each classical fidelity is an upper bound of F_process.
- [P] Eq. (17) (p. 4): with F_{n->f_n} = 1 - epsilon, F_{k->g_k} - epsilon <= F_process <= F_{k->g_k}. Eq. (21) and Eq. (22) (pp. 4-5): C >= 2 F_entangle - 1 and C >= 2 (F_Z + F_X) - 3 for the concurrence of a CNOT.
- [P] Conditions stated by the paper: E is a "linear map" (p. 1); the process matrix chi is "a positive hermitian matrix with a trace of one (or less for conditional operations with a limited probability of success)" (p. 2). The text names neither trace preservation nor complete positivity as a hypothesis of Eqs. (15) and (16), and defines no renormalization. Because rho_out = E(rho_in), the p(f|n) in Eqs. (2) and (10) are the output probabilities of the map as given, so for a conditional operation they include the success probability.
- [P] The paper states the bound for the same map E on every input of both tables (one E appears in Eqs. (2), (4) and (10)), and says nothing about postselection beyond the quoted parenthetical.
- [P] The conversion F_bar = (N F_process + 1)/(N + 1) to the average pure-state fidelity is cited from [7], [13], [14] and [22] (p. 4); the passage does not address postselected maps.

## Relation to the ascella CZ ensemble case

[I] docs/design/ascella-cz-compilation-ensemble-case.md measures a CZ through two tables, ZX (control in Z, target in X) and XZ, and scores each by the sum of the four correct rates over the sum of the four eligible rates. The CZ tables are the paper's CNOT tables of Eq. (20) with a Hadamard on the target basis (CZ = H_t CNOT H_t), and F_process is unchanged by that local relabeling. The paper treats CNOT only and gives no CZ statement.

[I] The paper's Eqs. (15) and (16) do not cover the design's case. The paper never applies them to a conditional operation and never states the bound for the trace-normalized fidelity chi_{0,0}/Tr chi of a postselected map, which is the design's quantity; its only remark on conditional operations is the p. 2 parenthetical on the process matrix. Read with that parenthetical, its derivation also holds for raw probabilities with Tr chi <= 1, where the bound reads F1 + F2 - 1 <= F_process <= min(F1, F2) with F_process = chi_{0,0} unnormalized. The design's extension rests on an argument outside the paper: the sum over the four inputs of one orthonormal table of the success probabilities is N Tr chi for both tables, so each rate-weighted score equals F_i/Tr chi with F_i from Eqs. (5) and (14), the normalized process fidelity is chi_{0,0}/Tr chi, and Eqs. (4), (5), (14) are linear in chi. The matrix chi/Tr chi is positive hermitian with trace one, the paper's trace-one case then yields the normalized bound, and the derivation uses only chi_{q,q} >= 0, the disjoint index sets and the trace, never trace preservation of E. The paper's text does not contain this step.

[I] Two further points lie outside the paper's text. Its derivation of Eqs. (13) and (14) is for the Fourier basis of Eq. (6), while its CNOT application (Eqs. (18) to (20)) uses Z and X bases and asserts the sufficiency of the eight probabilities without a separate derivation. In the Pauli operator basis the two operator groups of the ZX and XZ tables, {Z^a (x) X^b} and {X^a (x) Z^b} applied to U0, share only the identity, which gives the same disjointness argument for the design's tables. The design's numerical check (random trace-non-increasing CP maps, no violation) is the evidence for the normalized form beyond that argument.

[I] The paper assumes one fixed E in Eqs. (2) and (10) and does not address the two hypotheses the design names: one process serving every setting, and an equal chance of a shot per attempt across settings.
