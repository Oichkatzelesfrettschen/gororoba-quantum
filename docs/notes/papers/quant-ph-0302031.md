## Results needed by the project

1. [P] A channel is entanglement breaking when `(I (x) Phi)(Gamma)` is
   separable for every input `Gamma`, entangled inputs included (abstract and
   Definition 1, pp. 1-2).
2. [P] Measure-and-prepare form: `Phi(rho) = sum_k R_k Tr(F_k rho)` with each
   `R_k` a density matrix; Eq. 1 introduces `{F_k}` as a POVM (p. 2), and the
   form with `F_k` only positive semidefinite is the one of Theorem 2 (p. 2)
   and Theorem 4(A) (p. 4). The paper describes it as a sender measuring `rho`, sending the outcome
   `k` through a classical channel, and a receiver preparing `R_k`. Theorem 2
   (p. 2) states that a channel has this form if and only if it is
   entanglement breaking; the channel is trace preserving if and only if the
   `F_k` form a POVM.
3. [P] Theorem 4 (p. 4) lists equivalent conditions, among them (A) the
   Holevo form of item 2, (B) entanglement breaking, (C) separability of
   `(I (x) Phi)(|beta><beta|)` for the maximally entangled state
   `|beta> = d^(-1/2) sum_j |j>|j>`, and (D) a Kraus decomposition by
   rank-one operators. The proof (pp. 5-6) takes the Choi correspondence
   `Phi <-> (I (x) Phi)(|beta><beta|)` of maps and states.
4. [P] The step (A) implies (B) (p. 5) shows
   `(I (x) Phi)(Gamma) = sum_k gamma_k R_k (x) Q_k` for arbitrary `Gamma`
   with `gamma_k = Tr(E_k Gamma)`: a convex mixture of product states. The proof
   writes the POVM elements `E_k` where Eq. 1 writes `F_k`, prints `Q_k` with
   `1/gamma_l`, and places `R_k` before `Q_k` although `R_k` is the output of
   `Phi` and `Q_k` the reference state.

## Section-by-section reading

1. Sec. 1 (pp. 2-4). [P] Eq. 1 is the Holevo form; Definition 1 and Theorem 2
   state that a map is entanglement breaking exactly when it has the form
   of Eq. 1 with positive semidefinite `F_k`, which is Eq. 2, with
   `sum_k |phi_k><phi_k| = I` for trace preservation. Theorem 3: the
   trace-preserving entanglement-breaking maps form a convex set. Examples:
   conjugation by a unitary is not EBT, a qubit map is EBT when the image of
   the Bloch sphere collapses to a plane or a line, and a channel with fewer
   than `d` Kraus operators is not EBT. Eq. 3 gives the Kraus operators
   `A_kmn = sqrt(R_k)|m><n| sqrt(F_k)`. Assumption: finite dimension `d`.
2. Sec. 2 (pp. 4-6), Theorem 4. [P] The six equivalent conditions A to F, with the
   Choi map-state correspondence as the tool. Eqs. 4 to 6 construct, from a
   separable Choi state `sum_n p_n |v_n><v_n| (x) |w_n><w_n|`, the map
   `Omega(rho) = d sum_n |w_n><w_n| Tr(rho p_n |v_n><v_n|)` and show
   `Omega = Phi`; trace preservation makes `{d p_n |v_n><v_n|}` a POVM.
   Assumption: separability is tested on the maximally entangled input only
   for (C), and the equivalence turns this single test into a test for every
   input. Remark (p. 6): there are channels that preserve PPT entanglement
   (references 5 and 9), so a PPT test is weaker than the conditions E and F
   in general.
3. Sec. 3 (pp. 6-12), Theorems 5 and 6, Lemmas 7 and 8, Eqs. 7 to 22. [P] The
   extreme points of the EBT set are the extreme CQ maps for `d = 2` and
   include non-CQ maps for `d >= 3` (the trine example of pp. 8-9 and the
   tetrahedron map of Eq. 19); a CPT map with fewer than `d` Kraus
   operators is not EBT (Theorem 6); for a CPT map whose Choi state has rank
   `d`, EBT holds exactly when `T o Phi` is completely positive (Lemma 7, from
   a result for rank-`d` states); a separable rank-`d` state with rank-`d`
   marginal needs at most `d` product terms (Lemma 8). Assumption: the
   statements concern trace-preserving maps unless a result says completely
   positive.
4. Sec. 4 (pp. 12-13), Eq. 23. [P] A basis `G_0 = d^(-1/2) I, G_1, ...` gives
   `rho = sum_j w_j G_j` and a `d^2 x d^2` matrix `T` for a map; a Holevo
   channel has `T = W^T U`, and trace preservation is the condition that the
   first row of `T` equals `(1, 0, ..., 0)`. For CQ or QC channels
   `rank T <= d`. For a basis in which positivity implies
   `sum_{i>=1} |x_i|^2 <= x_0^2`, EBT implies `sum_{j>=1} |t_jj| <= 1`
   (details in reference 18).

## Repository use

Each item below is [I] and names the design element it supports.

[I] Claim level C4, gate G6 and `reference_preservation`. A channel
that is entanglement breaking leaves the reference separable from the output,
for a reference of any dimension and any input state. The proof of (A) implies
(B) on p. 5 traces only the input factor with `T_2` and uses neither the
dimension of the reference nor `sum_k F_k = I`; it applies to a reference of
any dimension and to a trace-nonincreasing accepted branch (a completely
positive map), because only the POVM clause of the trace-preserving version
uses trace preservation. The check on the maximally entangled input suffices:
(C) implies (A) implies (B) turns separability of the single `d x d` Choi
state into separability for every reference. A reference-output witness
that exceeds its separable bound therefore excludes every measure-and-prepare
accepted branch. The 'accepted-branch' reading is a deduction from the proof
and not a statement of the paper, which defines entanglement breaking for
trace-preserving maps.

[I] Gate G5 and the benchmark of `success_weighted_fidelity`. Theorem 2 makes
the class of fixed single-copy measure-and-prepare instruments the same as the
class of entanglement-breaking maps, so the design's bound over the latter
is a bound over the former. The derivation of `F_succ <= 2/3` through the
Choi state is in [quant-ph-9807091](quant-ph-9807091.md).

[I] The design's Choi-PPT benchmark (`J >= 0`, `J^{T_in} >= 0`,
`tr_out J <= I/2`). `tr_out J <= I/2` is the trace-nonincreasing condition of
an accepted instrument. For a qubit-to-qubit accepted map `J` is a 2x2 state,
for which PPT is exact for separability (quant-ph/9605038), so by (C) the
PPT test decides entanglement breaking. The remark of p. 6 and Lemma 7 show
the limit: channels exist that keep PPT entanglement and break other
entanglement, and the rank-`d` condition of Lemma 7 is a rank restriction.
For a larger accepted sector (higher dimension or multiphoton output) PPT
over-admits, so the benchmark of that case needs a separability test beyond
PPT.

[I] Depolarizing links (the two-hop composition of the design). For the
qubit Pauli basis `G_j = sigma_j/sqrt 2`, a depolarizing channel of weight
`p` has `t_jj = p`, and the necessary condition `sum_j |t_jj| <= 1` of p. 13
says the channel can be EBT only if `p <= 1/3`. The Choi state of that channel
is a noisy singlet of weight `p`, separable exactly for `p <= 1/3` (the separability range on p. 5 of
[quant-ph-9807091](quant-ph-9807091.md) at `d = 2`), so by (C) the condition
is also sufficient for this channel. The average fidelity `f = (1+p)/2`
(Eq. 14 of that paper at `d = 2`) equals `2/3`, the classical bound of the
design, at `p = 1/3`.

[I] Theorem 6 and the choice of an ideal model. A qubit map with a single
Kraus operator (an isometry on the logical subspace, as an ideal linear-optical
transfer is) is not EBT. The statement is for the logical qubit and says
nothing about the physical photon modes.

[I] Optimization over accepted operations. The set of EBT maps is convex
(Theorem 3), and for `d = 2` its extreme points are the extreme CQ maps
(Theorem 5(D), proved in the companion preprint). The grids of
`measure_and_prepare_grid` hold rank-one measurements with pure prepared
states; whether they exhaust the extreme trace-nonincreasing instruments is
not established by this paper.

[I] Qubit-channel relation to teleportation: a teleportation protocol whose
effective channel is entanglement breaking cannot deliver a state that stays
entangled with a reference. Pair this note with the average-fidelity bound of
[quant-ph-9807091](quant-ph-9807091.md).
