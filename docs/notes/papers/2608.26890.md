## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Vertices of the commutativity graph `Gamma_C(A)` are the lines
  `Fa + C_A` in `A/C_A`, with `[a]` and `[b]` adjacent exactly when
  `ab = ba` (Definition 2.3). For `n>=2` the center of `A_n` is `F`
  (Lemma 4.1).
- [P] Lemma 4.2 (quoted from the author's earlier work): for `t(a)=0`,
  `a!=0` and `n(a)!=0`, the centralizer is `C(a) = F + Fa + O(a)`, where
  `O(a)` is the orthogonalizer `{b : ab = ba = 0}`. In an algebra with
  anisotropic norm, such as the real main-sequence algebras, every
  nonzero trace-zero element qualifies (Remark 4.3).
- [P] Corollary 4.4: an element of the real sedenions `S = M_4` whose
  imaginary part is not a zero divisor is an isolated vertex of
  `Gamma_C(S)`.
- [P] Proposition 4.6 (after Biss, Dugger and Isaksen): `(a,b)` with `a,b`
  octonions is a zero divisor of `S` exactly when `n(a)=n(b)` and
  `1, a, b` are orthogonal. Every sedenion zero divisor has zero trace
  (Lemma 3.2(1)).
- [P] Theorem 4.13: the subgraph `Gamma_C^Z(S)` on the lines `Ra + R` with
  `a` a zero divisor is one connected component of `Gamma_C(S)` and has
  diameter 3. Proposition 4.7 gives the lower bound 3 from the pair
  `(a,b)`, `(b,a)`, whose centralizers meet only in `R`; an earlier result
  of the author's relation-graphs paper gave connectedness and diameter at
  most four. The proof of the upper bound constructs a path of length at
  most three by solving four linear equations in five unknowns.
- [P] Section 3 restates, for algebras with anisotropic norm, the
  double-hexagon subgraph of the orthogonality graph (Theorem 3.4) and the
  divisibility-by-four result for annihilator dimension (Theorem 6.11 of the
  author's doubly-alternative paper, cited rather than proved here).

## Repository bearing

[I] The paper concerns the 16-dimensional sedenions only. The same proof
structure rests on Proposition 4.6 and on the Biss-Dugger-Isaksen
orthogonalizer of dimension 4, both specific to `M_4`.
For the repository's Cayley-Dickson work the result gives a commutativity
counterpart to the orthogonality components of
[Guterman and Zhilina](2608-26903.md): the commuting relation on sedenion
zero divisors joins any two zero-divisor lines by a path of at most three
commuting edges, and the centralizer of a zero divisor is `R + Ra` plus its
four-dimensional orthogonalizer. Any use in a circuit candidate generator needs an explicit map
from these projective vertices to the repository's basis-pair census.

[I] Zero divisors of dimension 32 or higher: none. The paper states results
for `M_4` and cites the divisibility theorem for general anisotropic
algebras without extending the graph analysis. Moreno's Stiefel question
(`math/0512517` Section 5): none; `math/0512517` appears only as reference
[12].
