## Hilbert functions over homogeneous rings (Section 4.1, pp. 147-155)

- [P] Standing assumptions (p. 147): the section works with a graded ring `R` whose degree-zero part `R_0` is an Artinian local ring and which is finitely generated over `R_0`, so that every finite graded `R`-module `M` has components `M_n` of finite length. After Definition 4.1.1 the section assumes `R = R_0[R_1]` (generated over `R_0` by elements of degree 1; such a ring is called homogeneous). Definition 4.1.1 (p. 147): for a graded `R`-module `M` with components of finite length, the Hilbert function is `H(M, n) = length(M_n)` and the Hilbert series is `H_M(t) = sum over n of H(M, n) t^n`.
- [P] Theorem 4.1.3 (Hilbert, p. 148), under those assumptions: for a finite graded `R`-module `M` of dimension `d`, `H(M, n)` is of polynomial type of degree `d - 1`, that is, equal to a polynomial in `n` of degree `d - 1` for `n >> 0` (the zero polynomial has degree `-1` by the convention of p. 148). The proof (pp. 148-149) inducts on `d` through a filtration of `M` by graded submodules with quotients `(R/p_i)(a_i)`, `p_i` graded primes, and uses a homogeneous element of degree 1 in the induction step.
- [P] Definition 4.1.5 (p. 149) and the paragraph after it (p. 150): for a finite graded module `M` of dimension `d`, the Hilbert polynomial `P_M` is written `sum for i = 0..d-1 of (-1)^(d-1-i) e_(d-1-i) binom(X + i, i)`, and the multiplicity is `e(M) = e_0` if `d > 0` and `e(M) = length(M)` if `d = 0`.
- [P] Corollary 4.1.8 (p. 150), with Lemma 4.1.7 (p. 150) behind it: for a finite graded `R`-module `M` that is not zero and has dimension `d`, there is a unique `Q_M(t)` in `Z[t, t^-1]` with `Q_M(1) != 0` such that `H_M(t) = Q_M(t) / (1 - t)^d`; if `Q_M(t) = sum of h_i t^i`, then `min{i : h_i != 0}` is the least `i` with `M_i != 0`.
- [P] Proposition 4.1.9 (p. 151), under the assumptions of 4.1.8: `e_i = Q_M^(i)(1) / i!` for `i = 0, ..., d - 1` (`Q_M^(i)` the `i`-th formal derivative), and `e(M) = Q_M(1)`.
- [P] Corollary 4.1.10 (pp. 151-152), under the assumptions of 4.1.8 and with `M` in addition Cohen-Macaulay, `Q_M(t) = sum of h_i t^i`: `h_i >= 0` for all `i`; moreover `e_i >= 0` for all `i` if `M_j = 0` for `j < 0`. The proof (p. 151) reduces to an infinite residue field of `R_0` by the base change to `R_0(Y)` with `Y` an indeterminate, then inducts on `d` by an `M`-regular element `a` in `R_1`, which gives `(1 - t) H_M(t) = H_(M/aM)(t)` and `Q_M = Q_(M/aM)`.
- [P] Remark 4.1.11 (p. 152): for a finite graded `R`-module `M` and an `M`-sequence `x` of elements of degree 1, `Q_M(t) = Q_(M/(x)M)(t)`. Proposition 4.1.12 (p. 152): for a finite graded `M` that is not zero, of dimension `d`, with `Q_M(t) = sum for i = a..b of h_i t^i` and `h_b != 0`, one has `H(M, b - d) != P_M(b - d)` and `H(M, i) = P_M(i)` for all `i >= b - d + 1`.
- [P] Lemma 4.1.13 (p. 153): for a finite graded `R`-module `M` of finite projective dimension with graded free resolution having graded Betti numbers `beta_ij`, `H_M(t) = S_M(t) H_R(t)` with `S_M(t) = sum over i, j of (-1)^i beta_ij t^j`; for `R = k[X_1, ..., X_n]` over a field `k` this reads `H_M(t) = S_M(t) / (1 - t)^n`. Corollary 4.1.14 (p. 153), for `R = k[X_1, ..., X_n]` and `M` finite graded of dimension `d`: `S_M(t) = (1 - t)^(n-d) Q_M(t)`.
- [P] Theorem 4.1.15 (p. 153), for `R = k[X_1, ..., X_n]` over a field `k` and a graded ideal `I` such that `R/I` is Cohen-Macaulay with a pure resolution of type `(d_1, ..., d_p)` (that is, the minimal resolution has the form `0 -> R(-d_p)^(beta_p) -> ... -> R(-d_1)^(beta_1) -> R -> R/I -> 0`, with `d_1 < ... < d_p`): (a) `beta_i = (-1)^(i+1) * product over j != i of d_j / (d_j - d_i)`; (b) `e(R/I) = (1/p!) * product of d_i`. The proof (pp. 154-155) takes `p = dim R - dim R/I` from the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula 1.3.3 and solves a Vandermonde system.

## Macaulay's theorem on Hilbert functions (Section 4.2, pp. 156-166)

- [P] Setting (p. 156): `R = direct sum of R_n` is a homogeneous `k`-algebra with `R_0 = k` a field. Definition 4.2.1 (p. 156): a non-empty set `M` of monomials in `X_1, ..., X_m` is an order ideal of monomials if every monomial dividing a member of `M` lies in `M`. Theorem 4.2.3 (Macaulay, p. 156): for a homogeneous `k`-algebra `R` over a field `k`, a `k`-basis `x_1, ..., x_m` of `R_1` and the `k`-algebra map `pi : k[X_1, ..., X_m] -> R` with `pi(X_i) = x_i`, there is an order ideal of monomials `M` such that `pi(M)` is a `k`-basis of `R`. Corollary 4.2.4 (p. 157): with `J` the ideal generated by the monomials outside `M`, `R` and `k[X_1, ..., X_m]/J` have the same Hilbert function, so every Hilbert function of a homogeneous ring arises from a ring whose defining ideal is generated by monomials.
- [P] Lemma 4.2.6 (p. 159): for a positive integer `d`, every `a` in `N` has a unique expression `a = binom(k(d), d) + binom(k(d-1), d-1) + ... + binom(k(1), 1)` with `k(d) > k(d-1) > ... > k(1) >= 0`; the book calls it the `d`-th Macaulay representation of `a` (p. 160). Definitions (pp. 161-162): after dropping zero summands, `a = sum for i = j..d of binom(k(i), i)` with `k(d) > ... > k(j) >= j >= 1`, `a^(d)` is `sum of binom(k(i) + 1, i + 1)` over the same `i`, `0^(d) = 0`, and `a_(d)` is `sum of binom(k(i) - 1, i)` over `i = j..d` with `j = min{i : k(i) >= i}`.
- [P] Corollary 4.2.9 (p. 161), for `S = k[X_1, ..., X_m]` over a field and `I` a lexsegment ideal (an ideal spanned in each degree by a lexsegment of monomials, p. 161), `R = S/I`: `H(R, n + 1) <= H(R, n)^(n)` for all `n`, with equality for a given `n` if and only if `I_(n+1) = (X_1, ..., X_m) I_n`.
- [P] Theorem 4.2.10 (Macaulay, p. 162): for a field `k` and a numerical function `h : N -> N`, these conditions are equivalent: (a) there is a homogeneous `k`-algebra `R` with `H(R, n) = h(n)` for all `n >= 0`; (b) the same with a homogeneous `k`-algebra having monomial relations; (c) `h(0) = 1` and `h(n + 1) <= h(n)^(n)` for all `n >= 1`; (d) with `m = h(1)` and `M_n` the first `h(n)` monomials of degree `n` in `X_1, ..., X_m` in the reverse degree-lexicographic order, `M = union of the M_n` is an order ideal of monomials. The text after the statement (p. 162) shows that `1 + 3t + 5t^2 + 8t^3` is not a Hilbert series of a homogeneous ring: `5 = binom(3, 2) + binom(2, 1)` and `5^(2) = binom(4, 3) + binom(3, 2) = 7 < 8`. The proof of (a) implies (c) (pp. 165-166) extends the field to an infinite one and uses Green's theorem and Lemma 4.2.13.
- [P] Theorem 4.2.12 (Green, p. 163): for a homogeneous `k`-algebra `R` over an infinite field `k` and an integer `n >= 1`, `H(R/hR, n) <= H(R, n)_(n)` for a general linear form `h`; "general" means that the property holds for all `h` in a non-empty Zariski-open subset of `R_1` (p. 163). Lemma 4.2.13 (p. 165), for positive integers `a`, `a'`, `d`: (a) `a <= a'` implies `a^(d) <= a'^(d)`; (b) with `k(d), ..., k(1)` the `d`-th Macaulay coefficients of `a` and `j = min{i : k(i) >= i}`, `(a + 1)^(d)` equals `a^(d) + k(1) + 1` if `j = 1` and `a^(d) + 1` if `j > 1`.
- [P] Corollary 4.2.14 (p. 166): for a homogeneous `k`-algebra `R` over a field `k`, `H(R, n + 1) = H(R, n)^(n)` for `n >> 0`. Proposition 4.2.15 (p. 166, statement only; its proof on p. 167 was not opened): for a field `k` and a finite sequence `h_0, ..., h_s` of positive integers (as printed), these are equivalent: (a) there are an integer `d` and a Cohen-Macaulay (reduced) homogeneous `k`-algebra `R` of dimension `d`, whose defining ideal is generated by squarefree monomials, with `H_R(t) = (sum of h_i t^i) / (1 - t)^d`; (b) `h_0 = 1` and `0 <= h_(i+1) <= h_i^(i)` for `i = 1, ..., s - 1`. The page introduces it as the combination of 1.5.12 with Macaulay's theorem.

## Filtered rings, Rees rings and associated graded rings (Section 4.5, pp. 182-187)

- [P] Definition 4.5.1 (p. 182): a filtration `F` on a ring `R` is a descending chain `R = I_0 contains I_1 contains I_2 ...` of ideals with `I_i I_j` contained in `I_(i+j)`; the extended Rees ring is `R(F) = direct sum over i in Z of I_i t^i` (with `I_i = R` for `i <= 0`) inside `R[t, t^-1]`, and the associated graded ring is `gr_F(R) = direct sum over i >= 0 of I_i / I_(i+1)`. For a module `M`, `R(F, M) = direct sum of I_i M t^i` and `gr_F(M) = direct sum of I_i M / I_(i+1) M`; for the `I`-adic filtration the notations are `R(I)`, `gr_I(R)`, `R(I, M)`, `gr_I(M)`. The page continues: "We will also encounter the Rees ring `R_+(F) = direct sum for i >= 0 of I_i t^i`" and the modules `R_+(F, M) = direct sum for i >= 0 of I_i M t^i`; the notations for `I`-adic filtrations "are to be modified accordingly".
- [P] Lemma 4.5.2 (p. 183): for a filtered ring, `t^-1` lies in `R(F)` and is `R(F)`-regular, `R(F)/t^-1 R(F)` is isomorphic to `gr_F(R)`, and `R(F)` localized at `t^-1` is `R[t, t^-1]`. The filtration is called Noetherian if `R(F)` is Noetherian (p. 183).
- [P] Theorem 4.5.6 (p. 185): for a filtered ring `R` with Noetherian filtration `F = (I_i)_(i >= 0)` and a finite `R`-module `M`, `R(F, M)` is a finite `R(F)`-module, (a) `dim R(F, M) = dim M + 1`, and (b) `dim gr_F(M) = sup{dim M_m : m a maximal ideal in Supp(M/I_1 M)}`; in particular `dim gr_F(M) <= dim M`, with equality if `I_1` lies in all maximal ideals of `R`.

## The Hilbert-Samuel function and reduction ideals (Section 4.6, pp. 188-191)

- [P] Setting and Definition 4.6.1 (p. 188): `(R, m)` is a Noetherian local ring, `M != 0` a finite `R`-module, and an ideal of definition of `M` is an ideal `I` contained in `m` with `m^n M` contained in `I M` for some `n`. The Hilbert-Samuel function is `chi^I_M(n) = H_1(gr_I(M), n) = sum for i = 0..n of length(I^i M / I^(i+1) M) = length(M / I^(n+1) M)`, and `e(I, M) = e(gr_I(M))` is the multiplicity of `M` with respect to `I`. Proposition 4.6.2 (p. 188), for `(R, m)` Noetherian local, `M != 0` finite of dimension `d`, and `I` an ideal of definition of `M`: (a) `chi^I_M` is of polynomial type of degree `d`; (b) `e(I, M) = lim as n -> infinity of (d! / n^d) length(M / I^(n+1) M)`.
- [P] Definition 4.6.4 (p. 189): for a Noetherian ring `R`, a proper ideal `I` and a finite `R`-module `M`, an ideal `J` contained in `I` is a reduction ideal of `I` with respect to `M` if `J I^n M = I^(n+1) M` for some (equivalently, as the page states, all) `n >> 0`. Lemma 4.6.5 (p. 189), for `(R, m)` Noetherian local, `M` finite, `I` an ideal of definition of `M` and `J` a reduction ideal of `I` with respect to `M`: `J` is an ideal of definition of `M` and `e(J, M) = e(I, M)`. Proposition 4.6.6 (p. 189), for a Noetherian ring `R`, proper ideals `J` contained in `I` and a finite module `M`: `J` is a reduction ideal of `I` with respect to `M` if and only if `R_+(I, M)` is a finite `R_+(J)`-module.
- [P] Definition 4.6.7 (p. 190): for `(R, m)` a Noetherian local ring, `I` a proper ideal and `M` a finite `R`-module, the analytic spread of `I` with respect to `M` is `lambda(I, M) = dim( R_+(I, M) / m R_+(I, M) ) = dim( gr_I(M) / m gr_I(M) )` (both equalities as printed on the page), and `lambda(I) = lambda(I, R)` is the analytic spread of `I`.
- [P] Proposition 4.6.8 (p. 190), under the hypotheses of Definition 4.6.7: `mu(J) >= lambda(I, M)` for every reduction ideal `J` of `I` with respect to `M`, where `mu(J)` is the number of generators of `J` in a minimal generating set (the proof reads `mu(J) = dim_k J / m J`). If in addition `R/m` is infinite, there is a reduction ideal `J` of `I` with respect to `M` such that `mu(J) = lambda(I, M)`. The two statements carry different hypotheses: the inequality holds without any condition on the residue field, and the existence of a reduction with equality uses the infinite residue field, through Noether normalization.
- [P] Remark 4.6.9 (p. 190), stated without proof: Northcott and Rees call `J` a minimal reduction of `I` if `J` is a reduction ideal of `I` and has no proper reduction, and they prove that minimal reductions exist (reference [291] of the book, a fact the book says it does not use explicitly). When the residue field `k` is infinite, a reduction `J` of `I` minimally generated by `x_1, ..., x_n` is a minimal reduction if and only if `x_1, ..., x_n` are analytically independent in `I` and `n = lambda(I)`; analytic independence is defined on the page by the condition that a homogeneous polynomial `f(X_1, ..., X_n)` over `R` of degree `m` with `f(x) in I^m m` has all coefficients in `m`.
- [P] Corollary 4.6.10 (p. 191), for a Noetherian local ring `(R, m)` with infinite residue class field, a finite module `M` and an ideal of definition `I` of `M`: there is a system of parameters `x` of `M` such that `(x)` is a reduction ideal of `I` with respect to `M`; in particular `e(I, M) = e((x), M)`.
- [P] Exercise 4.6.13 (p. 191, stated without proof), for `(R, m)` a Noetherian local ring and `I` a proper ideal: (a) `lambda(I R_p) <= lambda(I)` for all `p` in `Spec R`; (b) if `I` is `m`-primary then `lambda(I) = dim R`; (c) `height I <= lambda(I) <= dim R`.
- [P] Opening of Section 4.7 (pp. 192-193): Definition 4.7.3 defines the multiplicity symbol `e(x, M)` of a multiplicity system `x` of a finite module `M` over a Noetherian local ring inductively, and Theorem 4.7.4 (Auslander-Buchsbaum, p. 193) states `e(x, M) = chi(x, M)`, the Euler characteristic of the Koszul homology of `x` on `M`, for a Noetherian local ring `(R, m)`, a finite `R`-module `M` and a multiplicity system `x` of `M`. The proof was not opened.
