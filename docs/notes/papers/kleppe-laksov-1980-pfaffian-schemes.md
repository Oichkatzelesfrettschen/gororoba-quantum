## Aims and notation (Section 1, pp. 167-168, and Section 2, pp. 168-169)

- [P] Introduction (pp. 167-168): the starting point is the observation of Buchsbaum and Eisenbud that every codimension three Gorenstein subscheme of an affine space is locally the zero set of pfaffians of an alternating matrix. The main part (Sections 2, 3, 4 and 6) proves that pfaffian schemes are Gorenstein by an argument in which, in the paper's words, "considerations of characteristic never enter";
  Sections 2, 3 and 4 prove that they are integral and Cohen-Macaulay, following Eagon and Hochster's proof for determinantal schemes; Section 5 determines the stratification of the singularities of generic pfaffians and builds deformations with a generic stratification of the singularities;
  Section 7 records Avramov's observation that pfaffian schemes are factorial. The paper states that in characteristic zero Jozefiak and Pragacz had determined all the syzygies of the pfaffian ideals (p. 167). It names as its most spectacular consequence that every codimension three pfaffian subscheme of `A^n` with `n <= 9` can be smoothed, and it gives an example of a codimension three Gorenstein (in fact regular) subscheme of `A^7` that is not (globally) pfaffian (p. 168).
- [P] Notation (p. 168): `R` is a Noetherian commutative ring with unity, `S` an `R`-algebra, and `T = (t(i,j))` an `a x a` antisymmetric matrix with entries in `S` (`t(i,j) = -t(j,i)`, `t(i,i) = 0`). `R[T]` is the `R`-subalgebra of `S` generated by the entries, `T(i_1, ..., i_p)` the `p x p` antisymmetric submatrix on the rows and columns `i_1 < ... < i_p`,
  `Pf_(2p)(T)` the ideal generated by the `2p`-pfaffians of all `2p x 2p` submatrices of `T`. For integers `0 = a_0 < a_1 < ... < a_m < a_(m+1) = a` with `a_i >= 2i - 1` for all `i` and `a >= 2m + 2`, `Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; c)` is the ideal of `R[T]` generated by the ideals `Pf_(2i)(T(1, 2, ..., a_i))` for `i = 1, ..., m + 1` together with the elements `t(1,2), ..., t(1,c)`; the values `c = 1, ..., a_1` give the same ideal.
  Example 1 (pp. 168-169) lists these ideals for `a = 3` and `a = 4`.
- [P] Standing hypothesis (head of Section 3, p. 171): from Section 3 on, the entries `t(i,j)` with `1 <= i < j <= a` are algebraically independent over `R`. Section 5 (p. 178) works over an algebraically closed field `k`.

## Primes, height and perfection of the ideals Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; c) (Sections 2 to 4, pp. 169-178)

- [P] Remark 2 (p. 169; the decomposition is Proposition 9(2), p. 1048, of Eagon and Hochster): for integers `c < d` with `a_n < c < d <= a_(n+1)`, let `P = Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; c)`, let `P_1` be `P` with the entries `t(1,c+1), ..., t(1,d)` adjoined, and let `P_2` be `P` with the `2n`-pfaffian ideal of `T(1, ..., c)` adjoined (the proof, p. 169). For the radicals `Q`, `Q_1`, `Q_2` of `P`, `P_1`, `P_2`: (i) `Q = Q_1 intersect Q_2`; (ii) `t(1,j) P_2` lies in `P` for `j = c + 1, ..., d`.
- [P] Proposition 8 (p. 172), under the standing hypothesis of Section 3: for an isolated prime `Q` of `P = Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; a_n)` the number
  `h = C(a - 2m, 2) + sum_(i=1..n-1) max(a_i - 2i + 1, 1) + sum_(i=n..m) (a_i - 2i + 1) + (n - 1)`
  is the depth of `Q` as printed; the proof (p. 173) calls the same number the height of `Q`. The proof is an induction on `a` that uses Lemma 6 (p. 171; elementary facts on isolated primes of `I S + (t_1, ..., t_m)` in a localized polynomial ring), Lemma 7 (p. 172) and Remarks 4 and 5 (p. 170).
- [P] Proposition 10 (pp. 174-175; Lemma 9, p. 173, and the row-index sequences constructed on p. 174 feed it): for `P = Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; a_n)` and a suitable product `t` of pfaffians of submatrices of `T`, the principal open subset `X_t` of `X = Spec R[T]/P` is dense, and when `R` is an integral domain its coordinate ring is the localization `S_t` of the polynomial ring `S` over `R` in the coordinates of `T` outside the sets `A_0, ..., A_(m+1)` of that proposition,
  which are `C(a, 2) - h` in number. Corollary 11 (p. 176): if `R` is an integral domain, the radical of `Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; a_n)` is a prime ideal of `R[T]`.
- [P] Theorem 12 (p. 176): `R` is an integral domain. For `c = a_n` or `c = a_n + 1` (some `n`), `P = Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; c)` is a perfect ideal; for `c = a_n` or `c = a_n + 1 > 2n` its height is
  `h(c) = C(a - 2m, 2) + sum_(i=1..n-1) max(a_i - 2i + 1, 1) + (c - 2n + 1) + sum_(i=n+1..m) (a_i - 2i + 1) + (n - 1)`;
  and for `c = a_n` or `c = a_n + 1 = 2n`, `P` is a prime ideal. The proof (pp. 176-177) repeats the Eagon-Hochster argument: Corollary 11 and Remark 2 yield the radical property (Proposition 13), which with the height formula of Proposition 8 gives the prime and height assertions, and Lemma 14 reduces the perfection assertion to the Cohen-Macaulay statement of Theorem 15 over a field.
- [P] Proposition 13 (p. 177): if `R` is an integral domain, the ideals `Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; c)` are radical (every admissible `c`). Lemma 14 (p. 177): the `Z`-module `Z[T]/P` is faithfully flat; with it Theorem 12 and Theorem 15 reduce to the case where `R` is a field.
- [P] Theorem 15 (p. 177; proof p. 178): `R` is a Cohen-Macaulay ring. For `c = a_n` or `c = a_n + 1`, the ring `R[T]/Pf(T; a_1, ..., a_m; c)` is Cohen-Macaulay; for `c = a_n` or `c = a_n + 1 > 2n` it has codimension `h(c)` in `R[T]`; and if `R` is an integral domain and `c = a_n` or `c = a_n + 1 = 2n`, the ring is integral.
- [P] Corollary 16 (p. 178): for a Noetherian ring `S` and an alternating `a x a` matrix `A` over `S`, the ideal `P = Pf(A; a_1, ..., a_m; a_n) S` has grade related to the number `h` of Proposition 8 (the scan reads "at least equal to"), and if the grade equals that number then `P` is perfect (a consequence of Lemma 14 and Theorem 12 by standard results, p. 178).
  [I] The inequality direction as printed fails for the zero matrix over a field (`P = 0` has grade 0, below `h`), and [Jozefiak and Pragacz 1979](jozefiak-pragacz-1979-ideals-generated-pfaffians.md) Theorem 2.1 bounds heights above; only the equality case (perfection) is recorded here as the usable statement.

## Generic pfaffian schemes and deformation (Section 5, pp. 178-185)

- [P] Definition (pp. 178-179): over an algebraically closed field `k`, the generic pfaffian scheme `P_(2c)(a)` is `Spec k[T]/Pf(T; 1, 3, ..., 2c - 3; 1)`, defined in the polynomial ring in `a(a-1)/2` variables by the ideal `Pf_(2c)(a)` of the `2c`-pfaffians of all alternating `2c x 2c` submatrices.
- [P] Theorem 17 (p. 179), over an algebraically closed field `k` in the notation just given: (i) `P_(2c)(a)` is an integral Gorenstein subscheme of `A^(a(a-1)/2)` of codimension `(a - 2c + 2)(a - 2c + 1)/2`; (ii) `P_(2c-2)(a)` is the singular locus of `P_(2c)(a)`. The proof (p. 179) takes integrality, Cohen-Macaulayness and the codimension from Theorem 15, and the Gorenstein property from Stanley's criterion
  (reference [19], Theorem 4.4, p. 71, as the paper applies it: the graded ring `k[T]/Pf_(2c)(a)`, Cohen-Macaulay and integral by Theorem 15, is Gorenstein if and only if its Hilbert series satisfies `F(t) = (-1)^d t^r F(1/t)` for some integer `r`, with `d = (c-1)(2a-2c+1)` as printed), whose verification is Section 6. Part (ii): the Jacobian matrix has the `(2c-2)`-subpfaffians as entries, so `P_(2c-2)(a)` lies in the singular locus; at a point outside it, a nonvanishing `(2c-2)`-subpfaffian puts the point in a principal open set that Proposition 10 shows is smooth.
  The theorem prints no range for `c` beyond the notation of the definition.
- [P] Definition 18 (p. 180): for an alternating `a x a` matrix `A` over a ring `S`, `P_(2c)(A) = Spec S/Pf_(2c)(A)`, the inverse image of `P_(2c)(a)` under the map `Spec S -> Spec k[T]` sending `t(i,j)` to `s(i,j)`; it is called a pfaffian subscheme of `Spec S` when it has pure codimension `(a - 2c + 2)(a - 2c + 1)/2`. By Theorem 17 and the standard results used for Corollary 16 such a scheme is Gorenstein (p. 180).
  The paper adds that property (ii) of Theorem 17 can fail for `P_(2c)(A)` in simple cases (p. 180).
- [P] Deformation statement (pp. 180-181; the paper derives it from Theorem 2 of Laksov, reference [15], and the proposition of Section 4): let `D_0` (the empty set) lie in `D_1`, `D_1` in `D_2`, and so on up to `D_c = D`, all irreducible subspaces of `A^q`, with `D_(i-1)` the singular locus of `D_i` and `D` Cohen-Macaulay, and let `f: A^p -> A^q` satisfy `codim(f^(-1)(D), A^p) = codim(D, A^q)`. Then there are a morphism `F: A^((p+1)q) x A^p -> A^q` and an open set `G` of `A^((p+1)q)` containing the origin `e` such that, for each rational point `g` of `G`, with `F_g` the restriction of `F` to `{g} x A^p`:
  (i) `F_e = f`; (ii) each `F_g^(-1)(D_i)` has the codimension of `D_i` (and is empty when that codimension exceeds `p`); (iii) `F_g^(-1)(D_(i-1))` is the singular locus of `F_g^(-1)(D_i)` for `i = 1, ..., c`; (iv) the projection `F^(-1)(D) -> A^((p+1)q)` is flat over `G`.
- [P] Theorem 18 (p. 181), over an algebraically closed field `k`: let `A` be an alternating `a x a` matrix with entries in `k[X_1, ..., X_p]` such that `P_(2c)(A)` is a pfaffian subscheme of `A^p`. Then there is a faithfully flat morphism `q: V -> W` from an algebraic variety to an open subset of an affine space, with `q^(-1)(e) = P_(2c)(A)` for a rational point `e`, such that for every rational point `g` of `W`: (i) `q^(-1)(g)` is isomorphic to `P_(2c)(A(g))` for an alternating `a x a` matrix `A(g)` with entries in `k[X_1, ..., X_p]`;
  (ii) each scheme in the chain of inclusions `P_0(A(g))` (the empty scheme), `P_2(A(g))`, ..., `P_(2c)(A(g))` is a pfaffian subscheme of `A^p`, with `codim P_(2i)(A(g)) = (a - 2i + 2)(a - 2i + 1)/2` for `i = 1, ..., c`; (iii) `P_(2(i-1))(A(g))` is the singular locus of `P_(2i)(A(g))` for `i = 1, ..., c`. A (global) deformation of a scheme `Y` is a faithfully flat morphism of algebraic schemes with `Y` isomorphic to a fiber at a rational point (p. 181).
- [P] Corollary 19 (pp. 181-182): for a pfaffian subscheme `X` of `A^p` of pure codimension three there is a deformation `q: V -> W` of `X` such that over each rational point `w` of an open subset of `W` the fiber has a stratification, a chain of inclusions `Y_c`, ..., `Y_1`, `Y_0 = q^(-1)(w)`, in which `Y_i` is a pfaffian subscheme of `A^p` of codimension `(2i + 3)(i + 1)`, empty if that number exceeds `p`. In particular, for `p < 10`, every pfaffian subscheme of codimension three in `A^p` deforms to a smooth variety. The proof (p. 182) notes that pure codimension three forces `a = 2c + 1` in the formula of Theorem 17.
- [P] Statement attributed to Buchsbaum and Eisenbud (p. 182; their paper is [Buchsbaum and Eisenbud 1977](buchsbaum-eisenbud-1977-finite-free-resolutions-codimension-3.md)): for a regular local ring `S` and an ideal `I` of codimension three, `S/I` is Gorenstein if and only if `I = Pf_(2c)(A)` for an alternating `(2c+1) x (2c+1)` matrix `A` with coordinates in `S` (the scan prints `R/I`). For a codimension three pfaffian ideal `I` in a polynomial ring `S = k[X_1, ..., X_p]` the paper writes the complex `0 -> S -> S^(2c+1) -> S^(2c+1) -> S -> S/I -> 0` and states that it is exact by the easy part of the Buchsbaum-Eisenbud proof, so `S/I` is Gorenstein and `Ext^3_S(S/I, S)` is cyclic (p. 182).
- [P] Example 20 (pp. 182-185): over a field `k`, let `N` be the `2 x 4` matrix with rows `(x_11, x_12, x_13, x_14)` and `(x_21, x_22, x_23, x_11 + 1)`, whose entries other than the corner `r_24` are independent variables, and `S` the polynomial ring in them (seven variables). The ideal `I` of the `2 x 2` minors of `N` has codimension three and `S/I` is Gorenstein (even regular), but `I` is the pfaffian ideal of no alternating matrix with entries in `S`
  (p. 183). The proof shows `Ext^3_S(S/I, S)` is not cyclic: the Eagon-Northcott resolution gives the module explicitly (p. 183), and a Picard group computation on `P^1 x P^1` minus a diagonal (an argument the paper credits to Ellingsrud and Stromme, p. 184) shows that a certain module `R^2/M` over a quotient ring `R` of `S` is not free of rank one (p. 185), which rules out a cyclic `Ext^3`. Every pfaffian ideal of codimension three has a cyclic `Ext^3` (p. 182).

## The Hilbert function (Section 6, pp. 185-187)

- [P] Proposition 21 (p. 185): the dimension `H(m)` of the `m`-th graded piece of `k[T]/Pf_(2c)(a)` is the number of order reversing maps `sigma` from the poset `P` of pairs `(i, j)` with `1 <= j <= 2c - 2` and `1 <= i <= a - j + 1` (partial order defined on p. 185) to the nonnegative integers such that `sum_(i=1..2c-2) sigma(a - i + 1, i) = 2m` and `sigma(a - (2i - 1) + 1, 2i - 1) = sigma(a - 2i + 1, 2i)` for `i = 1, ..., c - 1`.
  The proof rests on the standard monomial basis of `k[T]/Pf_(2c)(a)` of De Concini and Procesi (reference [5], Theorem 6.5, p. 351): products of pfaffians of even orders `s_1 >= ... >= s_r` between 2 and `2c - 2` satisfying the index conditions listed on p. 185, of degree `(s_1 + ... + s_r)/2`.
- [P] Corollary 22 (p. 186): the Hilbert series `F(t) = sum_i H(i) t^i` of `k[T]/Pf_(2c)(a)` satisfies a functional equation `F(t^(-1)) = (-1)^(c-1) t^q F(t)`, with the exponent printed as `q = 2(c-1)^2` (the proof, p. 187, computes the same value as a sum `sum_(i=1..c-1) (4i - 2)`). The proof uses Stanley's reciprocity theorem (reference [18], Theorem 10.1, p. 29) and the `delta`-chain property of the poset (reference [18], Lemma 18.2, p. 66).
  [I] The exponent as printed disagrees with the case `a = 4`, `c = 2`: `Pf_4(T)` of a `4 x 4` alternating matrix is one quadric in six variables, with Hilbert series `(1 + t)/(1 - t)^5` satisfying `F(1/t) = -t^4 F(t)`, whereas `2(c-1)^2 = 2`; the sign `(-1)^(c-1)` agrees. Stanley's criterion in the proof of Theorem 17 asks only for some integer `r`, so the Gorenstein conclusion does not depend on the exponent. This note does not use the printed exponent.

## Factoriality (Section 7, p. 188)

- [P] Proposition 23 (p. 188; observed by Avramov, reference [1]): `R` is factorial if and only if `R[T]/Pf_(2c)(a)` is. The proof takes `t = Pf T(1, 2, ..., 2c - 2)`; by Theorem 12, `t` generates a prime ideal of height one in `A = R[T]/Pf_(2c)(a)`, so by Nagata's lemma (Lemma 1, p. 143 of Nagata, J. Math. Soc. Japan 9 (1957), as the proof cites it) `A` is factorial if and only if the localization `A_t` is; Proposition 10 with `m = c - 1` identifies `A_t` with a localization `S_t` of a polynomial ring over `R`, and Theorem 12 shows `t` is irreducible in the polynomial ring over `R` on the entries of `T(1, ..., 2c - 2)`, so by the same lemma `S_t` is factorial if and only if `R` is.
  The statement prints no hypothesis on `R`; its proof invokes Theorem 12, whose hypothesis is that `R` is an integral domain. The introduction (p. 168) combines the proposition with Murthy's theorem (a factorial Cohen-Macaulay quotient of a regular ring is Gorenstein) as a second proof that pfaffian schemes are Gorenstein.

- [I] Citing context.   The program's ideals that this paper bears on are those of ledger rows A5-416, A5-418, A5-421 and A5-423 in `docs/evidence/a5-exceptional-fibers/ledger.tsv`: the 126 principal four-pfaffians of a Schur-form matrix (the weight vector of A5-421 has nine entries, and `C(9,4) = 126`).
- [I] What the paper supplies for the generic matrix. For the generic alternating `9 x 9` matrix over an algebraically closed field of any characteristic, Theorem 17 (with `a = 9`, `c = 2`) makes the four-pfaffian scheme `P_4(9)` an integral Gorenstein subscheme of `A^36` of codimension `(9-4+2)(9-4+1)/2 = 21`, and its singular locus is `P_2(9)`, which is the origin because the 2-pfaffians are the entries. Theorem 12 gives the same height 21 for a base ring `R` that is an integral domain, and Theorem 15 gives the Cohen-Macaulay property over any Cohen-Macaulay base ring, so over `Q(i)` and over the prime fields at 32009 and 32029 as fields.
  The number 21 equals `mu(2, 9)` of Theorem 2.1 of [Jozefiak and Pragacz 1979](jozefiak-pragacz-1979-ideals-generated-pfaffians.md) and the height formula `(l+1)(2l+3)` quoted on p. 479 of [Buchsbaum and Eisenbud 1977](buchsbaum-eisenbud-1977-finite-free-resolutions-codimension-3.md) at `l = 2`. Theorem 17 is stated over algebraically closed fields, and the paper states no descent to `Q(i)` or `F_p` for the Gorenstein property. Theorem 17(i) asserts the Gorenstein property of every generic pfaffian ideal in every characteristic, which matches the information that Jozefiak and Pragacz attribute to Laksov in their Remark 3.7 (the note records that attribution and no citation of this paper).
- [I] What the paper does not supply. (a) Every statement concerns the quotient `k[T]/Pf_(2c)(T)` and its generic scheme; the fiber cone of the program is the algebra generated by the four-pfaffians modulo the maximal ideal of the Rees algebra, a different ring (compare [Baetica 1998](baetica-1998-rees-algebra-pfaffians.md)), and the paper treats no Rees algebra, power of a pfaffian ideal, symbolic power, integral closure or fiber cone, so its Hilbert series (Proposition 21) is not the Hilbert function `h_d` of the ledger.
  (b) The program's matrices are specializations. The entries of the program's `9 x 9` alternating matrices are functions of at most 15 coordinates (the split coordinates `z'_1, ..., z'_15` of the strata scripts), below the generic codimension 21, so a nonempty four-pfaffian subscheme there has codimension below 21, is not a pfaffian subscheme in the sense of Definition 18, and falls outside the equality case of Corollary 16, Theorem 18 and Corollary 19.
  (c) The deformation results concern pfaffian subschemes of affine space in the sense of Definition 18, which (b) excludes for the slice matrices, so the paper gives no deformation of the program's fibers.
