## Setting and conventions

- [P] The paper bounds the generation degree `b_0` and the concentration degree `topdeg` of the torsion part `A_k(I)` of the symmetric algebra of `I` in the Rees presentation (the "defining equations of `R(I)`" beyond the symmetric ones), for `I` an ideal of minors of a matrix, of a symmetric matrix, or of Pfaffians of an alternating matrix over a polynomial ring `R = K[x_1, ..., x_d]`, under the assumption that `I` has generic height (abstract and introduction, pp. 1-2). The entries need not be generic or linear; the method specializes from the generic matrix and controls the specialization map between the Rees rings through height conditions on the lower size minors (p. 2).
- [P] Data 5.1.1 (p. 22): `R` standard graded in `d` indeterminates, `A` an `n x n` alternating matrix with entries homogeneous of one degree `delta`, `I = Pf_(2t)(A)` of generic alternating height, `X` the generic alternating matrix, `B = R[X]`, `J = Pf_(2t)(X)`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Proposition 5.1.2 (p. 22): under the specialization hypotheses stated there (`J` homogeneous of one degree `q`, `Y_i = X_i - A_i` regular on `B/J`, resolutions `E^k` of `J^k(kq)`, height conditions `ht K_i R >= min{i + 1, d}`), `b_0(A_k(I)) <= max{b_0(A_k(J)), b_0(E^k_(d-1)) - d + 1}` and `topdeg(A_k(I)) <= max{topdeg(A_k(J)), b_0(E^k_d) - d}`.
- [P] Theorem 5.4.3, submaximal Pfaffians (p. 34): `K` a field, `n >= 3` odd, `A` an `n x n` alternating matrix with entries homogeneous of degree `delta`, `I = Pf_(n-1)(A)` of generic alternating height (`ht I = 3`) and `ht Pf_(2j)(A) >= min{n - 2j + 2, d}` for `2 <= 2j <= n - 3`. Then (a) for `k >= d` and `d <= n - 1`: `b_0(A_k(I)) <= (d - 1)(delta - 1)` and `topdeg(A_k(I)) <= d(delta - 1)`; (b) for `d` even, `d <= n - 1` and `k = d - 1`: `b_0(A_k(I)) <= (d - 1)(delta - 1)` and `topdeg(A_k(I)) <= (d - 1)(delta - 1) + (delta/2)(n - d + 1) - 1`; (c) for `d` odd and `k = d - 1`: both vanish (`-infinity`); (d) for `d >= n` or `k <= d - 2`: both vanish.
- [P] Corollary 5.4.4 (p. 34, with its tail on p. 35): under the height condition `ht Pf_(2j)(A) >= min{n - 2j + 2, d}`, `I = Pf_(n-1)(A)` is of linear type if `d >= n`, of fiber type if `delta = 1`, and `A_k(I) = 0` for `k <= d - 2` (and for `k <= d - 1` when `d` is odd). The paper states that these are implications of Kustin, Polini and Ulrich's Theorem 6.1.b (its reference [41]) and that the Pfaffian case reduces to their technique.
- [P] Theorem 5.4.5, Pfaffians of size `n - 2` (p. 35): `K` of characteristic zero, `n >= 4` even, `A` alternating with entries of degree `delta`, `I = Pf_(n-2)(A)` of generic alternating height (`ht I = 6`) and `ht Pf_(2j)(A) >= min{C(n - 2j + 2, 2), d}`. For `k >= n - 2` the bounds are `b_0(A_k(I)) <= (d - 1)(delta - 1) + delta (n - 4)^2 / 8` and `topdeg(A_k(I)) <= d(delta - 1) + delta (n - 4)^2 / 8` when `4` divides `n`, and the same with `delta (n - 2)(n - 6) / 8` when it does not.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The repository's object is the fiber cone `F(I)` of a kernel-coordinate ideal of non-maximal Pfaffian type on a localized orbit-closure ring ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)). The bounds of this paper limit the `x`-degree of the Rees torsion `A_k(I)`, and fiber type states only that the Rees ideal is generated by the equations of the fiber plus the symmetric ones. No stated bound limits the degree of the equations of `F(I)`, so no theorem here supplies the stopping degree the fiber-cone computations lack.
- [I] The hypotheses fail for the repository ideals on three counts: `R` is a localized quotient and not a polynomial ring over a field, the generators are inhomogeneous, and the generic-height assumption is unestablished. If the generators were the maximal minors of a `16 x 8` matrix of kernel-basis entries, the generic height `(16 - 8 + 1)(8 - 8 + 1) = 9` would exceed the dimension of the germs (at most 6), so the ideal could not have generic height.
- [I] The technique of Proposition 4.8 (specializing a resolution of the generic case when lower minors have large height, named on p. 2) applies once a kernel-coordinate ideal is exhibited as a Pfaffian or determinantal ideal of an explicit matrix; no source in the registry supplies that matrix.
