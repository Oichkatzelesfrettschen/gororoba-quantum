## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are PDF page indices.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): satellite-based distribution of entangled photon pairs to two locations 1203 km
  apart, through two satellite-to-ground downlinks whose summed length varies from 1600 km to 2400 km;
  the experiment observes "a survival of two-photon entanglement and a violation of Bell inequality by
  2.37 +- 0.09 under strict Einstein locality conditions". The effective link efficiency at 1200 km is more
  than 12 orders of magnitude above direct transmission through the best commercial telecommunication
  fiber (0.16 dB/km).
- [P] Introduction (p. 2): an entangled source of photon pairs at a 10 MHz count rate, sent through two
  600 km fibers of 0.2 dB/km, would give `10^-17` two-photon coincidences per second.
- [P] Setup (pp. 2-4): the satellite (Micius) flies a sun-synchronous orbit at about 500 km altitude at
  7.6 km/s; the satellite-to-station distance varies from 500 km to 2000 km. A 405 nm continuous-wave
  laser pumps a periodically poled KTiOPO4 crystal that emits polarization-entangled pairs near 810 nm;
  the ground stations are Delingha, Lijiang and Nanshan, and Delingha and Lijiang are 1203 km apart.
- [P] Results (pp. 3, 6): the two-downlink attenuation varies from 64 dB to 82 dB; the average
  two-photon count rate is 1.1 Hz with a signal-to-noise ratio of about 8:1; the state fidelity is
  `0.869 +- 0.085`.
- [P] Bell test (p. 7): the CHSH quantity
  `S = |E(phi_1, phi_2) - E(phi_1, phi_2') + E(phi_1', phi_2) + E(phi_1', phi_2')| <= 2`, with the angle
  pairs `(0, pi/8)`, `(0, 3 pi/8)`, `(pi/4, pi/8)`, `(pi/4, 3 pi/8)` chosen randomly fast enough to close
  the locality and freedom-of-choice loopholes. Over 1167 trials in an effective 1059 s the paper finds
  `S = 2.37 +- 0.09`, a violation of `S <= 2` by 4 standard deviations. The state fidelity on
  page 7 reads `F >= 0.87 +- 0.09`.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Size: the paper tests entanglement survival, not a relativistic phase. The satellite sits about
  500 km above the ground stations, a potential difference of order `g h / c^2 = 5 x 10^-11`
  (an upper bound, with `g` held at its surface value), and `S = 2.37 +- 0.09` constrains no
  proper-time or Sagnac coupling at that level. The repository's retained records move by order-one
  fractions of readout probability and transmittance
  ([tuna-17.md](../quantum_inspire/tuna-17.md), [platform-specifications.md](../quandela/platform-specifications.md)),
  so no repository observable resolves an effect near `5 x 10^-11`.
- [I] Signature: a gravitational or rotational coupling to Bell correlations would appear as an `S`
  that depends on the satellite's altitude or on the orientation of the ground-station baseline relative to
  Earth's rotation; the paper's single pass shows no such dependence at its 4 sigma statistics.
