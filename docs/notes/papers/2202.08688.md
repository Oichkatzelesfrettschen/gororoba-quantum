## Main claims

[P] The t-flat tester for Reed-Muller codes over F_q is optimal in its dependence on the degree d and the proximity delta, and its dependence on the field size q is polynomial, q^(-O(1)), where earlier analyses gave a tower-type function of q.

- [P] Abstract: a tester for degree-d polynomials F_q^n -> F_q with proximity parameter delta is optimal if it makes O(q^d + 1/delta) queries, which are "known to be necessary". Optimal testers were given by Bhattacharyya et al. for q = 2 and by Haramaty et al. for all prime powers q.
- [P] Definitions 1.1 and 1.2: delta_d(f) is the Hamming distance fraction from f to the nearest function of degree at most d. The t-flat test picks a uniform t-dimensional affine subspace T and accepts iff deg(f restricted to T) <= d; epsilon_(t,d)(f) is its rejection probability. The dimension is t = ceil((d + 1) / (q - q/p)), the least t at which every f of degree above d fails with positive probability. For q = 2, t = d + 1.
- [P] Sec. 1.1: for q = 2 the first analysis (Alon, Kaufman, Krivelevich, Litsyn, Ron) is printed as epsilon_(t,d)(f) >= q^(-t) delta_d(f); the optimal analysis (Bhattacharyya, Kopparty, Schoenebeck, Sudan, Zuckerman) gives epsilon_(t,d)(f) >= min(c, q^t delta_d(f)) for an absolute constant c > 0. Theorem 1.3 (Haramaty et al.): epsilon_(t,d)(f) >= c(q) min(1, q^t delta_d(f)).
- [P] Theorem 1.4: the same bound with c(q) = q^(-O(1)). Theorems 1.5 and 1.6 extend it to lifted affine-invariant linear codes. The proof studies the set S of erroneous t-flats in the affine Grassmann graph, shows 1 - Phi(S) >= 1/q, applies a global-hypercontractivity expansion theorem (Theorem 2.4) to locate a point x* that lies in almost all erroneous flats, and corrects f(x*) (Sec. 3).
- [P] Lemma 2.1 (from Haramaty et al., Lemma 4.6): for f: F_q^(k+1) -> F_q and k >= t, (1) deg(f) > d implies epsilon_(k,d)(f) >= 1/q, and (2) d < deg(f) < (k+1)(q-1) implies epsilon_(k,d)(f) > 1/q. Here the k-flats of F_q^(k+1) are its hyperplanes. Lemma 2.2: for f on F_q^n and k >= t, epsilon_(k+1,d)(f) <= q epsilon_(k,d)(f).
- [P] Sec. 1.4, case q = 2: t = d + 1; flipping the value of f at a single point x lowers the rejection probability by Theta(2^(d-n)), because a degree d + 1 polynomial on a (d+1)-dimensional subspace of F_2^n has degree at most d after one point is flipped; after changing f on O(2^(n-d) epsilon) points the function has degree at most d.
- [P] Sec. 5 lists open questions, including the regime where the tester's acceptance guarantee is only 1/q + delta (the "1% regime").

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Statement under adjudication (context: gororoba_quantum/DESIGN_NOTES.md, Design C, "phase oracles of degree up to n - 2"): a degree-d Boolean function is distinguishable from a random function with 2^(d+1) value queries on an affine cube, D_(h_1)...D_(h_(d+1)) f = 0.
  - Source: Definition 1.2 with q = 2, Theorem 1.4 with c(2) an absolute constant, and the abstract's O(q^d + 1/delta).
  - Model map: for q = 2 the test draws a random (d+1)-flat F = x + span(h_1, ..., h_(d+1)) and accepts iff deg(f restricted to F) <= d. The restriction to a (d+1)-dimensional flat has degree at most d iff the sum of f over the 2^(d+1) points of F is zero (that sum is the coefficient of the top monomial of the restriction), which is the (d+1)-fold derivative D_(h_1)...D_(h_(d+1)) f (x). A function of degree at most d is accepted with probability 1. A function that is delta-far from degree at most d is rejected with probability at least c min(1, 2^(d+1) delta), so O(1/min(1, 2^(d+1) delta)) flats of 2^(d+1) points decide with constant error, O(2^d + 1/delta) queries in all. A uniformly random f is Omega(1)-far from degree at most d while sum_(k<=d) C(n, k) is below (1 - c) 2^n by a counting bound (about d <= n/2 - c sqrt(n)), so 2^(d+1) queries per flat and O(1) flats suffice there. The learning count sum_(k<=d) C(n, k) = dim RM(d, n) is not in this paper (note elkies-2013-m256).
  - Label: known theorem reproduced. The q = 2 optimal tester is the work cited as [1] and [2] in the paper; this paper's contribution is the field-size dependence, which is irrelevant at q = 2.
  - Not established by the source: a lower bound for deciding a specific known function, a statement about learning, or a statement about quantum access. The "known to be necessary" bound is a worst-case bound over inputs and is cited, not proved, here.
- [I] No repository claim: Close-to-low-degree inputs. Lemma 2.1(1) is scoped to f on F_q^(k+1), so on F_2^n with t < n - 1 it does not give a constant rejection probability for every f of degree above d; Theorem 1.4 is the operative statement. The union bound over the 2^(d+1) points of a flat gives epsilon_(d+1,d)(f) <= 2^(d+1) delta_d(f). The OR-type rung omega_(1, 2^(n-1)) (gororoba_quantum/verify.py, check_associator_walsh_hierarchy) equals 1 + [live bits = 0] on m = n - 2 live bits, which differs from the constant 1 on 2^(n-m) = 4 of 2^n points, so delta_d <= 2^(-m) for every d, and epsilon_(d+1,d) <= 2^(d+1-m) for d < m. A tester of this form then needs on the order of 2^(m-d-1) flats of 2^(d+1) points, about 2^(n-2) queries, to detect that the rung has degree above d. This is the 1/delta term of the O(q^d + 1/delta) bound.
- [I] No repository claim: Edge case d = n - 2. For f on F_2^n, t = n - 1 and the (n-1)-flats are the hyperplanes, so Lemma 2.1(1) applies as stated: any f of degree n - 1 or n is rejected with probability at least 1/2 by one random hyperplane, at 2^(n-1) queries. Every element of H_n has degree at most n - 2 (literature/notes/left_mult_sign_rank.md) and passes this test with probability 1.
- [I] No repository claim: scope of the paper against a blanket classical bound. For the task of distinguishing degree at most d from a uniformly random function, the q = 2 t-flat tester (the work cited as [1] and [2] in the paper) uses O(2^(d+1)) classical queries, which refutes any blanket Omega(2^(n/2)) classical lower bound for this task whenever d + 1 < n/2. The necessary-query bound O(q^d + 1/delta) depends on d and delta, not on n alone. The paper contains no hidden-shift, oracle-model, or quantum statement, so it neither establishes nor bounds any quantum cost.
