## Framework (PDF p. 1-3)

- [P] Certification ensures the correct functioning of a device in terms of output accuracy; benchmarking "assigns a
  reproducible performance measure to a quantum device". (PDF p. 1)
- [P] A certificate is best phrased as a distance with operational meaning: trace distance for states, diamond norm for
  channels, fidelity for pure targets, average gate fidelity for gates. The fidelity bounds the trace distance by
  1 - sqrt(F) <= d <= sqrt(1 - F) and so certifies worst-case performance, whereas the average gate fidelity "can
  therefore certify performance typically only on average" and bounds the diamond norm without a dimension factor only
  in special situations. (Box 1, PDF p. 2)
- [P] Every protocol is classified by the information it returns (point estimate, region, fidelity, witness, property),
  by its assumptions on the device, the measurement apparatus and the user, and by three complexities: measurement
  settings, samples and classical post-processing. A device-independent setting assumes nothing about the apparatus and
  uses only the final data. (PDF p. 2)
- [P] The weight of the criteria shifts with the application: exploratory science prizes information gain and avoids
  assumptions on the device under test; large-scale devices prize efficiency. (PDF p. 3)

## Protocol families and their stated assumptions (PDF p. 3-6)

- [P] Full tomography needs trusted measurements and i.i.d. copies; regions come from maximum likelihood plus resampling
  (heuristic), Bayesian credibility regions (smaller, prior dependent) or confidence regions (rigorous). Tomography
  returns "actionable advice" because it exposes the deviation from target. (PDF p. 3)
- [P] Compressed sensing, tensor-network tomography and PAC learning cut the cost under structural assumptions (low rank,
  near-unitarity, low entanglement); learning a restricted Hamiltonian assumes the model class. (PDF p. 3-4)
- [P] Fidelity estimation, fidelity witnesses and property witnesses (entanglement) trade information for cost. (PDF p. 4)
- [P] Randomized benchmarking estimates an average gate error robustly against state-preparation and measurement error by
  amplifying error with sequence length; it assumes (as listed in Table I) i.i.d. and SPAM-robust data. (Box 2, PDF p. 3)
- [P] Validation against classical simulation "in certain classically simulable regimes" builds trust, and extending that
  trust to the full device assumes "that no additional sources of errors arise when moving out of the strictly
  certifiable regime again". (PDF p. 4)
- [P] "An important drawback of most schemes discussed so far, however, is that they assume i.i.d. state preparations";
  quantum de Finetti arguments give non-i.i.d. regions. Standard tomography also requires trusted, calibrated measurement
  devices, which needs well-characterized probe states, a vicious cycle that gate set tomography and RB-style protocols
  break by self-consistent estimation. (PDF p. 4-5)
- [P] Certifying a quantum advantage cannot rely on classical verification of the full output: exponentially flat output
  distributions need exponentially many samples; the routes are weaker targets (distinguishing from uniform), noise
  assumptions (cross-entropy), trusted single-qubit measurements, or interactive protocols (Box 3, PDF p. 4).
- [P] Self-testing certifies states and measurements from observed statistics alone, typically via maximal violation of a
  Bell inequality, up to local isometries. (PDF p. 5)
- [P] Table I assigns each protocol an information type, a measurement/sample/post-processing complexity and an
  assumption column, for example RB: average gate fidelity, i.i.d. and SPAM robust; gate set tomography: point
  estimates, i.i.d. and fully self-consistent. (PDF p. 7-8, Box 4 on PDF p. 5)

## Mapping to this repository

Relevance: (a) low, (b) none, (c) none; the source grounds [critical peer review of hardware results](../../runbooks/critical-peer-review.md).

- [I] A reviewer asks of any hardware certificate which of the three building blocks (device, measurement apparatus,
  classical processing) it trusts and which assumption column of Table I it sits in; a claim stated at the strength of a
  tighter column than the data supports is overclaimed.
- [I] A result validated in a simulable regime and extended to a larger hardware regime carries the Eisert statement of
  PDF p. 4 as an explicit untested assumption in its claim wording.
- [I] Repeated acquisitions across calibrations violate the i.i.d. premise of PDF p. 4; the reviewer checks for a drift
  test (see [van Enk and Blume-Kohout](1302.0932.md)) before pooling.
