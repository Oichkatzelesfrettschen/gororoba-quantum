## Setting and conventions

- [P] Section 1.2 (pp. 6-7): over a field `k` of characteristic 0, `X` is a generic `m x n` matrix with `m <= n`, `A_t` is the `k`-subalgebra of `k[X]`
  generated by the `t`-minors (graded with the `t`-minors in degree 1), `S_t = Sym(E tensor F*)` with `E = wedge^t V`, `F = wedge^t W` (`dim V = m`, `dim W = n`),
  `pi : S_t -> A_t` is the natural map and `J_t = ker(pi)` is the ideal of relations; `G = GL(V) x GL(W)` acts and `J_t` is a `G`-subrepresentation of `S_t` (pp. 7-8). The algebra `A_t` is the
  coordinate ring of the Zariski closure of the image of `phi -> wedge^t phi` (p. 2).
- [P] Remark 1.2 (p. 7): `J_t = 0` when `t = 1` or `n <= t + 1`; when `t = m`, `A_t` is the coordinate ring of the Grassmannian `G(m, n)` and `J_t` is generated by the Plucker relations in degree 2.
  Proposition 1.3 (p. 8): `A_t(n, n)` is isomorphic to `A_(n-t)(n, n)`. Proposition 1.4 (p. 8): `A_t(m, n)` is a graded retract of `A_(t')(m', n')` when `n' - n, m' - m >= t' - t`, for `t' >= t` (the sentence that introduces the proposition on p. 8 states that restriction; the displayed statement does not repeat it).
- [P] Definition 1.24 (p. 17): shape relations, the minimal relations `(gamma | lambda)` of `J_t` identified through bi-predecessors by Theorem 1.23 (p. 16); the paper states that it does not know whether all minimal relations are shape relations.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Abstract and introduction (pp. 1-3): the Plucker relations generate the relations of the maximal minors; for `t < min(m, n)` the paper exhibits minimal relations of degree 2 (non-Plucker in general)
  and of degree 3, and gives computational evidence for the conjecture that these generate. The introduction (p. 2) gives the cubic relation (0.1), the determinant of a `3 x 3` array of `2`-minors of a `3 x 4` matrix, as already minimal for `t = 2`.
- [P] Section 2.1 (pp. 18-19): `(J_t)_2` is isomorphic to the sum of `L_(tau_u) V tensor L_(tau_v) W*` over `u, v` in `{0, ..., t}` with `u + v` even and `u` different from `v`, with `tau_u = (t + u, t - u)`;
  the highest weight vector is the polynomial `f_(u,v)` of (2.2) (Lemma 2.1, p. 18). Remark 2.2 (p. 19): `f_(u,v)` is a Plucker relation if and only if `u = 0` or `v = 0`, so for `t >= 3` the Plucker relations are not the only quadratic relations.
- [P] Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.5 (p. 20): the bi-diagram `(gamma_u | lambda_u)` of (2.3) is a `T`-shape relation of degree 3, hence a minimal irreducible representation of `J_t(m, n)` provided `u <= m - t` and `2u <= n - t`;
  `J_t` has minimal generators of degree 3 apart from the cases of Remark 1.2. Theorem 2.7 (p. 20): the bi-diagram `(rho_u | sigma_u)` of (2.4) is a shape relation of single `wedge^t`-type, minimal of degree 3, provided `u <= m - t` and `2u <= n - t + 1`.
- [P] Remarks 2.3 and 2.6 (p. 20) and the discussion on p. 22: a new type of minimal cubic relation appears each time `t` increases by 1, the even type `(gamma_(t/2) | lambda_(t/2))` for even `t` and the odd type `(rho_((t+1)/2) | sigma_((t+1)/2))` for odd `t`;
  Lemma 2.10 (p. 23) and equations (2.7) and (2.10) (pp. 24-25) give explicit highest weight polynomials `g_u` and `h_u`.
- [P] Conjecture 2.12 (p. 26): for all `t, m, n` the quadrics `f_(u,v)` and the cubics `g_u, g'_u, h_u, h'_u` generate `J_t(m, n)` as a `G`-ideal. Remark 2.13 (p. 26): (c) for the algebra of 2-minors of a symmetric `n x n` matrix with `n <= 5` the ideal of relations is generated in degree 2 (computed with the methods of Section 3.2);
  (d) De Negri's Theorem 1.4 (cited as reference [10]) states that in characteristic 0 there are no degree 2 relations between the `2t`-pfaffians of an alternating `n x n` matrix, for arbitrary `t` and `n`.
- [P] Theorem 3.1 (p. 28): apart from the cases of Remark 1.2, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of `A_t` is `mn - ceil(mn/t)` when `m + n - 1 < floor(mn/t)`, and `mn - floor(m(n + k_0)/t)` with `k_0 = ceil((tm + tn - mn)/(m - t))` otherwise. The introduction (p. 3) and Section 3 (p. 27) state that the conjecture was verified for `t = 2` and `m, n <= 5` and for `m = 4` with arbitrary `n`, that for `t = 2` the only minimal relations of degree 3 are the described ones and no minimal relation of degree 4 exists, and that for `t = 3` no other minimal relations of degree 3 exist (the verifications rest on computer calculations and are cited, not reproduced).
  Theorem 3.1 is stated for `t, m, n` outside Remark 1.2 and rests on the Cohen-Macaulay property of `A_t` from the authors' earlier work (cited on p. 28).

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 program computes the special fiber (fiber cone) of Rees algebras and finds Hilbert functions equal to the quadric values through the degrees computed
  ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)). For an ideal generated by forms of one degree, the fiber cone is the algebra generated by those forms, and `A_t` is this algebra
  for the ideal of `t`-minors of a generic matrix. Corollary 2.5 and Theorem 2.7 give the generic expectation: the relations of `A_t` need cubic generators in addition to quadrics, once the matrix is large enough.
  A fiber cone cut out by quadrics alone is therefore a special fact against this baseline, as is the absence of cubic relations in the measured degrees.
- [I] Remark 2.13(d), quoted second hand from De Negri, gives the Pfaffian counterpart: for the `2t`-pfaffians of a generic alternating matrix there are no quadratic relations. The repository's fibers do have quadrics (the K2 counts of the findings),
  so they differ from the generic Pfaffian special fiber in that respect.
- [I] The source concerns generic matrices. The repository ideals are restrictions to orbit closures, and no comparison with `J_t` was made; the cubic relations of Theorem 2.4 also need the matrix size bounds `u <= m - t`, `2u <= n - t`, which a repository fiber would have to meet.
