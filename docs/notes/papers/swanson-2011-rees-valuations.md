## Conventions and the definition of Rees valuations (Section 1, pp. 1-6)

- [P] Conventions (pp. 1-2): all rings are commutative with identity and most are Noetherian domains. `Q(R)` is the field of fractions of a domain `R`; a valuation ring `V` has the unique maximal ideal `m_V`; the center of `V` on `R` is `m_V` intersected with `R`; a Noetherian valuation is normalized when its value group lies in the integers with greatest common divisor 1.
  A valuation ring `V` is divisorial with respect to a subdomain `R` when `Q(R) = Q(V)` and the transcendence degree of `kappa(m_V)` over `kappa(p)` is `ht p - 1`, with `p = m_V` intersected with `R`; every divisorial valuation with respect to a Noetherian ring is essentially of finite type over it (p. 2). For an ideal `I`, the `I`-adic order is `ord_I(x) = sup{n : x in I^n}` (p. 2).
- [P] Definition 1.1 (pp. 2-3): for a ring `R` and an ideal `I`, a set `{V_1, ..., V_s}` of Rees valuation rings of `I` consists of valuation rings such that (1) each `V_i` is Noetherian and not a field, (2) each `V_i` lies between `R/P_i` and `Q(R/P_i)` for some minimal prime `P_i` of `R`, (3) `closure(I^n) = intersection_i (I^n V_i) intersect R` for all `n`, and (4) the set is minimal with these properties.
  Page 3 restates condition 3 as `closure(I^n) = {r : v_i(r) >= n v_i(I) for all i}` for the corresponding valuations and records that `RV(I) = RV(closure(I))` for every ideal `I`.
- [P] Basic properties (pp. 3-4): `RV(W^(-1) I) = {V in RV(I) : m_V intersect W is empty}` for a multiplicatively closed set `W` (p. 3). For a ring map `R -> S` with `S` faithfully flat or integral over `R`, `closure(I) = closure(I S) intersect R` (proofs cited to Propositions 1.6.1 and 1.6.2 of Huneke and Swanson, pp. 3-4), and if Rees valuations of `I S` exist then `RV(I)` lies in `{V intersect Q(R) : V in RV(I S)}`;
  equality holds when `S` is the integral closure of the Noetherian domain `R` in its field of fractions (p. 4). For a Noetherian local ring `(R, m)` and an `m`-primary ideal `I`, `closure(I) R-hat` is the integral closure of `I R-hat`, so `RV(I) = {V intersect Q(R) : V in RV(I R-hat)}`;
  if in addition `R-hat` is a domain, `I` and `I R-hat` have the same number of Rees valuations (p. 4).
- [P] Theorem 1.2 (Katz and Validashti, p. 4): let `I` be an ideal in a Noetherian local ring `(R, m)` that is not contained in any minimal prime ideal, let `w` be a Rees valuation of `I R-hat` with center `m R-hat`, and let `Q` be the corresponding minimal prime of `R-hat` such that `w` is a valuation on `kappa(Q)`. Then the restriction of `w` to `kappa(Q intersect R)` is a Rees valuation of `I` with center `m`,
  and the map that sends `w` to its restriction to `kappa({r in R : w(r) = infinity})` is one-to-one and onto from the Rees valuations of `I R-hat` with center on `m R-hat` to the Rees valuations of `I` with center on `m`.
- [P] Proposition 1.3 (p. 4, proof cited to Propositions 10.4.3 and 10.4.4 of Huneke and Swanson): for a Noetherian domain `R` and a valuation ring `V` divisorial with respect to `R`, some ideal `I` of `R`, primary to `P = m_V` intersected with `R`, has `V` among its Rees valuation rings. Conversely, for an ideal `J`, a Rees valuation ring `W` of `J` and `P = m_W` intersected with `R` with `R_P` formally equidimensional,
  `W` is divisorial with respect to `R` and `R_P`. The page concludes that on Noetherian locally formally equidimensional domains the Rees valuations of nonzero ideals are the divisorial valuations with respect to `R`.
- [P] Examples (pp. 4-5): a maximal ideal `m` of a regular ring has one Rees valuation, the `m`-adic one (Example 1). For a monomial ideal `I` of a polynomial ring `k[X_1, ..., X_d]` over a field, the integral closure of `I^n` is generated by the monomials with exponent vector `a` satisfying `c_1 a_1 + ... + c_d a_d >= n` for the coefficient vectors `(c_1, ..., c_d)` of the
  non-coordinate bounding hyperplanes `c_1 X_1 + ... + c_d X_d = 1` of the Newton polyhedron of `I`, and the monomial valuations defined by the irredundant bounding hyperplanes are the Rees valuations of `I` (Example 2, p. 5). In `k[X, Y, Z]` the ideals `(X, Y)` and `(X, Z)` have one Rees valuation each, and the product `(X, Y)(X, Z)` has more than the two (Example 4, p. 5).
- [P] Proposition 1.4 (Muhly and Sakuma, p. 5): for nonzero ideals `I` and `J` of a Noetherian domain `R`, `RV(I) union RV(J)` lies in `RV(I J)`, with equality if `I` is locally principal or if `R` is Noetherian, locally formally equidimensional, of dimension at most 2.
- [P] Page 6: `closure(I) = closure(J)` for ideals `I` inside `J` if and only if `I V = J V` for every Rees valuation ring `V` of `I`; `RV(I) = RV(I^n)` for every positive `n`; `closure(I^m) = closure(J^n)` for some positive `m, n` implies `RV(I) = RV(J)`, and the converse fails (the example `I = (X^2, Y)^5 intersect (X, Y^2)^4`, `J = (X^2, Y)^4 intersect (X, Y^2)^5`).
  The page defines a reduction of `I` as an ideal `J` inside `I` with `closure(J) = closure(I)` and calls the paper of Northcott and Rees the first crucial paper on reductions ([Northcott and Rees 1954](northcott-rees-1954-reductions-ideals-local-rings.md)).

## Existence, uniqueness and constructions (Section 2, pp. 6-10)

- [P] Page 6: Rees proved existence and uniqueness first for zero-dimensional ideals in equicharacteristic Noetherian local rings, then existence for ideals of Noetherian domains under an Artin-Rees-like condition, then existence for all ideals of Noetherian rings. Theorem 2.1 (p. 6, proofs cited to Theorems 10.1.6 and 10.2.2 of Huneke and Swanson): for a Noetherian ring `R` and any ideal `I` of `R`, a set of Rees valuation rings exists,
  and if `I` is not contained in any minimal prime ideal of `R`, the set is uniquely determined.
- [P] Pages 6-7: the existence proof reduces to a nonzero principal ideal in a Noetherian domain, where the integral closure of the ring is a Krull domain (Mori-Nagata theorem) and the localizations at the finitely many relevant primes are the Rees valuation rings; the general case passes to the extended Rees algebra, `RV(I) = {V intersect Q(R) : V in RV(t^(-1) R[I t, t^(-1)])}`.
  Proposition 2.2 (p. 7): for a Noetherian ring `R` and an ideal `I` not contained in any minimal prime ideal, with `T_P` the set of Rees valuations of `I (R/P)` for each minimal prime `P` (viewed as valuations on `R` with `{r : v(r) = infinity} = P`), the union of the `T_P` is the set of Rees valuations of `I`. The proof on p. 7 shows that no valuation in the union is redundant.
- [P] Constructions (pp. 7-9): for `I = (a_1, ..., a_r)`, `RV(I) = union_j RV(a_j R[I/a_j])` (pp. 7-8; the hypotheses are not restated at the display). The set of Rees valuation rings of `I` also equals the set of `closure(R[I t])_P` intersected with `Q(R)`, `P` over the primes of `closure(R[I t])` minimal over `I closure(R[I t])` (p. 8). `RV(J) = RV(I)` when the integral closures agree, so `I` may be replaced by a minimal reduction or by a reduction of a power of `I`;
  in a Noetherian local ring every ideal has a power with a reduction generated by at most `dim R` elements, and with infinite residue field the ideal itself has such a reduction (p. 8, citing Northcott and Rees and Propositions 8.3.7 and 8.3.8 of Huneke and Swanson).
- [P] Theorem 2.3 (Sally, p. 8): let `(R, m)` be a Noetherian formally equidimensional local domain of dimension `d > 0` and `I` an `m`-primary ideal minimally generated by `d` elements; then for any `a` in `I` that is part of a minimal generating set, every Rees valuation ring of `I` is the localization of the integral closure of `R[I/a]` at a height one prime ideal minimal over `a`.
  The unnumbered statement after it (p. 8): if `x_1, ..., x_r` is a regular sequence in a Noetherian domain `R`, then every Rees valuation ring `V` of `I = (x_1, ..., x_r)` satisfies `x_i V = I V` for every `i` and is the localization of the integral closure of `R[I/x_i]` at a height one prime containing `x_i`.
- [P] Theorem 2.4 (Huebl and Swanson, pp. 8-9): if `R` is integrally closed, then the associated graded ring `gr_I(R)` is reduced if and only if all powers of `I` are integrally closed and `v(I) = 1` for each normalized integer-valued Rees valuation `v` of `I`. The second sentence of the theorem, which opens with "Also", states that `R/closure(I) + closure(I)/closure(I^2) + ...` is reduced if and only if `v(I) = 1` for each integer-valued Rees valuation `v` of `I`; the page does not say whether that sentence inherits the hypothesis that `R` is integrally closed.
- [P] Example (p. 9): for `R = C[X, Y, Z]/(X^2 + Y^3 + Z^5)`, an integrally closed domain and a rational singularity (cited to Flenner), the maximal ideal `m` has one Rees valuation, found from the chart `X = X'Y`, `Z = Z'Y` of the blowup with `v(X') = 1`, `v(Z') = 0`, `v(Y) = 2`, `v(X) = 3`, `v(Z) = 2`, and `gr_m(R)` is not reduced.
- [P] Theorem 2.5 (Burch and McAdam, p. 9): let `R` be a Noetherian ring, `I` an ideal and `P` a prime ideal of `R`. If the analytic spread of `I R_P` equals `dim R_P`, then `P` is the center of a Rees valuation of `I`. If `R` is locally formally equidimensional and `P` is the center of a Rees valuation of `I`, then the analytic spread of `I R_P` equals `dim R_P`.
  Before the theorem (p. 9) the analytic spread of an ideal `I` of a Noetherian local ring `(R, m)` is defined as the Krull dimension of `R/m + I/mI + I^2/m I^2 + ...`; if `R/m` is infinite it equals the number of generators of any minimal reduction of `I`, and in general some power of `I` has a reduction generated minimally by that many elements (cited to Northcott and Rees).
- [P] Rees degree function (pp. 9-10): for an `m`-primary ideal `I` of `(R, m)` and `x` in `m` with `dim R/(x) = dim R - 1`, `d_I(x)` is the multiplicity of `I (R/(x))`, and Rees proved `d_I(x) = sum_v d(I, v) v(x)` over the Rees valuations `v` of `I` that are divisorial with respect to `R`, for positive integers `d(I, v)` depending only on `I` and `v` (p. 10).

## One-fibered ideals (Section 3, pp. 10-13)

- [P] Definition 3.1 and the page after it (p. 10): an ideal is one-fibered when it has exactly one Rees valuation; equivalently the radical of `t^(-1) closure(R[I t, t^(-1)])` is prime, equivalently the radical of `I closure(R[I t])` is prime. The page reports Zariski's theorem for two-dimensional regular local rings (every divisorial valuation centered on `m` is the only Rees valuation of some ideal),
  its extensions by Lipman and Goehner, Muhly's examples of two-dimensional analytically irreducible local domains where it fails, and Cutkosky's two-dimensional complete integrally closed local domain in which every `m`-primary ideal has at least two Rees valuations; no concrete ring of that kind is known (p. 10).
- [P] Theorem 3.2 (Sally, p. 11): if `(R, m)` is a Noetherian local ring that is analytically unramified (its `m`-adic completion is reduced) and has a one-fibered `m`-primary ideal, then `R` is analytically irreducible (its completion is a domain). Page 11 reports Katz's lower bound: for a formally equidimensional Noetherian local ring, the number of Rees valuations of an `m`-primary ideal is at least the number of minimal primes of the completion,
  Nagata's example of a zero-dimensional one-fibered ideal in a ring that is not analytically unramified, and the equivalences of Fedder, Huneke and Huebl for an analytically unramified one-dimensional local domain (the integral closure is local, a nonzero one-fibered ideal exists, every nonzero ideal is one-fibered).
- [P] Page 12: for a Noetherian local analytically irreducible domain `(R, m)` and an `m`-primary ideal `I`, `I` is one-fibered if and only if some integer `b` satisfies `x y in I^(2n+b)` implies `x in I^n` or `y in I^n` for all positive `n` and all `x, y` (Huebl and Swanson). Theorem 3.3 (p. 12, citing a paper of Huebl) gives a sufficient condition for one-fiberedness in an analytically unramified local ring of dimension `d`
  through a positive integer `l` with this property: if `f` lies in `m` outside `I^n`, then there are `g_2, ..., g_d` in `I` with `I^(n+l)` inside `(f, g_2, ..., g_d)` and `v(I^(n+l)) >= v(f)` for all Rees valuations `v` of `I`; then `I` is one-fibered. The printed statement quantifies no `n` and does not introduce `I`.
- [P] Remark 3.4 (p. 12): the quadratic transformation of the localization of `C[X, Y, Z]/(X^2 + Y^2 + Z^c)` at `(X, Y, Z)`, for `c >= 3`, is integrally closed (Lipman proved this for two-dimensional rational singularity rings), yet the maximal ideal has exactly two Rees valuations, so integral closedness of the first blowup does not make the maximal ideal one-fibered. Muhly and Sakuma's lemma (pp. 12-13), for one-fibered ideals `I_1, ..., I_r` with `RV(I_j) = {V_j}` and pairwise inequivalent valuations `v_j` in a two-dimensional universally catenary Noetherian integral domain,
  states that the matrix `(v_i(I_j))` has nonzero determinant.

## Upper bounds on the number of Rees valuations (Section 4, pp. 13-17)

- [P] Proposition 4.1 (p. 13): for a Noetherian local domain `(R, m)` of dimension `d > 1` and an `m`-primary ideal `(x_1, ..., x_d)`, the union over `n` of `RV(x_1^n, x_2, ..., x_d)` is not a finite set, and there is no upper bound on `|RV(I)|` as `I` varies over `m`-primary ideals.
- [P] Monomial ideals (pp. 13-14): for a zero-dimensional monomial ideal in a polynomial or power series ring (or in a regular system of parameters of a regular local ring), the number of Rees valuations is the number of non-coordinate bounding faces of the Newton polyhedron, at most the binomial coefficient of the number of generators over `dim R`. A sharper bound of Miller (p. 14), for `n` generators, `m = n - d - 1` and `d` the dimension,
  is a polynomial in `n` of degree `floor(d/2)` for fixed `d`; its two displayed cases (`d` odd, `d` even) are not reproduced here.
- [P] Reductions for local rings (pp. 14-15): the number of Rees valuations of `I` is at most that of `I R-hat`, a reduction to complete local domains and, after replacing `I` by a power, to a `d`-generated `m`-primary ideal; when `R` contains a field, to `R = k[[X_1, ..., X_d, Z]]/(Z^n + a_1 Z^(n-1) + ... + a_n)` with `a_i` in `(X_1, ..., X_d)^i k[[X_1, ..., X_d]]`
  (p. 15). Proposition 4.2 (p. 15, proof p. 16): with that notation, `|RV((X_1, ..., X_d) R)| <= n`.
- [P] Table (p. 16, with Guerrieri): for `R = k[[X, Y, Z]]/(X^a + Y^b + Z^c)`, `2 <= a <= b <= c`, the table gives the number of Rees valuations of `(X, Y, Z)` for fields `k = R` (the reals) and `k = C`: one for `(2, 2, 2)`, `(2, 3, c >= 3)`, `(2, 4, 4)`, `(2, 5, c >= 5)`, `(2, 6, 6)` and `(3, 3, 3)`; one over the reals and two over `C` for `(2, 2, c >= 3)`, `(2, 4, c >= 5)` and `(2, 6, c >= 7)`;
  two over the reals and three over `C` for `(3, 3, c >= 4)`. Proposition 4.3 (pp. 16-17): for `R = C[[X, Y, Z]]/(X^a + Y^b + Z^c)` or `R = C[X, Y, Z]/(X^a + Y^b + Z^c)` with integers `2 <= a <= b <= c`, the ideal `(X^a, Y^b, Z^c)` has exactly one Rees valuation.

## The Izumi-Rees theorem (Section 5, pp. 17-18)

- [P] Theorem 5.1 (Rees, p. 17): a Noetherian local ring `(R, m)` is analytically irreducible if for at least one `m`-primary ideal `I`, and only if for all `m`-primary ideals `I`, there are constants `C` and `C'`, depending only on `I`, with `ord_I(xy) - ord_I(y) <= C ord_I(x) + C'` for all nonzero `x, y` in `R`.
  Theorem 5.2 (Rees, p. 18): for a complete Noetherian local domain `(R, m)` and divisorial valuations `v, w` centered on `m`, some constant `C` satisfies `v(x) <= C w(x)` for all nonzero `x` in `R`. The page states a stronger version of Huebl and Swanson for analytically irreducible excellent local domains and reports that, after conversations with Izumi, the excellence assumption can be dropped.
- [P] Theorem 5.3 (Huebl and Swanson, p. 18): for a Noetherian local ring `(R, m)` the following are equivalent: `R` is analytically irreducible; integers `a, b` exist with `x y in m^(an+b)` implying `x in m^n` or `y in m^n` for all `n`; for every `m`-primary ideal `I` integers `a, b` exist with `x y in I^(an+b)` implying `x in I^n` or `y in I^n` for all `n`.
  Page 18 also reports consequences for linear equivalence of ideal topologies and for Artin functions (Rond).

## Adjoints of ideals (Section 6, pp. 19-20)

- [P] Definition 6.1 (Lipman, p. 19): for a regular domain `R` with field of fractions `K`, the adjoint `adj I` of an ideal `I` is the intersection over the divisorial valuations `V` with respect to `R` of `{r in K : r J_(V/R) in I V}`, with `J_(V/R)` the Jacobian ideal of `R -> V`. The page states that `adj I` is an integrally closed ideal containing the integral closure of `I`, that `adj(I) = adj(closure(I))`, and that `adj(x I) = x adj(I)` for `x` in `R`.
  Howald's formula for monomial ideals, the extension of Huebl and Swanson to ideals generated by monomials in a regular sequence satisfying a regularity condition on the quotients, and the statement that in a two-dimensional regular local ring the Rees valuations suffice to compute adjoints are reported; in general the Rees valuations do not suffice (p. 19).
  The open question on p. 20 asks, for an `m`-primary ideal `I` of a regular local ring, whether finitely many valuations compute the adjoints of all powers of `I`.
