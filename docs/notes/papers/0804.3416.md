## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Indexing (pp. 2-3). Units carry the indices `0, 1, ..., 2^N - 1` with the product index equal to the XOR of the
  factor indices. Trips are the triples `(a, b, c)` with `a xor b = c` written in cyclic positive order (CPO), the first
  two ordered so that their product is `+` the third. The quaternion rule is `(1, 2, 3)`; the seven octonion trips are
  `(1,2,3), (1,4,5), (1,7,6), (2,4,6), (2,5,7), (3,4,7), (3,6,5)`.
- [P] Rule 0 (p. 2): these seven trips stay associative triplets at every higher level. Rule 1 (p. 2): with `G = 2^N`
  the generator of the `2^(N+1)`-ions, `(L, G, G + L)` is a CPO trip for every `L < G`. Rule 2 (pp. 2-3): from a trip of
  a prior level, hold one index fixed, add `G` to each of the other two and switch their positions; for the octonions this
  yields `(1,3+4,2+4)`, `(3+4,2,1+4)`, `(2+4,1+4,3)`, which rotate to `(1,7,6), (2,5,7), (3,6,5)`.
- [P] Trip count (p. 3): a set of `2^N`-ions has `(2^N - 1)(2^N - 2)/3!` trips: 1, 7, 35 and 155 for the quaternions,
  octonions, sedenions and the 32-dimensional pathions. The 32-ions are named pathions here, and the 64-ions chingons
  (p. 14).
- [P] Sedenion box-kites (pp. 3-6). With `G = 8`, an assessor is a pair of units `(L, U)` with `L < G < U`, whose two
  diagonals `e_L +- e_U` hold the zero divisors; there are 42 assessors (84 diagonals) in seven box-kites of six
  assessors each, indexed by the strut constant `S`. The octahedral box-kite has three struts (non-zero-dividing
  assessor pairs), eight sails (triangles), one zigzag sail with all three edges `[-]` and four vents; the trefoil sails
  are `(a,d,e), (d,b,f), (e,f,c)`. For an assessor `(M, m)` and its strut-opposite, `m xor (G + S) = G + m_opp = M` (p. 8).
- [P] Table 1, the sedenion brocade (p. 9): the 42 cells give, for each pair `(L, U)` with `L in 1..7`, `U in 9..15`,
  the strut constant and vertex letter of its assessor; the long diagonal `L = U - 8` is empty.
- [P] Catamaran twists (pp. 5-7): swapping L- or U-indices between assessors sharing an edge, `(A + a)(B - b) = 0`
  implies `(A + b)(B + a) = 0` in the sedenions, produces assessor pairs of another box-kite; three catamarans per
  box-kite collect all seven sedenion box-kites into one brocade (the Royal Hunt Diagram, Figure 2, p. 7). The
  Roundabout Theorem of reference [2] (Placeholder Substructures II) is quoted as "all edges support zero-divisor
  currents or none do", and the number of box-kites sharing one `S` equals the trip count of the `2^(N-2)`-ions.
- [P] Pathions (pp. 10-13). With `G = 16` there are 15 candidate strut constants times 7 box-kites per emanation table,
  105 slots. Seven are zero-padded sedenion box-kites; seven (`S = 8`) are Type I; Type II box-kites, which have exactly
  two of their three strut orientations reversed, number 21 and come from the trefoil trips of the `S = 8` box-kites by
  Rule 2. Box-kites with `8 < S < 16` are three Type I and four hidden box-kites (HBKs, whose edges carry no zero-divisor
  current) per `S`; each of the 7 sedenion box-kites explodes into one spandrel of four HBKs, 28 in all (p. 13).
- [P] Emanation-table formula (p. 14): with `g` the generator of the `2^(N-1)`-ions, a cell of the fixed-`S` emanation
  table with row label `R`, column label `C` and `P = R xor C` is filled exactly when `R`, `C` or `P` equals `g` or
  `S mod g` (the gist of Theorem 14 of reference [3]). Figure 5 (p. 15) shows the 8 pathion tables for `S = 8..15`;
  the caption separates 24 darker cells from 48 parallel cells. Theorem 15 of reference [3] (p. 12) states that
  zero-dividing L/U pairings stop zero-dividing when `S` gains a new leftmost bit, and Theorem 16 (p. 16) that a second
  augmentation turns the edges on again.
- [P] Cowbird Theorem (p. 18): each hidden box-kite of a Type I or Type II spandrel contains a zero-divisor-free copy
  of the octonions, the "egg", made of the real unit, the L-indexed units of one source sail, their U-index partners in
  the hidden box-kite and one more unit indexed by the spandrel's `X`. For Type I the egg lies exactly in the zigzag sail
  whose L-index set equals that of the source sail. Table 2 (p. 18) lists the four sedenion sails of `S = 1`
  `(3,6,5), (3,4,7), (4,6,2), (7,2,5)` and their pathion U-indices `(26,31,28), (26,29,30), (29,31,27), (30,27,28)`
  at `(S, X, G) = (9, 25, 16)`.
- [P] Lemmas 1 to 7 (pp. 18-20) describe the explosion as a four-node loading of the PSL(2,7) Fano triangle, a Klein
  group of double exchanges, and the invariant that the number of reversed lines is even; Lemma 7 concludes that
  "Type III" and "Type IV" box-kites (one or three struts reversed from the Type I pattern) cannot occur.
- [P] Sections 2 and 3 (pp. 21-28): the proof of the Cowbird Theorem by twist products (pp. 21-23), the flowmorphism
  between Type I and Type II presentations (pp. 24-25) and the semiotic reading (Greimas square, Levi-Strauss bricolage,
  pp. 26-28) carry no further claim about zero divisors; the references on p. 29 include Moreno's `q-alg/9710013`.

## Repository bearing

[I] Zero divisors and box-kites. Table 1 and the strut-constant relation match the retained 16-dimensional census
(`docs/evidence/box_kite_census/census.json`, summarized in the
[monograph inventory](../topics/box-kites/inventory-monographs.md)); the emanation-table formula supplies a closed form
for which zero-divisor pairs exist in dimension 32 for `S > 8`, which the repository's two-term census of dimension 32
(588 zero divisors, annihilator dimensions 4, 8 and 12 in the counts 168, 168 and 252, see the
[sparse census](../topics/cayley_dickson_sparse_census.md)) covers by exact rank. The claim `cd-sign-cocycle-degree` and the
Rocq-checked trip counts of `cd-associator-census-rocq` concern the same XOR-indexed sign cocycle that Rules 0 to 2
describe; the paper's rules are a bitwise presentation of that cocycle, and the trip-set equality above is a finite
confirmation to `N = 8`, not a proof for all `N`.

[I] Dimensions 64 and above. The paper makes the first statements here about the 64-ions (chingons: the seven box-kites
sharing the strut `b = 16`, `e = 1` at `S = 17`, p. 14) and the recursion of hiding and filling to arbitrarily large `N`
(Theorems 15 and 16 of [3], p. 16). These are transcribed from the paper, not recomputed beyond the trip checks above.

[I] Stiefel question (`math/0512517` Section 5) and the claims `a5-stiefel-universal`, `a6-stiefel-witness`: none; the
paper treats two-term zero divisors and does not discuss Stiefel elements.
