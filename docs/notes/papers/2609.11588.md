## Transcribed statements

- [P] Convention (p. 3, Eq. 2.1): `A_0 = R`, `A_n = A_(n-1) + A_(n-1)` with `(a, b)(c, d) = (ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))` and
  `conj((a, b)) = (conj(a), -b)`; the standard basis gives the Euclidean inner product. `V_n = Im(A_n)`.
- [P] Lemma 2.1 (p. 4): `xy` conjugates to `conj(y) conj(x)` and `x conj(x) = conj(x) x = |x|^2`; for imaginary `u`, `u^2 = -|u|^2`.
  Definition 2.2 and Lemma 2.3 (p. 4): `u x_n v = (uv - vu)/2` is bilinear, alternating and imaginary, and `uv = -<u, v> + u x_n v`.
  Proposition 2.4 (p. 5): `(alpha + X)(beta + Y) = (alpha beta - <X, Y>) + alpha Y + beta X + X x_n Y`; only the alternating product
  needs a recursive evaluation.
- [P] Proposition 3.1 (p. 5): for `X = (u, gamma + s)`, `Y = (v, delta + t)` in `V_n` with `u, v, s, t` in `V_(n-1)` and real `gamma, delta`,
  `X x_n Y = (D + gamma t - delta s, <s, v> - <t, u> + delta u - gamma v - H)` with `D = u x v - s x t` and `H = u x t + s x v`.
  Remark 3.2 (p. 6): this is the coordinate form of the Brown-Hopkins trace-zero commutator recursion (Brown and Hopkins,
  Comm. Algebra 23 (1995)), which the paper credits for the structure.
- [P] Theorem 3.3 (p. 7), the two-call formula: over `C = R(iota)` with `iota^2 = -1` central and distinct from coefficientwise
  conjugation, with `Z+- = C_(n-1)(u +- iota s, v +- iota t)` and `lambda = <s, v>_bil - <t, u>_bil`,
  `C_n(X, Y) = ((Z+ + Z-)/2 + gamma t - delta s, lambda + delta u - gamma v + iota (Z+ - Z-)/2)`. Corollary 3.4 (p. 8): for real
  input one complex call suffices, `X x_n Y = (Re Z + gamma t - delta s, lambda + delta u - gamma v - Im Z)`. Algorithm 3.5 and
  Proposition 3.6 (pp. 9-10) state the full procedure and its correctness; `C_1` is identically zero.
- [P] Theorem 4.1 (p. 10): `O(N log N)` real operations; Theorem 4.2 (p. 11): the real bilinear rank of the multiplication
  tensor of `A_n` is at most `(9n - 15) 2^(n-1) + 10`, with `C_n = (9n - 21) 2^(n-1) + 12` for the alternating product; Proposition 4.3
  (p. 12): `O(N)` auxiliary storage. Proposition 4.4 (p. 13) and Table 1 (p. 15): exact counts for `n >= 3`,
  `M_QL = (9n - 15) N/2 + 10`, `A_QL = (23n - 61) N/2 + 36`, `S_QL = (2n - 7) N/2 + 4`, `T_QL = (34n - 83) N/2 + 50`; Table 1
  lists `N = 2` to `1024`, for example `M = 58, 178, 490, 1258, 3082, 7306` at `N = 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256`, against direct
  multiplication `N^2` and the Cariow-Cariowa count `N(N - 1)/2 + 2`.
- [P] Section 5 (pp. 14-17), Table 2: the C11 library fastCD and a NumPy interface; on one core of an Intel Xeon Gold 6148 the
  quasilinear method has the lowest mean time at every `N >= 32` and is about 16 times faster than direct multiplication at
  `N = 1024` (17.5 against 274.5 microseconds per product). The agreement criterion is `|p_b - p_ref|_inf <= 128 N eps |x| |y|`
  with `eps = 2^(-52)`.
- [P] Section 6 (p. 17): no optimality claim or lower bound; open problems are a forward-error analysis, cache-aware scheduling
  and application tests.

## Repository bearing

[I] Sign cocycle and dimension 32 and above. The algorithm evaluates the same product as the repository's table-free
`cd_sign` and reaches `N = 256` in this replay with a recursion that uses no sign table; `cd-sign-row-degree` and
`cd-sign-cocycle-degree` (algebraic degree of the sign rows and cocycle) concern the table of signs, which the algorithm
bypasses by recursion over the imaginary subspace. The paper offers a classical-simulation cost model for Cayley-Dickson
products (`O(N log N)` rather than `N^2`); the repository's estimator batching (see the
[vectorized-fit runbook](../../runbooks/vectorized-fits.md)) is a separate numerical concern and the paper adds no
statement on floating-point error (Section 6 lists it as open).

[I] Zero divisors, annihilator dimensions, Stiefel elements, automorphisms and box-kites: none; the algorithm is
agnostic to zero divisors ("no associativity, alternativity or norm multiplicativity", p. 3). Moreno's Stiefel question
(`math/0512517` Section 5): none. Dimensions 64 and above: the replay covers `N = 64, 128, 256`; the paper's benchmarks reach
`N = 1024`.

## Boundaries

[I] The fastCD source, the benchmark data and the Cariow-Cariowa comparison counts were not read or replayed; only the
algebraic algorithm and the multiplication count were replayed. The `fastCD` and benchmark claims are transcribed.
The paper is a single-author preprint that discloses substantial use of generative AI; the exact replay above confirms
correctness of the algorithm, not of the benchmark claims.

### What changed from version 1 to version 2

- [P] Scope. Version 1 treats the real tower. Version 2 (Eq. 2.1, p. 4) works over a unital commutative ring `R` with `2` a
  unit and doubling parameters `mu_k` in `R^x`, with product `(a, b)(c, d) = (ac + mu_n conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))`; the standard
  real tower is `R = R`, `mu_k = -1`. The quadratic form is `q_n(a, b) = q_(n-1)(a) - mu_n q_(n-1)(b)` with coordinate weights
  `w_n = (w_(n-1), -mu_n w_(n-1))`. The algorithm needs a fixed commutative `R`-algebra `S`, finitely generated as an
  `R`-module, with elements `rho_k`, `rho_k^2 = mu_k` (`k >= 3`), and an `R`-linear map `pi : S -> R` with `pi(1) = 1`
  (Theorem 1.1, p. 3).
- [P] Statements renumbered or replaced. The two-call formula is Theorem 3.3 in both versions; version 2 states it for `n >= 3`
  with `Z+- = C_(n-1)(u +- rho_n s, v +- rho_n t)`, result `((Z+ + Z-)/2 + mu_n (delta s - gamma t), lambda + delta u - gamma v - (Z+ -
  Z-)/(2 rho_n))` (Eq. 3.7), where version 1 stated it over `C` for `n >= 2` with `iota`. For `mu = -1`, `rho = iota` the two forms agree
  by substitution, because `-1/(2 iota) = iota/2`. Corollary 3.4 changes from "Specialization to real coefficients" to the
  "One-call root" with projections `pi_0, pi_1 : S -> R`, defined for `n >= 3`. The explicit level-two formula (six products,
  p. 9) is a new base case, and the recursion also admits a "specified higher base case". The nonrecursive bilinear map `B_n`
  (Eq. 3.8) and its evaluation through the coefficient multiplication table (Eqs. 3.9 and 3.10) are new.
- [P] Algorithm 3.5 keeps its number. It now has three procedures `C_n`, `R_n` and `M_n` as in version 1, gains the base-case
  dispatch, evaluates the nonrecursive terms through `B_n^S`, and `R_n` offers the one-call root when `pi_0, pi_1` are given.
  Proposition 3.6 (Correctness) keeps its number and now covers the general rings.
- [P] Section 4. Version 1 stated Theorem 4.1 (`O(N log N)` real operations), Theorem 4.2 (real bilinear rank at most `(9n - 15)
  2^(n-1) + 10`), Proposition 4.3 (`O(N)` storage) and Proposition 4.4 (exact counts). Version 2 states Theorem 4.1 (arithmetic
  counts in the constants `kappa_A`, `kappa_E` and `beta_S`), Corollary 4.2 (`O(n 2^n) = O(N log N)` operations in `R`),
  Corollary 4.3 (bilinear complexity `R_R(T_(A_n)) <= (3 beta_S (n - 3) + 6) 2^n + 12 beta_S - 8`, and the one-call-root bound with
  `(3 beta_S (n - 3) + 12) 2^(n-1) + 6 beta_S - 8`), Corollary 4.4 and Proposition 4.5 (`O(N)` auxiliary storage).
  Corollary 4.4 is new: for a field `F` of characteristic not 2 with square roots of every `mu_k` in a finite extension `K`,
  the bounds hold with `S = K`; with `K = F(sqrt(epsilon))`, `d = 2` and `beta_S = 3` (Karatsuba-type formula), the tensor rank
  satisfies `R_F(T_(A_n)) <= (9n - 21) 2^n + 28` for `n >= 2` over `R` and over every finite field of odd characteristic, and `(9n - 15)
  2^(n-1) + 10` (Eq. 4.7) when `mu_n` is a non-square (for the standard real tower, every real tower with `mu_n < 0`, and over `F_q` with
  `mu_k = -1` exactly when `q = 3 mod 4`). The version 1 rank bound is the case (4.7) of this corollary.
- [P] Arithmetic counts (Section 5.1, Table 1). Version 2 adds an octonion base case for `N >= 16` to the real schedule, and cites a
  separate technical note for the schedule and the proof of the counts, where version 1 derived the counts in
  Proposition 4.4. Quasilinear input-dependent multiplications `M` for `N = 2, 4, ..., 1024` read 4, 16, 58, 160, 454, 1186, 2938,
  7018, 16330, 37258 (version 1: 4, 16, 58, 178, 490, 1258, 3082, 7306, 16906, 38410); additions `A` read 2, 12, 62, 242, 762, 2106,
  5402, 13210, 31258, 72218 (version 1: 2, 12, 68, 284, 900, 2500, 6436, 15780, 37412, 86564); totals `T` read 6, 28, 120, 417,
  1274, 3468, 8816, 21432, 50504, 116328 (version 1: 6, 28, 126, 474, 1442, 3922, 9970, 24242, 57138, 131634). The direct and
  Cariow-Cariowa columns are unchanged. For `N <= 8` the `M` entries equal the bound (4.7); for `N >= 16` they fall below it.
- [P] Implementation and benchmark (Sections 5.2 and 5.3, Table 2). fastCD adds an `AUTO` setting that selects an algorithm per
  dimension and per mode (single or batched products): direct multiplication at `N = 8, 16` for single products and `N = 8` for
  batches, Cariow-Cariowa at `N = 16` for batches, quasilinear otherwise. The protocol states 10,000 random input pairs per
  dimension with operand norms in `[0.5, 2)`, 24 subprocess blocks, one core of an Intel Xeon Gold 6148 at 2.40 GHz, GCC 16.2.1 with
  `-O3 -DNDEBUG -fno-fast-math -ffp-contract=off`, SIMD batches at `16 <= N <= 512`, and the agreement bound `|p_b - p_ref|_inf <= 128 N
  eps |x|_2 |y|_2` with `eps = 2^(-52)`. At `N = 1024` the mean times are 15713.75 ns (quasilinear) against 270678.46 ns (direct) for
  single products and 15426.99 ns against 272762.00 ns for batches, ratios of 17.2 and 17.7 (version 1 reported 17.5 and
  274.5 microseconds, a ratio of 15.7). The paper states that Cariow-Cariowa uses fewer multiplications than the quasilinear
  schedule at several small dimensions and cautions that its timings do not show the method to be generally slower.
- [P] Sections 6 and 7. The outlook adds the questions whether the logarithmic overhead can be removed, whether specialized
  low-dimension base cases reduce the constants, and whether specific fields or rings admit a faster method; the forward-error
  analysis, cache-aware scheduling, accelerator ports and application tests of version 1 remain open. Section 7.1 introduces the
  TANUKI-CD program. Section 7.2 names the generative AI models used and narrates how the extension to a fixed coefficient algebra
  arose; Section 7.3 declares that the author develops fastCD and founded the program; the acknowledgements credit a suggestion
  to generalize to arbitrary fields. The code is hosted at a tanuki-cd.com Git instance with a GitHub mirror, at a pinned revision.

  Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 3.6 by substitution. The version 1 table of `M` counts equals the bound (4.7) at every
