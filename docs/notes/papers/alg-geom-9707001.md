## Setting and conventions

- [P] Introduction (p. 1): `Y` a smooth complex projective subvariety of `P^n` of codimension `r`; `d_Y` is the least degree `d` such that `Y` is a scheme-theoretic intersection of hypersurfaces of degree at most `d`, and `e_Y` is the least integer `e` such that `H^i(P^n, I_Y^k(p)) = 0` for all `i > 0`, `k >= 0` and `p >= e + (k - 1) d_Y`. Theorem 1 (p. 1, from Bertram, Ein and Lazarsfeld) gives `e_Y <= d_1 + ... + d_r - n` when `Y` is cut out by equations of degrees `d_Y = d_1 >= ... >= d_m`.
- [P] Complete skew forms, classical construction (p. 13): for a vector space `V` with `l = [dim(V)/2]` and `U = P(wedge^2 V) - Delta_l`, the rational map `P(wedge^2 V) --> P(wedge^4 V) x ... x P(wedge^(2l) V)`, `alpha -> (alpha ^ alpha, alpha ^ alpha ^ alpha ^ alpha, ...)`. The degeneracy locus `Delta_i` is the locus of indeterminacy of the component into `P(wedge^(2i) V)`. The closure `P` of the graph of this map restricted to `U` is the space of complete skew forms on `V`.
- [P] Remark (p. 13): the map is a "square root" of the even part of the wedge map. The value of `wedge^(2i) alpha` on a decomposable wedge is the determinant of a principal minor of the skew form `alpha: V -> V*`, hence a square, with the Pfaffian as its square root. The Pfaffians of the `2i x 2i` principal minors give the linear series of the rational map `P(wedge^2 V) --> P(wedge^(2i) V)`, and their common zero scheme is reduced and equals `Delta_i`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 3.3 (p. 14): let `X = bl(P(wedge^2 V), G(2, V*))`, blow up the other degeneracy loci in order as before (`X_1 := X`, `f_i: X_i -> X_(i-1)` the blow-up of the strict transform of `Delta_(i+1)`, `f = f_2 o ... o f_(l-1)`), and let `E_i` be the strict transform of the `f_i`-exceptional divisor for `1 <= i <= l - 1`. Then (a) each strict transform of `Delta_(i+2)` in `X_i` is smooth, so `X-tilde` is smooth, and `E_1 + ... + E_(l-1)` is a normal crossings divisor on `X-tilde`; (b) the inclusion `U -> P` extends to an isomorphism `X-tilde -> P`. The proof reads "See the appendix."
- [P] Proposition 3.4 and Corollary 3.5 (p. 14): for `k = dim V`, general linear combinations of the `2i x 2i` principal Pfaffians give a log resolution of `(X, F^g)` with explicit discrepancies; with `n_l = 1` for even `k`, `n_l = 3` for odd `k` and `n_i = 4` otherwise, the pair `(X, F^g)` is log canonical and `F^g` is numerically `(n - 3) H - r E`. The paper concludes `e_Y <= -3` "in case (c)" through its Proposition 2.1.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The repository's kernel-coordinate ideal is a non-maximal Pfaffian ideal restricted to a subvariety of the skew-matrix space `L_a`. The closure of the graph of the single rational map given by its generating Pfaffians is the blow-up of that ideal, and it is the image of `P` under the projection to `P(wedge^2 V) x P(wedge^(2i) V)`. The exceptional fiber of the universal single-ideal blow-up over a point of a smaller rank stratum is then a Grassmannian of kernel planes, and `Proj F(I)` at a point of the orbit closure is a closed subset of that container ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)). Theorem 3.3 is the container statement; it supplies no equation of the fiber cone.
- [I] The paper contains no stopping bound for the equations of a fiber cone. Corollary 3.5 bounds cohomology of powers of the ideal of a Grassmannian in a projective embedding (`e_Y`), a statement about a generic `V` and not about a restriction.
- [I] The paper works over the complex numbers (p. 1) and the appendix over an algebraically closed field; the repository's computations run over `F_32009`.
