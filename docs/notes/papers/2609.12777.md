## Statements

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): average-case hardness of Betti number estimation on random clique complexes by reduction from planted clique. Under the classical planted-clique conjecture no randomized polynomial-time estimator reaches additive error below 1/2 with constant advantage; under a new quantum planted-clique conjecture the same holds for quantum polynomial-time algorithms.
- [P] Section 1 (p. 3): the efficiency guarantee of the Lloyd-Garnerone-Zanardi normalized-Betti-number algorithm rests on three promises: a clique-dense complex, a Laplacian spectral gap at least inverse polynomial, and a normalized Betti number that is not exponentially small.
- [P] Section 1 (p. 3): computing Betti numbers of a clique complex exactly is #P-hard and approximating them within a multiplicative factor is NP-hard (citing Schmidhuber and Lloyd); the sharpest estimation calibration cited is that estimating low-lying spectral density of a succinctly specified sparse Hermitian matrix is DQC1-hard.
- [P] Section 1 (p. 3): Schmidhuber and Lloyd observed that generic complexes have normalized Betti numbers so small that the estimate carries no information where state preparation is easiest.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The nullity of a matrix is the dimension of a kernel, the same object as a Betti number of a Laplacian. Quantum nullity estimators return the normalized nullity (kernel dimension over matrix dimension) to additive error. A 32 x 32 left-multiplication matrix has normalized nullity 4/32 to 12/32, easily resolved, and its exact nullity costs microseconds classically; the fiber program's truncated-rank matrices need exact integer ranks over F_p, which a normalized additive-error estimator does not return.
- [I] Supports the standing finding that no quantum algorithm beats exact elimination for F_p ranks at the program's sizes, through the access-model and exactness mismatch, not through a rank lower bound.
