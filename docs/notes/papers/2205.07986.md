## Main claims

- [P] The algebra D(n) doubles by (a + b g_n)(c + d g_n) = (ac - conj(d) b) + (da + b
  conj(c)) g_n (Eq. 2.1, read from the rendered page 3; the text extraction drops the
  overbars), and e_A is the left-to-right product of the generators
  g_i^(a_i) (Definition 1), so e_A e_B = (-1)^sigma(A, B) e_(A XOR B).
- [P] Proposition 1 derives sigma(A + a_n 2^n, B + b_n 2^n) = sigma(A, B)(1 + b_n) +
  sigma(B, A) b_n + sigma(B, B) a_n + a_n b_n from Eqs. 2.5, 2.10 and 2.12, crediting the
  recursion to Albuquerque and Majid.
- [P] Theorem 1: with deg A the lowest set bit of A (Definition 4) and l = max(deg A,
  deg B, deg(A XOR B)), sigma vanishes when AB = 0, equals 1 when A = B != 0, and for
  distinct nonzero A, B equals b_l + c, 1 + a_l + c or a_l + c as deg A >, < or = deg B,
  where c = sum_(i = l)^(n-1) phi(a_i, b_i) and phi(a, b) = a + b + ab.
- [P] Section 5 gives the split Cayley-Dickson twist through the relation between the two
  algebras. No degree, Walsh spectrum or ANF statement appears.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Convention map: sigma_RZ(A, B) = [cd_sign(2^n, A, B) = -1] = phi_n(A, B), the
  untransposed convention, so the Proposition 1 recursion is Corollary 4.3 of
  Albuquerque and Majid with its arguments exchanged, F_AM(a, b) = cd_sign(b, a)
  ([math-9802116.md](../math-9802116.md)). `check_closed_form` in
  [cd_phase_degree.py](../../../scripts/cd_phase_degree.py) (gate core-320) requires
  Theorem 1 to equal the cd_sign table on every pair through n = 8 and its transpose to
  differ from n = 2 on.
- [I] Theorem 1 states the sign rows in closed form and no degree. The exact degrees of
  [cd_phase_degree.md](../cd_phase_degree.md), n - 1 for every left row, the leading layer
  sum_j m_j prod_(l != j) x_l and max(1, bitlen(m) - 2) for the right rows, are not stated
  there; the closed form makes the OR-shaped dependence on the bits above the lowest set
  bit visible, which the degree proofs reach through the recursion instead.
- [I] Bales 2011 defines twisted group algebras by a sign function on a group and
  constructs the Cayley-Dickson and Clifford twists; Flaut and Boboescu build the basis
  products of the Cayley-Dickson algebras by an algorithm on the twist tree and prove the
  twisted group algebra structure (Theorem 2.8). Neither states a degree, ANF or Walsh
  property of the sign, within the scope read.
