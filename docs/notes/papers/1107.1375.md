## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page
indices. Binary literals `pq` denote the bitwise exclusive or of `p` and `q` (Section 8, p. 10).

- [P] Definition 2.1 (p. 2): for a group `G`, unit basis vectors `{i_p | p in G}` and a sign function
  `alpha : G x G -> {-1, 1}`, "`i_p i_q = alpha(p, q) i_(pq)`", extended to linear combinations by
  distribution (Definition 2.2). Notation 2.3 writes `[G, alpha, F]` for the twisted group algebra.
- [P] Definition 3.3 (p. 3): "If `alpha(p, q) alpha(pq, r) = alpha(p, qr) alpha(q, r)` for `p, q, r` in `G`,
  then `alpha` is said to be an associative twist on `G`." Theorem 3.4 (p. 3): an associative twist
  gives `i_p (i_q i_r) = (i_p i_q) i_r`.
- [P] Definition 4.1 (p. 5): a twist is proper when `alpha(p, q) alpha(q, q^-1) = alpha(pq, q^-1)` and
  `alpha(p^-1, p) alpha(p, q) = alpha(p^-1, pq)`.
- [P] Section 6 (p. 9), the Cayley-Dickson construction: `S_0 = R`, `S_(n+1) = S_n x S_n`, with
  `(a, b)(c, d) = (ac - d b*, a* d + c b)` and `(a, b)* = (a*, -b)`.
- [P] Section 7 (p. 10): the basis is numbered by shuffling the pair, `i_(2n) = (i_n, 0)` and
  `i_(2n+1) = (0, i_n)`; the paper notes that this "produces a different numbering from those in common
  use for octonions and upwards".
- [P] Section 8 (p. 10): the group is `G = {0, 1, 2, ...}` under bitwise exclusive or with `G_n = {p | 0 <= p < 2^n}`,
  and the product satisfies `(2p)(2q) = 2pq`, `(2p)(2q+1) = 2pq+1`, `(2p+1)(2q) = 2pq+1`, `(2p+1)(2q+1) = 2pq`.
- [P] Theorem 8.1 (p. 10): "There is a twist `gamma(p, q)` mapping `G x G` into `{-1, 1}` such that if
  `p, q` in `G`, then `i_p i_q = gamma(p, q) i_(pq)`." The proof is an induction on `n`.
- [P] Corollary 8.2 (p. 12), the requisite properties of the sign function: (1) `gamma(0, 0) = 1`;
  (2) `gamma(2r, 2s) = gamma(r, s)`; (3) if `r != 0` then `gamma(2r, 2s+1) = -gamma(r, s)`;
  (4) `gamma(0, 2s+1) = gamma(0, s) = 1`; (5) `gamma(2r+1, 2s) = gamma(s, r)`; (6) if `r != 0` then
  `gamma(2r+1, 2s+1) = gamma(s, r)`; (7) `gamma(1, 2s+1) = -gamma(s, 0) = -1`.
- [P] Theorems 8.3 and 8.4 (p. 12): for `0 != p != q != 0`, `gamma(p, p) = -1`,
  `gamma(p, q) = -gamma(q, p)` and `gamma(p, q) = gamma(q, pq) = gamma(pq, p)`; and `gamma` is a proper twist.

## Main claims

- [P] A twisted group algebra is a group, a basis indexed by it and a sign function; the Cayley-Dickson
  and Clifford algebras are examples (abstract, p. 1).
- [P] The Cayley-Dickson twist `gamma` exists and obeys the seven recursion rules of Corollary 8.2,
  from which `i_9 i_11 = -i_2` is worked out as an example (p. 12).
- [P] The abstract conjectures that the Hilbert space `l^2` of square summable sequences is a
  Cayley-Dickson algebra; Section 9 tests the conjecture.
- [P] No degree, polynomial, algebraic normal form or Walsh statement about `gamma` appears (full-text
  search of the PDF).

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Bales 2011 defines twisted group algebras by a sign function on a group and constructs the
  Cayley-Dickson and Clifford twists; it states no degree, ANF or Walsh property of the sign, within
  the scope read.
- [I] The convention differs from [Ren and Zhao](2205.07986.md) in two ways: the product of Section 6
  differs from Eq. 2.1 there, and Section 7 places the newest doubling on the lowest index bit, where
  Ren and Zhao place it on the highest. Evaluating the recursion of Corollary 8.2 and comparing it with
  `cd_sign` of [cd_core.py](../../../gororoba_quantum/cd_core.py) for n = 2 to 5 gives
  `gamma(p, q) = cd_sign(2^n, rev(q), rev(p))`, with `rev` the n-bit reversal; the check was a one-off
  comparison and no gate carries it.
