## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page
indices. The stabilizer generators of the N-qubit GHZ state are written S_k, and tilde versions mark
the paper's own notation for the witness-building operators.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): "We investigate how stabilizer theory can be used for constructing sufficient
  conditions for entanglement"; the witnesses "require only a small effort for an experimental
  implementation and are robust against noise".
- [P] Eq. 1 (p. 1): an observable S_k is a stabilizing operator of |psi> if S_k |psi> = |psi>.
- [P] Eqs. 2 and 3 (p. 2): for |GHZ_N> = (|0>^N + |1>^N)/sqrt(2), the generators are
  S_1 = X^(1) X^(2) ... X^(N) and S_k = Z^(k-1) Z^(k) for k = 2, ..., N.
- [P] Eq. 6 (p. 2): an entanglement witness W has Tr(W rho) >= 0 for all separable states and
  Tr(W rho) < 0 for some entangled states.
- [P] Theorem 1, Eqs. 11 and 12 (p. 3): W_m = 1 - S_1 - S_m (m = 2, ..., N) detects entanglement
  around the GHZ state; the proof uses the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
  <X^(i)>^2 + <Z^(i)>^2 <= 1 to give <S_1> + <S_m> <= 1 for pure product states, and "the bound is
  also valid for mixed separable states". The measurement needs two local settings, X...X and Z...Z.
- [P] Eq. 13 (p. 3): the witness 1 - S_1 - S_m - S_1 S_m "rule[s] out full separability", proved the
  same way with <X>^2 + <Y>^2 + <Z>^2 <= 1; it needs three local settings, X...X, Y...Y and Z...Z.
- [P] Eqs. 14 to 16 (p. 3): for the state p_noise 1/2^N + (1 - p_noise)|Psi><Psi|, the witness of Eq. 11
  detects entanglement for p_noise < 1/2 and the witness of Eq. 13 for p_noise < 2/3; Eq. 11 is
  optimal among stabilizer witnesses with two correlation terms, Eq. 13 among those with three.
- [P] Eq. 19 (p. 4): the projector-based witness for genuine N-qubit entanglement is
  W~ = (1/2) 1 - |GHZ_N><GHZ_N|; "the constant 1/2 corresponds to the maximal squared overlap between
  the GHZ state and the pure biseparable states". Its expectation is -1/2 only for the GHZ state.
- [P] Eq. 20 (p. 4): the projector decomposes over the stabilizer generators as
  |GHZ_N><GHZ_N| = product over k of (S_k + 1)/2, so the projector-based witness is itself a stabilizer
  witness, and "the number of settings needed seems to increase exponentially with the number of
  qubits".
- [P] Eq. 46 (p. 8): the fidelity of an intended GHZ state is F := Tr(|GHZ_N><GHZ_N| rho).

## Derivation for N = 2 (repository interpretation)

- [I] For N = 2 the GHZ state is the Bell state (|00> + |11>)/sqrt(2), with S_1 = XX and S_2 = ZZ.
  Their product is XZ tensor XZ = (-iY) tensor (-iY) = -YY. Eq. 20 then gives
  |Phi+><Phi+| = (1 + XX)(1 + ZZ)/4 = (1 + XX - YY + ZZ)/4, so
  F = (1 + <XX> - <YY> + <ZZ>)/4. This form is derived here from Eqs. 3 and 20; the paper prints no N = 2
  case.
- [I] For two qubits the biseparable states are the separable states, so the constant 1/2 of Eq. 19
  is the largest overlap of a product state with the Bell state, and <W~> < 0 reads F > 1/2, that is
  <XX> - <YY> + <ZZ> > 1. Eq. 13 at N = 2 is 1 - XX - ZZ + YY = 4 W~, the same condition. The
  white-noise limit p_noise < 2/3 of Eq. 13 matches the condition F = (1 - p) + p/4 > 1/2 on a
  Bell state mixed with the maximally mixed state.
- [I] The two-setting witness of Eq. 11 at N = 2, <XX> + <ZZ> <= 1 for separable states, needs the
  XX and ZZ settings only and tolerates p_noise < 1/2; the Y setting buys the larger noise tolerance
  at the price of a third setting, as the paper states for general N.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Tuna-17 fixed-pair entanglement witness. A fixed pair of Tuna-17 qubits prepared as a Bell state
  and read in the three Pauli settings XX, YY and ZZ gives F through the N = 2 form above; the bound
  F > 1/2 is sound on raw counts under symmetric, independent bit-flip readout with probability e,
  because each correlator scales by (1 - 2e)^2 and F_meas = 1/4 + (1 - 2e)^2 (F - 1/4) <= F, so
  readout error costs detection power and creates no false detection. Asymmetric readout
  (p(0|1) different from p(1|0)) adds a positive bias to the sum <XX> - <YY> + <ZZ>, and a
  readout-mitigated F inherits the calibration's error, so a reported witness value names which
  readout treatment it used (no design document for this witness exists in this tree; the device is
  described in [backends.md](../quantum_inspire/backends.md)). The scaling is derived here and the
  paper does not state it.
- [I] The coherence-reconstruction settings XX, XY, YX and YY of the two-qubit case in
  [algebra_circuit_rules.md](../algebra_circuit_rules.md) overlap the witness's XX and YY settings, so a
  dataset acquired for one reads both after adding ZZ.

## Boundaries

[I] The paper's witnesses detect entanglement; they do not bound the fidelity of a non-Bell target.
The N = 2 case is a specialization of N-qubit statements and was not checked against the paper's
appendices. The readout-error remark in the mapping is repository reasoning and has no counterpart in
the paper.
