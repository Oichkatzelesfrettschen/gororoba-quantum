## Setting and conventions

- [P] `R` a Noetherian ring, `E` a finitely generated torsionfree `R`-module of rank `e` with a fixed embedding
  `f : E subset G = R^e`. The divisors of `E` are ideals attached to it: the Fitting ideals `F_i(E)` and `F_i(G/E)`, the order
  determinant `det_0(E) = det_f(E)` (the image of `wedge^e f` in `wedge^e R^e = R`, isomorphic to `wedge^e E / tau_R(wedge^e E)`; the paper also
  writes it `det_0(E)` because it does not depend on `f`), the norm `[[E]]_R` of Villamayor and the (generic) Bourbaki ideals `I(E)` of Simis, Ulrich and
  Vasconcelos. Section 2 shows `det_0(E) = F_0(G/E)` is a representative of `[[E]]_R`.
- [P] The Rees algebra of `E` is `R(E) = Sym(E) / tau_R(Sym(E))`; its `n`-th graded piece is `E^n`. For `(R, m, k)` local the fiber
  cone is `F(E) = R(E) tensor k = sum_n E^n / m E^n`, the Krull dimension of `F(E)` is the analytic spread `ell(E)`, and `ell(wedge^e E)`
  is the quantity of the Simis-Smith-Ulrich form of Zak's inequality.
- [P] A reduction `U subset E` of the same rank; a minimal reduction has `mu(U) = ell(E)` when `k` is infinite. `E` is an
  ideal module if `E subset G` with `grade G/E >= 2`. A generic Bourbaki ideal `I_U(E)` is a rank one quotient `E'' / F` over a
  Nagata extension `R'' = R(Z)` with `F` free of rank `e - 1`; it requires `G_2` and `grade E/U >= 2`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Proposition 2.3 (p. 8): for `R` local and `E subset G = R^e` torsionfree of rank `e > 0` and not free,
  `ell(wedge^e E) >= ht F_0(G/E)`; for an ideal module, `ell(wedge^e E) >= ht F_e(E)`. Proposition 2.4: a module `E_1` of
  projective dimension 1 and rank `e` with `E_1 / tau(E_1) = E` exists and `ell(wedge^e E) >= ht F_e(E_1)`. Corollary 2.5: for
  `proj dim E = 1`, `ell(wedge^e E) >= ht F_e(E)`.
- [P] Proposition 2.6: `F_0(G/E) subset F_e(E)`, with equality for ideal modules of projective dimension 1. Proposition 2.7: `E`
  free iff `F_0(G/E)` is principal generated by a nonzerodivisor iff `det_0(E)` is invertible. Corollary 2.8 prints
  `V(det_0(E) det_0(E)^(-1)) = Spec R \ V(F_e(E))`. As printed, the right side is the free locus: for `E = (x, y)` in
  `R = k[x, y]`, `F_1(E) = (x, y)`, the non-free locus is the origin, and `det_0(E) det_0(E)^(-1) = (x, y)`. The proof's
  equivalence (`E_p` free iff `det_0(E)_p` invertible) gives non-free locus `V(det_0(E) det_0(E)^(-1)) = V(F_e(E))`, so the
  printed complement reads as a misprint. Proposition 2.9 and Theorem 2.10 (p. 11): for reduced `R`, the blow up
  `pi : X -> Spec R` with center `det_0(E)` makes `pi^* E / tor` locally free of rank `e` and is universal among birational
  `gamma : Y -> Spec R` with that property (universal flattening, an alternative to Villamayor's norm construction and to the
  Nash transformation of Oneto and Zatini).
- [P] Section 3: Proposition 3.1 (for `proj dim = 1` modules, `U` is a reduction of `E` iff `F_e(U)` is a reduction of `F_e(E)`),
  Proposition 3.3 (the generators of a minimal reduction are linearly independent mod `mE`, `mu(E) = mu(U) + mu(E/U)`) and
  Corollary 3.4 (a reduction is not contained in `mE`).
- [P] Section 4: Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.4 compare `E` with the quotient `Ebar = E''/F_(e-1)`: `mu(Ebar) = mu(E) - e + 1`, `Ebar` is free
  iff `E` is free, finite projective dimensions agree, and a minimal reduction of `E` yields one of `Ebar` with
  `mu = ell`.
- [P] Section 5: Theorem 5.3 and Corollary 5.4 identify a generic Bourbaki ideal with the Fitting ideal `I_(n-e)(psi)` of a
  submatrix `psi` of a presentation of `E''`; when `E` is not free and `grade I_(n-e)(psi) >= 2`, `I` is perfect of grade 2 with
  resolution `0 -> R''^(n-e) -> R''^(n-e+1) -> I -> 0`. Proposition 5.5: `proj dim E = 1` iff a (equivalently every good) generic
  Bourbaki ideal is perfect of grade 2. Proposition 5.7 (p. 23): if `mu(E) = e + 1` then `E` is of the principal class, and if
  `ell(E) = e + 1` with `k` infinite then `E` is equimultiple; in both cases `grade F_e(E) = ht F_e(E) = 2 = ht I = grade I`.
- [P] Section 6: Proposition 6.1, `[[E tensor S]]_S = [[E]]_R tensor S` for flat `R -> S`; Proposition 6.2, `I = I_(n-e)(psi) subset
  I_(n-e)(rho) subset F_e(E'')` with `I_(n-e)(rho)` the norm `det_0(E) R''`, with equality of the last inclusion for `proj dim E
  = 1`; Corollary 6.3 (p. 26), for a contracted torsionfree module of rank `e` over a 2-dimensional regular local ring there is a
  good generic Bourbaki ideal `I = F_e(E'') = [[E'']] = det_0(E) R''`, and for integrally closed `E` the Rees algebra of `E` is
  Cohen-Macaulay.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The repository's fiber cone `F(I)` of an ideal of the A_5 strata ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md))
  is the case `e = 1` of this paper's `F(E)`, with `E = I` an ideal module and `ell(I)` the dimension of the exceptional fiber.
  The results of Section 2 are lower bounds on `ell`, while the repository's "germ dimension bound on the fiber" column holds
  upper bounds on the fiber dimension; the two do not compare directly.
- [I] For `e = 1` the paper's Fitting ideals `F_1(I)` and the order determinant `det_0(I)` reduce to statements about `I`
  and its Fitting ideals; this specialization was not derived here, and no lower bound on the repository fiber dimension beyond
  the standard `ell(I) >= ht I` is claimed.
- [I] The blow up at `det_0(E)` with its universal flattening property (Theorem 2.10) is the module analogue of the blow-up of
  `Y` along the kernel-coordinate ideal, whose exceptional fiber the repository studies; the repository's kernel 8-planes along the
  limiting directions are a flattening datum of the same kind. A formal identification needs `E` to be the kernel module, which this
  note does not establish.
- [I] The generic Bourbaki ideal construction (Section 5) reduces a module of rank `e >= 2` to an ideal; the repository's
  kernel bundles have rank 4 or 8, so a Bourbaki reduction is available in principle, and the paper's hypotheses (`G_2`,
  `grade F_e(E) >= 2`, a reduction `U`) are unverified for them.
