## Main claims and evidence

- [P] A bipolar (Net Zero, NZ) flux pulse realizes a 40 ns CZ gate with 99.1 percent fidelity and 0.1 percent leakage in a circuit-QED processor (abstract).
- [P] The pulsed qubit sits 0.5-1 GHz below its flux sweetspot during the gate, so a small amplitude change during the pulse changes the acquired phase strongly; this makes the phase very sensitive to line distortions (history-dependence section).
- [P] Uncorrected distortions produce phase errors of about 80 degrees in the history experiment; a predistortion filter leaves under 10 degrees for the first 500 ns but a history-dependent residual beyond; NZ pulses together with the filter remove the history dependence (Fig. 2 discussion).
- [P] The conditional phase of a unipolar pulse is first-order sensitive to an applied DC flux offset and that of an NZ pulse only second-order sensitive (Fig. 3); the NZ half-pulses act as a built-in echo against low-frequency flux noise.
- [P] The abstract attributes the remaining fidelity limit to high-frequency flux noise and the remaining leakage to short-timescale distortions.
- [P] Flux waveforms are generated by an arbitrary waveform generator (Zurich Instruments HDAWG) with a 20 ns FIR predistortion filter.

## Limits

- [P/I] One device and one qubit pair; no stationarity analysis of conditional phase across consecutive shots or jobs.
- [P/I] Noise treatment concerns quasi-static and 1/f flux noise and line distortion; periodic pickup at a single frequency is not examined.
- [P/I] The second-order DC sensitivity of NZ is not zero, so a periodic flux or amplitude modulation can still imprint a conditional-phase modulation.

## Mapping to this repository

[I] The conditional phase of each CZ rung of a ladder is set by a flux-pulse amplitude and duration; a periodic disturbance of the flux line or of the AWG output amplitude modulates that phase to first order in the amplitude change, with the NZ echo suppressing only the quasi-static part.
