## Terminology and the aims of the paper (pp. 447-451)

- [P] Introduction (pp. 447-450): the paper studies algebra structures on finite free resolutions of cyclic modules over a local Noetherian ring and uses them to prove structure theorems for two classes of ideals of codimension 3, analogous to Hilbert's structure theorem for perfect ideals of codimension 2. In the introduction `R` is a regular local ring; the body often generalizes to ideals of finite homological dimension in arbitrary local rings (p. 448).
  The two classes are Gorenstein ideals of codimension 3, which are the ideals of `(n-1)`-th order pfaffians of an `n x n` alternating matrix of rank `n - 1` (p. 448), and almost complete intersections of codimension 3 (p. 449).
- [P] Terminology (pp. 450-451): for a local ring `R` with maximal ideal `J`, the grade of a proper ideal is the length of a maximal `R`-sequence in it; an ideal `I` of grade `g` is perfect if `p.d.(R/I) = g`; the type of a perfect ideal of grade `g` is `dim_(R/J) Ext^g_R(R/I, R/J)` (the form restated on p. 475; p. 451 prints the arguments as `(R/J, R/I)`); a perfect ideal is Gorenstein if its type is 1; a perfect ideal of grade `g` is an almost complete intersection if it can be generated by `g + 1` elements.
  For a map `f: F -> G` of `R`-modules, `I_n(f)` is the ideal of `n x n` minors and the rank of `f` is the largest `r` with `Lambda^r f` nonzero.
- [P] Note added in proof (p. 450): Avramov communicated the existence of a counterexample to Conjecture 1.2 below, in an appendix by V. Khimich to Avramov's paper "On the Hopf algebra of a local ring" (Math. USSR Izv. 8 (1974)); the weaker Conjecture 1.2' is stated there: for an ideal `I` of a local ring `R` and an `R`-sequence `x_1, ..., x_g` in `I`, the minimal free resolution `P` of `R/I` is a differential graded module over the Koszul complex `K` on `x_1, ..., x_g` such that the comparison map `K -> P` extending the projection `R/(x_1, ..., x_g) -> R/I` is a map of `K`-modules.

## Algebra structures on free resolutions (Section 1, pp. 451-456)

- [P] Proposition 1.1 (p. 451): over a commutative ring `R`, the projective resolution `P` of a cyclic module `R/I` can be given the structure of a commutative, homotopy-associative differential graded algebra; the proof builds the product from the symmetric square `S_2(P)` and a comparison map (pp. 451-452).
  The remark on p. 452 says that the divided power square is the right object in general and that the first nontrivial divided powers occur in degree 4.
- [P] Conjecture 1.2 (p. 453): over a local ring, the minimal free resolution of a cyclic module has the structure of a commutative, associative differential graded algebra. Proposition 1.3 (p. 453): with the notation of Proposition 1.1, if `P_n = 0` for `n >= 4`, then `P` is a commutative associative differential graded algebra (associativity needs checking only on degree-one elements, and `d_3` is a monomorphism).
- [P] Proposition 1.4 (pp. 453-454): `R` is a domain, `I` an ideal, `P` a free resolution of `R/I` that has the structure of an associative commutative differential graded algebra, and `x_1, ..., x_g` in `I` an `R`-sequence with Koszul complex `K` on them. Then there is a monomorphism `K -> P`; in particular `rank P_k >= C(g, k)` and `rank d_k >= C(g-1, k-1)`.
- [P] Theorem 1.5 (p. 455): `R` is a local ring, `I` a Gorenstein ideal of grade `g`, and `F: 0 -> F_g -> ... -> F_1 -> R` the minimal free resolution of `R/I` equipped with a commutative multiplication `F (x) F -> F` for which the differential is a derivation (the structure of Proposition 1.1; pp. 454-455). Identifying `F_g` with `R`, the multiplication induces maps `s_k: F_k -> F*_(g-k)`, and for each `k <= g` the map `s_k` is an isomorphism.
  The proof shows the induced map of `F` to its dual commutes with the differentials up to sign and then uses that a map of minimal resolutions that is an isomorphism in degree 0 is an isomorphism. The remark on pp. 455-456 says that the multiplication induces the usual algebra structure on `Tor^R_*(R/I, K)` for the residue field `K`, and that the theorem then yields the result of Avramov and Golod that for regular `R` and Gorenstein `R/I` this Tor algebra is a Poincare algebra.

## The structure theorem for Gorenstein ideals of grade 3 (Sections 2 and 3, pp. 456-467)

- [P] Definitions (p. 456): for a finitely generated free module `F`, a map `f: F* -> F` is alternating if its matrix in some (equivalently every) basis and dual basis is skew symmetric with zero diagonal, equivalently if the element `phi` of `F (x) F` corresponding to `f` lies in `Lambda^2 F`. For `F` of even rank, `det(f) = (Pf(f))^2`. For a free summand `G` of `F` of even rank `n` with projection `pi`, `g = pi f pi*` is alternating and its pfaffian is a pfaffian of order `n` of `f`; `Pf_n(f)` is the ideal generated by all `n`-th order pfaffians of `f`.
- [P] Theorem 2.1 (pp. 456-457; Structure Theorem): `R` is a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal `J`.
  1) Let `n >= 3` be an odd integer, `F` a free `R`-module of rank `n`, and `f: F* -> F` an alternating map with image contained in `JF`. If `Pf_(n-1)(f)` has grade 3, then `Pf_(n-1)(f)` is a Gorenstein ideal, minimally generated by `n` elements.
  2) Every Gorenstein ideal of grade 3 arises as in 1).
  Corollary 2.2 (p. 457): the minimal number of generators of a Gorenstein ideal of grade 3 is odd.
- [P] Remarks after Corollary 2.2 (p. 457): (1) the proof of part 1 constructs a complex that shows that for `F` free of odd rank `n` and `f: F* -> F` alternating of rank `n - 1`, `grade(Pf_(n-1)(f)) <= 3`; (2) the paper states that if `R` is complete and 2 is a unit in `R`, two alternating maps `f`, `f'` satisfying the conditions of part 1) with `Pf_(n-1)(f) = Pf_(n-1)(f')` differ by an automorphism `a` of `F` with `f' = a f a*`, and that for noncomplete `R` it has neither a proof nor a counterexample; the pages read carry no proof of the complete case.
- [P] Multilinear algebra (pp. 457-462). Lemma 2.3 (pp. 457-458): for `F` free of even rank `2m` and `f` represented by `phi` in `Lambda^2 F`, `Lambda^(2m) f` is `(-1)^m phi^(m) (x) phi^(m)` (with `phi^(m)` the `m`-th divided power), so `det(f) = (Pf_(2m)(f))^2`; part 2 gives the expansion `Lambda^t f (a) = sum_(i >= 0) (-1)^i phi^(i)(a)(phi^(t-i))` for `a` in `Lambda^t F*`, which the paper reads (p. 460) as expressing the `t x t` minors of `f` through pfaffians of `f` of order below `t` for even `t` and below `t - 1` for odd `t`.
  Lemma 2.4 (p. 460): for `phi` in `Lambda^2 F` and `a` in `Lambda^(2m+1) F*`, `a(phi^(m+1)) = phi^(m)(a)(phi)`; Corollary 2.5 (p. 462): for an alternating `f` and each `m >= 1`, `Pf_(2m+2)(f)` lies in `Pf_(2m)(f)`.
  Corollary 2.6 (p. 462), for an alternating `f: F* -> F` and `m >= 1`: 1) `I_(2m)(f)` is contained in `Pf_(2m)(f)`, which is contained in `Rad(I_(2m)(f))`; 2) `I_(2m-1)(f)` is contained in `Pf_(2m)(f)`.
- [P] Proof of part 1 (pp. 463-464): with `e` a generator of `Lambda^n F*` and `g(b) = phi^((n-1)/2)(e)(b)`, the image of `g: F -> R` is `Pf_(n-1)(f)`, and `E: 0 -> R -> F* -> F -> R` (maps `g*`, `f`, `g`) is a complex that is exact under the hypotheses of part 1, hence a minimal free resolution of `R/Pf_(n-1)(f)` of length 3 with last term `R`.
  Exactness is checked by Theorem 3.1 (p. 464, the criterion of reference [B-E-1]): for a Noetherian ring `R`, a complex `0 -> F_n -> ... -> F_0` of free modules is exact if and only if for each `k`, `rank f_k + rank f_(k+1) = rank F_k` and `grade I(f_k) >= k` or `I(f_k) = R`, where `I(f_k)` is the ideal of minors of size `rank f_k`.
  Corollary 2.6 supplies the ranks and the grades; minimality holds because `f(F*)` lies in `JF`.
- [P] Proof of part 2 (pp. 465-466): for a Gorenstein ideal `I` of grade 3 with `n` minimal generators and minimal resolution `F: 0 -> F_3 -> F_2 -> F_1 -> R`, Proposition 1.3 and Theorem 1.5 give a perfect pairing `F_2 (x) F_1 -> F_3 = R` that identifies `F_2` with `F_1*` and makes `f_2` alternating (the product formula gives `f_(ij) = -f_(ji)` and `f_(ii) = 0`); Proposition 3.2 (p. 465: for an exact sequence `F_2 -> F_1 -> F_0` of free modules, `Rad I(f_2)` contains `Rad I(f_1)`, an argument the paper cites to reference [B-E-3], Theorem 2.1a) with Corollary 2.6 gives `grade Pf_(n-1)(f_2) >= 3`.
  Comparing with the complex `E` of part 1 for `f_2` gives a unit `u` with `u g = f_1`, so `Pf_(n-1)(f_2) = I`.
- [P] Hilbert functions (pp. 466-467): for a homogeneous Gorenstein ideal `I` of grade 3 in `R = k[X_0, ..., X_m]` with `k` a field, the resolution `E` of part 1 is graded with numbers `r_i` defined on p. 466 (`s = sum r_i`, `deg g_i = (s - r_i)/2`, `deg f_(ij) = (r_i + r_j)/2`, all `r_i` of one parity), and Proposition 3.3 (p. 467) gives the Hilbert function `H_I(t)` of `R/I` (the dimension of the forms of degree `t` in `R/I`):
  `H_I(t) = C(m+t, m) - sum_i C(m + t - (s - r_i)/2, m) + sum_i C(m + t - (s + r_i)/2, m) - C(m + t - s, m)`, with `C(h, k) = 0` for `h < k`.

## The multiplication on the minimal resolution (Section 4, pp. 467-471)

- [P] Theorem 4.1 (p. 469): for a Gorenstein ideal `I` of grade 3 requiring `n` generators, with `f`, `phi`, `e` and `E` as in Section 3 and `E'` the rewriting of `E` through the isomorphisms `Lambda^k F ~ Lambda^(n-k) F*: a -> a(e)` (pp. 467-468), the products `F (x) F -> Lambda^2 F -> Lambda^(n-1) F` (exterior product, then `lambda`) and `F (x) Lambda^(n-1) F -> Lambda^n F` (exterior product), with `lambda(a) = a wedge phi^((n-3)/2)`, make `E'` an associative commutative graded differential algebra, and the product of three degree-one elements is the composite `F (x) F (x) F -> Lambda^3 F -> Lambda^n F` of the exterior product and `lambda`.
  The proof (pp. 469-471) verifies two formulas on the Koszul differential of `g` with Lemma 2.4 and the identities of the appendix.
- [P] Uniqueness (p. 471), stated without proof ("we will not prove it here"): if `R` is a graded ring `sum_(i >= 0) R_i` and `I` a Gorenstein ideal of grade 3 in `R_+`, or `R` is a complete local ring with 2 a unit and `I` a Gorenstein ideal of grade 3, then the commutative differential graded algebra structure on `E'` is unique up to isomorphism (homogeneous maps in the graded case). Remarks (p. 471): the authors do not know whether the hypothesis on `R` can be dropped; in the local case an almost complete intersection of grade 3 can have a nonunique structure, while no homogeneous example is known.

## Linkage and almost complete intersections (Section 5, pp. 471-478)

- [P] Definition (p. 471): for ideals `I`, `J` of a Macaulay ring `R` of pure height (= grade) `g`, `I` is linked to `J` if there is an `R`-sequence `x_1, ..., x_g` in `I intersect J` with `J = (x_1, ..., x_g) : I`. Proposition 5.1 (p. 472; Peskine and Szpiro, Proposition 1.3): for a perfect ideal `I` of grade `g` in a Gorenstein local ring `R` and an `R`-sequence `x_1, ..., x_g` in `I`, `J = (x_1, ..., x_g) : I` is perfect of grade `g` and `(x_1, ..., x_g) : J = I`.
  Proposition 5.1a (p. 472; Peskine and Szpiro, Proposition 2.6) gives `R/J` a resolution of length `g` as the dual of the mapping cylinder of the comparison map from the Koszul complex to the resolution of `R/I`, modulo a subcomplex.
- [P] Proposition 5.2 (p. 472), for perfect ideals `I`, `J` of the same grade `g` in a Gorenstein local ring `R` with `I` linked to `J` by an `R`-sequence `x_1, ..., x_g`: 1) if `J` is Gorenstein, `I` is an almost complete intersection; 2) if `I` is an almost complete intersection and `x_1, ..., x_g` form part of a minimal generating set of `I`, then `J` is Gorenstein.
- [P] Theorem 5.3 (pp. 473-474), for a regular local ring `R` with maximal ideal `m`, a Gorenstein ideal `J` of grade 3 generated by `n` elements, an `R`-sequence `x_1, x_2, x_3` in `J`, and a lifting `alpha: R^3 -> R^n` of the inclusion of `(x_1, x_2, x_3)` in `J` through `g: R^n -> R`: 1) the almost complete intersection `I = (x_1, x_2, x_3) : J` is generated by `x_1`, `x_2`, `x_3` and the image of the composite `R = Lambda^n R^(n*) -> Lambda^3 R^(n*) -> Lambda^3 R^(3*) = R` (maps `lambda*` and `Lambda^3 alpha*`);
  2) every almost complete intersection `I` of grade 3 in `R` arises this way for suitable `J` and `x_1, x_2, x_3`, and if `I` has type `t`, they can be chosen so that `n = t` for odd `t` and `n = t + 1` for even `t`. The proof of part 2 (pp. 474-476) chooses four generators of `I`, any three forming an `R`-sequence by prime avoidance, and uses Proposition 5.2 and Theorem 2.1.
- [P] Example (pp. 476-477): in `R = k[[W, X, Y, Z]]` the ideal `I = (X^2 - WY, YZ - W^3, Z^2 - WXY, XY^2 - W^2 Z, Y^3 - WXZ)` is a perfect ideal of grade 3 with 5 generators and type 3, the kernel of `R -> k[[t]]` sending `W, X, Y, Z` to `t^7, t^8, t^9, t^12`; its minimal resolution is `0 -> R^3 -> R^7 -> R^5 -> R`. It shows that a perfect ideal with `n` generators and type `n - 2` need not have the form `(I_0, x)` with `I_0` perfect of grade 2 and `x` a nonzerodivisor modulo `I_0`.
- [P] Corollary 5.4 (p. 477): an almost complete intersection `I` of grade 3 has the form `(I_0, x)`, with `I_0` perfect of grade 2 and `x` a nonzerodivisor modulo `I_0`, if and only if `type(R/I) = 2`. The statement names no ring; its proof applies Theorem 5.3 (so `R` regular local). The remark on p. 478 says that the lifting problem of Grothendieck is solvable for `R/I` when `I` is a Gorenstein ideal of grade 3 or an almost complete intersection of grade 3.

## Examples (Section 6, pp. 478-481)

- [P] Setting (p. 478): `G_n(k)` is the generic alternating `(2n+1) x (2n+1)` matrix over `R_n(k) = k[{x_ij}_(1 <= i < j <= 2n+1)]`. Proposition 6.1 (p. 479): for a field `k` and every `n >= 1`, `Pf_(2n)(G_n(k))` is a Gorenstein ideal of height 3 in `R_n(k)`, so `R_n(k)/Pf_(2n)(G_n(k))` is a Gorenstein ring, which is moreover a normal domain, nonsingular in codimension 6.
  The proof (pp. 479-480) applies Theorem 2.1 part 1, the height formula `height(Pf_(2n-2l)(G_n(k))) = (l+1)(2l+3)` quoted from Room's book (p. 196 of "The geometry of determinantal loci"; the proof states it without a range for `l`), the Krull-Serre normality criterion, and the localization at a `(2n-2)`-pfaffian that reduces the ring to a localized polynomial ring; connectedness of the spectrum follows from homogeneity (p. 480).
- [P] Proposition 6.2 (p. 480): for odd `n >= 3` and `R = k[[X, Y, Z]]` with `k` a field, the `n x n` alternating matrix `H_n` whose entries above the diagonal are `X` at `(i, i+1)` for odd `i`, `Y` at `(i, i+1)` for even `i` and `Z` at `(i, n-i+1)` has `Pf_(n-1)(H_n)` a Gorenstein ideal of height 3 with `n` generators (the proof: it contains powers of `X`, `Y`, `Z`, so its grade is 3, and Theorem 2.1 part 1 applies). By part 2 of Theorem 2.1 every Gorenstein ideal of grade 3 in `R` has an odd number of generators at least 3 (p. 480).
- [P] Proposition 6.3 (p. 481): for each integer `t >= 2`, the ideal `I_t` of the ring `S_t` constructed on pp. 480-481 (from the generic alternating matrix `G_(bar t)`, `bar t = (t-1)/2`, for odd `t`, and by specialization of `S_(t+1)` for even `t`) is a generic almost complete intersection of height 3 with `type(S_t/I_t) = t`, and a normal prime ideal. The paper omits the proof, which "follows from Theorem 5.3" and resembles that of Proposition 6.1; the ring `k` is the commutative ring of Section 6 a), and the proposition prints no further hypothesis on it.
- [P] Appendix (pp. 481-484): the exterior algebra as a divided power bialgebra, the mutual module structures of `Lambda F` and `Lambda F*` (Proposition A.1, p. 483), the measuring identity (A.2, p. 483), the contraction rules (A.3, pp. 483-484) and `b(a^(p)) = b(a) wedge a^(p-1)` for `a` of even degree and `b` in `F*` (A.4, p. 484); they supply the identities used in Sections 2 and 4.
