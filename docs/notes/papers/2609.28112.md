## Statements

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): for fixed r and prime p, deciding whether H_r(K, Z) of a clique complex K = Cl(G) has an invariant factor divisible by p (p-torsion) is NP-hard; related problems named include the Bockstein homomorphism, Smith normal form, lattice saturation and cohomology.
- [P] Abstract: a quantum one-sided torsion witness for a finite prime set P outputs WITNESS (H_r or H_{r-1} has p-torsion for some p in P) or INCONCLUSIVE, with a near-quadratic quantum speedup claimed in a stated regime and a possible superpolynomial speedup for estimating the total number of p-torsions to multiplicative accuracy on constructed examples.
- [P] Eq. 2 (p. 3): H_r(X; Z) is isomorphic to Z^(beta_r) plus the torsion part, a direct sum of cyclic groups Z_{r_i} with r_i dividing r_{i+1}; tensoring with R or C loses the finite cyclic factors (p. 3).
- [P] Introduction (p. 2): the method rests on the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups and the universal coefficient theorem, so torsion appears in the dimensions of mod-p homology groups; the algorithm uses state preparation, block encoding and QSVT.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The rank of an integer matrix over F_p equals its rational rank except at the primes dividing its Smith normal form invariant factors, which is the torsion phenomenon. The repository measures a related prime-dependent effect: reduction mod 2 sends every structure constant +-1 to 1 and erases the sign cocycle, so every tested A_5 operand (invertible and zero-divisor alike) has F_2 rank 16 (`docs/notes/topics/quantum-linear-algebra-fit-a5.md`), against real ranks 32 to 20. That is sign erasure under reduction, not a drop confined to the zero-divisor locus. This paper shows that detecting prime-dependent rank behavior is NP-hard in the general clique-complex setting, so a quantum witness would not make the fiber program's F_p ranks cheap either.
- [I] The input model is a clique complex with simplex-state preparation, not an explicit integer matrix, so the speedup claims do not apply to the A_5 left-multiplication matrices; no experiment follows.
