## Units and definitions

- [P] Metric signature `eta = diag[1, -1, -1, -1]` and "rationalised Lorentz-Heaviside natural units
  (k_B = hbar = c = eps0 = mu0 = 1) with e^2 = 4 pi alpha" (PDF p. 2, end of Section 1).
- [P] `kappa^2 = 16 pi G_N`, with `G_N` the Newtonian constant, in the Lagrangian pieces `L_gr` proportional to
  `R/kappa^2` (R the Ricci scalar) and `L_em = -F_{mu nu} F^{mu nu}/4 - ...` (Eq. A.2, PDF p. 9).
- [P] The metric is expanded as `g_{mu nu} = eta_{mu nu} + kappa h_{mu nu}` in the source reference, so `h` carries
  energy dimension; the dimensionless amplitude of the literature is `h_tilde = kappa h`, and Eq. A.6 is converted
  by `h(0,t) = h_tilde(0,t)/kappa` (PDF p. 10, opening of Appendix B).
- [P] Equations of motion (A.3) hold in the TT gauge (`h_{0 mu} = 0`, `h_ii = 0`, `d_j h_{ij} = 0`); the slowly
  varying envelope approximation linearizes them to `(omega + i d_z) Psi + M Psi = 0` (Eq. A.4, PDF p. 10) for
  `Psi = (h_x, h_+, A_x, A_y)^T`, with `h_x` and `h_+` the cross and plus polarizations (PDF p. 10).

## The graviton-photon mixing term

- [P] Eq. A.5 (PDF p. 10): `M^x_{g gamma} = kappa k B_x / (omega + k)`, `M^y_{g gamma} = kappa k B_y / (omega + k)`,
  `M_x = -Pi_xx/(omega + k)`, `M_y = -Pi_yy/(omega + k)`, `M_CF = -Pi_xy/(omega + k)`. B is the external field
  component transverse to the propagation direction, `k` the common wave-number of photon and graviton, and
  `Pi` the photon polarization tensor of the magnetized medium.
- [P] "All the particles participating in the mixing are assumed to be relativistic, namely `omega + k ~ 2k`"
  (PDF p. 10). With `omega = k` the mixing term reduces to `kappa B / 2`. The paper prints Eq. A.5 and the
  approximation `omega + k ~ 2k`; the reduced form `kappa B/2` is one substitution away and is not printed as a
  separate equation. [I]
- [P] For a field transverse to the path (`B = (B_x, 0, 0)`), `M^y_{g gamma} = 0` and `M_CF = 0`. With no gas or
  plasma the refractive contributions `M_x` and `M_y` are neglected, and the vacuum-polarization contribution is
  "completely negligible" because laboratory fields are a few tesla (PDF p. 10).
- [P] Eq. A.7 (PDF p. 11): `Delta_{x,y} = sqrt(M_{x,y}^2 + 4 (M^{x,y}_{g gamma})^2) / 2`.
- [P] Eq. A.6 (PDF pp. 10 to 11): the closed solution for a homogeneous field, which for a photon amplitude
  that starts at zero gives, in Eq. B.8 (PDF p. 11), `A_x(z) = -(M^x_{g gamma} sin(Delta_x z) / (kappa Delta_x))
  exp[i (omega + M_x/2) z] h_tilde_x(0)` and the analogue for `A_y` with `h_tilde_+`.

## The conversion probability

- [P] The paper prints no conversion-probability formula and no SI form. It works with the energy flux of the
  generated photons instead (Eq. 2, below).
- [I] The amplitude ratio of Eq. A.6 gives the probability. With `A(0) = 0` and `M_x = 0`, `Delta = M^x_{g gamma}`,
  so `P(g -> gamma) = (M/Delta)^2 sin^2(Delta z) = sin^2(Delta z)`, which for `Delta z << 1` is
  `(kappa B z / 2)^2 = 4 pi G_N B^2 z^2` in the paper's natural units (`kappa^2 = 16 pi G_N`). The paper states the
  small-`Delta z` condition: "the propagation distance z is small with respect to the oscillation length ...
  `Delta_{x,y} z << 1`" (PDF p. 4).
- [I] The SI form `4 pi G B^2 L^2 / (mu0 c^4)` used in `scripts/pais_claim_bounds.py` is the repository's
  conversion of that natural-units product: with `eps0 = mu0 = c = hbar = 1` the field energy density is `B^2/2`,
  which is `B^2/(2 mu0)` in SI, so `G B^2` becomes `G B^2 / (mu0 c^4)` with the length unit restored.
- [E] `4 pi G B^2 L^2 / (mu0 c^4)` at B = 10 T and L = 1 m is 8.26e-36 (CODATA 2022 `G`), matching the topic note's
  8.3e-36; at B = 1e10 T and L = 1 m it is 8.26e-18.
- [P] Eq. 2 (PDF p. 5): the photon energy flux generated by a stochastic background of characteristic amplitude
  `h_c`, `Phi^graph(z, omega_f; t) = (M^x_{g gamma})^2 int [ sin^2(Delta_x z)/Delta_x^2 + sin^2(Delta_y z)/Delta_y^2 ]
  h_c^2(0, omega) omega / (2 kappa^2) d omega`, which for `Delta z << 1` is
  `int B^2 z^2 h_c^2(0, omega) omega / 4 d omega`. [E] Substituting `M^x = kappa B/2` and both bracket terms
  `-> z^2` reproduces the right-hand side, so `kappa` cancels and Eq. 2 is an energy-flux statement with no
  explicit `G_N`.

## The experimental bound the paper reports

- [P] Abstract (PDF p. 1) and Conclusions (PDF p. 9): "the first experimental upper limits on the presence of
  stochastic gravitational waves in a frequency band with frequencies above 1 THz", `h_c^min ~ 6 x 10^-26`
  in `(2.7 - 14) x 10^14 Hz` and `h_c^min ~ 5 x 10^-28` in `(5 - 12) x 10^18 Hz`, both at 95% confidence level.
  The limits come from published data of three existing axion-like-particle ("light shining through a wall")
  experiments: ALPS I at DESY, OSQAR at CERN and CAST at CERN (PDF pp. 3 to 4).
- [P] The limits bound an isotropic stochastic background from cosmological sources (and, for CAST, solar thermal
  emission); the three detectors cannot point at a source (PDF pp. 2 to 3). They concern gravitational waves
  converting to photons. The paper contains no statement about electromagnetic-to-gravitational generation or
  about any claimed generator.
- [P] Eq. 3 (PDF p. 5): `h_c^2(0, omega) >= 4 N(omega, t) / (B^2 L^2 A(L) epsilon_gamma(omega))`, from requiring the
  converted flux of Eq. 2 to equal or exceed the detector's background flux of Eq. 1, with `z = L`.
  With `N = N_exp / Delta omega` this gives Eq. 4 (PDF p. 5):
  `h_c^min ~ sqrt(4 N_exp / (A B^2 L^2 epsilon_gamma Delta omega)) ~ 1.6 x 10^-16 (1 T / B) (1 m / L)
  sqrt[ (N_exp / 1 Hz)(1 m^2 / A)(1 Hz / Delta f)(1 / epsilon_gamma) ]`.
- [P] Table 1 (PDF p. 5), columns `N_exp` (mHz), `A` (m^2), `B` (T), `L` (m), `Delta f` (Hz):
  ALPS I 0.61, 0.5e-3, 5, 9, 9e14; OSQAR I 1.76, 0.5e-3, 9, 14.3, 5e14; OSQAR II 1.14, 0.5e-3, 9, 14.3, 1e15;
  CAST 0.15, 2.9e-3, 9, 9.26, 1e18. The quantum efficiency `epsilon_gamma(lambda)` is plotted in Fig. 2 and is not
  tabulated.
- [E] Eq. 4 with the table and an efficiency read from Fig. 2: OSQAR II at `epsilon = 0.9` gives 6.3e-26 and CAST at
  `epsilon = 0.65` gives 5.4e-28, which reproduce the abstract's 6e-26 and 5e-28. ALPS I (1.4e-25 at 0.9) and
  OSQAR I (1.1e-25 at 0.9) are weaker, so the abstract's 6e-26 is the OSQAR II value.
- [P] Section 5 projections for proposed facilities (PDF pp. 6 to 8): `h_c^min ~ 2.8e-30` (ALPS IIc) and `2e-32`
  (JURA) in the infrared with a Fabry-Perot cavity (Eq. 11), and `~1e-29` for IAXO in X-rays. Not recomputed: the
  text layer of Eq. 11 does not fix which factors sit under the square root.

## Match against the topic note

The comparison covers the `[P]` statements of [pais-claims-falsification.md](../topics/pais-claims-falsification.md)
(section "The gravitational-wave arithmetic is consistent; its inputs are not") and the docstring of
`gertsenshtein` in `scripts/pais_claim_bounds.py`.

- Match: `kappa^2 = 16 pi G`, rationalized Lorentz-Heaviside units, and the mixing term `kappa B/2` (as the
  `omega + k ~ 2k` limit of Eq. A.5) all agree with the paper. The recomputed 8.3e-36 agrees.
- Mismatch in provenance label: the topic note tags the conversion probability `4 pi G B^2 L^2/(mu0 c^4)` as a
  paper statement. The paper prints neither a conversion probability nor an SI form; both follow from Eq. A.6 and
  the unit statement on PDF p. 2. The correct tag is derived (`[I]`), and the paper's printed observable is the
  flux of Eq. 2.
- Scope note: the paper's limits (6e-26 and 5e-28) concern absorbed or converted cosmological gravitational
  waves, so they do not test a generator. The topic note does not cite them, and none of its numbers changes.
- Reciprocity: the note applies the graviton-to-photon coefficient to the photon-to-graviton process of the
  gravitational-wave patent. The paper calls graviton-photon mixing "the inverse process of photon-graviton
  mixing studied in Refs. [15,21-24]" (PDF p. 2) and does not derive the reverse rate; the two share the mixing
  term in the standard treatment, which this note leaves unchecked.
