## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Setting (pp. 3-5): `A{gamma}` has the product `(a, b)(c, d) = (ac + gamma conj(d) b, da + b conj(c))` and the involution `(a, b) -> (conj(a), -b)`;
  the main sequence over `R` takes every `gamma = -1`. Norm and trace satisfy `Tr((a, b)) = Tr(a)`, `Norm((a, b)) = Norm(a) - gamma Norm(b)`;
  every element satisfies `x^2 - Tr(x) x + Norm(x) = 0`. Proposition 2.1: a real Cayley-Dickson algebra is locally complex (every non-scalar
  generates a copy of `C`) exactly on the main sequence. Standard bases `L_n` (any characteristic) and `E_n` (characteristic not 2), `e_m` for `m < 2^n`.
- [P] Alternative elements (pp. 5-6): Proposition 2.2: if `a, b` are alternative and either one lies in `F` or `F + F a = F + F b`, then `(a, b)` is
  alternative. Corollary 2.3: every basis element and every `alpha e_(2k) + beta e_(2k+1)` is alternative; Remark 2.4: `alpha e_k + beta e_(k + 2^(n-1))` is
  alternative.
- [P] Polynomials (pp. 6-7): `A[x] = A tensor F[x]` with the central indeterminate and coefficients on the left; substitution `f(lambda)`; companion polynomial
  `C_f(x) = f(x) conj(f)(x) = Norm(f(x))` with central coefficients. Lemma 2.5: over quaternion or octonion algebras every root of `f` is a root of
  `C_f`. **Example 2.6 (p. 7):** in the real sedenions `a = e1 + e10` and `b = e7 + e12` give `ab = 0`; `f(x) = ax` has root `b` while `C_f(x) = 2x^2` has only
  the root 0; for `g(x) = x^2 + ax + 2` one has `g(b) = 0` and `C_g(x) = x^4 + 6x^2 + 4` does not vanish at `b`.
- [P] Spherical roots (pp. 7-13): a root `lambda` outside `F` is spherical when every `r` with `p_lambda(r) = 0` (`p_lambda(x) = x^2 - Tr(lambda) x + Norm(lambda)`) is a root
  of `f`; Proposition 3.3: `r` outside `F` satisfies `p_lambda(r) = 0` iff `Tr` and `Norm` agree ("quadratically equivalent"); Example 3.5:
  quadratic equivalence differs from conjugacy at `n >= 4` (`b a b^(-1) = 0` while `Norm(a) = 2`); Proposition 3.6: on a locally complex algebra
  equivalence means equal real part and equal `|Im|`; Example 3.9: two conjugate roots need not be spherical at `n >= 4` (`f(x) = ax`).
  Theorem 3.12: `lambda` is a spherical root of `f` iff `f = g p_lambda` for some `g`; Theorem 3.13: a spherical root of `f` is a root of `C_f`;
  Corollary 3.14: at most `floor(n/2)` classes of spherical roots for degree `n` on a locally complex algebra; Algorithm 3.16 (companion roots, reduction
  `x^2 = Tr x - Norm`); Theorem 3.19 and Corollary 3.20 (factorization into `p_lambda_i` on quaternion and octonion division algebras).
- [P] Derivatives and critical points (pp. 13-18): Theorem 4.2: spherical critical points lie in the convex hull of the roots of `C_f` on a locally
  complex algebra; Theorem 4.3: for octonion quadratics, all roots of `f'` lie in that hull; Example 4.4: false for the sedenion quadratic `a x^2`
  (the roots of `f'(x) = 2ax` include the line `R b`, while the hull of the roots of `C_f(x) = 2 x^4` is `{0}`). Theorem 4.8: all roots of `f'` lie in the Gauss-Lucas
  snail `sn(f)`, the union of the hulls `K_(C_I)(f_I)` over unit pure `I`; Lemma 4.9, Theorem 4.10, Corollary 4.12: radii `R_1, R_2, R_3` bound `rho(f)` and `rho(f')`;
  Corollary 4.15 (octonions, non-monic) and Example 4.16 (fails for sedenions: all of `R b` are roots of `ax`); Example 4.17: a boundary critical point that
  is not a root.
- [P] Jensen (pp. 18-19): Jensen sphere of `lambda` (center `Re(lambda)`, radius `|Im(lambda)|`); Proposition 5.1: non-real roots of real-coefficient
  polynomials are spherical; Theorem 5.2: spherical critical points of a real-coefficient `f` lie on or within the Jensen spheres of its spherical roots.

## Convention map and cd_core check

[I] Product. With `gamma = -1` the printed doubling `(ac + gamma conj(d) b, da + b conj(c))` is `cd_core`'s `(a,b)(c,d) = (ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))`, and the
printed `e_m` of the main sequence are `cd_core`'s basis vectors with the XOR index (`e_(m + 2^(n-1)) = (0, e_m)`). Example 2.6 uses no other convention
fact, so the paper's sedenion statements transfer verbatim.

[I] Check results: no mismatch. A scratch script (ignored `docs/local/`, run with `-I`) used exact rational arithmetic over `cd_core` at `D = 16`:

## Repository bearing

[I] Zero divisors. The pair `e1 + e10`, `e7 + e12` of Example 2.6 is a sedenion zero-divisor pair of the kind `cd_core` enumerates: both
factors are two-term elements whose basis indices have the same XOR (`1 xor 10 = 7 xor 12 = 11`), and the product vanishes exactly. The paper
uses the pair for polynomial consequences only (a root of `ax` that is not a root of `C_f`, a root line `R b`, a quadratic-equivalence class larger than a
conjugacy class); it states no annihilator dimension, Stiefel, associator or box-kite result. Claims `sedenion-determinant-membership`,
`a5-stiefel-universal`, `a6-stiefel-witness`, `cd-associator-census`, `dimension32-global-topology`: none.

[I] Alternativity. Proposition 2.2 and Remark 2.4 give a sufficient condition for alternative elements, which [Moreno](../math-0512517.md) and
[the 2004 alternative-elements paper](math-0404395.md) characterize completely among two-term elements; the paper's `alpha e_k + beta e_(k + 2^(n-1))` family is a
subset of those. Dimensions 64 and above: Propositions 2.1, 2.2 and 3.6, Theorems 3.12, 3.13 and 4.2, Corollary 4.12 and the Jensen results are stated for every
`n`; only `D = 16` was replayed. Moreno's Stiefel question (`math/0512517` Section 5): none.
