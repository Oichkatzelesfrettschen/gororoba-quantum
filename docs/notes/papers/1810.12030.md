## Statements

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): Koiran, Nesme and Portier showed that Simon's problem over F_p has quantum query complexity Theta(n); this paper shows the Omega(n) lower bound persists when f is promised linear, although a classical n-query algorithm exists for linear f.
- [P] Definition (Linear Simon's problem, p. 2): given a linear f : F_p^n -> F_p^n, decide whether |ker f| = 1 or |ker f| = p.
- [P] Theorem 1 (p. 2): any T-query quantum algorithm for the Linear Simon's problem with success probability at least 2/3 has T = Omega(n).
- [P] Method (p. 2): the polynomial method of Beals et al., with the degree lower bound of Koiran et al. (their Lemma 5) simplified by averaging over linear functions only.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Deciding full rank against nullity 1 for an F_p matrix is the Linear Simon's problem, so this paper (cited as reference 21 of Childs-Hung-Li, arXiv 2102.11349) is the likely real source behind the "van Apeldoorn et al., Quantum algorithm for the rank problem (2021)" citation recorded as "attribution not found" in `docs/notes/topics/finite-field-qlsa-alternatives.md`; the authorship (van Apeldoorn, Gribling) and the year (2018, not 2021) differ from the draft's citation, and the result is a lower bound, not a counting algorithm. Verify against the draft's author before editing that row.
- [I] A quantum counting estimate of the kernel size would have to beat Omega(n) queries for a nullity-1 test; the theorem excludes that in the oracle model.
- [I] The theorem concerns oracle access to f. The kernel-map circuit's operator is known, so the bound is context and not a limit on the circuit.
