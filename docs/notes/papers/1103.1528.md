## Results needed by the project

The paper writes `F_MP(N)` for the best fidelity a classical memory reaches
when the input pulse holds exactly `N` identical photons, `F_coh(nbar)` for its
Poisson-weighted average over coherent pulses of mean photon number `nbar`,
and "efficiency" for the retrieved-to-incident mean pulse energy.

1. [P] A classical memory measures the input qubit, stores the outcome
   classically and prepares a new qubit from it, the intercept-resend attack of
   quantum key distribution (p. 2 and Supplementary, "Classical memories and
   coherent input pulses", p. 6). Its best average fidelity for single input
   photons is 2/3, credited to Massar and Popescu (reference 25, p. 2; 35, p.
   6).
2. [P] For input pulses of fixed photon number `N`, a classical memory that
   measures the identical photons individually in different bases reaches
   `F_MP(N) = (N+1)/(N+2)` (Eq. 1, p. 6), credited to Massar and Popescu
   (reference 35).
3. [P] For a coherent pulse the photon number is Poisson,
   `p(nbar, N) = nbar^N exp(-nbar)/N!` (Eq. 2, p. 6), and the maximal classical
   average fidelity is
   `F_coh(nbar) = sum_{N>=1} F_MP(N) p(nbar, N)/(1 - p(nbar, 0))` (Eq. 3,
   p. 6). The sum runs over non-empty pulses only. At `nbar = 1` the value is
   70.9 % (p. 6); the paper concludes that a coherent-pulse test needs this
   raised threshold.
4. [P] A classical memory can also simulate a non-unit efficiency: it emits
   nothing at low incident photon number and emits only at high photon number,
   which raises the fidelity at the cost of efficiency; as the efficiency tends
   to zero, fidelities approach 100 % (p. 6). The paper states no formula for
   this strategy. Supplementary Fig. 1 (p. 7) plots the resulting threshold as
   contours from 0.675 to 0.925 over `nbar` from 0 to 10 and efficiency from 0
   to 1. Its upper efficiency boundary starts at 1 for small `nbar` and falls
   as 1/N at large `nbar`, because the output holds at most one photon. At
   `nbar = 1` and efficiency 9.3 % the threshold is 80.1 % (p. 7).
5. [P] Efficiency is defined as the ratio of mean retrieved to mean impinging
   pulse energy (Supplementary, "Definition of the storage efficiency",
   p. 6). Because the memory stores at most one photon, this is a lower bound
   on the single-photon efficiency; a perfect memory at `nbar = 1` scores 63 %.
   The alternative definition, the success probability per non-empty input
   pulse, is an upper bound on the single-photon efficiency (p. 6). The
   measured memory efficiency is (9.3 +- 1) % (p. 2), with an independently
   measured photon production efficiency of 56 % (p. 2).
6. [P] The overall detection efficiency for a photon leaving the cavity is 41 %,
   the product of fiber coupling 86 %, optical transmission 96 % and detector
   quantum efficiency 50 %. These losses are identical for reflected and
   retrieved photons and therefore cancel in the efficiency; the input energy
   is inferred from the reflected signal corrected by 0.71 (cavity loss) and
   0.87 (detection of reflected against emitted light), so the 9.3 % counts
   retrieved photons relative to photons impinging on the cavity, mode
   mismatch included (Supplementary, "Photon detection efficiency", p. 6).
   The classical threshold of result 4 therefore takes the device efficiency
   as the acceptance parameter and treats the detection chain as common to
   memory and reference.
7. [P] The fidelity is the overlap of the reconstructed output density matrix
   with the ideal input state (p. 2). Because no systematic dependence of the
   efficiency on the input polarization is observed, the process tomography
   normalizes the density matrices and so removes the efficiency (p. 3).
   Measured fidelities for the six inputs at 2 us storage are 92.2, 92.0, 91.9,
   90.9, 95.1 and 94.2 %, mean 92.7(2) %, above both the 2/3 and the 80 %
   thresholds (pp. 2-3).
8. [P] Noise terms named for the infidelity: photon emission independent of
   the input at an efficiency below 1.3 %, caused by incomplete optical
   pumping; stray light and dark counts at 0.3 % of the incoming photon count
   (p. 3). The retrieved light has `g2(0)` consistent with a single photon, a
   two-photon to single-photon ratio of 0.5 % explained by stray light and dark
   counts (Fig. 2 caption, p. 2). The memory stores one photon even when the
   input is coherent (p. 2).
9. [P] The write-read fidelity crosses the 2/3 limit after 82 us without a
   guiding field and after 184 us with one (p. 3, Fig. 4); the storage-time
   claim therefore rests on the same single-copy threshold as result 1.

## Reproduction and repository reading

[I] The efficiency-simulating threshold has a reconstruction that matches the
paper. Let the classical memory observe `N`, emit with weight `w_N` in `[0,1]`
and fill the high-`N` end of the Poisson tail until `sum_N p(nbar, N) w_N`
equals `eta * nbar`, with `eta` the energy-ratio efficiency of result 5. The
mean fidelity of the emitted pulses is then
`sum_N p w_N F_MP(N) / sum_N p w_N`. At `nbar = 1` and `eta = 0.093` this gives
0.8010 against the printed 80.1 %. Under this energy-ratio normalization the
emitted probability `eta * nbar` cannot exceed `1 - exp(-nbar)`, so the
feasible range is `0 < eta <= (1 - exp(-nbar))/nbar`, 0.632 at `nbar = 1`.
At that endpoint every non-empty pulse emits and the reconstruction gives
0.7090, Eq. 3's value. A different efficiency normalization changes the threshold:
the per-non-empty-pulse normalization of
[Gündoğan et al.](1201.4149.md) gives 0.8125 at the same inputs. The reading
is a repository deduction; the paper fixes the strategy in words only.

[I] The advantage of the strategy comes only from photon number `N >= 2`. For
`N = 1` the classical memory has no photon-number information to select on,
and `F_MP(1) = 2/3` holds at any acceptance. The single-copy classical bound
of [`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`](../../../scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py)
(2/3 for the uniform six-state ensemble under free post-selection) is the `N =
1` limit of Eq. (1), and post-selection alone leaves it intact, as the design
states ([benchmark paragraph](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md)).
The same design paragraph names accepted multiphoton sectors that carry extra
input copies as a case voiding the single-copy bound; this paper supplies the
form of that void for identical copies: the bound moves toward
`(N+1)/(N+2)` on the multiphoton weight, and toward 1 when the strategy may
select on a high-`N` tail at low acceptance.

[I] `F_MP(N) = (N+1)/(N+2)` is the Massar-Popescu value for a uniform
(Haar) ensemble of input states. The rehearsal scores the six Pauli
eigenstates. The octahedron is a spherical 3-design, so under equal priors a
fidelity that is a polynomial of degree `N + 1` in the Bloch vector averages
identically over the six states and over the sphere for `N = 1` and `N = 2`.
That deduction would carry Eq. (1) to two identical copies under equal priors
and no further, and it fails for the attempt-weighted priors of
`attempts_benchmark`. Its falsifier is an N = 2 Choi program on the symmetric
subspace returning a different value for the six-state ensemble. The
single-qubit Choi program of `qd_classical_benchmark.py` (`DIMS = (2, 2)`)
does not cover that case, and the check was not run.

[I] Per-event dependence. The benchmark treats each pulse as an independent
draw of `N` from one Poisson law and a strategy that acts on that pulse alone.
A classical null whose acceptance or measurement choice depends on earlier
events, a drifting `nbar`, or an input-dependent efficiency (result 7 relies
on none being observed) is outside Eqs. (1) to (3) and outside the
efficiency-simulating threshold.

  detection-efficiency cancellation (result 6) holds for a reference that sees
  the same losses as the memory; whether the classical comparator in the
