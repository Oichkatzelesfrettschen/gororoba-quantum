## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Setting (p. 2). For a `*`-algebra `A` over a commutative ring `K` and `mu` in `K` nonzero, the Cayley double
  `Cay(A, mu)` is `A + A` with `(u, v)(x, y) = (u x + mu y* v, v x* + y u)`. An `A`-involution `gamma` extends `*`, and the
  standard one is `alpha(x, y) = (x*, -y)`. The nuclei `N_l, N_m, N_r`, the nucleus `N`, the commuter `C`, the center `Z` and the
  skew `*`-elements are defined on pp. 2-3.
- [P] Lemma 1 (pp. 3-4): ten equivalences among skew, `*`-, and Cayley elements; item (x): a skew `*`-element `a` satisfies
  `2 a^2 = 0`, so `a^2 = 0` over a 2-torsion-free algebra.
- [P] **Theorem 1 (p. 5):** `gamma` is an `A`-involution of `Cay(A, mu)` exactly when `gamma(x, y) = (x* + a y*, b y)` for `a, b` in `A`
  with `a` in `N(A)` a skew `*`-element, `b` in `N_l(A)` with `b^2 = 1`, `a = b a`, and `mu x y = a^2 (x y) + mu (x b*)(b y)` for all
  `x, y`; the induced norm and trace are `(x, y) gamma(x, y) = (x x* + x a y* + mu (b y)* y, (b + 1) y x - y^2 a)` and
  `(x, y) + gamma(x, y) = (x + x* + a y*, (b + 1) y)`.
- [P] Corollary 1 (p. 6): `alpha` and `beta(x, y) = (x*, y)` are always `A`-involutions, and if `A` has no zero divisors they are the
  only ones; `alpha = beta` exactly when `2A = 0` (Remark 2, p. 7). Example 1 (p. 7): for the split quaternions `M_2(K)`, `char K
  != 2` gives only `alpha` and `beta`, and `char K = 2` gives further involutions from `b_11^2 + b_12 b_21 = 1`. Proposition 1
  (p. 7): over a 2-torsion-free algebra with cancellable `mu`, conditions (3) and (5) reduce to `b` in `N(A)`, `b^2 = 1`, `b* = b`.
  Proposition 2 (p. 8): if `A` has no nonzero skew `*`-element, neither has any `Cay(A, mu)` with an `A`-involution.
- [P] Proposition 3 (p. 9, after McCrimmon, Pacific J. Math. 116 (1985) 85-109): the left, middle and right nuclei, the nucleus,
  the commuter and the center of `Cay(A, mu)` in terms of those of `A`; for instance `C(Cay(A, mu)) = {(u, v) : u, mu v in C*(A),
  v Sk(A) = 0}`. Proposition 4 (p. 9): the nuclear, commuting, central and scalar `A`-involutions are the `gamma` of Theorem 1 with
  extra conditions; the scalar one is `alpha` alone, so the classical involution is the unique scalar involution.
- [P] **Theorem 2 (p. 11):** the algebra isomorphisms `Cay(A, mu_1) -> Cay(A, mu_2)` extending the identity of `A` are
  `phi(x, y) = (x + c y*, d y)` with `c` in `N(A)` a skew `*`-element, `d` in `N_l(A)` with `x -> d x` bijective, and
  `mu_1 x y = c^2 (x y) + mu_2 (x d*)(d y)`. Proposition 5 (p. 12): over a 2-torsion-free algebra with `mu_1` cancellable this reads
  `d` in `N(A)` and `mu_2 d* d = mu_2 d d* = mu_1`. Proposition 6 (p. 13): the inverse is `(x - c (d^(-1))* y*, d^(-1) y)`.
  The paper recalls Eakin and Sathaye (J. Algebra 129 (1990) 263-278): over a field of characteristic other than 2 or 3 the
  automorphism group of `A_(n+1)` is `Aut(A_n)` times a finite group, `Z/2` or `S_3`.
- [P] Proposition 7 and Corollary 2 (pp. 14-15): `Cay_(gamma_1)(A, mu_1)` and `Cay_(gamma_2)(A, mu_2)` are `*`-isomorphic over `A`
  exactly when `a_1 = (1 + b_2) c* + a_2 d*` and `b_1 = d^(-1) b_2 d` for some admissible `c, d`; in particular `alpha` is
  `*`-isomorphic only to `alpha`. Proposition 8 and Table 1 (p. 15): over `R`, the `*`-algebras of dimensions 1, 2 and 4 from all
  admissible involutions are `R`, `C` and `R + R`, `H`, `M_2(R)`, `C + C` and `R^4` with the stated involutions (Figure 1, p. 15).
- [P] Remark 4 (p. 17), stated without full proof: if `A_2` is `H` or (`A_1 = C` and `A_2 = M_2(R)`), then for every `n >= 2` the
  algebra `Cay(A_n, mu_n)` has no `A_n`-involutions other than `alpha` and `beta`, because `N(A_(n+1)) = C(A_n) = R` for all
  `n >= 2` by McCrimmon's description of the nucleus and center of Cayley doubles.

## Convention map and cd_core check

[I] Product. With `mu = -1` the printed product `(u, v)(x, y) = (u x - y* v, v x* + y u)` is `cd_core`'s doubling
`(a, b)(c, d) = (ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))` term by term, so `Cay(A_(n-1), -1)` is `cd_core`'s algebra of dimension `2^n`
with basis index `e_(2^(n-1) + i) = (0, e_i)`.

[I] Check results: no mismatch. A scratch script (ignored `docs/local/`, run with `-I`) tested with exact integer arithmetic:

| Paper statement | Result under `cd_core` |
| --- | --- |
| `Cay(A, -1)` equals the `cd_core` product | equal on 20 random pairs at each of `D = 16` and `D = 32` |
| `alpha` and `beta` are `A`-involutions (Corollary 1), `D = 32` | both are involutions and anti-automorphisms on 30 random pairs |
| a candidate `gamma(x, y) = (x*, e_1 y)` (`b = e_1`, `b^2 = -1`) is excluded by `b^2 = 1` | fails the anti-automorphism test on 10 of 10 random pairs |
| Remark 4: `N(A_n) = C(A_n) = R` for `n >= 3` | the left, middle and right nuclei, the nucleus and the commuter each have dimension 1 at `D = 8, 16, 32` |

[I] The nucleus and commuter checks cover `D = 8, 16, 32`; Remark 4 claims `N(A_(n+1)) = C(A_n) = R` for all `n >= 2`, which at
`n = 4` is the dimension-32 statement checked above. The classification "only `alpha` and `beta`" at `D = 32` was not
searched exhaustively: it follows from Theorem 1 once `N(A_4)` and `N_l(A_4)` are `R`, which the check confirms (so `a = 0`
and `b = +-1`).

## Repository bearing

[I] Zero divisors. Corollary 1 excludes extra involutions only when `A` has no zero divisors, which covers the doubling of the
octonions into the sedenions but not the doubling of the sedenions; Remark 4 covers the higher levels through the nucleus
and the commuter. The dimension-32 doubling of the sedenions, whose lower half `A_4` has zero divisors, is thus handled by
the nucleus argument and not by Corollary 1.

[I] Claims in `docs/state/research_state.json`: `sedenion-g2-bundle`, `a5-stiefel-universal`, `a6-stiefel-witness`,
`dimension32-global-topology` and the associator claims: none; the paper concerns involutions and isomorphisms over the
identity of the base, and its Theorem 2 supplies a parameter `d` in `N(A)` with `d d* = 1`, which at `A = A_4` is
`d = +-1`. The involution of [cd_core.py](../../../gororoba_quantum/cd_core.py) is the classical `alpha`, `(x, y)* = (x*, -y)`; the paper proves it is the unique scalar involution and the unique `A`-involution once zero
divisors are absent.

[I] Dimensions 64 and above. Remark 4 states the result for every `n >= 2` and Theorem 2 relates to the Eakin-Sathaye
decomposition `Aut(A_(n+1)) = Aut(A_n) x G`, `G` equal to `Z/2` or `S_3`; the paper does not compute `Aut` at any level.
Moreno's Stiefel question (`math/0512517` Section 5): none.
