## Setting and conventions

- [P] Introduction (p. 1): `X` is a smooth complex variety of dimension `d`; `X_m = Hom(Spec C[t]/(t^(m+1)), X)` is the space of
  `m`-th order arcs, a smooth variety of dimension `d(m+1)` with truncation maps `tau_(m+1,m)` that make each space a `C^d`-bundle
  over the previous one; `X_infinity` is the inverse limit, which parametrizes the formal arcs, with natural maps `psi_m : X_infinity -> X_m`.
- [P] For a nonzero ideal sheaf `a` defining a subscheme `Y`, `ord_gamma(a)` is the order of vanishing of the pulled-back ideal along
  an arc `gamma` (footnote 1, p. 1), and the contact locus is `Cont^p(a) = { gamma in X_infinity : ord_gamma(a) = p }`; these loci are
  locally closed cylinders (p. 1) and an irreducible component of `Cont^p(a)` is the inverse image of a component of `Cont^p(a)_m`
  for `m >= p` (p. 2). The introduction (p. 4) presents Theorem A as a consequence of a result of Denef and Loeser on the fibers of the maps `mu_m`.
- [P] Section 1 (p. 4): a cylinder is a set `psi_m^(-1)(S)` with `S` constructible; its codimension is defined as `codim(S, X_m) = (m+1)d - dim S`;
  a subset of `X_infinity` is thin when a proper closed subscheme `Z` of `X` contains it in the sense `T subset Z_infinity`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem A (p. 2): fix a log resolution `mu : X' -> X` of `a` with `a . O_X' = O_X'(- sum r_i E_i)` and relative canonical divisor
  `K_(X'/X) = sum k_i E_i` (the introduction assumes all `r_i` positive, which holds when `mu` is an isomorphism over `X \ Y`).
  Then for every positive integer `p`, `Cont^p(a)` is the finite disjoint union of the sets `mu_infinity(Cont^nu(E))` over the
  multi-indices `nu` with `sum nu_i r_i = p`; each such set is a constructible cylinder of codimension `sum nu_i (k_i + 1)`; and
  every irreducible component `W` of `Cont^p(a)` has a unique multi-index `nu` such that `Cont^nu(E)` dominates `W`.
- [P] Corollary B (p. 3, attributed to Mustata): the log canonical threshold satisfies `lct(X, Y) = min over l of codim(Y_l, X_l)/(l + 1)`,
  where `lct(a) = min_i (k_i + 1)/r_i`. The paper derives one inequality from Theorem A by the chain (4) on p. 3 and calls the reverse inequality elementary.
- [P] Theorem C (p. 3): let `C` be an irreducible cylinder in `X_infinity` that does not dominate `X`, with valuation `val_C(f) = ord_gamma(f)` for a
  general arc `gamma` in `C` on the function field `C(X)`. Then there are an ideal `a`, an integer `p > 0` and an irreducible
  component `W` of `Cont^p(a)` with `val_C = val_W`, and `val_C` is a divisorial valuation, meaning that it agrees up to a constant
  with the order of vanishing along a divisor in a suitable blow-up of `X`. The introduction identifies that divisor as the
  exceptional divisor of a weighted blow-up of `X'` along the intersection of the relevant `E_i` of Theorem A.
- [P] Proposition 1.1 (p. 4): a nonempty cylinder is not thin. Lemma 1.2 (p. 5): a decreasing sequence of nonempty cylinders has
  nonempty intersection. Lemma 1.3 (p. 5): for a proper birational morphism `mu : X' -> X` of smooth varieties, every induced map
  `mu_m : X'_m -> X_m` is surjective. Proposition 1.4 (p. 5): the image under `mu_infinity` of a cylinder `(psi'_m)^(-1)(S')` is a cylinder when `S'` is a union of fibers of `mu`.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 program studies limiting directions of arcs in its arc and jet records
  ([arc rank check](../../evidence/a5-exceptional-fibers/arc-rank-check.json)) and needs a source for the passage between
  arcs and divisorial valuations. Theorem C is that source in the form: a valuation defined by an irreducible cylinder of arcs is
  divisorial, and it is realized by a contact locus of some ideal. Theorem A describes the components of the contact loci through an
  explicit log resolution and the numerical data `r_i` and `k_i`.
- [I] The hypotheses are over `C` and on a smooth variety `X`. A repository orbit closure at a singular point is not smooth, and the
  computations run over `Q(i)` and over finite fields, so the statements apply only after a reduction to a smooth ambient variety
  over `C` with the ideal `a` defining the subscheme; no such reduction is carried out here.
