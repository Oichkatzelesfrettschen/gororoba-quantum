## Main claims

[P] For univariate quadratic and cubic polynomials over a large prime field, a graph-hiding black box reveals the polynomial with constant success probability in time polylogarithmic in p, while the classical query cost grows polynomially in p.

- [P] Sec. 2 (Hidden Polynomial Function Graph Problem): Q in F_p[X_1..X_m] has total degree at most n and zero constant term. The black box B: F_p^(m+1) -> F_p satisfies B(r, s) = B(r', s') iff some z has s = Q(r) + z and s' = Q(r') + z; equivalently B = pi(s - Q(r)) for an unknown bijection pi of F_p. An algorithm is efficient if its running time is polylogarithmic in p for constant n and m.
- [P] Sec. 2: restricting B to s = 0 gives the Hidden Polynomial Problem black box h(r) = sigma(Q(r)) of Childs, Schulman, and Vazirani; the authors note that no algorithm is known for h, while algorithms exist for B with univariate quadratic and cubic Q.
- [P] Sec. 2: the classical query complexity is stated to be polynomial in p, from the observation that determining a degree-n univariate Q needs n points of equal B value and an n-fold collision is less likely than a 2-fold collision of probability 1/p. The text gives this as a collision argument, not as a numbered theorem.
- [P] Sec. 3 and Corollary 1: one evaluation on a uniform superposition, followed by discarding the output, leaves the polynomial function state rho_Q, the average over z of |phi_(Q,z)><phi_(Q,z)| with |phi_(Q,z)> = p^(-m/2) sum_r |r>|Q(r) + z>. The fidelity of two such states for different polynomials of degree at most n is at most n / sqrt(p) (Schwartz-Zippel).
- [P] Theorem 1: the quantum query complexity is at most 4 C(n + m, m), independent of p, for p sufficiently large; at least C(n + m, m)/m - 1 copies are needed for success probability 1/2 (Harrow-Winter bounds).
- [P] Sec. 5: in the Fourier basis of the second register rho_Q is block diagonal with entries omega_p^([Q(b) - Q(c)] x); a von Neumann measurement built from solution sets of the system Phi^(n)(b) x = w identifies Q.
- [P] Sec. 6 (quadratic): two copies give success probability at least 1/4 - O(1/p), with the solution sets computed by root finding in O(log p) operations. Sec. 7 and Appendix (cubic): the solution sets have at most 10 elements for x not in {0, 1, -1} and y not in {0, -1, -x, x + 1, -x - 1}; the success probability is at least 1/100 - O(1/p); a Buchberger computation reduces the system to univariate polynomials of degree at most 6.
- [P] Sec. 8: extension to non-prime fields is called "straightforward" and is not proved; higher degree and multivariate polynomials are listed as open.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Repository claim: "The higher-degree forms are phase-oracle benchmarks whose nonuniform Walsh spectra fall outside standard HSP coset sampling." (gororoba_quantum/DESIGN_NOTES.md, Design C).
  - Source: Sec. 3 (the standard approach for hidden subgroup problems applied to a graph-hiding oracle), Sec. 5 (block-diagonal form of rho_Q).
  - Model map: the oracle of this paper is a coset-label function B on F_p^(m+1), not a phase oracle on Z_2^n. Specializing the Sec. 5 block form to p = 2 and m = n variables, a Fourier transform on the second register of |phi_(Q,z)> = 2^(-n/2) sum_x |x>|Q(x) + z> leaves, in the w = 1 block, the phase state (-1)^z 2^(-n/2) sum_x (-1)^Q(x) |x>. This is the state Design C prepares with Q = omega_ab before its Hadamard readout (the w = 0 block is the uniform state). The note author derives this from the Sec. 5 formula; the paper treats no p = 2 case.
  - Label: new implementation or measurement (the measured Walsh spectra). The statement holds relative to abelian coset sampling of the phase block alone; the graph-oracle problem of this paper uses a coset state and a joint measurement of the Bacon-Childs-van Dam type, which the abelian readout does not perform.
  - Not established by the source: any property of Q over F_2. Corollary 1's bound n / sqrt(p) exceeds 1 for p = 2 and n >= 2 and gives no information; conditions (10) of the cubic analysis exclude every element of F_2, and the support count (p - 3)(p - 5) + 1 is meaningful only for large p.
- [I] No repository claim: Scope of the refutation of "cubic and higher is unsolved". Sec. 6 and Sec. 7 give an efficient quantum algorithm for hidden univariate quadratic and cubic polynomials in the graph model over F_p with p large and prime. They do not give one for Boolean cube phase oracles of degree n - 2, for any field of characteristic 2, or for degree growing with the number of variables; Theorem 1's query bound 4 C(n + m, m) grows with both n and m, and the paper fixes both as constants.

## Action items

1. Replace any blanket sentence of the form "no efficient quantum algorithm is known for hidden cubic phase functions" by a scoped statement naming the model: graph oracle over F_p, p large prime, degree at most 3, one variable (this note); constant degree and constant number of variables over every finite field (note 1107.2189); nothing for phase oracles on Z_2^n of degree growing with n.
2. When citing this paper, name the successor (Decker, Draisma, Wocjan 2009) for degree above 3 and mark it not read in this note set.
3. Record the F_p versus F_2 distinction beside every use of the term "hidden polynomial" in repository notes, so that the n / sqrt(p) fidelity bound is not read as informative at p = 2.
