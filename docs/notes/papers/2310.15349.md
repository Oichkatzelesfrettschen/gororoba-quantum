## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page
indices.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): a "clear-box approach that harnesses a fully modeled virtual replica of the
  photonic chip"; the model "estimates individual passive phases, crosstalk, beamsplitter
  reflectivity values and relative input/output losses"; validated on a 12-mode Clements
  interferometer with 126 phase shifters at "an average 99.77 % amplitude fidelity on 100
  implemented Haar-random unitary matrices".
- [P] Imperfection list (Section I, p. 2): beamsplitter reflectivity off its target value, passive
  phases from waveguide length and index inhomogeneity, crosstalk from reconfigurable components
  ("heat produced by a thermo-optic phase shifter diffuses and distorts the action of other phase
  shifters"), and inhomogeneous input and output transmissions.
- [P] Eq. 2 (p. 3): the phase-voltage relation phi = sum over k >= 1 of C_k V^(.k) + c_0, where c_0
  holds the passive phases, the C_k are n_PS x n_PS matrices whose off-diagonal elements account
  for crosstalk, and, for thermo-optic shifters, "it is sufficient ... to keep only the passive
  phases and the V^(.2) term, because of the V^2-dependence of Joule heating"; an optional V^(.4)
  term covers the change of heater resistance with temperature.
- [P] Section III-A (p. 6): the "Clements decomposition" assumes a uniform reflectivity of 0.5 and "is
  strongly affected by beamsplitter reflectivity deviating from the ideal 0.5 value" in the
  simulation (Fig. 3e); the corrected-Clements and local-correction compilers perform similarly,
  and global optimization has an advantage at high reflectivity error.
- [P] Section IV (p. 8): the validation chip has 126 thermo-optic phase shifters and 132 directional
  couplers; the characterization including data acquisition "took approximately 18 hours"; the
  fitted reflectivities deviate strongly from 0.5 "because the single-photon source emission
  wavelength and the PIC fabrication wavelength are 11 nm apart".
- [P] Section IV (p. 8): the replica stabilizes at a test total variation distance TVD_test of about
  2.9 %, "one order of magnitude higher than the expected value of 0.1 %", which "hints towards
  additional PIC imperfections not taken into account by the virtual replica"; the circuit amplitude
  fidelity for 100 random phase configurations is F_a = (99.92 +- 0.02) %; the paper observes
  empirically F_a ~ 1 - TVD_test^2.
- [P] Section IV (p. 8): for 100 Haar-random 12 x 12 targets compiled with the local-correction
  method and detector relabeling, F_a = (99.77 +- 0.04) %, or (99.85 +- 0.04) % after correcting for
  the estimated output transmissions; Section V (p. 8) restates the first as 0.23 % amplitude
  infidelity.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The paper supplies the error model whose fitted parameters Belenos-class compilation relies
  on: static passive phases, per-coupler reflectivity, port loss and a thermal crosstalk matrix. The
  Belenos acceptance and routing controls of
  [bdsc-qualification.md](../../design/bdsc-qualification.md) test whether the compiled unitary a
  job receives matches such a model; the observation that unmodeled imperfections leave a 2.9 %
  TVD floor (p. 8) bounds how closely a calibrated replica can be expected to predict outputs.
- [I] The V^(.2) heating law is the mechanism by which a drift in heater efficiency or crosstalk (the
  series of [bdsc_drift_series.py](../../../scripts/bdsc_drift_series.py)) reaches the implemented
  unitary; the survey in [literature.md](../quandela/literature.md) (source 16) carries the fuller
  reading.
