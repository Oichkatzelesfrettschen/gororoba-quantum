## Setting and conventions

- [P] Section 2 (p. 2): `K = Q(alpha)` is a simple extension with minimal polynomial `f` in `Q[t]`; `X = {x_1, ..., x_n}` are the variables and `t` an extra variable; `S = Q(alpha)[X]`, `T = Q[X, t]`; the product ordering `>_K = (>_1, >)` on monomials of `(X, t)`
  takes a global ordering `>_1` on `X` first and the ordering on `t` second, so that `X^a > t^b` for every nonzero `a`. For a set `H~` of polynomials `g_i(X, t)` of `T`, `H = {g_i(X, alpha)}` generates `I` in `S`, and `I~` in `T` is generated by `H~` together with `f`.
- [P] Section 2 (p. 2): `p` is a prime that divides no denominator of the coefficients of `f` and `H~`; the reduction of `f` is `f_p = prod f_(i,p)` into irreducible factors over `F_p`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 5.1 (p. 4): if `G~` is the reduced Groebner basis of `I~ = <H~, f>` in `Q[X, t]` with respect to `>_K`, then `(G~ minus {f})` evaluated at `t = alpha` is the reduced Groebner basis of `I` with respect to `>_1` in `Q(alpha)[X]`. The proof (pp. 4-5) shows that
  `f` belongs to `G~` and that every other element has a monic leading coefficient in `Q[t]`, through the extended Euclidean algorithm and the irreducibility of `f`.
- [P] Definitions 5.2 to 5.4 (pp. 5-6): a prime `p` is admissible of type A with respect to `f` if `f_p` is reducible and square-free over `F_p`; admissible of type B (weak version, Definition 5.3) when additionally none of the leading coefficients `S_g` of the generators is divisible by a factor of `f_p`, and in the strong version
  (Definition 5.4) when for all `i` different from `j` the reduced bases `G~_(i,p)` and `G~_(j,p)` have the same size and the same leading monomials once the factor is removed. Three small examples (p. 6) over `Q[x, y, t]` with `f = t^3 + t + 1` and `p = 3` show that the weaker conditions can still let the reconstruction fail.
- [P] Definition 5.5 (p. 6, with reference [10], Idrees, Pfister and Steidel, see [1005.5663](1005.5663.md)): `p` is lucky for `I~` if and only if `Lm(G~_p) = Lm(G~)`, and unlucky otherwise.
- [P] Remark 5.6 (p. 7): for a prime that is admissible of type B and lucky, the set `G~_p` is the reduced Groebner basis of `I~_p`, and the reduced basis of `I~_(i,p)` is obtained from the pieces `S_i union {f_(i,p)}` "with high probability"; conversely the Chinese remainder lifting of the factor bases (Algorithm 1)
  yields the reduced Groebner basis of `I~_p` with high probability. The paper phrases the scheme as three levels (Figure 1, p. 7): the modular reductions at level 1, the factor-wise bases at level 2, and their combination at level 3 before the reconstruction over `Q`.
- [P] Algorithms 2 and 3 (p. 9): Algorithm 2 loops over random primes admissible of type A, factors `f_p`, computes the reduced bases `G~_(i,p)` for each factor, applies the polynomial Chinese remainder algorithm (Algorithm 1, p. 4) to them where the prime is admissible of type B, removes unlucky primes with the procedure `DeleteUnluckyPrimesSB`, lifts by the Chinese remainder theorem and the Farey rational map, and
  accepts after the test `pTestSB` and two checks (that `I~` reduces to zero with respect to `G~`, and that `G~` is the reduced basis of `<G~>`). Algorithm 3 (`nfmodStd`) maps `alpha` to `t`, joins `f`, calls Algorithm 2 and maps `t` back to `alpha`.
- [P] Paragraph after Algorithm 3 (p. 9): Algorithm 2 is probabilistic, since the test of lines 17 to 18 "does not guarantee" that `<G~> = I~`; for homogeneous `I` the result of Algorithm 3 can be verified along the lines of Theorem 7.1 of the Arnold paper, which makes the algorithm deterministic. The abstract (p. 1) states that an effective final verification is known only
  for homogeneous ideals or local monomial orderings. The paper does not mention weighted gradings in the pages read.
- [P] Section 7 (pp. 10-11): the worked example takes `I = <x^2 + a y, a x y - x + a>` in `Q(a)[x, y]` with `a` a zero of `f = t^2 + 1` (the field `Q(i)`), the degree reverse lexicographic ordering and the primes `p_1 = 5` and `p_2 = 13`, which both split `t^2 + 1`; Singular computes the factor-wise bases, `chinrempoly` combines them,
  `chinrem` and `farey` recover the rational coefficients, and substitution of `t` by `a` gives the reduced basis `{y^2 + a x + a y, x y + a x + 1, x^2 + a y}`, the same as the direct computation (the paper skips the final test in this illustration).
- [P] Section 8 and Table 1 (pp. 11-13): Algorithm 3 is implemented as `nfmodStd` in the Singular library `nfmodstd.lib` (reference [4]) and compared with `modStd` of `modstd.lib`, the Singular command `std` and Magma's `GroebnerBasis` on nine benchmark ideals (the appendix, pp. 13-15, lists them), all in the degree reverse lexicographic ordering; Remark 8.1 states that Magma ran on one core only.
  Table 1 (p. 12) gives, for the ideal I1 with minimal polynomial `m_1 = a^2 + 1`, 1241.98 s for Magma, 1.51 s for `std`, 1.24 s (one core) and 0.37 s (32 cores) for `modStd`, and 0.22 s and 0.13 s for `nfmodStd`; in the other rows `nfmodStd` is faster than `std` for I1, I2, I3b, I5, I6 and I8 and slower than `std` on one core for I3a, I4 and I7, and the abstract states that it outperforms other known methods by far for most examples.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 program computes standard bases over `Q(i)` and modulo the primes 32009 and 32029. The paper treats `K = Q(i)` as the case `f = t^2 + 1`, and the primes the program uses are split primes of that polynomial (`t^2 + 1` has the two roots 10754 and 21255 modulo 32009), the case of `p_1 = 5` and `p_2 = 13` in Section 7. Theorem 5.1 gives the correctness of the reduction
  of a computation over `Q(i)` to one in `Q[X, t]` with the product ordering that puts `X` before `t`; Definitions 5.2 to 5.5 and Remark 5.6 give the lucky and admissible primes of the factor-wise method, and the method is that of Singular's `nfmodstd.lib` (Section 8).
- [I] The paper's final verification (p. 9) is available for homogeneous ideals or local orderings only; for the global monomial orderings of the A_5 ideals, which are not homogeneous in the standard grading, it supplies no verified statement over `Q(i)`. The statement for the non-homogeneous case over `Q` ([1005.5663](1005.5663.md), Theorem 2.4, by weighted homogenization, which admits a counterexample as printed; see that note) is not carried over to number fields
  by this paper. A result obtained through `nfmodStd` without a separate verification over `Q(i)` is therefore a statement with high probability in the sense of Remark 5.6, and agreement of two primes does not alter that status.
- [I] The primes 32009 and 32029 were chosen by the program; that each is admissible of type B for the program's ideals is not checked in this note.
