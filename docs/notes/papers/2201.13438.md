## Primary result and model boundary

[P] The paper separates shot acquisition time `c1`, circuit-switch time `c2`
and classical/quantum communication time `c3` (Section 2.4, p. 6).
Shot acquisition includes reset, circuit execution and measurement.
Circuit-switch time includes compilation and loading; communication time
includes the classical/quantum round trip. A named single-shot function
estimator can require one physical shot for each constituent circuit
(Section 1.3); it is not necessarily one physical device shot.

[P] Table 1 counts costs at their actual repetition level. SHOALS incurs
`c2*(2*t_f+t_g)+2*c3` overhead per iteration, whereas the compared stochastic
gradient iteration incurs `c2*t_g+c3`. SHOALS can use more samples per
iteration while needing fewer iterations. Which method is faster depends
on those separate costs, the requested accuracy and the model assumptions.
The paper's hypothetical timing constants are simulation inputs rather than
measurements of this repository's IBM backends.

[P] Theorem 2.1 assumes a lower-bounded objective, a Lipschitz gradient and
estimators satisfying Conditions A.1-A.2, and bounds iterations to a stated
stationarity level with high probability. It is an optimization result,
not a stopping rule for a hardware hypothesis test. Sections 2.1-2.2 replace
unknown variances with estimates from previous iterations in practice; that
implementation choice does not establish an independent finite-sample
confidence guarantee for arbitrary adaptive shot allocation.

## Repository accounting and qualification gates

[I] Use a cost ledger with separate physical shots, active circuits/PUBs,
Runtime jobs, communication rounds and local compilation. Apply a fixed job
charge once per actual job, then add its repeated execution charges. A batch
with several PUBs and a tensor-packed PUB are different accounting objects.
For a fused shot, price the common schedule and reset/measurement policy;
summing marginal per-qubit sample counts does not recover billed QPU time.

[I] The objective must name its time boundary. Local compilation and network
latency belong in wall-clock throughput. Provider-reported QPU usage and
service ceilings define a separate quantum-time budget. Include a charge in
each ledger only when the relevant service or timing evidence establishes it.
The source motivates explicit accounting; it supplies no current IBM pricing
constant or service-timing guarantee.

[I] This separation complements [Fisher-information allocation](quant-ph-0411093.md):
selecting another informative circuit can activate another fixed charge.
For a finite experiment family, compare integer allocations after recomputing
all job grouping and overhead. A per-shot greedy score can be misleading
when it ignores activation costs or prices a shared overhead repeatedly.
Covariance, matching constraints and minimum coverage determine whether
samples can be pooled; cost savings alone establish none of those properties.

Before adapting a production planner, verify zero-shot candidates, first-job
activation, additional shots in an existing job, additional PUBs, separate
dynamic/static jobs, chunk boundaries and packing on exhaustive small
fixtures. Compare the claimed optimum with complete enumeration where
possible and label a heuristic as such. Retain actual returned job usage
against predictions after any separately approved acquisition. An adaptive
optimization paper does not authorize an unapproved change to shots, QPY,
case ceilings or sequential-inference rules.
