## Results needed by the project

The paper encodes a qubit as `alpha|0> + sqrt(1 - alpha^2) exp(i phi)|1>` on
one spatial mode, with `|0>` the vacuum and `|1>` one photon (Eq. 3, p. 2). `V`
is the fringe visibility of an unbalanced interferometer fed by two copies of
that state, `V_T` the visibility of the teleported state against a probe copy,
and `lambda` the conditional purity of the source.

1. [P] Apparatus (pp. 3-4): a Quandela quantum-dot source in resonance
   fluorescence at 928.05 nm with about 10 % collection into single-mode fiber
   and avalanche photodiodes of about 30 % efficiency. A 79 MHz pulsed laser is
   doubled by a delay line, and the excitation power below a pi pulse sets the
   one-photon population `1 - alpha^2`. The pulse sequence is the qubit at
   `t = 0`, a one-photon state at `t = tau`, and the probe copy at `t = 2 tau`
   with `tau` about 6 ns.
2. [P] Protocol (pp. 2-3): a one-photon state on a symmetric beam splitter
   makes the Bell state `(|10> + |01>)/sqrt 2` (Eq. 4). Alice's partial Bell
   measurement on a second symmetric beam splitter succeeds with probability
   `p_pm = 1/4` and leaves Bob in `|psi>` or `sigma_z|psi>`; probabilistic
   routing of the qubit and the probe lowers the success probability to
   `p_pm = 1/16` (p. 5).
3. [P] Postselection (pp. 4-5): Bob's single-count traces are recorded
   conditioned on one photon in Alice's station about 6 ns earlier, inside a
   two-fold coincidence window of 1.5 ns. Unconditioned counts show no fringes
   (Fig. 3b, p. 5). The tested states lie on parallel planes of the Bloch
   sphere, since the measurement retrieves no phase (Fig. 3 caption, p. 5).
4. [P] Choice of figure of merit (p. 5): the average fidelity is the standard
   figure, but references 50 and 51 show that it can fall below the classical
   bound even with genuine quantum resources, and reference 52 discusses other
   witnesses. The paper compares the fringe visibility `V_T` of the teleported
   state with that of the target, `V`, instead.
5. [P] Threshold detection (Supplementary Note I, p. 13, Eqs. 1 to 5): for two
   input copies on a beam splitter and threshold detectors, a single photon
   fires with probability `eta` and a two-photon component with
   `1 - (1 - eta)^2`; the single-click probability is Eq. (3), and in the
   high-loss limit `eta -> 0` the visibility is `V = |alpha|^2` (Eq. 5). With
   Hong-Ou-Mandel visibility `V_HOM` and conditional purity `lambda` it is
   `V = lambda^2 sqrt(V_HOM) |alpha|^2` (Eq. 8, p. 14).
6. [P] Conditional purity (Note I, p. 14, Eq. 7): the state conditioned on
   detection events is `rho = lambda rho_pure + (1 - lambda) rho_mixed`, with
   `rho_mixed = diag(|alpha|^2, |beta|^2)`; `lambda` does not depend on the
   loss. The fit of fringe visibility against single-count rate gives
   `lambda` about 0.98 (Fig. 3a inset, p. 5; Methods, p. 9).
7. [P] Classical region (Supplementary Note III, p. 15): the closest classical
   output to the target is `rho_CT = F|psi><psi| + (1 - F)|psi_perp><psi_perp|`
   with `|psi_perp> = beta*|0> - alpha*|1>` and `F` in `[1/3, 2/3]` (Eq. 11),
   credited to Massar and Popescu; at `F = 2/3` this is
   `(1/3)|psi><psi| + (1/3)|0><0| + (1/3)|1><1|` (p. 15; also p. 6). Bob's
   click probability for `rho_CT` against a probe copy is the mixture of
   Eq. (12) with the `|psi_perp>` term of Eq. (13). In the high-loss limit the
   visibility is
   `V_CT = 2|alpha|^2|beta|^2 |2F - 1| / (1 + F(2|beta|^2 - 1))` (Eq. 14),
   equal to `2V(1 - V)|2F - 1| / (1 + F(1 - 2V))` with `V = |alpha|^2`
   (Eq. 15). The classically accessible region lies between zero and the larger
   of the `F = 1/3` and `F = 2/3` curves (Supplementary Fig. 2, p. 16; shaded
   purple in Fig. 3a, p. 5).
8. [P] Quantum model (Note IV, pp. 15-19): after a single click in Alice's
   detector 2, the unnormalized state on Bob's mode contains the ideal term, a
   routing term from the non-deterministic separation of qubit and probe, and a
   two-photon term `(gamma/2)(a_2^dagger)^2` that threshold detectors cannot
   tell from one click (Eq. 23, p. 17). The normalized teleported state has
   diagonal entries `|alpha|^2 + |gamma|^2` and `|gamma|^2/2` and off-diagonal
   entries `+-alpha gamma*/sqrt 2`, all divided by
   `|alpha|^2 + (3/2)|gamma|^2` (Eqs. 25 and 26), against the ideal
   `|psi><psi|` of Eq. (24). With
   photon-number-resolving detectors and deterministic routing the visibility is
   `V_T = |alpha|^2` (Eq. 31); with threshold detectors in the lossy limit it is
   `V_T = 2|alpha|^2/(3 - |alpha|^2)` (Eq. 33, p. 18), and with `lambda` and
   the two Hong-Ou-Mandel visibilities
   `V_T = 2 lambda^2 sqrt(V_HOM^Alice V_HOM^Bob) V / (3 lambda^2 sqrt(V_HOM^Alice) - V)`
   (Eqs. 34 and 35, Methods p. 9). The model is stated in the regime of high
   losses (p. 9).
9. [P] Source figures (Methods p. 9; Eq. 66, p. 23): `g2(0)` is 0.0146 +- 0.0006
   at Alice and 0.0192 +- 0.0007 at Bob; `V_HOM` is 0.9055 +- 0.0015 and
   0.8987 +- 0.0012. The `g2(0)` values appear in the text only as a cause of
   the shortfall of the Bell-state fringe visibility from unity (Note II,
   p. 14, with a partially asymmetric beam splitter); no equation of the
   classical region or of Eq. (35) contains `g2`.
10. [P] Table I (p. 19) lists six states: `|alpha|^2` = 0.211, 0.323, 0.425,
    0.545, 0.632, 0.769, probe visibilities `V` = 0.197, 0.303, 0.398, 0.510,
    0.591, 0.720, and teleported visibilities `V_T` = 0.13 +- 0.02, 0.21 +- 0.02,
    0.26 +- 0.02, 0.36 +- 0.03, 0.41 +- 0.04, 0.52 +- 0.05. The text states that
    the points agree with the quantum model within two standard deviations in
    the worst case and lie more than two standard deviations from the
    measure-and-prepare region (p. 5-6).
11. [P] Visibility estimator (Note VII, pp. 23-24, Eqs. 67 to 71; Methods
    p. 9): a count trace is a Poisson sample of
    `N (1 + V cos phi(t))/2` per 50 ms bin, and
    `V^2 = 2(<n^2> - <n>^2 - <n>)/<n>^2` (Eq. 71) under
    `<cos phi> = <cos 2 phi> = 0`, which the paper shows is less biased than the
    max-min estimator at low counts (Supplementary Fig. 6). Traces cover about
    `10^5` bins (about one hour) with 5 to 100 photons per bin. Quoted
    uncertainties are one standard deviation from Poisson counts (Fig. 3
    caption, p. 5).
12. [P] The swapping run gives `V_ave = 0.896 +- 0.001` and the fidelity
    `F_ave = (1 + V_ave)/2 = 0.9480 +- 0.0005` (p. 7); the model with
    distinguishability predicts `V_theo = 0.902` (Note V, p. 22).

## Checks and repository reading

[I] Script checks of the printed equations. Eq. (3) gives the single-click
fringe visibility `2|alpha|^2/(2 - |beta|^2 eta)`: 0.4000, 0.4396 and 0.5714 at
`eta` = 0, 0.3 and 1 for `|alpha|^2 = 0.4`, so `V = |alpha|^2` holds only as
`eta -> 0`. A numerical evaluation of Eqs. (12), (13) and (3) at `eta = 1e-6`
returns Eq. (15) to five decimals. The classical envelope is the larger of the
two endpoint curves (maxima 0.1716 at `V = 0.586` for `F = 1/3`, 0.1910 at
`V = 0.691` for `F = 2/3`, crossing at `V = 0.5`). Eq. (35) from Eqs. (8) and
(34) holds algebraically. The six measured `V_T` of Table I exceed the envelope
by 2.1, 4.3, 5.5, 6.4, 5.7 and 6.6 of their standard deviations, and lie 0.2 to
1.9 standard deviations below Eq. (35).

[I] The classical region and the quantum curve rest on different models. The
region follows from Bob-side detection of a prepared state `rho_CT` against the
probe (Eqs. 12 to 15). The quantum curve follows from the three-station model
with Alice's single-click postselection, the routing term and the two-photon
term (Eqs. 16 to 35). The supplement gives the classical strategy no Alice-side
outcome and no routing term, so the margin between the curves compares
detection of a prepared state with a postselected event. This reading rests on
Eqs. (11) to (15) and Supplementary Fig. 2; the paper does not state it.
Confidence: moderate; a derivation of the region that carries Alice's click
would settle it.

[I] The per-state cap `F <= 2/3` is the single-copy optimum of an unrestricted
(Haar-uniform) ensemble of inputs, and a fixed ensemble has its own optimum.
For `|alpha|^2 = 0.5` and eight to sixteen equal-prior phases on the ring the
repository's single-qubit program returns a certified single-copy classical
bound of 0.7500 under free post-selection and 0.7500 with every input
accepted; for `|alpha|^2 = 0.2` or 0.8 it returns 0.8606 under free
post-selection and 0.8400 with every input accepted. The six
uniform Pauli states return 2/3. The paper specifies no prior for the tested
planes and fixes the phase reference at zero (p. 2). Eq. (15) evaluated at the
all-accepted ring value of `F` for each `|alpha|^2` of Table I (0.8335, 0.7813,
0.7556, 0.7520, 0.7674, 0.8224) gives 0.140, 0.182, 0.212, 0.256, 0.301 and
0.407, against measured 0.13, 0.21, 0.26, 0.36, 0.41 and 0.52. The first point
then lies inside the region and the others lie 1.4 to 3.5 standard deviations
above it. That evaluation is an illustration of the dependence on the cap: it
assumes the optimal ring strategy outputs a state of the form of Eq. (11),
which the paper does not show. Confidence in the ring values: high (certified
dual bound); confidence that they change the paper's verdict: low until a
derivation of the visibility for the optimal output exists.

[I] Loss and attempt profile. Both the quantum curve and the classical region
are `eta -> 0` limits (result 5, and p. 9), and the finite-`eta` visibility
changes (the first check above). The paper reports no count of optical
attempts and no acceptance rate; the success probabilities 1/4 and 1/16 are
design values (pp. 3, 5), and the bin counts of result 11 (5 to 100 photons per
50 ms bin over about `10^5` bins) are the only count information. A
fidelity-style pooled benchmark with an attempt denominator, as the rehearsal
needs, cannot be formed from the paper's data.

[I] Multiphoton emission. The measured `g2(0)` of 1.5 to 1.9 % enters no
equation. Source imperfection enters through `lambda` (conditional purity on
detection events) and `V_HOM`, and the multiphoton terms that the model
tracks are the routing and two-photon components of the interferometric
protocol (result 8), not emission of two photons by the dot. Postselection on
one click per station keeps those components in the accepted mass, which is
the situation of the rehearsal's threshold-accepted multiphoton events; the
paper models their effect on visibility and builds no classical bound for them.

[I] Per-event dependence. The classical region is a per-state map evaluated at
fixed `F`; dependence between events is outside Eqs. (11) to (15). The
estimator of Eq. (71) assumes Poisson counts about a mean that depends on the
phase alone. If the mean intensity `N` of each bin also jitters multiplicatively
with relative variance `r^2`, independent of the phase, the estimator returns
`V^2 + 2 r^2 (1 + V^2/2)` in place of `V^2`, because the excess variance enters
`<n^2>`. The bias is upward, toward a more nonclassical reading: at `V = 0.2`
and `r = 0.05` the estimate moves to about 0.212, and the lowest teleported
visibility of Table I (0.13) moves to about 0.148 at the same `r`. The paper
reports Poisson uncertainties only and states no test of the Poisson
assumption.

## Relevance

- [I] Rehearsal benchmark (`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`,
  `scripts/qd_teleportation_rehearsal.py`,
  [the design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md)):
  moderate for method, low for transfer of numbers. The paper is a worked case
  of threshold detection, single-click postselection and a loss-limit
  comparison, and it shows that a postselected multiphoton component lowers the
  quantum visibility from `V` to `2V/(3 - V)` at `eta -> 0`. Its figure of
  merit is the coherence of a vacuum-one-photon superposition on one mode; the
  rehearsal teleports dual-rail qubits and scores a pooled conditional
  fidelity, so neither Eq. (15) nor Table I applies to it.
- [I] The rehearsal's `classical_benchmark` is a single-qubit Choi-cone program
  and treats one input copy. Its ensemble dependence (ring 0.75 to 0.86, six
  uniform states 2/3) matches the observation above that the 2/3 value belongs
  to a uniform ensemble; `attempts_benchmark` already moves the bound with
  unequal priors, and the paper's use of one cap for every tested state does not.
- [I] What the paper does not settle: a classical benchmark for multiphoton
  input components under postselection, per-input success profiles, attempt
  denominators, or dependence between events. For multiphoton components and
  the attempt profile, [Specht et al.](1103.1528.md) and
  [Gündoğan et al.](1201.4149.md) supply the constructions; this paper
  supplies the postselected threshold-detector model.
- [I] Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware, Cayley-Dickson structure
  and factoring resource estimates: none.
