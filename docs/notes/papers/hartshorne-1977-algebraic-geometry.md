## Blowing up (Section II.7, pp. 163-169)

- [P] Definition (p. 163): for a noetherian scheme `X` and a coherent sheaf of ideals `I`, with the sheaf of graded algebras `S = sum over d >= 0 of I^d` (`I^0 = O_X`), the scheme `X~ = Proj S` is the blowing-up of `X` with respect to `I`; when `Y` is
  the closed subscheme of `I`, it is the blowing-up of `X` along `Y`, or with center `Y`.
- [P] Example 7.12.1 (p. 163): for `X = A^n_k` and `P` the origin, `X~` is the closed subscheme of `P^(n-1)_A`, `A = k[x_1, ..., x_n]`, defined by the polynomials `x_i y_j - x_j y_i`, and it agrees with the blowing-up of Section I.4.
- [P] Definition and Caution 7.12.2 (p. 163): for a morphism `f : X -> Y` and an ideal sheaf `I` on `Y`, the inverse image ideal sheaf `f^(-1) I . O_X` is the ideal generated by the image of `f^(-1) I`; it is the image of the module pull-back `f^* I`
  in `O_X` and can differ from `f^* I`.
- [P] Proposition 7.13 (p. 164): for a noetherian scheme `X`, a coherent ideal sheaf `I` and the blowing-up `pi : X~ -> X`, (a) the inverse image ideal sheaf `pi^(-1) I . O_X~` is invertible (the proof identifies it with `O(1)`), and (b) with `Y`
  the closed subscheme of `I` and `U = X - Y`, the map `pi^(-1)(U) -> U` is an isomorphism.
- [P] Proposition 7.14 (p. 164), the universal property: for a noetherian scheme `X` and a coherent ideal sheaf `I`, every morphism `f : Z -> X` with `f^(-1) I . O_Z` invertible factors uniquely through `pi`. The proof (pp. 164-165) reduces to
  `X = Spec A` with `A` noetherian and `I` generated by `a_0, ..., a_n`; the surjection `A[x_0, ..., x_n] -> S`, `x_i -> a_i` in degree one, embeds `X~` as a closed subscheme of `P^n_A`, and its kernel is the homogeneous ideal generated by all
  homogeneous polynomials `F` with `F(a_0, ..., a_n) = 0` in `A`.
- [P] Corollary 7.15 (p. 165): for a morphism `f : Y -> X` of noetherian schemes, a coherent ideal sheaf `I` on `X` and `J = f^(-1) I . O_Y`, there is a unique morphism `f~ : Y~ -> X~` of the blowing-ups over `f`, and `f~` is a closed immersion when
  `f` is. Definition (p. 165): for a closed subscheme `Y` of `X`, the closed subscheme `Y~` of `X~` is the strict transform of `Y`. Example 7.15.1 (pp. 165-166): for a closed subvariety `Y` of `A^n_k` through the origin `P`, other than `P` itself,
  the strict transform is the closure of `pi^(-1)(Y - P)`, so the definition agrees with that of Section I.4 for closed subvarieties of `A^n_k`, and the book concludes that the earlier blowing-up is intrinsic.
- [P] Proposition 7.16 (p. 166): for a variety `X` over `k` (an integral separated scheme of finite type over an algebraically closed field) and a nonzero coherent ideal sheaf `I`, (a) `X~` is a variety, (b) `pi` is birational, proper and
  surjective, and (c) `X~` is quasi-projective (projective) when `X` is, with `pi` a projective morphism.
- [P] Theorem 7.17 (pp. 166-168): for a quasi-projective variety `X` over `k` and a birational projective morphism `f : Z -> X` from a variety, there is a coherent ideal sheaf `I` on `X` such that `Z` is isomorphic to the blowing-up of `X` with respect
  to `I`, with `f` corresponding to `pi`. Remark 7.17.1 (p. 168): the ideal sheaf is not unique.
- [P] Example 7.17.3 (pp. 168-169): for a noetherian scheme `X` over a ring `A`, an invertible sheaf `L` and global sections `s_0, ..., s_n` that generate `L` on an open set `U`, there is a coherent ideal sheaf `I` whose closed subscheme has support
  `X - U` such that the morphism `U -> P^n_A` given by the sections extends to a morphism from the blowing-up of `I` to `P^n_A`.
- [P] Exercise 7.11 (p. 171): on a noetherian scheme, (a) `I^d` and `I` give isomorphic blowings-up for `d >= 1`, and (b) so do `I` and `I . J` for an invertible ideal sheaf `J`. Exercise 7.12 (p. 171): for closed subschemes `Y`, `Z` of a noetherian
  scheme, neither containing the other, the strict transforms of `Y` and `Z` in the blowing-up of `Y intersect Z` do not meet. These are exercises, stated without proof.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Equations of the blow-up. The proof of Proposition 7.14 (p. 164) presents the blowing-up of `Spec A` along `I = (a_0, ..., a_n)` inside `P^n_A` by the ideal of all forms that vanish on the generators. This is the defining ideal of the Rees
  algebra of [Huneke and Swanson 2006](huneke-swanson-2006-integral-closure-ideals-rings-modules.md) (p. 110), which the A_5 fiber-cone program (`docs/evidence/a5-exceptional-fibers/ledger.tsv`, rows A5-416 and A5-418) computes by elimination
  ([Greuel and Pfister 2008](greuel-pfister-2008-singular-introduction-commutative-algebra.md), Lemma 1.8.16).
- [I] Graph closure. Section II.7 states no sentence of the form "the blowing-up is the closure of the graph of `x -> [a_0(x) : ... : a_n(x)]`". Proposition 7.13 (b), Proposition 7.16 and Example 7.17.3 give the parts (an isomorphism over the
  complement of the center, integrality for a variety, and the extension of the map to projective space); the statement itself is Proposition IV-22 of [Eisenbud and Harris 2000](eisenbud-harris-2000-geometry-of-schemes.md).
- [I] Hypotheses. The blowing-up statements of the pages read assume a noetherian scheme and a coherent ideal sheaf (the definition of the inverse image ideal sheaf and Caution 7.12.2, p. 163, assume neither); Proposition 7.16 and Theorem 7.17 assume a variety over an algebraically closed field. The program's local rings are Noetherian; its base
  fields `Q(i)` and `F_p` are not algebraically closed, so transferring Proposition 7.16 and Theorem 7.17 to them needs geometric integrality and the compatibility of blowing up with flat base change, which the pages read do not state; for integrality alone, the fact that Fulton's B.6.4 cites suffices: the Rees algebra of an ideal of a domain is a domain.
- [I] What the pages read do not give. (a) The term exceptional divisor and a description of the fiber of the blowing-up over a point of the center do not occur in the pages read; see
  [Fulton 1998](fulton-1998-intersection-theory.md), B.6.3. (b) No statement on fiber cones, analytic spread or integral closure. (c) No comparison between characteristics.
