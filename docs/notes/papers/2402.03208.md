## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are PDF page indices.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): synchronous monitoring of cosmic-ray detectors and the energy-relaxation dynamics
  of 10 transmon qubits on a `5 x 5 x 0.35 mm^3` silicon chip shows that cosmic rays caused correlated
  errors "at a rate of `1/(592 +48 -41 s)`, accounting for `17.1 +- 1.3 %` of all such events". The
  qubits responded to essentially all of the cosmic rays and secondary particles incident on the chip,
  consistent with the independently measured arrival flux.
- [P] Data set (pp. 2-3): 266.5 hours between 2023-06-07 and 2023-06-29, 62.82 billion measurement
  cycles at a cycle time of 15.3 microseconds; 9,460 spatiotemporally correlated relaxation events, an
  observed rate `r_Q = 1/(101 +- 1 s)`. The event template is a one-sided exponential with a 5 ms
  recovery time constant.
- [P] Coincidences (pp. 3-4): 222 qubit-detector coincidences, of which `6.36 +- 0.07` are accidental
  (`1/(41.9 +- 0.9 h)`); the coincidence rate from individual cosmic rays is `1/(74 +6 -5 min)`.
- [P] Coverage and flux (pp. 4-5): the detector coverage is `C_QS = 13.3 +- 0.4 %`, from the cross
  section for a cosmic ray to hit the qubit array `sigma_Q = 0.131 cm^2`, the coincidence cross section
  `sigma_QS = 0.0189 cm^2` and a 92 % efficiency. The measured laboratory flux is
  `Phi = 0.0133(8) s^-1 cm^-2`, which gives an expected impact rate `sigma_Q Phi = 1/(574 +38 -33 s)`.
  The paper concludes that likely every cosmic-ray impact produced a detectable correlated event.
- [P] Fraction (p. 5): cosmic rays account for `17.1 +- 1.3 %` of the correlated events; the remainder
  is attributed to gamma rays from the laboratory and apparatus, with phonon and quasiparticle
  burst processes also possible.
- [P] Spatial extent (pp. 6-7): a qubit participates in an event if its initial decay-rate change exceeds
  `1/(5 us)`; the probability that a given qubit participates ranges from 47 % to 67 %. The distribution
  of participating qubits is bimodal, peaking at 4 and at 10 qubits; cosmic-ray events most often affect
  all 10 qubits, and non-cosmogenic sources peak at four.
- [P] Recovery (pp. 3, 6): the average recovery shows two time constants set by the superconducting gap
  near the Josephson junction: five qubits recover with `tau ~ 6 ms` and five with `tau ~ 0.7 ms`.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Rate: the cosmic-ray correlated-error rate scales with flux times chip area, `sigma_Q Phi`; at the
  paper's `0.131 cm^2` array cross section that is one event per 574 s, so a device with area `A` sees
  about `(A / 0.131 cm^2) / 574 s` events at the paper's laboratory flux. The repository documents record
  no die area for IBM Heron r2, so that area is the input a prediction for the retained Heron runs still
  lacks.
- [I] Signature in the retained records: chip-wide, simultaneous elevated error across all qubits of one device that recovers within about
  1 to 25 ms and spans many consecutive shots, with no dependence on the sidereal angle. The retained
  shot-ordered bit strings under `results/live/*/raw/<job_id>/` hold the data for such a test; this note
  carries no such test. The paper's chip shows an all-source rate
  of `1/(101 s)` with a 17 % cosmic-ray share; the share on a repository device is unmeasured, and a
  coincidence detector is the paper's route to measuring it.
