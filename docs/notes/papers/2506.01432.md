## Statements

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): block-encodings of the Laplacians (and persistent Laplacians) of a complex given by a structured classical description are constructed efficiently; Betti numbers follow from them by stochastic rank estimation, with complexity polylogarithmic in the number of simplices in both simplex-sparse and simplex-dense regimes.
- [P] Abstract: prior spectral methods pay an overhead for estimating the Laplacian kernel when the Betti number is small; the paper replaces it by homology tracking and homology property testing, claims exponential speedups under sparsity and structure assumptions, and gives homology triviality and equivalence tests with time nearly linear in the number of simplices when the boundary operator has large rank.
- [P] Table of contents (p. 2): Section 1.2.2 block-encodes {boundary-adjoint times boundary}; Appendix B treats inverting ill-conditioned matrices; Section 5 builds block-encoded projections onto ker(delta^r).

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The block-encoding of a boundary-type operator from a structured classical description is the closest published analogue of the repo's block encoding of L_a from cd_core; it targets real or complex Laplacian kernels, so it addresses nullity over R or C and not over F_p.
- [I] The paper's route to small Betti numbers (tracking and testing) states its advantage under sparsity and structure assumptions that dense 32 x 32 left-multiplication matrices (32 nonzero entries per row only for generic a; two per row for the two-term operands) meet only in the two-term case, where classical rank costs microseconds. Treat as context, not a lead for an experiment.
