## Primary result and limits

[P] Equation (13), printed p. 5, assumes the true data-generating system lies
in the stated model set. The paper explicitly calls that assumption
questionable in practice. Measurements repeat the same configured experiment;
the state-estimation model treats the effective POVM and noise model as known.
This design framework does not itself remove unknown SPAM.

[P] Section 2.3 bounds the mean squared Frobenius error of an unbiased state
estimator below by `trace(G^-1)`, where `G=sum_gamma n_gamma*G_gamma` is the
Fisher information on the trace-constrained parameter space; see equations
(28)-(30), printed pp. 9-10. The inverse requires identifiability within the
model. A small lower bound is not an upper bound on a finite-shot estimator's
error, and asymptotic attainability does not establish finite-shot coverage.

[P] Equation (35), printed p. 11, minimizes that criterion over continuous
configuration fractions at a surrogate state. The relaxed problem is convex;
integer shot counts require a separate allocation. The paper compares rounded
and relaxed objectives in equations (36)-(37), and emphasizes in footnote 5
that the design is optimized for the surrogate rather than the unknown truth.
Section 2.3 also discusses conditioning and a sequential estimate/redesign
loop; it explicitly leaves convergence and efficiency of that loop unresolved.

[P] Section 3.2, equations (80)-(82), extends the same construction to process
parameters on the trace-preserving constraint subspace. Section 3.1 gives
`d^4-d^2` real parameters for an unrestricted trace-preserving process.
Sections 2.4 and 3.3 explicitly identify configuration-switch time as a cost:
using fewer optical wave-plate settings can be valuable even when the shot
count increases. The paper does not present the repository's per-Runtime-job
billing model or solve its grouped activation-cost problem.

## Cost-constrained repository design

[I] A future local planner can retain candidate circuit likelihoods and
compute Fisher information only after proving preparation/measurement rank
and removing [GST gauge directions](2009.07301.md). Evaluate several plausible
surrogate models, report small eigenvalues and estimator sensitivity, and
compare with uniform allocation. A locally favorable Fisher matrix does not
prove discrimination between separated noise mechanisms or goodness of fit.
Those decisions need explicit model predictions, contrasts and error control.

[I] For a fixed set of activated jobs, replace the equal-shot resource
constraint with measured or scenario-priced `sum_gamma c_gamma*n_gamma <= B`.
Keep a separate fixed charge for each activated job or batch. Integer shots,
minimum samples, matching constraints and job activation change the numerical
problem; the paper's continuous convex optimum is only a reference bound.
After rounding, recompute rank, objective, total cost and every constraint.

[I] For independent arm means with contrast coefficients `a_i`, variances
`sigma_i^2` and positive per-shot costs `c_i`, a transparent comparison rule
minimizes `sum_i a_i^2*sigma_i^2/n_i` subject to the fixed active-job budget.
The stationarity equation is
`-a_i^2*sigma_i^2/n_i^2 + lambda*c_i = 0`, giving
`n_i proportional to abs(a_i)*sigma_i/sqrt(c_i)` for positive-weight arms.
This is an elementary local derivation, not a theorem stated in the paper.
Unknown variances, zero-variance estimates, integer limits and paired or
packed-arm covariance need their own treatment. In particular, fixed job
charges cannot be counted again per shot or ignored when opening another job.

Qualification requires exhaustive small-instance allocation comparisons,
explicit failure for singular designs, conservative variance/scenario inputs,
full cost recomputation after batching and rounding, and simulated coverage
and misspecification checks. Keep [shot/switch/communication accounting](2201.13438.md)
separate from scientific validity. These are design prerequisites; the
source adds no permission to change a prepared hardware execution.
