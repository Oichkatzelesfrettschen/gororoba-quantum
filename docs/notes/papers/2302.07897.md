## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page
indices. "Model 1" is the learned Hamiltonian of the commented article.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): the commented article "claims to observe traversable wormhole dynamics in an
  experiment", "based upon performing a teleportation protocol using a Hamiltonian that consists of
  seven Majorana fermions with five fully-commuting terms", generated "via a machine-learning
  procedure designed to replicate the teleportation behavior of the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model".
  The comment finds "(i) in contrast to these claims, the learned Hamiltonian does not exhibit
  thermalization; (ii) the teleportation signal only resembles the SYK model for operators that were
  used in the machine-learning training; (iii) the observed perfect size winding is in fact a
  generic feature of small-size, fully-commuting models, and does not appear to persist in larger-size
  fully-commuting models or in non-commuting models at equivalent system sizes."
- [P] Eq. 1 (p. 1): Model 1 is H = -0.36 psi1 psi2 psi4 psi5 + 0.19 psi1 psi3 psi4 psi7 - 0.71 psi1 psi3
  psi5 psi6 + 0.22 psi2 psi3 psi4 psi6 + 0.49 psi2 psi3 psi5 psi7, with Majorana operators
  {psi_i, psi_j} = delta_ij; each term commutes with every other, a property "alluded to in the
  Supplemental Material of [1]". The comment lists the five properties the commented article
  analyzes: scrambling and thermalization, a teleportation signal consistent with a negative-energy
  shockwave, perfect size winding, causal time-ordering and a Shapiro time delay.
- [P] Thermalization (pp. 2-3, Fig. 1): the two-point functions G_i(t) of individual Majorana
  operators "exhibit strong revivals as a function of time" in Model 1, while those of the N = 10 SYK
  model decay; "the agreement in the thermalization behavior between the SYK model and Model 1
  observed in Fig. 3b of [1] is an artifact of averaging over the two-point correlation functions, and
  ... Model 1 does not thermalize". The four-point functions behave alike (Fig. 1d-f).
- [P] Teleportation signal (p. 3, Fig. 2): Model 1 is trained to reproduce the symmetric-protocol
  mutual information I_PT of the SYK model for the operator pair psi1, psi2, and shows a single peak
  for it; for pairs psi_i, psi_j with i < j in [3, 7], not used in training, "the mutual information
  does not exhibit single-peak behavior, but rather, displays large oscillations as a function of
  time that strongly vary for different input operators", whereas in the SYK model "the mutual
  information exhibits a single consistent peak in time for any pair of input operators".
- [P] Size winding (pp. 3-5, Fig. 3): in a fully-commuting Majorana Hamiltonian a time-evolved
  operator has non-zero coefficients on up to 2^floor(N/2) strings against 2^(N-1) in a non-commuting
  one; Model 1 has 8 non-zero coefficients, the N = 7 SYK model 64 and the N = 10 SYK model 512. Model
  1 has phase alignment r_l of at least 0.95 for all l against about 0.75 for N = 10 SYK. Random
  coefficients on the five terms, or random commuting terms, "also produces perfect size winding", and
  "in the cases that we have examined, perfect size winding does not persist to larger system sizes or
  to non-commuting models at equivalent system sizes" (both p. 1).
- [P] Linear slope and training bias (p. 5): the trained operators psi1 and psi2 have a large linear
  slope metric chi, some untrained operators do not, and "the trained operators exhibit a relatively
  high degree of linearity compared to other operators", which "suggests that the authors'
  machine-learning procedure may have introduced a bias among the trained operators".
- [P] Closing question (p. 5): because the observed perfect size winding "seems reliant on small-size,
  fully-commuting models", the comment "raises the question of whether the observed perfect size
  winding is indeed connected to gravitational physics in a substantive manner".

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Vacuum and quantum-energy-teleportation notes
  ([vacuum_field_run_opportunities.md](../vacuum_field_run_opportunities.md)). The comment states the
  three controls that a learned-Hamiltonian emulation claim has to pass: individual-operator
  correlators in place of their average, operators outside the training set, and a null ensemble of
  random Hamiltonians of the same structure. The repository's quantum-energy-teleportation case
  carries the corresponding arms (correct feedback, randomized feedback, product-state reference).
- [I] The comment's counts (8, 64 and 512 non-zero string coefficients) are derivable from the
  commutation structure and support the reading that a property seen only at N = 7 with commuting terms
  is a finite-size property; the [authors' reply](2303.15423.md) disputes the inference and not the
  counts.
