## Source results relevant to a hardware interpretation

[P] Section II.A, pages 4-5, quantizes an LC oscillator with flux and charge
operators and gives

```text
[Phi, Q] = i hbar,
H = Q^2/(2C) + Phi^2/(2L) = hbar omega (a^dagger a + 1/2).
```

The flux and charge zero-point fluctuation scales depend on the circuit
impedance. The vacuum has nonzero quadrature fluctuations despite zero
excitation number. The review drops the scalar zero-point energy in later
Hamiltonians by convention.

[P] Section IV.B, page 21, relates the cavity operator to input and output
fields through input-output theory, including the relation
`b_out - b_in = sqrt(kappa) a`. The derivation uses assumptions about the
coupling spectrum near resonance and the rotating-wave approximation. The
review explains that a master equation for the system alone does not supply
all measured output-field information.

[P] Section IV.C, page 22, includes finite-temperature excitation and relaxation
terms, proportional to `gamma nbar` and `gamma(nbar+1)`, and a separate pure
dephasing contribution. Zero-temperature simplifications have a physical
scope; residual excited-state populations can violate those simplifications.
Relaxation and coherence times describe a specified noise model.

[P] Section V.B, pages 28-29, discusses phase-space quasiprobabilities and
quadrature detection. Simultaneous quadrature measurement by heterodyne
detection adds vacuum noise; the measured law is associated with the
Husimi-Q description rather than an ideal noiseless joint sample of the
Wigner function.

Reading scope: sections II.A, IV.B, IV.C, and the Wigner/Husimi-Q and heterodyne
discussion in V.B, plus bibliographic metadata and a rights-term scan. The
82-page review and its complete measurement-chain derivations were not
exhaustively audited.

## What an encoded qubit experiment would establish

[I] Measuring Pauli operators of a finite qubit register can estimate an
encoded oscillator observable. Claiming a physical microwave-voltage or
vacuum-noise measurement requires a calibrated map from that encoded
observable to the physical field and the detector chain. The processor's
superconducting construction does not itself provide that map.

[I] A scalar energy shift changes closed-system evolution only by global
phase. A claim about a force from a vacuum-energy expression needs a specified
geometry or control parameter, the parameter dependence of the reference
energy, physical units, and the experimentally accessible derivative. An
arbitrary constant added to a finite encoded Hamiltonian supplies none of
those ingredients. When a block is controlled coherently, its phase relative
to the control branch must be modeled explicitly.

[I] Hardware residuals can constrain a circuit-level instrument model without
identifying microscopic circuit-QED parameters. A measured probability
distribution has contributions from preparation, coherent evolution, noise,
measurement, and classical assignment. Different parameter combinations can
produce the same measured probabilities. Estimation therefore requires an
identifiability argument and independently calibrated nuisance parameters.

## Candidate proof and experiment boundaries

1. Prove the finite encoded observable and its expectation in the specified
   ideal state. Keep the basis, normalization, dimensions, and units explicit.
2. Prove the connection between each measurement setting's outcome
   probabilities and the desired expectation. Model the measurement as an
   instrument when joint records or feedback matter.
3. State a preparation and readout model, then test its identifiable
   parameters with calibration data. A theorem about the model does not
   certify that hardware satisfies its premises.
4. Propagate certified numerical, truncation, and statistical bounds through
   the estimator. Include detector-added noise when comparing physical
   quadrature measurements to ideal predictions.
5. Treat a model-rejection result as a contradiction between a specified
   model-plus-bound and data. Distinguishing coherent errors, readout errors,
   heating, memory, and drift requires matched interventions or additional
   observables.

[I] Full state or process tomography needs an informationally complete
measurement/preparation design and a statistical reconstruction method.
The circuit-QED review supplies physical measurement background; the inspected
sections do not certify a repository-specific tomography protocol or an
adaptive stopping rule. Those require separate sources, coverage proofs, and
hardware budgets.
