## Setting and conventions

- [P] Introduction (pp. 1-2): `X` is a projective scheme in `P^r` over an algebraically closed field; `span(X)` is the smallest linear subspace containing `X`. Every variety satisfies `deg(X) >= 1 + codim(X, span(X))` (the condition (*) of p. 1), and `X` has minimal degree when equality holds. `X` is `d`-regular in the sense of Castelnuovo-Mumford if `H^i(I_X(d - i)) = 0` for all `i > 0`, equivalently if the `j`-th syzygies of the homogeneous ideal `I_X` are generated in degrees at most `d + j`.
- [P] Definitions (p. 2): a finite scheme `Y` is linearly independent if its linear span has dimension `deg Y - 1`, that is `deg Y = 1 + dim span(Y)` (page 2 and the abstract print "dimension of the linear span of Y is 1 + deg Y", a typo: a reduced point has degree 1 and a span of dimension 0, and Proposition 0.3 needs the corrected form); a scheme `X` is small if `Y = Lambda cap X` is linearly independent for every linear subspace `Lambda` that meets `X` in a finite scheme; a sequence of closed subschemes `X_1, ..., X_n` is linearly joined if `(X_1 cup ... cup X_i) cap X_(i+1) = span(X_1 cup ... cup X_i) cap span(X_(i+1))` for `i = 1, ..., n - 1`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 0.1 (p. 2, Del Pezzo and Bertini): a projective variety of minimal degree in its span is a linear space, a quadric hypersurface in a linear space, a rational normal scroll, or a cone over the Veronese surface in `P^5`.
- [P] Theorem 0.2 (p. 2, Eisenbud and Goto): a variety `X` in `P^r` has minimal degree in its linear span if and only if `X` is 2-regular.
- [P] Proposition 0.3 (p. 2, from an argument of Lazarsfeld): any 2-regular scheme `X` in `P^r` is small.
- [P] Theorem 0.4 (p. 3): for an algebraic set `X` in `P^r` the following are equivalent: (a) `X` is small; (b) `X` is 2-regular; (c) `X = X_1 cup ... cup X_n` for a linearly joined sequence `X_1, ..., X_n` of varieties of minimal degree. The text states that (c) implies (b) is easy (Proposition 3.1), that (b) implies (a) is a special case of Proposition 0.3, and that most of the paper proves (a) implies (c).
- [P] Example 0.5 and Corollary 0.6 (p. 3): the union of a line `L_0` in `P^4` with three general lines meeting it is 2-regular, and the linear joining of a sequence depends on the ordering; if `X` is a 2-regular algebraic set, the union of any two irreducible components of `X` is 2-regular, and the paper notes that three components do not suffice by Example 0.5.
- [P] Abstract (p. 1): the paper proves that 2-regular schemes are small and, for reduced schemes (algebraic sets), the converse; the reduced structure of a 2-regular scheme is 2-regular, and so is the projection from any point of `X`.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 fiber cone of the pair E8-bar to VB has tracked Hilbert function values `h = 10, 34, 80, 155` and `21 = C(11, 2) - 34` quadrics, the count `C(7, 2)` of a variety of minimal degree 7 and codimension 6 in `P^9` ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md); numerator test in the [D'Cruz, Raghavan and Verma note](math-0212119.md)). By Theorem 0.2, if `Proj F` is a variety (reduced and irreducible) in its span and has that Hilbert function, it has minimal degree exactly when it is 2-regular; by Theorem 0.1, a variety of minimal degree of codimension 6 is a rational normal scroll, since a linear space has codimension 0, a quadric hypersurface 1 and a cone over the Veronese surface 3. A reducible reduced fiber falls under Theorem 0.4: it is 2-regular exactly when it is a linearly joined union of varieties of minimal degree, which is checkable from the components and their spans.
- [I] The falsifier is direct: a minimal free resolution of `k[y]/K2` with generators in degrees above 2 or a nonlinear first syzygy refutes 2-regularity of the quadric scheme. The hypotheses (algebraically closed field, reduced scheme) differ from the repository's computations over `F_32009` and are not established for the radical of `K2`.
