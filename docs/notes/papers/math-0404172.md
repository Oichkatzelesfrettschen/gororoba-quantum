## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Hopf construction (pp. 1-2): for `n = 1, 2, 3` the map `F_n(x, y) = (2 x y, |y|^2 - |x|^2)` restricts on `S^(2^(n+1) - 1)`
  to the Hopf maps `S^3 -> S^2`, `S^7 -> S^4`, `S^15 -> S^8`. For `n >= 4` the zero set is `X_n^infinity = {(x, y) : F_n(x, y) = 0}`;
  `X_n^r = {(x, y) : x y = 0, |x| = |y| = r}` and `X_n = X_n^1`. The set `X_n` appears in Cohen's approach to the Arf
  invariant one problem and in the Adem-Lam construction of normed and nonsingular bilinear maps (p. 2).
- [P] Chain of inclusions (pp. 2, 9): `X_n subset W_(2^(n-1) - 1, 2) subset V_(2^n - 2, 2) subset V_(2^n - 1, 2)` for `n >= 3`, where
  `V_(m,2)` and `W_(m,2)` are the real and complex Stiefel manifolds of 2-frames in `R^m` and `C^m`.
- [P] Section 1 (pp. 3-5): doubly pure elements, `alpha~ = alpha ee0`, and `H_alpha = span{e0, alpha~, alpha, ee0}` is a copy of the
  quaternions (Corollary 1.2); Lemma 1.1 collects the relations among `a~`, `b`, `(ab)~` used in the companion papers.
- [P] Section 2 (pp. 5-10). Lemma 2.1 (p. 6): for `alpha = (a, b)` and `alpha^ = (b, a)`, `alpha` is orthogonal to `alpha^` exactly when
  `a` is orthogonal to `b`, and `alpha~` is orthogonal to `alpha^` exactly when `|a| = |b|`. `V_(2^n - 1, 2)` is the set of
  `(a, b)` with `a, b` pure, unit and orthogonal (p. 6). Corollary 2.3 (p. 7): `S^1` acts freely on `V_(2^n - 1, 2)` by
  `(r, s) . alpha = r alpha + s alpha~`. Proposition 2.4 (p. 8): `H_n(a, b) = 2<a, b> - 2i<a~, b>` is a Hermitian inner product
  on `Im~ A_n`. Proposition 2.5 (p. 8): `W_(2^(n-1) - 1, 2) = {(a, b) in V_(2^n - 2, 2) : b in H_a^perp}`, a bundle over
  `S^(2^n - 3)` with fiber `S^(2^n - 5)` and dimension `2^(n+1) - 8` (p. 9); `dim V_(2^n - 2, 2) = 2^(n+1) - 7`. Lemma 2.7 (p. 9):
  a free `T = S^1 x S^1` action `(a, b) -> (r a + s a~, p b + q b~)` on `W` and on `X_n`.
- [P] Section 3 (pp. 10-16). Corollary 3.2 (p. 11): for nonzero `alpha` in `H_eps^perp`, with `eps = e_(2^(n-1))`,
  `O_alpha = span{e0, eps~, eps, ee0, alpha~, alpha eps, eps~ alpha, alpha}` is eight-dimensional. Lemma 3.3 (pp. 12-13): `alpha` is in
  `W` exactly when `alpha^` lies in `O_alpha^perp`. **Theorem 3.4 (pp. 12-13):** for `alpha` in `W` and `n >= 4` the following
  are equivalent: (i) `alpha` is in `X_n`; (ii) `(alpha, alpha, eps) = 0`; (iii) `V(alpha; eps) = span{e0, alpha, eps, alpha eps}` is closed and
  isomorphic to the quaternions; (iv) `O_alpha` is closed and isomorphic to the octonions; (v) `alpha^` lies in `Ker L_alpha`
  inside `O_alpha^perp`. The proof computes `alpha(alpha eps) = -|alpha|^2 eps + 2 (0, (b a)~)` and `alpha alpha^ = (2 a b, |b|^2 - |a|^2)`,
  the Hopf construction. Theorem 3.5 (pp. 13-14): `H_eps^perp` is a left `H_eps`-module; Corollary 3.6 and Theorem 3.8 (pp. 14-15):
  `S^3 = S(H_eps)` acts freely and orthogonally on `H_eps^perp`, `X_n` and `W` are `S^3`-invariant, and `O_alpha = O_beta` as
  subspaces exactly when `alpha` and `beta` lie in one `S^3` orbit.
- [P] Section 4 (pp. 16-21): `X_n` is a subset of the monomorphisms `M(A_3, A_(n+1))`. Lemma 4.4 (pp. 18-19): for `alpha` in
  `H_eps^perp`, `O_alpha` is isomorphic to `A_3` exactly when `(alpha, alpha, eps) = 0`, and `(alpha, alpha, eps) = (0, -(a, ee0, b))`. Theorem 4.5
  (pp. 20-21): `P(n)` (pairs with `a`, `b` C-collinear) and `X-bar_n = {(a, b) : a, b != 0, a b = 0}` lie in
  `E_n = {alpha in H_eps^perp : (alpha, alpha, eps) = 0}`, `P(n)` and `X-bar_n` are disjoint, and `R(a, b) = (a, d)` with `b = c + d`,
  `c` in `H_a`, `d` in `H_a^perp`, is a continuous retraction `E_n \ P(n) -> X-bar_n`. Remark 2 (p. 21): `E_n = w_n^(-1)(0)`
  for the quadratic map `w_n(a, b) = (a, ee0, b)`, a real algebraic set with `0` a singular value.

## Repository bearing

[I] Zero divisors and the Stiefel set. The pair `alpha = (a, b)` of `A_n` with `a b = 0` and `|a| = |b|` is a zero divisor of
`A_(n+1)` with partner `alpha^ = (b, a)` (`alpha alpha^ = 0`), and the paper places these pairs inside the Stiefel set `V_(2^n - 1, 2)`
of pure orthogonal equal-norm pairs. Moreno's open question in [math-0512517](../math-0512517.md) Section 5 asks whether
every element of that Stiefel set, viewed in `A_(n+1)`, is a zero divisor. This paper gives only the inclusion of `X_n` in the
Stiefel set; the question is settled in the repository by `a5-stiefel-universal` (every nonzero `A_5` Stiefel element is a
zero divisor of nullity at least 4) and `a6-stiefel-witness` with `a6-stiefel-generic-invertibility` (an `A_6` Stiefel
element with invertible left multiplication, and invertibility on an open dense full-measure subset). The elements of `X_4` are zero divisors of `A_5` with pure halves; this note does not compare the dimension of `X_4`
with that of the set of all `A_5` zero divisors.

[I] Other claims in `docs/state/research_state.json`: `sedenion-g2-bundle` (bundle `SU(2) -> G2 -> V_2(R^7)`): none directly; Proposition 4.3 (p. 17) states `M(A_2, A_3) = V_(7,2)`, the
base of the fibration `G2 -> V_(7,2)` that [math-0512516](math-0512516.md) Proposition 2.4 identifies with the sedenion bundle's
fiber structure. `a5-rank-locus-local-dimension`,
`dimension32-global-topology` and `principal-skew-atlas`: none; the paper counts no ranks.

[I] Dimensions 64 and above. Theorem 3.4 and Theorem 4.5 hold for every `n >= 4`, so they apply to `A_6` (the 64-ions) and
beyond: the elements of `X_5` give 64-dimensional zero divisors of the form `(a, b)` with `a b = 0` in `A_5` (the 32-ions); no counts or
nullities are given. The paper's Arf-invariant and Adem-Lam motivations (p. 2) are not tested here.
