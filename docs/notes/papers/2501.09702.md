## Main claims

Sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalization (SKQD) projects the Hamiltonian onto
the span of bitstrings sampled from time-evolved Krylov states and diagonalizes
it classically; the paper proves polynomial convergence for sparse ground states
and runs it on up to 85 qubits and about 6,000 two-qubit gates on ibm_fez.

- Algorithm: prepare a reference state, prepare |psi_k> = exp(-i k H dt)|psi_0>
  for k = 0..d-1 with M = O(poly(n)) copies each, measure in the computational
  basis, project H onto the span of the unique sampled bitstrings, diagonalize.
  Under noise the samples are repaired by configuration recovery from the U(1)
  symmetry of the problem. [from-paper, Sec. II, Fig. 1]
- Sparsity: a state is (alpha_L, beta_L)-sparse on L bitstrings if their
  squared amplitudes sum to at least alpha_L and each is at least beta_L.
  [from-paper, Def. 1]
- Theorem 1: with time step dt = pi/DeltaE_(N-1), the energy error is at most
  sqrt(8) ||H|| (1 - sqrt(alpha_L))^(1/2), provided all L important bitstrings
  are sampled, which holds with probability at least 1 - eta when the samples per
  Krylov state exceed d^2 log(L/eta) / (|gamma_0|^2 (beta_L - 2 sqrt(eps~))),
  where eps~ = 2 - 2 sqrt(1 - eps/DeltaE_1) and
  eps = 8 DeltaE_(N-1) ((1 - |gamma_0|^2)/|gamma_0|^2)
  (1 + pi DeltaE_1/DeltaE_(N-1))^(-(d-1)); gamma_0 is the overlap of the
  reference state with the ground state. [from-paper, Sec. II A, Eq. 5; formula
  checked against the rendered page]
- Models: single-impurity Anderson model (SIAM) with 41 bath sites (42 spinful
  modes), U in {1, 3, 7, 10}; four-impurity model with 7 bath modes per impurity
  (32 spinful modes), U = 10, hybridization V = 0.16, hopping t = -1 and
  t' = -0.5. [from-paper, Sec. II B]
- Hardware: ibm_fez, a Heron r2 processor with 156 fixed-frequency transmons on
  a heavy-hex lattice; 61 qubits for 29 bath sites, 85 qubits for 41 bath sites,
  1e5 shots per Krylov dimension. [from-paper, Sec. V E]
- Accuracy: SIAM relative energy error against DMRG falls from about 1e-4 to
  about 1e-5 as U rises from 1 to 10; four-impurity relative error about 1e-4 in
  the k-adjacent natural-orbital basis and about 6e-5 after orbital
  optimization; two-point spin and density correlators agree with DMRG.
  [from-paper, Sec. II B, Fig. 3]

  samples from the device and on uniform random bitstrings of the same length
  and count gives an energy error orders of magnitude lower for the device
  samples. The uniform-sample error is near 3e-1 and the device error between
  1e-5 and 6e-5 across U (read from the log-scale bars of Fig. S3, approximate).

## Methods

Reuse: Jordan-Wigner map, second-order Trotter step compiled into shallow
Givens-rotation brickwork, sample at every step, then classical recovery and
diagonalization.

1. Map fermions to qubits with Jordan-Wigner; |psi_k> is a k-fold second-order
   Trotter product exp(-i dt/2 H2) exp(-i dt H1) exp(-i dt/2 H2), with H1 the
   one-body part and H2 the interaction. [from-paper, Sec. V D]
2. exp(-i dt H1) is a fermionic Gaussian unitary, realized exactly by a Givens
   brickwork of depth equal to the number of modes per spin species; for dt << 1
   it is near the identity and is compiled approximately into depth 3 (SIAM) or
   depth 9 (four-impurity) by maximizing the Hilbert-Schmidt overlap of the
   Givens matrix with exp(-i dt h), using ADAM and JAX autodiff. [from-paper,
   Sec. V D]
3. H2 is diagonal in the position and diagonal-bath bases and compiles to a
   controlled-phase gate between the spin-up and spin-down impurity qubits via an
   auxiliary qubit joining the two chains. In the k-adjacent natural-orbital
   basis a single-particle rotation over 4 modes (SIAM) or 12 modes
   (four-impurity) diagonalizes H2, avoiding O(((K+1) L)^4) depth. [from-paper,
   Sec. V D]
4. Reference state per spin species: superposition of excitations of the three
   electrons nearest the Fermi level into the four nearest empty modes, prepared
   with 7 Givens gates of angle pi/4. [from-paper, Sec. V D]
5. Two-step basis choice: run SKQD in the diagonal-bath basis, build the
   one-body reduced density matrix, form k-adjacent natural orbitals that mix
   each impurity with the bath modes nearest the Fermi level, and run SKQD again.
   [from-paper, Sec. II B, Fig. 2]
6. Configuration recovery with carry-over: configurations whose amplitude exceeds
   tau = 1e-8 in one recovery iteration stay in the next iteration's subspace
   (four-impurity runs only), followed by 5 cycles of self-consistent orbital
   optimization. [from-paper, Sec. V F, Sec. II B]
7. Transpile with Qiskit; classical stage ran on the Frontier supercomputer.
   [from-paper, Sec. IV and abstract]

## Relevance

(a) Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: medium. It gives a dated
per-run calibration baseline for ibm_fez (the Heron r2 backend named in the
project) and a null-control method for judging whether a noisy device output
carries signal, but it reports no DD, twirling, readout mitigation, or layout
selection.

(b) Cayley-Dickson, octonion, zero-divisor structure: none. The circuits are
fermionic Gaussian unitaries and diagonal interaction phases; no algebraic
structure of that kind appears.

(c) Shor and factoring resource estimates: none. Phase estimation is named only
as out of reach for current devices and no factoring estimate appears.

(d) Action items for gororoba-quantum:

1. Treat the Sec. V E table as a historical baseline and compare it with the
   live target properties of ibm_fez before each run: median readout 8e-3 to
   1.5e-2, single-qubit error 2.2e-4 to 2.6e-4, two-qubit error 2.7e-3 to
   3.8e-3, T1 131 to 155 us, T2 89 to 101 us. A live median outside these bands
   marks a calibration that differs from the published runs. [inference;
   figures from-paper, Sec. V E]
2. Add the uniform-random null to every result that reads structure out of
   bitstring samples (zero-divisor pair detection, kernel entries from counts):
   draw uniform bitstrings of the same length and number, push them through the
   identical post-processing, and report the null error beside the device error.
   [from-paper, Supplementary Sec. VIII; transfer to gororoba is inference]
3. Use conserved quantities for sample repair. SKQD recovers configurations
   through particle-number and spin symmetry; for a gororoba circuit, identify
   a conserved or parity-constrained quantity of the compiled circuit and
   post-select or repair on it. `qiskit-addon-sqd` and `ffsim` are not installed
   in the project environment. [from-paper, Sec. II and V F; adoption is
   inference; package absence verified]
4. Budget gates against signal: about 6,000 two-qubit gates at a median error of
   2.8e-3 is about 17 expected gate faults, a raw all-correct probability near
   exp(-17) = 3e-8. The device output is therefore useful only after symmetry
   repair and classical diagonalization; do not read raw output fidelity from
   circuits of this size. [inference; arithmetic on from-paper figures]
5. Approximate-compile near-identity or near-diagonal blocks into shallow
   brickwork by maximizing |tr(G^dagger Lambda)| with gradient descent, as in
   Sec. V D, when a gororoba block has a small-angle or sparse structure.
   [from-paper, Sec. V D; adoption is inference]
6. For 1D-structured problems on heavy-hex, place the chain along a heavy-hex
   path and bridge parallel chains with auxiliary qubits, as in Fig. 3(a) and (d);
   the paper does not describe how the physical path was chosen. [from-paper,
   Fig. 3]

## Open points and discrepancies

- Qubit count of the four-impurity run: Sec. I, Sec. III, and the Fig. 3 caption
  give 70 qubits; Sec. V E gives 64 ("the number of qubits was 64"). Inference: 64 equals
  32 modes times 2 spin species, and the remaining qubits are the auxiliary
  interaction qubits shown in green in Fig. 3(d), but 70 - 64 = 6 is not
  reconciled in the text.
- Sec. V E prints "T1 = 155.0 us and T1 = 101.3 us" for the four-impurity run; the
  second value is a typo for T2.
- Supplementary Sec. VIII gives the subspace dimension as D = 2.56e6 in the text
  and as D = 256 M in the Fig. S3 legend.
- Sec. V E states error rates as "average median error rates"; the averaging set
  (runs, qubits used, or time) is not defined.
