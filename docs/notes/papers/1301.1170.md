# Chiribella and Xie -- Classical fidelity threshold for probabilistic (postselected) operations

[P] identifies a statement in the source. [I] identifies a repository
application or deduction. The paper supplies the optimal fidelity of probabilistic
quantum operations and of probabilistic measure-and-prepare operations for an
arbitrary input ensemble (its Theorems 1 and 2), and specializes them to coherent-state
amplifiers; this note implements no benchmark and no hardware case.

## Results needed by the project

The paper writes `{rho_x}` for the input states with priors `p_x`, `psi_x` for
the pure target states, `tau = sum_x p_x rho_x` for the average input state,
`F'` for the fidelity of a probabilistic operation conditional on its occurrence,
`F^prob` for the optimal quantum value of `F'`, and `F~^prob` for the optimal
value over probabilistic measure-and-prepare operations (the classical fidelity
threshold, CFT).

1. [P] The general task is `rho_x -> psi_x` for possibly mixed inputs `rho_x` with
   priors `p_x` and pure targets `psi_x = |psi_x><psi_x|` (Letter, p. 2). A deterministic
   process is a channel with average fidelity `F = sum_x p_x <psi_x|C(rho_x)|psi_x>`.
   A probabilistic process is a trace-non-increasing operation `Q` whose fidelity,
   conditional on its occurrence, is `F' = sum_x p_x <psi_x|Q(rho_x)|psi_x> /
   (sum_x' p_x' Tr[Q(rho_x')])` (p. 2). The optimal fidelity is the supremum over
   all deterministic (probabilistic) processes, written `F^det` (`F^prob`) (p. 2).
2. [P] Theorem 1 (p. 2): `F^det = inf over sigma > 0, Tr sigma = 1 of ||A_sigma||_inf`
   with `A_sigma = sum_x p_x |psi_x><psi_x| (x) (sigma^(-1/2) rho_x sigma^(-1/2))^T`
   (Eq. 1), and `F^prob = ||A_tau||_inf` (Eq. 2). For the probabilistic case the
   supplement (p. 7) achieves Eq. (2) in finite dimensions with the top eigenvector
   `|Psi>` of `A_tau`, using the state `(I (x) tau^(-1/2))^T |Psi>`, normalized, as the resource of a
   probabilistic teleportation protocol.
3. [P] A classical protocol measures the input with a POVM `{P_y}` and prepares a
   state `rho'_y` for outcome `y`. The deterministic fidelity is that of the channel
   `C~(rho) = sum_y Tr[P_y rho] rho'_y`. In the probabilistic case the POVM
   includes an outcome `y = ?` that produces no output state, and the fidelity is
   that of `Q~(rho) = sum_{y != ?} Tr[P_y rho] rho'_y` (p. 2). The paper writes
   `F~^det` and `F~^prob` for the two classical values.
4. [P] Theorem 2 (p. 2): `F~^det = inf over sigma > 0, Tr sigma = 1 of ||A_sigma||_x`
   (Eq. 3) and `F~^prob = ||A_tau||_x` (Eq. 4), with the injective cross norm
   `||A||_x = sup over unit vectors phi, psi of <phi|<psi|A|phi>|psi>`. The classical
   value of the probabilistic case needs no infimum over `sigma`: the average input
   state `tau` fixes the operator. The remark on pp. 2-3 reads Eqs. (2) and (4) as
   equating the quantum-classical gap with the gap between the largest Bell-type
   correlation of entangled and of separable states.
5. [P] Proof of Theorem 2, probabilistic case (p. 8). Inserting `Q~` into Eq. (14)
   gives `F = Tr[A_tau Sigma]` with `Sigma` a separable state, so `F~^prob <= ||A_tau||_x`.
   In finite dimensions the bound is reached by `Q~(rho)` proportional to
   `|psi><psi| <phi| tau^(-1/2) rho tau^(-1/2) |phi>` for unit vectors `|psi>` and
   `|phi>` that attain the cross norm: one rank-one accepting effect and one fixed pure
   output state. The proof needs `tau` strictly positive (p. 6) and, in infinite
   dimensions, a truncation of the vector `tau^(-1/2)|phi>` and a limit (p. 8; the
   truncation argument, pp. 12-13).
6. [P] Eq. (14) (p. 6): `F' = Tr[Phi_(tau,Q) A_tau]/Tr[Q(tau)] = Tr[Sigma_(tau,Q) A_tau]` with
   `Sigma := Phi/Tr Phi`. `Tr[Q(tau)]` is the total acceptance probability under
   the prior `p_x`. The paper states no bound on the acceptance of the optimal
   classical operation and places no acceptance constraint in Eqs. (3) and (4).
7. [P] Theorem 5 and Eq. (8) (p. 4; proof pp. 11-12): for a Gaussian prior of
   variance `1/lambda` over coherent states and gain `g`, the CFT is
   `F~_(g,lambda) = (1 + lambda)/(1 + lambda + g^2)` "both for deterministic and probabilistic
   protocols", reached by heterodyne measurement and conditional preparation of
   `|g alpha_hat/(1 + lambda)>` (Eq. 26, p. 11). The proof evaluates `||A_(g,lambda,tau)||_x`
   at `sigma = tau` (p. 11) and obtains the probabilistic bound from Eq. (14) (p. 12). At `g = 1`
   the value reduces to the coherent-state storage and teleportation benchmark of
   Hammerer, Wolf, Polzik and Cirac (reference 23; p. 3). Footnote 35 (p. 5) gives the
   uniform-prior values `F = 1/4` and `F~ = 1/5` at `g = 2`, `lambda = 0`.
8. [P] Eq. (7) and Theorem 4 (p. 3): the optimal probabilistic quantum value is
   `(lambda + 1)/g^2` for `lambda <= g^2 - 1` and 1 above, reached by truncated
   number-diagonal operations `Q_N` (proofs pp. 10-11). The Introduction (p. 1)
   states the motivation: for probabilistic amplifiers the photon number is
   necessarily finite, and "it would be unfair" to compare an experiment with the lower
   CFT of the uniform prior.
9. [P] Fair sampling (p. 4). The Zavatta et al. experiment (reference 22) tests three
   amplitudes with fidelities near 0.99, 0.91 and 0.67 against a CFT of 50 % at
   `g = 2`, `lambda = 3`. The authors write that the small number of amplitudes
   precludes an accurate analysis, that an average over few `alpha` is sensitive to
   fluctuations, and that the ideal setup draws the inputs from the Gaussian prior.
   The benchmark of Eqs. (3) and (4) assumes that the tested inputs follow the
   stated prior `p_x`.
10. [P] The quoted deterministic fidelity of 85 % at `g = 2`, `lambda = 3` (pp. 2
    and 4) disagrees with the printed Eq. (6), which gives 3/4 there; the CFT of 50 %
    agrees with Eq. (8). The classical values are unaffected.

## Reproduction and repository reading

[I] Eq. (4) is the repository's pooled, free-post-selection figure. The
`pooled` figure of
[`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`](../../../scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py)
maximizes `sum_j p_j f_j/sum_j p_j s_j` over separable Choi operators without a
success profile, which is `F~^prob` of Eq. (4) for `rho_j = psi_j`. Two independent
computations agree. A numerical maximization of the cross norm of `A_tau` over
product vectors and the repository's solver (`six_state_benchmark`, certified
value) give 0.66667 for the uniform six-state ensemble, 0.75345 for the
non-uniform priors +X 0.30, -X 0.10, +Y 0.15, -Y 0.15, +Z 0.20, -Z 0.10, and 0.85355
(`(2 + sqrt 2)/4`) for the three positive Pauli eigenstates at equal weight. The
operator norm of Eq. (2) is 1 for the six-state ensemble, the teleportation
fidelity of an ideal postselected protocol.

[I] Postselection alone leaves the six-state bound at 2/3. `F~^det` of Eq. (3) is
at most the Eq. (4) value (take `sigma = tau`) and at least 2/3 (measure `Z`,
prepare the outcome), and Eq. (4) evaluates to exactly 2/3 at `tau = I/2`. The design's
sentence "Postselection alone leaves the bound intact"
([benchmark paragraph](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md))
follows from the theorem for the uniform six-state ensemble; the theorem does not
extend it to unequal priors, which raise the value (0.75345 above), or to a
sub-ensemble such as the three positive axes (0.85355). Any average over fewer
than the six inputs or over an unequal input mix therefore carries its own higher
threshold, and 2/3 is the wrong comparison for it.

[I] Free postselection and fixed acceptance. Eq. (4) is a supremum over operations
with no acceptance constraint, and by Eq. (14) the value of `F'` does not depend on
the overall scale of `Q~`. A protocol of the paper's class with a prescribed acceptance
probability is one such operation, so its fidelity is at most `F~^prob`, and
the six-state value 2/3 caps it at every acceptance. The paper does not construct
the optimal fixed-acceptance classical operation: a fixed success profile, an
acceptance floor and the dependence of the optimum on acceptance are outside Eqs.
(3) and (4), which is the case that [`classical_benchmark`](../../../scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py)
treats through its `success` argument and that
[Gündoğan et al.](1201.4149.md) treat for photon-number-resolving devices.

[I] Photon-number sectors. When the input of a pulse is `rho_x = sum_N w_N psi_x^(N copies)`
with sectors orthogonal and a fixed weight `w_N` independent of `x`, `tau` and
`A_tau` are block-diagonal over the input sectors and each block equals the
operator of the `N`-copy ensemble with its own average state. A unit input vector
that spans sectors weights the block values by its sector probabilities, so
`F~^prob` is the largest sector value and no weighted mean over sectors enters.
This is the free-selection limit of the efficiency-constrained benchmarks of
[Specht et al.](1103.1528.md) and Gündoğan et al., obtained here for any ensemble.
For the six Pauli eigenstates with `N` identical copies and a single-qubit target, a
numerical maximization of the cross norm gives 0.66667 for `N = 1`, 0.75000 for
`N = 2` and 0.83333 for `N = 3`. The uniform-sphere value `(N+1)/(N+2)` of Massar
and Popescu therefore holds for the six-state ensemble at `N = 1` and `N = 2`
(the octahedron is a spherical 3-design) and falls below the six-state value at
`N = 3` (0.80000 against 0.83333). The `N = 2` result answers the falsifier that
[Specht et al.](1103.1528.md) leave open; the `N = 3` result shows that a finite
ensemble is easier to guess than the sphere once the fidelity polynomial exceeds
degree 3. Each value is bracketed: restarted local search over product vectors gives a lower
bound on the cross norm, and the positive-partial-transpose relaxation of the same program
(a semidefinite program over the Choi operator on the symmetric subspace) gives an upper
bound; the two agree to six digits at `N = 1, 2, 3`, so the values are exact for the
six-state ensemble. The `N = 4` and higher sectors were not computed.

[I] Priors and the attempt profile. `tau` and `A_tau` depend on the priors `p_x`, and
the pooled figure of the rehearsal weights each input by its matching-basis
attempts (`attempts_benchmark` in the module above), so the attempt allocation
enters the threshold through `tau`. The threshold of Eq. (4) is a function of the
prior vector alone with no acceptance parameter: a known allocation fixes it, and an
unknown one needs a restriction on the priors, because the value tends to 1 as the prior
concentrates on one input (0.857, 0.979 and 0.998 at weights 1/2, 0.9 and 0.99 with the
remainder shared, from the repository solver). The paper does not bound the value over a
family of priors.

[I] Scope of the model. Eqs. (1) to (4) treat one use of the process on one member
of a prior-weighted ensemble, with no state carried between uses. A classical
strategy whose measurement or acceptance depends on earlier events, a drifting
prior, or inputs sampled differently from the stated prior (result 9) is outside
the theorem. The paper analyzes none of these.

## Relevance

- [I] Rehearsal benchmark (`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`,
  `scripts/qd_teleportation_rehearsal.py`,
  [the design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md)): high,
  as the source of the pooled free-post-selection definition. Eq. (4) with the
  probabilistic fidelity `F'` of p. 2 states the quantity that `classical_benchmark`
  certifies and gives it a closed form (`||A_tau||_x`) independent of the SDP; the
  reproduction above fixes agreement at three ensembles. The result also
  supplies the free-selection value for photon-number sectors (the largest sector
  value), which bounds from above the multiphoton correction of the design's open
  question for identical-copy sectors, and the six-state values 3/4 at `N = 2` and 5/6 at
  `N = 3`. Under free post-selection an accepted two-copy sector sets the six-state classical
  value to at least 3/4 however small its weight, provided the sectors are orthogonal, the
  weights do not depend on the input, the extra photons are identical copies and the classical
  device resolves photon number. The covariant estimate reaches `(N+1)/(N+2)` on every state, so
  the six-state value is at least that, and a source with unbounded photon number (coherent or
  down-conversion inputs) has no free-selection limit below 1. Only an acceptance constraint of
  the [Specht et al.](1103.1528.md) and [Gündoğan et al.](1201.4149.md) kind restores a
  weight-dependent value.
- [I] What the paper does not settle: a fixed acceptance or success profile (the
  `success` figures), dual-rail photons, accepted events that are not identical
  copies of the input (the failed CNOT branches of the rehearsal), per-event
  dependence, input-dependent acceptance, and an unknown attempt profile. Its
  qubit content is the general Theorems 1 and 2; its worked example, coherent-state
  amplification with a Gaussian prior and Eqs. (6) to (8), concerns continuous
  variables and has no counterpart in the rehearsal.
- [I] Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware, Cayley-Dickson structure
  and factoring resource estimates: none.
