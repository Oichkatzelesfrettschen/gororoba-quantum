## Results needed by the project

1. [P] Model (Sec. 2, pp. 3-7). A line of five classical computers A, X, O,
   Y, B runs `N` trials. In trial `n` the source O sends arbitrary
   information to the measurement stations X and Y, possibly depending on
   all earlier trials; the randomizers A and B each send a setting label 1
   or 2 that the transcript requires to be independent fair coin tosses; X
   and Y each output +1 or -1 computed from what they hold, and neither
   holds the label sent to the other; A and B then publish their labels;
   X, O, Y may exchange anything between trials, and their programs and data
   may be rewritten (items 1 to 5, p. 6). The model demands neither
   stationarity nor independence (p. 4).
2. [P] Statistic (pp. 7-8). `N^=_ab` counts trials with equal outcomes
   under labels `(a,b)`; `rho_ab = (N^= - N^!=)/N_ab`; the CHSH contrast is
   `S = rho_12 - rho_11 - rho_21 - rho_22`; the count statistic that replaces
   it is `Z = N^=_12 - N^=_11 - N^=_21 - N^=_22`, with
   `Z ~ N (S - 2)/8` because each `N_ab` is close to `N/4`.
3. [P] Theorem (p. 8). Whatever the programs, the stored data and the
   communication between trials, `Pr{Z >= k sqrt(N)} <= exp(-k^2/2)` for
   every `k >= 0`. At `N = 15000` and `k = 12.25` the threshold
   `k sqrt(N)` is about `N/10` (the quantum value of `Z/N` is
   `(sqrt(2) - 1)/4`, p. 14) and the bound is at most `1e-32`.
4. [P] Supermartingale structure (Sec. 3, p. 9). With
   `Delta^(n)_ab = 1{X = Y, (A,B) = (a,b)}` and
   `Delta^(n) = Delta_12 - Delta_11 - Delta_21 - Delta_22`, the process
   `Z^(n) = sum_{m <= n} Delta^(m)` starts at 0, has increments in
   `{-1, 0, 1}` (range at most 2), and each increment has nonpositive
   expectation given the past. That is the definition of a supermartingale
   with bounded increments.
5. [P] Hoeffding's maximal inequality (p. 10). A supermartingale that starts
   at 0 with increment range at most 2 obeys
   `Pr{max_{n <= N} Z^(n) >= k sqrt(N)} <= exp(-k^2/2)`. The paper contrasts
   this with the Chebyshev bound `1/k^2` and notes that independent `+-1`
   increments give variance `N` and a normal tail of the same exponential
   order, so no large improvement exists.
6. [P] Random time change (p. 10). Look at the process only at the times when
   `Delta != 0`. By Doob's optional stopping theorem the thinned process is
   still a supermartingale, provided the selection of times never looks
   ahead, and its increment range is still 2. The clock runs faster, so
   `N` becomes `cN` with `c > 1`. The paper states a factor 1.75 on `k`
   (12.25 to 21.5) from the nonzero fraction 0.325 of the quantum
   prediction; it gives no proof that the factor survives a fraction
   that is itself random.
7. [P] Proof of the supermartingale property (p. 11). Condition on the past
   and on whatever the operator placed on the machines between trials. Clone
   the stations; give each clone each possible label; then `X = x_A`,
   `Y = y_B`, and `Delta_ab = 1{x_a = y_b} 1{(A,B) = (a,b)}`. The
   conditional expectation is `1{x_a = y_b}/4` because the randomizers
   still produce independent fair coins given the past. The four indicators
   in `Delta` then sum to 0 or -2, because `(x1 y2)(x1 y1)(x2 y1)(x2 y2) = +1`
   forces an even number of equalities among the four pairs. The paper
   says only this randomness drives the conclusion (p. 11).
8. [P] Memory loophole (Sec. 2, pp. 4-5). A drifting or correlated state
   makes consecutive outcomes correlated and the distributions nonstationary;
   past information accumulates and can propagate between subsystems. An
   analysis that assumes independent (Poisson) statistics, as the 30
   standard deviation claim of Weihs et al. does, does not bind a local
   realist with memory. The probability calculation must run from the local
   realist's side, using only the randomizers' coins as the source of
   probability (p. 5).
9. [P] Setting bias (p. 12). The unbiasedness of the two randomizers is
   not crucial; the Weihs experiment had head probabilities 0.48 and 0.42.
   The paper states no modified bound. Bierhorst gives one for the
   Eberhard-type statistic ([1312.2999](1312.2999.md), Appendix 4, Eq. 43).
10. [P] Post-selection (Sec. 4, p. 15). The 15,000 events of Weihs et al.
    were post-selected from a much larger set of time windows, most of them
    empty. The paper states that Bell's argument fails when the binary
    outcomes come from post-experimental conditioning on other variables.
    Bell's remedy has the source emit an "alert" at random times while
    settings stream continuously; one then selects the measurements that
    follow a saved alert after the run, which the paper calls post-pre-
    selection. Continuous-time extensions need "unbiased detection"
    assumptions that the paper calls untestable.
11. [P] Role of randomization (Sec. 3, p. 12; Sec. 6, pp. 25-26). Fisher's
    randomized design gives control over uncontrollable factors. The Bell
    conclusion requires that the experimenter generate randomness and that
    physically separate randomizers are independent; Bell's conditional
    independence says that pressing button 1 carries only the fact that it
    was button 1.
12. [P] Appendix 1 (p. 27). The 16 counts of the Weihs data sum to
    `N = 14573`. The four correlations recomputed from them give
    `S = 2.728`, in line with the stated 2.73.

## Do the settings need to be randomized and unknown to the device?

Randomized in every trial: [P] yes (item 7, p. 11; items 1, 2 and 5, p. 6).
The conditional expectation of item 7 equals `1{x_a = y_b}/4` only because
the labels `(A,B)` are independent fair coins given everything before the
trial; no other step bounds `Delta`.

[I] If the labels followed a known order, the conditional expectation of
`Delta` in a trial scheduled for cell (1,2) would be `+1{x_1 = y_2}`, which
the machines can set to +1, and no nonpositive drift follows. Machines that
know the order reach `S = 4` with certainty by matching outcomes in cell
(1,2) and mismatching them in the other three cells.

Unknown to the device: [P] in the Bell game each station holds its own label
and lacks the label of the other station (item 3, p. 6); the other wing's
setting carries the locality assumption, and the independence of each label
from the past carries the memory assumption.

[I] The teleportation mapping changes the roles. The input state is the
preparer's label, and the classical model being rejected must not hold it.
The rehearsal sets the input of each iteration in that iteration's compiled
circuit parameters, so the compiled device can behave differently per label.
The design lists input-label access among the conditions that void the 2/3
bound itself
([design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md),
"Teleportation benchmark"); that is a separate failure from memory, and it
arises before any martingale argument. The existing benchmark treats the input
as a quantum state that the classical model cannot read. None of the three
papers supplies or tests that premise for a chip whose input is a compiled
parameter.

[I] Against one fixed classical instrument applied in every trial,
independent of the schedule, the existing Hoeffding radius in
`analyze_results` stays valid under the six fixed blocks. A classical drift
that tracks the block schedule, or a model with memory or schedule knowledge,
reaches conditional fidelity 1 by outputting the block's state; the design
lists this as "Independence is assumed".

## Transfer to the teleportation benchmark

[I] For a classical measure-and-prepare instrument with accepted operators
`M_k` and prepared states `sigma_k`, with the six Pauli eigenstates drawn
uniformly, the design's identities give
`E[1{accepted and correct}] <= (2/3) E[1{accepted}]`
(`sum_i p_i tr[rho_i E(rho_i)] <= sum_k tr M_k / 3` against
`sum_k tr M_k / 2` accepted mass; [design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md),
"Teleportation benchmark"). Then `D_n = 1{accepted and correct} - (2/3) 1{accepted}`
has nonpositive conditional expectation given the past whenever the input of
trial `n` is drawn uniformly, independently of the past, and the instrument
is fixed given the past. `D_n` ranges over an interval of length 1
(`-2/3` to `1/3`), so Hoeffding's maximal inequality for range `r` reads
`Pr{max_n sum D >= t} <= exp(-2 t^2 / (N r^2))`. This is a deduction from the
paper's argument, not a statement in it; it needs per-trial random input
labels.

[I] Hoeffding's one-sided radius for independent `{0,1}` outcomes,
`sqrt(ln(1/alpha)/(2n))`, is the `r = 1` case. The repository function
`hoeffding_radius` in `scripts/qd_teleportation_rehearsal.py` therefore
carries over to the supermartingale null with the same constant when the
count `n` is fixed in advance. The maximal form of item 5 bounds
the running maximum for `n <= N` at a threshold fixed in advance from the cap
`N` (`t = k sqrt(N)`); it does not validate a radius computed at a count `n`
that depends on the data, such as the number of accepted events. For such a
count, preregister a count `m0` and pad with wrong outcomes, the convention of
[1312.2999](1312.2999.md) (item 6 and Appendix 2); the design rule that
stopping is fixed before confirmation is the same requirement.

[I] The random time change of item 6 is the step that the accepted-event
subsequence needs. Accepted events are a vanishing fraction of attempts (the
design models three-click probabilities of 3.3e-5 to 5.0e-5 per attempt), so
a statistic summed over every attempt has little power, and summing over the
accepted attempts only is the same thinning. Bierhorst proves the thinning for
conditional inequalities ([1312.2999](1312.2999.md), Appendix 2, Proposition).

## Relevance

- [I] Memory in the classical null: high for the argument, none for the
  current design. The paper shows how a bounded-increment supermartingale
  tolerates arbitrary drift, adaptive source behavior and communication
  between trials, and it names the one requirement that buys this: per-trial
  random labels. The rehearsal's six fixed blocks do not supply them, so the
  Hoeffding radius in `analyze_results` remains a memoryless-null statement.
  A rehearsal that wants the memory-robust claim needs the input of every
  attempt drawn at random inside one compiled circuit family; that is a
  design change, not an analysis change.
- [I] Unknown attempt profile: partial. The paper counts every trial and
  gives each a label and an outcome, so it presumes `N` attempts that the
  analysis can index. The service reports no optical attempts
  ([Perceval 798](https://github.com/Quandela/Perceval/issues/798)); the
  thinning of item 6 shows that conditioning on accepted events removes the
  need for the acceptance probability, though not for an attempt-level
  inequality.
- [I] Multiphoton input components: none. The inequality is exact for the
  class of single-copy classical instruments; accepted multiphoton sectors
  carry extra input copies and need a new inequality for the larger null
  set ([quant-ph-9807091](quant-ph-9807091.md)).
- [I] Benchmark module `scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`: the paper adds
  no value. The 2/3 bound comes from the separable Choi-cone program there;
  this paper supplies only the tail inequality that would turn an accepted
  count into an error probability under memory.
- [I] Not settled: the paper's CHSH counts are not a teleportation
  statistic; the factor 1.75 of the random time change; the effect of the
  compilation seeds of each iteration on the null; and any systematic
  allowance, because the paper treats the machines as unconstrained.
