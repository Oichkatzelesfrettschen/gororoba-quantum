## Setting and conventions

- [P] Introduction (p. 1): `G` is a complex simple Lie group, `P_S` the parabolic subgroup attached to a set `S` of nodes of the Dynkin
  diagram, `lambda = sum_(i in S) omega_i`, and `G/P_S` has a minimal homogeneous embedding in `P V_lambda`. The paper determines the
  varieties of linear spaces on `G/P_S` and the local differential geometry of the embedding. The keywords include "octonion".
- [P] Section 2.1 to 2.3 (pp. 2-6): fundamental forms, prolongation `A^(l)` of a space of forms, osculating spaces, and the tangent
  space `T_x X` of `X = G/P` as a module over the semisimple part `H` of the Levi factor of `P`. Definition 2.6 (p. 5): a fundamental weight is
  minuscule when the Weyl group acts transitively on the weights of its representation, and cominuscule when the highest root has coefficient 1 on the node.
- [P] Section 3.1 table (p. 8): the minuscule `G`-varieties (four infinite series and two exceptional spaces), among them the Grassmannian `G(k, n+1)`,
  the quadrics, the Lagrangian Grassmannian, the spinor variety, the Cayley plane `OP^2 = E_6/P_1` and `G_omega(O^3, O^6) = E_7/P_7`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 3.1 (p. 8, strict prolongation): for a minuscule variety `X = G/P_(omega_i) subset P V_(omega_i)` and `x` in `X`, the higher fundamental
  forms are the prolongations of the second, `|FF^(k+1)| = |FF^2|^((k-1))` for `k >= 2`; Corollary 3.6 (p. 11) gives
  `Base |FF^k| = sigma_(k-1)(Base |FF^2|)` and `|FF^k| = I_k(Base |FF^k|)`.
- [P] Proposition 3.4 (p. 10): the tangent and normal spaces of the classical irreducible minuscule varieties and of the two
  exceptional ones, as `H`-modules; for `OP^2` the second normal space is `W_(omega_6)` and for `G_omega(O^3, O^6)` it is `W_(omega_1)`.
- [P] Theorem 4.3 (p. 14): for `X = G/P_S` in its minimal embedding, `F_1(X)` is the product over `j` in `S` of the spaces `F_1^j(X)` of lines of class `alpha_j`; for `alpha_j` not an exposed short root, `F_1^j(X) = G/P_(S minus alpha_j union N(alpha_j))`, and for an exposed short root it is the union of an open `G`-orbit and its boundary.
  Theorem 4.9 (p. 16) describes the spaces `F_k^alpha(X)` of higher linear spaces for a root that is not an exposed short root as disjoint unions of homogeneous varieties, and Theorem 4.14 (p. 17) states that for an exposed short root `F_k^alpha(X)` is a finite union of `G`-orbits (at least two).
- [P] Proposition 4.17 (p. 18): the case where the closed orbit `X_end` is an adjoint variety, that is `V = g`. Item 3 states that
  `Lambda^[2] g` is the kernel of the bracket `Lambda^2 g -> g`, that the adjoint varieties attached to elementary representations
  are those of the exceptional groups `(G_2, omega_2)`, `(F_4, omega_1)`, `(E_6, omega_2)`, `(E_7, omega_1)`, `(E_8, omega_8)`, and that
  `V_(end-1) = Lambda^[2] g` for each except `G_2`. Example 4.16 (p. 18): `E_6/P_2` is the adjoint variety, with `V_(omega_2) = e_6`.
- [P] Remark 4.18 (p. 18): for type `A_n` the adjoint variety `G/P_(1,n)` is the flag variety of lines in hyperplanes of `P^n`; its
  space of lines is the disjoint union of two flag varieties, embedded non-minimally because `Lambda^[2] sl_(n+1) = V_(2 omega_1 + omega_(n-1)) + V_(omega_2 + 2 omega_n)`.
- [P] Section 6 (pp. 21-26): Proposition 6.1 (p. 22) identifies `G_2/P_1` with the quadric `Q^5` in `P(Im O)`; Proposition 6.3 (p. 22) gives the space of lines on `G_2/P_1` dimension seven and `G_2/P_2` (the `G_2`-homogeneous lines) dimension 5, and Remark 6.4 (p. 22) calls `G_2/P_2` an adjoint variety because `V_(omega_2)` is the adjoint representation of `G_2`.
  Proposition 6.5 (pp. 23-24) shows `F_4/P_4` is a generic hyperplane section `{tr = 0}` of the Cayley plane in the projectivized traceless part `J_3(O)_0` of the Jordan algebra, with a nine-dimensional base locus of the second fundamental form; Proposition 6.7 (pp. 24-25) gives the space of lines on `F_4/P_4` dimension 23, Proposition 6.8 (p. 25) lists its maximal linear spaces,
  Proposition 6.9 (p. 25) treats `F_4/P_3` (the `F_4`-homogeneous lines, dimension 20), Proposition 6.10 (p. 25) gives the space of `P^1`'s on `F_4/P_3` dimension 24, and `F_4/P_(2,4)` has dimension 22 (p. 26).
- [P] The paper states no degree of any adjoint variety, and no dimension or degree of the adjoint variety of `SL_4`; Remark 4.18 is the
  only statement on type `A_n` adjoint varieties in the pages read.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 findings record that the degree-20 component `P` of the W8 -> N- fiber has projective dimension 5 and 9 linear generators in 24
  ambient coordinates ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)). Remark 4.18 describes the adjoint variety of
  `SL_4` as the flag variety of lines in hyperplanes of `P^3`, which the repository reads as the incidence divisor `{(p, H) : p in H}` of bidegree (1,1) in `P^3 x P^3*`; it then has dimension 5, and its degree is
  `(H_1 + H_2)^6 = C(6,3) = 20` in the Segre embedding (repository arithmetic, not a statement of the paper); it spans `P(sl_4) = P^14`, and
  24 - 9 = 15 = dim sl_4 variables remain if the 9 linear generators are independent. Dimension, degree and span agree. The match of three numbers is a lead, not an identification; the
  paper contributes only the description of the variety, and the identification needs the quadric equations of the component compared with those of the incidence divisor.
- [I] The octonionic models of `G_2/P_1` and `F_4/P_4` (Propositions 6.2 and 6.6) concern the 8-dimensional octonions and the
  27-dimensional exceptional Jordan algebra, not the sedenions or higher Cayley-Dickson algebras; the repository's Cayley-Dickson work starts at 16 dimensions.
