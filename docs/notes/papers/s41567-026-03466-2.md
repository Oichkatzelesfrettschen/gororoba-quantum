## Main claims

Microwave-drive-induced a.c.-Stark shifts dominate the fidelity loss under
parallel driving of a five-qubit silicon array on one shared control line, and
a calibration that measures only pairwise phase shifts and sums them removes
the loss. [from-paper, abstract, PDF p. 1]

- Individually addressed primitive X and Y gate fidelities exceed 99.99 percent
  for all five qubits, some approaching 99.999 percent. [from-paper, abstract,
  Fig. 2a]
- Fidelities stay above 99.99 percent during simultaneous operation of up to
  three qubits and remain near the 99.9 percent fault-tolerance target at five
  qubits. [from-paper, abstract, Figs. 4h and 5c]
- Calibration cost for N simultaneously driven qubits is N(N - 1) pairwise
  measurements instead of the 2^N - N - 1 simultaneous driving combinations.
  [from-paper, Discussion, PDF p. 8]
- Crosstalk phase shifts add linearly across drivers. [from-paper, Fig. 4d,
  PDF p. 6]

## Methods

- Device: five quantum dots in an isotopically enriched Si/SiGe quantum well
  (9 nm, residual Si-29 at 800 ppm), three overlapping aluminium gate layers,
  two charge sensor dots at the array ends, cobalt micromagnet; one microwave
  gate (the MW screening gate) drives all five qubits by EDSR, the shared control
  line. Charge configuration (3,1,1,3,1). [from-paper, Fig. 1a, Methods "Device
  fabrication"]
- Initialization and readout: pairs Q1,2 and Q4,5 by Pauli spin blockade (PSB)
  with feedback initialization, 10 us integration; Q3 by quantum non-demolition
  readout (CROT with Q2 as target, PSB on Q1,2, repeated twice); the register
  starts in |up down down up down> and one cycle returns three bits.
  [from-paper, Fig. 1b-e]
- Field and spectrum: B = 0.42 T, qubit spacing about 150 MHz so that adjacent
  qubits are individually addressable. [from-paper, Fig. 1f]
- Pulses: Kaiser-window control pulses suppress spectral-leakage crosstalk;
  they require a linear Rabi-versus-amplitude regime, confirmed up to fR of
  about 8 MHz. Gate times of the primitive pi/2 gates are 125, 83, 83, 83 and
  125 ns for Q1 to Q5; idle time between gates is 2 ns, the hardware limit.
  [from-paper, Fig. 1h, Fig. 2a-b]
- Benchmarking: Clifford set from X and Y pi/2 gates, identity replaced by
  4 x X or 0 x X; 35 random sequences per Clifford length; error bars are 95
  percent confidence intervals and fidelity uncertainties come from the fit
  covariance. Simultaneous randomized benchmarking (SRB) applies independent
  random Clifford sequences to several qubits at once, combining the pulses with
  a microwave combiner. [from-paper, Fig. 2, Results "Simultaneous operation of
  two qubits"]
- Phase calibration: Hahn-echo sequence on the target while the other qubit
  applies 4N X gates; the accumulated crosstalk phase per gate, Delta phi_ij on
  qubit i when qubit j runs, is offset in the microwave phase of following gates
  (fixed compensation per pulse). [from-paper, Fig. 3a-e]
- Amplitude calibration: 16 simultaneous X gates followed by 1 or 3 more, which
  place the qubits in |+x,+x> or |-x,-x>; a Nelder-Mead optimizer minimizes the
  summed probability difference between the two sequences. [from-paper,
  Fig. 3f]
- Five-qubit readout: tomographic readout (ZZ and ZI measurements) on the PSB
  pairs, each random sequence repeated twice. [from-paper, Fig. 5a-b]

## Quantitative results

- Coherence at B = 0.42 T: T2* of about 10.3 us (average), Hahn-echo decay of
  about 60 us, driven coherence TR2 of about 100 us, idle dephasing about 10 us;
  residual exchange J below 16.6 kHz for all neighboring pairs. [from-paper,
  Fig. 1g, PDF p. 3, Extended Data Fig. 1]
- Individually addressed primitive fidelity Fp (Fig. 2a): Q1 99.9929(11), Q2
  99.9979(2), Q3 99.9982(2), Q4 99.9957(4), Q5 99.9943(7) percent. Interleaved RB
  gives similar X and Y fidelities (Extended Data Fig. 3). [from-paper, Fig. 2a]
- RB fidelity of Q3 stays above 99.99 percent over a detuning window of about
  0.4 MHz, wider than the typical frequency fluctuation of about 0.1 MHz.
  Optimal gate speed gives about 99.999 percent for Q1 to Q4 and about 99.995
  percent for Q5. [from-paper, Fig. 2c-d]
- Two-qubit SRB on Q3 and Q4 (Fig. 3g): Q3 99.9834(57) percent without and
  99.9912(25) percent with calibration; Q4 99.9966(7) percent without and
  99.9970(4) percent with calibration. Measured crosstalk phases Delta phi_34 =
  0.0379(2) rad and Delta phi_43 = -0.0026(1) rad; the larger value follows from
  Q3 needing less drive power than Q4 at equal speed. [from-paper, Fig. 3c-g]
- a.c.-Stark phase scaling: the crosstalk phase fits A V_amp^alpha with average
  exponent alpha = 1.91(5), consistent with the quadratic a.c.-Stark scaling;
  for the shift of Q3 under an X2 gate on Q4 (166 ns), the resonance shift is
  about -0.8 MHz with alpha = 2.15(5), A = -3.2(3). The sign is positive when
  the driven qubit lies above the target in frequency and negative below, and
  the phase follows Omega^2/(x - f0) + C against the second-tone frequency.
  [from-paper, Fig. 4d, Extended Data Fig. 6]
- Additivity: phases measured for Q3 with Q2 driven, Q4 driven and both driven
  (Fig. 4a-c) agree with the sum of the two single-driver fits; the three-qubit
  phases for the Q2,3,4 drive include Delta phi_23 = 0.0059(1), Delta phi_32 =
  -0.0234(13), Delta phi_34 = 0.0319(4), Delta phi_43 = -0.0023(7), Delta
  phi_24 = 0.0158(4), Delta phi_42 = -0.0035(4) rad. [from-paper, Fig. 4d-e]
- Three-qubit SRB on Q2, Q3, Q4 at 250 ns: Q2 99.9926(7), Q3 99.9929(16), Q4
  99.9966(7) percent. [from-paper, Fig. 4h, Extended Data Table 1]
- Five-qubit SRB: at 250 ns Extended Data Table 1 lists 99.96(1), 99.92(3),
  99.977(8), 99.93(4), 99.82(7) percent for Q1 to Q5; at 500 ns 99.978(4),
  99.91(2), 99.96(2), 99.94(5), 99.8(1) percent. Four of five qubits exceed
  99.9 percent. A full calibration (phase measured while the other four qubits
  run) matches the pairwise calibration in performance. [from-paper, Fig. 5c,
  Extended Data Table 1]
- Power scaling: the similar fidelities at 250 and 500 ns suggest about 99.9
  percent for ten qubits at 500 ns, where total microwave power equals the
  five-qubit 250 ns case; correction becomes negligible for strongly detuned
  pairs because phase shifts scale inversely with detuning. [from-paper,
  Results and Discussion, PDF p. 7-8]
- Limits: the calibration assumes qubits are effectively uncoupled during
  microwave control; with exchange active, conditional phases arise and need
  separate calibration. [from-paper, Discussion, PDF p. 8]

## Relation to this repository

Retained run: `results/quantum_inspire/runs/2026-10-16T211635Z-tuna_17/`
(`analysis.json`, `cases.json`, produced by
`scripts/qi_crosstalk_teleport_cases.py`). Everything below is inference.

- [inference] The additivity case is a transmon translation of the pairwise
  phase model. The target runs X90, an explicit idle, and a quadrature Y90, so
  the outcome probability P1 = (1 + sin phi)/2 reads the accumulated z rotation
  phi. Neighbors A and B idle or run an even number of X gates (40) inside the
  idle in the configurations none, A, B and AB. The pairwise model sums phases,
  so phi(AB) - phi(A) - phi(B) + phi(none) is the falsifier of additivity,
  the same quantity that Fig. 4d tests with the dashed sum curve. Five disjoint
  (T, A, B) stars of the Tuna-17 coupling map ran.
- [inference] Retained residuals (`analysis.json`, `additivity`): 0.018, -0.053,
  0.032, -0.054, 0.021 rad with z-scores 1.15, -3.38, 2.04, -3.43, 1.35 against
  sigma = 0.0156 rad. Two stars sit beyond three sigma, so additivity holds to
  a few hundredths of a radian but is not statistically confirmed at this shot
  budget; the four configurations ran as sequential jobs, so drift between jobs
  enters every difference. The retained phase shifts per star range from about
  0.01 rad to 0.70 rad over 40 drive pulses, which is 0.0003 to 0.017 rad per
  pulse, the same order as the paper's per-gate 0.0379 rad and -0.0026 rad
  between Q3 and Q4.
- [inference] The simultaneous-drive crosstalk finding corresponds to the
  paper's statement that simultaneous operation shifts the optimal drive
  amplitude beyond idle-qubit phase shifts (residual driven-gate errors). The
  script's driver outcomes read the same effect: a driver's own probability
  after its even X count changes when the other driver runs (the first star
  shows a change of 0.197 and 0.847 for drivers 1 and 2 with z-scores 48.5
  and 153.6), which a pure z-phase additivity model does not predict. A
  residual ZZ coupling or a spectator-induced amplitude shift is a candidate;
  the data do not discriminate.
- [inference] The paper attributes its phase shifts to the microwave a.c.-Stark
  effect on a shared control line of a spin array. The retained transmon run
  establishes no mechanism for its phase shifts, so it tests the additive-phase
  model and says nothing about the Stark exponent alpha = 1.91(5) or the
  N(N - 1) versus 2^N - N - 1 calibration count beyond the three-body residual
  per star.
- Relevance (inference): (a) calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: low,
  pairwise-measured crosstalk phases applied as virtual-Z offsets are a
  calibration pattern that a compiler could use, and the paper's calibration
  count contrasts with full simultaneous-drive characterization; (b)
  Cayley-Dickson structure: none; (c) factoring resource estimates: none.

## Open points and discrepancies

- The abstract states that five-qubit fidelities remain above 99.9 percent;
  Extended Data Table 1 lists Q5 at 99.82(7) percent (250 ns) and 99.8(1)
  percent (500 ns), and the main text says four of five qubits exceed 99.9
  percent. The abstract rests on the four-qubit statement or on rounding of Q5.
  [from-paper, abstract, Extended Data Table 1]
- The Q1 83 ns fidelity in Extended Data Table 1 is 99.96(2) percent, below the
  99.99 percent individual claim, because the individual Fig. 2a value for Q1
  uses the 125 ns gate. [from-paper, Fig. 2a, Extended Data Table 1]
- The Supplementary Information is not retained; heating and micromagnet design
  values attributed to it stay unread.
