# Strobel et al. -- Quantum-dot teleportation with a purity model, a wanted-event ratio and a 2/3 comparison

[P] identifies a statement in the source. [I] identifies a repository
application or deduction. The paper supplies a teleportation experiment between two remote
quantum dots with three conjugate inputs, temporal postselection, a process model with a
single-photon-purity (`g2(0)`) term and a comparison of the three-state mean with 2/3; this note
implements no benchmark and no hardware case.

## Results needed by the project

The paper writes `f^(a->a)` for the fidelity of the teleported state to the input `a`
(`a` in {H, D, R}), `f_bar` for their mean, `V_TPI` for the remote two-photon-interference
visibility, `M_p` for the polarization mode overlap, `k` for the ratio of wanted to all
detected three-fold coincidences, `B` for the single-photon-source brightness at the
detector and `g2(0)` for the zero-delay second-order correlation.

1. [P] Experiment (pp. 1-3; Methods, pp. 7-8). QD1 emits the single photon to be
   teleported (the biexciton photon, prepared in `|H>`, `|D>` or `|R>`); QD2 emits an
   entangled pair. Two polarization-preserving quantum frequency converters bring both
   biexciton photons to 1515 nm, where a fiber beamsplitter with polarizing outputs
   projects onto `|Psi->` or `|Psi+>` (Eq. 1, p. 2); the exciton photon of QD2 is analyzed.
   Excitation is pulsed two-photon excitation at 304.8 MHz with a biexciton preparation
   fidelity of 45(7) %; converter efficiencies are 49 % and 47 %, each converter adds about
   50 kHz of noise counts, and the telecom detectors have 85 % efficiency and 300 Hz dark
   counts (p. 8). The results discuss the projection on `|Psi->` (other combinations are
   in the supplement, not read).
2. [P] Remote interference (p. 4; Fig. 2). The visibility is limited to `V_TPI = 30(1) %`
   without temporal postselection; the cascade bound is `V_TPI,max = gamma_XX/(gamma_XX +
   gamma_X) = 59 %`; `V_TPI,70ps = 79(1) %` for a 70 ps window centered on zero delay,
   the minimal window of the teleportation experiment (Fig. 2c). Linewidths are 4.3(1) and
   5.2(4) GHz with a spectral offset of 0.43(27) GHz from pump drift.
3. [P] Temporal postselection (p. 4). Three-photon coincidences between the Bell
   measurement and photon 3 are evaluated for windows from 70 ps to 290 ps, "the latter
   being a trade-off between covering the entire interference peak and minimizing
   unwanted background coincidences", with averaged coincidence rates of 0.11(3) mHz at
   70 ps and 2.5(7) mHz at 290 ps.
4. [P] Fidelities (p. 5; Fig. 3). At 70 ps, `f^(H->H) = 0.860(23)`, `f^(D->D) = 0.630(38)`
   and `f^(R->R) = 0.672(34)`. For longer windows `f^(H->H)` stays above 0.7, and `f^(D->D)`
   and `f^(R->R)` drop to about 0.55 and 0.6. Fidelities to the two conjugate states deviate
   by up to 0.1 from 1/2. Error bars are one standard deviation of a Monte-Carlo
   distribution of 10,000 runs under Poissonian statistics.
5. [P] The mean and the 2/3 comparison (p. 6; Fig. 4). `f_bar = (f^(H->H) + f^(D->D) +
   f^(R->R))/3`. The authors state that repeating the experiment with every state on the
   Poincare sphere would give `f_bar` as the expected average, so they take it as the figure
   of merit, successful above the classical threshold 2/3. `f_bar` at 70 ps is 0.721(33), which
   the text puts 1.6 standard deviations above 2/3; it stays above 2/3 up to 190 ps and falls to a
   steady 0.630(12) for long windows. The abstract calls 0.721(33) "significantly above the classical limit"
   and the Introduction (p. 2) says the value "conclusively proves" success; the text of
   p. 6 gives 1.6 standard deviations and states that success is shown "when temporal
   post-selection is applied".
6. [P] Classical correlations (p. 6). "Higher teleportation fidelities of `f^(H->H)` are a
   result of the BSM basis choice (`|H>`, `|V>`) leading to additional classical correlations
   between the BSM and Photon 3." The diagonal imbalance `rho_HH` against `rho_VV` of the
   teleported state is attributed to classical correlations favoring `|H>`.
7. [P] Output-state model (Methods, p. 8, Eq. 2): `rho_out(rho_in) = M_p rho_teleported(rho_in)
   + (1 - M_p) |H><H|`, with `M_p` in [0.8, 0.9] (`M_p^FSS = 0.94`). The added `|H><H|` term is
   "specific to the chosen alignment procedure, representing a worst-case scenario". The
   process formalism (references 33 and 61) includes limited single-photon purity,
   fine-structure splitting and decoherence; the parameters of Fig. 3 are `M = 0.85`,
   `T_2 = 35` ps, `tau_HV = 5` ns, `tau_ss = 5` ns, `tau_X = 171` ps, `V = 79 %` and `k = 0.85`
   (Fig. 3 caption).
8. [P] Purity model and wanted-event ratio (Methods, p. 9, Eq. 3). The ratio of unwanted
   three-fold coincidences is derived from the measured `g2(0)` of all three interacting
   photons in individual time windows; the two-photon component is bounded by
   `p_2 <= [1 - B g2(0) - sqrt(1 - 2 B g2(0))]/g2(0)` (reference 64), with `B` the source brightness at
   the detector and the assumption `p_(>=3) = 0` ("see supplement", not read). Every combination of
   detector clicks that yields an unwanted three-fold coincidence is counted, and the ratio of
   wanted to all detected coincidences for a 70 ps window is `k_70ps = 0.85`.
9. [P] Sources of non-unity fidelity (p. 6). "The two main contributors" are the limited
   interference visibility and multiphoton contributions from the frequency-conversion
   process (anti-Stokes Raman scattered photons at the target wavelength). For larger
   windows the visibility drops while the fraction of background counts increases, which
   lowers the fidelities.
10. [P] Table of Fig. 5 (p. 7): the modeled mean fidelity without temporal postselection, for
    FSS 2.1 or 0 ueV and `g2(0)` nonzero or zero, with `M_p = 1`, `T_2 = 35` ps, `tau_HV =
    tau_ss = 10` ns, `k = 0.85` when present. At `V_TPI = 30 %` (this experiment) the entries
    are 0.58, 0.58, 0.60, 0.60; at 59 %, 0.67, 0.67, 0.70, 0.72; at 83 %, 0.73, 0.77, 0.83, 0.85; at
    100 %, 0.84, 0.87, 0.95, 0.99 (columns: FSS 2.1 with `g2(0)` nonzero, FSS 0 with nonzero,
    FSS 2.1 with `g2(0) = 0`, FSS 0 with `g2(0) = 0`). The text states that "TPI visibility
    was identified as the most crucial parameter".

## Reproduction and repository reading

[I] The `g2(0)` entry is a model parameter in this paper. Multiphoton emission reaches the
fidelity through `k`, the wanted-over-all three-fold ratio, which Eq. (3) and the
per-window `g2(0)` data supply, and through the `g2(0)` column of the Fig. 5 table. At
unity visibility the table puts the effect of a nonzero `g2(0)` at 0.11 to 0.12 of fidelity
(0.84 against 0.95 at FSS 2.1 ueV), and 0.02 at `V_TPI = 30 %` (0.58 against 0.60). To first order
Eq. (3) reads `p_2 = B^2 g2(0)/2`: the two-photon weight is the second-order correlation times
half the squared brightness. The paper reports no `B` and no per-window `g2(0)`
in this PDF (the supplement may). The repository's design lists accepted multiphoton
mass at `g2` 1.1 % and no registered bound for it
([the design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md), review of
2026-10-26); this paper treats the same quantity as a noise fraction (`1 - k`) inside a
fidelity model, not as extra input copies inside a classical bound.

[I] The three-state mean equals the sphere average only for a map with `t_x + t_y + t_z = 0`.
For an affine qubit map `r_out = T r_in + t` the fidelity to the input state with Bloch
vector `r` is `(1 + r.(T r + t))/2`. The sphere average is `(1 + tr T/3)/2`, and the mean
over the three positive axes is the sphere average plus `(t_x + t_y + t_z)/6`. The paper's
claim that `f_bar` is the expected sphere average (result 5) therefore needs a vanishing
offset. Its own model carries one: the `(1 - M_p)|H><H|` term of Eq. (2) is an offset
`t_z = 1 - M_p` toward the axis of `|H>`, and the text names classical correlations that
favor `|H>` (result 6). For `M_p` in [0.8, 0.9] the offset inflates the mean by 0.017 to
0.033, so the sphere-average estimate from 0.721(33) is 0.688 to 0.704, a margin of 0.021 to
0.038 over 2/3, which is 0.6 to 1.1 standard deviations of the printed uncertainty. This
estimate takes the unital part of the map as given and uses `M_p` as printed; its
confidence is medium, to rise with six-input data (the opposite inputs `|V>`, `|A>`, `|L>`)
that give the offset directly.

[I] An ensemble reading of the three-state test has a higher classical threshold. If the three
inputs `|H>`, `|D>`, `|R>` at equal weight are the whole ensemble, the repository solver
([`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`](../../../scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py)) gives a
classical optimum of 0.8536 under free post-selection and 0.8333 for a device that always
outputs; an independent semidefinite program over entanglement-breaking maps returns
0.83333 for the deterministic value (the solver run for that profile printed an
accuracy warning). The measured 0.721 lies below both, so the comparison with
2/3 stands only through the process reading of result 5, and a verdict that uses
three inputs states which reading it takes. The classical value for the full six-state
ensemble is 2/3 ([Chiribella and Xie](1301.1170.md)).

[I] The BSM-basis correlation is the basis-dependent selection of the design. The Bell
measurement distinguishes `|Psi+>` from `|Psi->` in the `|H>`/`|V>` basis, so events
heralded by one outcome correlate with an input in that basis (result 6). The design lists
"basis-dependent selection" among the conditions that void the 2/3 bound
([the design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md), "Teleportation
benchmark"). The paper identifies the effect and its worst-case alignment and does not
subtract it from the fidelity.

[I] Temporal postselection and the window. Windows of 70 to 290 ps trade `V_TPI` (79 % to
30 %) against rate (0.11 to 2.5 mHz) in one family; `f_bar` is 0.721 at the smallest window,
which the paper calls minimal, and 0.630(12) at the longest. The 70 ps window is
reported as the headline although the paper does not state it was fixed before the data;
the 1.6 standard deviations do not account for a choice among windows. For a free window
choice the threshold of [Chiribella and Xie](1301.1170.md) (free post-selection, any
input-independent window) stays 2/3 for the six-state ensemble, and a window chosen on the
measured fidelity is not input-independent.

[I] Attempts are knowable here. The excitation repetition rate is 304.8 MHz, so 0.11 mHz and
2.5 mHz correspond to about 3.6e-13 and 8.2e-12 accepted three-fold events per excitation
pulse for the `|Psi->`-heralded data at 70 ps and 290 ps. The paper does not use the
per-attempt acceptance as a denominator; the classical comparison is the unconstrained 2/3.
(The count uses the printed averaged rates and assumes they are per excitation pulse of
QD2; the supplement, not read, carries the count rates.)

[I] Per-event dependence. The three inputs run as separate acquisitions with polarization
compensated "between measurement runs" and a pump-laser drift visible in the spectral offset
(0.43(27) GHz); the printed uncertainties come from Poissonian Monte-Carlo
resampling and list no drift or run-to-run term. Accepted events are scored as independent
draws of one process.

## Relevance

- [I] Rehearsal benchmark (`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`,
  `scripts/qd_teleportation_rehearsal.py`,
  [the design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md)):
  high, as the closest published counterpart. It reports a purity (`g2`) model with a
  wanted-over-all three-fold ratio `k`, temporal postselection, a classical correlation
  that favors the BSM basis, and a three-state mean compared with 2/3, the same four
  issues the rehearsal's benchmark has to settle. It supplies a quantitative effect of
  `g2(0)` on a teleportation fidelity (Fig. 5 table) that the rehearsal's `g2` forecast
  can be compared against, and it exposes the three-state-versus-six-state
  equivalence that the rehearsal avoids by scoring six inputs.
- [I] What the paper does not settle: a classical bound with multiphoton inputs or
  an efficiency or acceptance constraint, the six-state mean (three inputs only), the
  attempt profile as a denominator, dependence between events and drift, and the
  supplement's derivations (not read).
