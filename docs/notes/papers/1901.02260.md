# Anderson et al. -- Teleportation of laser qubits with a telecom C-band quantum dot and a 2/3 comparison

[P] identifies a statement in the source. [I] identifies a repository
application or deduction. The paper supplies a quantum-dot teleportation experiment
with six weak-coherent input polarizations, a measured dot autocorrelation, and a
comparison of postselected mean fidelities with the single-copy value 2/3; this note
implements no benchmark and no hardware case.

## Results needed by the project

The paper writes `g2(0)` for the dot's multiphoton emission probability, `eta`
and `alpha^2` for the dot and laser intensities of the two-photon-interference
model, `beta` for the uncorrelated background, `F_P` for the teleportation fidelity
and `g^(3)_P`, `g^(3)_Q` for third-order correlations at Bob.

1. [P] Protocol (Sec. IV, p. 5). The input is a polarization-encoded weak coherent
   laser qubit (Alice). The exciton photon X of a biexciton-exciton cascade meets it on
   a 99:1 beamsplitter; a polarizing beamsplitter with detectors `D_H` and `D_V`
   performs the Bell-state analysis (Charlie), and the biexciton photon XX is
   analyzed at Bob with `D_P` and `D_Q`. The entangled state is
   `(|H_XX H_X> + e^(i dE tau/hbar) |V_XX V_X>)/sqrt 2` (Eq. 6, p. 5), a state that
   evolves at the fine-structure beat (FSS 5.7 +- 0.2 ueV; maximum entanglement
   fidelity 91.0 +- 0.5 %, Sec. III, p. 3).
2. [P] Inputs and score (p. 5). The six inputs H, V, D, A, R and L are sent as weak
   coherent laser qubits, "six symmetrically distributed polarization states on the
   Poincare sphere". A successful Bell measurement is heralded by `tau_Charlie = 0`,
   and the fidelity is `F_P = g^(3)_P / (g^(3)_P + g^(3)_Q)`, the ratio of third-order
   correlation functions for the expected and the unexpected output polarization. The
   laser intensity is set to match the dot intensity (`eta/alpha^2 = 1` under the
   teleportation conditions, p. 5). The paper states no mean photon number per input
   pulse.
3. [P] Dot multiphoton emission (Sec. III, pp. 4-5). The dot autocorrelation
   follows a 4-level model, `g^(2)_QD(tau) = 1 - (1 + X + Y) e^(-|tau|/tau_1) +
   X e^(-|tau|/tau_2) + Y e^(-|tau|/tau_3)` (Eq. 4, p. 4), corrected for an
   uncorrelated background by `g^(2)_HBT = (g^(2)_QD + 2 beta + beta^2)/(1 + beta)^2`
   (Eq. 5, p. 4). The fit gives the "multi-photon emission probability" `g2(0) = 0.177` at
   saturation power `P_sat` and 0.095 at `P_sat/20` (the second value "data not shown",
   p. 5).
4. [P] Where `g2(0)` enters (pp. 3-5). It enters the two-photon-interference
   correlation function of the dot photon with the laser photon,
   `g^(2)(tau) = 1 + [2 eta alpha^2 (1 + e^(-|tau|/T_2) cos(dE_L tau/hbar) cos^2 phi) +
   eta^2 (g^(2)_QD(tau) - 1) + alpha^4] (eta + alpha^2 + beta)^(-2)` (Eq. 3, p. 3), through
   the term `eta^2 (g^(2)_QD - 1)`. The abstract and Sec. III state that the interference
   visibility is limited by the dot's multiphoton emission and the intensity ratio; the
   measured peaks are 58.6 +- 0.5 % at `P_sat` (`eta/alpha^2 = 1`) and 72.4 +- 0.7 % at `P_sat/20`
   (`eta/alpha^2 = 2.5`), above 85 % of the value expected at `g2(0) = 0` (p. 5, Fig. 2(e)).
   No equation in the paper relates `g2(0)` to the teleportation fidelity.
5. [P] Temporal postselection (p. 5). Third-order correlations are windowed in
   `tau_Charlie` and `tau_Bob`. The mean fidelity is 83.6 +- 2.2 % for an equivalent
   window of 203 ps, the window "corresponding to the most statistically significant
   teleportation", and rises to a maximum of 88.4 +- 4.0 % at 103 ps (the abstract
   writes 88.3 +- 4 %). The text states the trade: smaller windows give higher fidelity
   and fewer teleported photons, larger windows wash the signal out (p. 5).
6. [P] The comparison (p. 5, Fig. 3(c), p. 6). The paper compares the mean fidelity with
   "the classical threshold of 2/3": 7.8 standard deviations above it for 83.6 % and
   5.4 for 88.4 %. It adds that 83.6 % lies 5.1 standard deviations above the limit for
   secure implementations of 6-state secret key sharing protocols (reference 31, Chau).
   Fig. 3(c) shows the six input fidelities and their mean with a dotted line at 2/3, and
   each input lies above it. The paper prints no per-input
   value and no per-input uncertainty.
7. [P] No other benchmark appears. The paper reports no acceptance probability or
   success rate of the windowed events, no accepted-event count, no optical attempt
   count, and no classical bound that depends on efficiency, photon-number law or
   acceptance; the only classical comparator is 2/3 (and the key-sharing limit of
   reference 31).
8. [P] Coherence and indistinguishability (Sec. II, pp. 2-3): `T_2 = 1058 +- 134` ps at
   `0.03 P_sat`, `T_2 = 299 +- 9` ps at `P_sat`, `T_1 = 1.765 +- 0.013` ns and
   `T_2/(2 T_1) = 0.298 +- 0.038`. The teleportation heralding peak has the width of
   the exciton coherence time, and the fidelity along the delay axis oscillates at the
   FSS beat (p. 6).

## Reproduction and repository reading

[I] Multiphoton emission is measured here and left out of the fidelity. The paper
reports `g2(0) = 0.177` at the operating power and scores fidelity directly as a ratio
of measured third-order correlations in a window. The window contains cascade-correlated
three-fold events and the events from additional dot photons and from multiphoton
components of the laser input, and the paper does not separate them: no purity model
or wanted-over-all event ratio appears (contrast [Strobel et al.](2411.12904.md)). The
`g2(0)` value limits the interference visibility (result 4); its effect on `F_P` is
unquantified and is a systematic of the reported mean.

[I] The 2/3 threshold applies under conditions the paper does not verify. For a
single-copy input and the six Pauli eigenstates with equal priors, free
post-selection leaves the classical value at 2/3
([Chiribella and Xie](1301.1170.md), Eq. 4, evaluated in that note), so a temporal
window as such leaves 2/3 intact if the window is fixed independently of the input
and the data. Two conditions fail in the paper as printed. The window sizes of
result 5 are chosen by the measured mean fidelity (the "most statistically
significant" window and the maximizing 103 ps window), so the quoted 88.4 +- 4.0 % is a
maximum over a family of windows, and the quoted standard deviations above 2/3 do not
account for that selection. The input is a weak coherent laser qubit with an unreported mean
photon number, so accepted events can carry two or more input photons, for which the classical value is
`(N+1)/(N+2)` for the uniform sphere ([Specht et al.](1103.1528.md),
[Gündoğan et al.](1201.4149.md)) and 3/4 at `N = 2` for the six states
([Chiribella and Xie](1301.1170.md), computed there). The paper reports neither a
mean photon number nor a photon-number-resolved or efficiency-constrained bound, and
the 5.4 and 7.8 standard deviations therefore compare against the lowest of those
thresholds.

[I] Arithmetic. The quoted margins reproduce from the printed numbers:
`(0.836 - 2/3)/0.022 = 7.7` and `(0.884 - 2/3)/0.040 = 5.4` against the printed
7.8 and 5.4. The paper gives no per-input counts or uncertainties, so the repository's
Hoeffding radius for a mean over inputs (the design's "Teleportation benchmark"
paragraph) cannot be rebuilt from it.

[I] Per-event dependence. Each windowed coincidence is scored as an independent
event of one stationary process. The paper reports a residual laser-dot detuning
(about 2 ueV of overlap accuracy against the 5.7 ueV splitting, p. 6) and a fidelity
that oscillates with delay, and it analyzes no dependence between events, drift of
the detuning or of the dot intensity, and no memory in the classical comparison.

  bound at equal shots and the bound at modeled attempts reports more than this paper
  does.
