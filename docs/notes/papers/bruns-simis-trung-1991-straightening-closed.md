## Setting and conventions

- [P] Section 2.1 (pp. 508-509): a graded ordinal Hodge algebra `A` over `B` on a finite poset `H` is a graded `B`-algebra `A = B[H]` with `B = A_0`, such that the standard monomials on `H` (products `h_1 ... h_n` with `h_1 <= ... <= h_n`) are linearly independent over `B`, and any two incomparable `h, k` in `H` satisfy a straightening relation `hk = sum b_M M` with nonzero `b_M` in `B` and standard monomials `M`,
  each containing a factor `l` in `H` with `l <= h` and `l <= k`. An ideal `Omega` of the poset is straightening-closed (Definition 2.1.1, p. 508) if every standard monomial in the straightening of incomparable `h, k` in `Omega` contains at least two factors from `Omega`.
- [P] Examples 2.1.3 and 2.1.4 (pp. 508-509): in the polynomial ring `B[X]` of an `m x n` matrix of indeterminates, the set `Gamma(X)` of maximal minors is a straightening-closed ideal of the Hodge algebra on the poset of all minors, and so is the subset `Gamma_r(X)` of maximal minors that involve the first `r` columns. Example 2.1.5 (pp. 509-510) introduces ideals of virtual maximal
  minors in determinantal rings, and Example 2.1.7 (p. 510) the ideal `{X_i Y_j}` of two skew linear subspaces.
- [P] Section 2.2 (p. 511): for `I = Omega R` generated by a straightening-closed ideal, the Rees algebra `R[It]` is a graded ordinal Hodge algebra on the poset `H union Omega t` (following Eisenbud and Huneke), with the four types of straightening relations listed on p. 511; Lemma 2.2.1 (p. 511) gives its presentation ideal `J` as generated by 1-forms and, for `h, k` in `Omega`, 2-forms.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 2.2.2 (p. 512): for a graded ordinal Hodge algebra `R` over `B` on `H` and a straightening-closed ideal `Omega`: (a) if `Omega` is linearly ordered, its elements form a d-sequence and the symmetric and Rees algebras of `Omega R` coincide; (b) if every standard monomial in a straightening relation `hk` contains at least two factors, the conclusion (ii) of (a) implies that `Omega` is linearly ordered.
  The paper adds (p. 512) that, since `J` is generated in degrees at most 2, the equality of the symmetric and Rees algebras reduces to `S^2(I) = I^2`.
- [P] Lemma 2.2.3 (p. 513): the filtration `{I^n}` is a standard filtration, `I^n` being the `B`-module generated by the standard monomials with at least `n` factors from `Omega`. Proposition 2.2.4 and Corollary 2.2.5 (p. 513): `gr_I(R)` is a graded ordinal Hodge algebra on the poset of leading forms; if `B` is reduced then so is `gr_I(R)`, and if `B` is Cohen-Macaulay and `H` is wonderful then so is `gr_I(R)`.
- [P] Proposition 2.3.2 and Corollary 2.3.3 (pp. 514-515): each power `I^n` (`I = Omega R`) has a finite filtration with cyclic quotients `R/Psi(h)R`, `h` in `Omega`, where `Psi` is the tail coideal of a standard monomial; the conormal modules `I^m / I^n` have analogous filtrations with quotients `R/(Omega union Psi(h))R`. Remark 2.3.4 (p. 515): the localization of `I^n/I^(n+1)` at an associated prime of `R/I` is free.
- [P] Theorem 3.1.1 (p. 516): for a Noetherian `B` and `I = Omega R`, `I^n = I^(n)` (powers equal symbolic powers) for all `n` if and only if `I_P^n = (I_P)^(n)` for every `n` and every associated prime `P` of `R/(Omega union Psi(h))R`, `h` in `Omega`. Theorem 3.1.2 (p. 516): if `B` is reduced Noetherian, `R/I` has finite homological dimension over `R` and `R` is locally formally equidimensional,
  then `I^n = I^(n)` for every `n`.
- [P] Theorem 3.2.1 (p. 517): under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.1.2 with `B` a normal domain, the Rees algebra `R(I)` is normal and its divisor class group is `Cl(R) direct sum Z^r`, with `r` the number of associated primes of `R/I` of height at least 2. Corollary 3.2.3 (p. 518) gives, for a normal domain `R` over `B` with `B` to `R` unramified in codimension one and `Cl(R_(B minus 0))` a free `Z`-module, `Cl(R(I))` isomorphic to `Cl(B)` plus `Z^(r+s)`, with `r` the number of minimal primes of `gr_I(R)` whose contraction to `R` has height at least two and `s` the rank of `Cl(R_(B minus 0))`.
  Proposition 3.2.4 (p. 518): under additional hypotheses (a wonderful poset, `Omega` a self-covering ideal containing the minimal elements), `gr_I(R)` is Gorenstein if and only if `R` is Gorenstein and `I` is unmixed.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 program studies the Rees algebra `R[It]` and its special fiber for Pfaffian-type ideals. The source supplies the mechanism by which a Rees algebra acquires a Hodge algebra structure when `I` is generated by a straightening-closed ideal: the Rees relations are the 1-forms and 2-forms of Lemma 2.2.1, so the Rees ideal of such an ideal is generated in degrees at most two in the Rees variables
  (Lemma 2.2.1, p. 511, and the remark after Theorem 2.2.2, p. 512, that `J` is generated by forms of degree at most 2). That quadric generation is the same shape the program observes for its fiber relations ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)); the source concerns ideals of minors, and no repository ideal is shown to be straightening-closed in a Hodge algebra.
- [I] The pages read treat maximal minors, virtual maximal minors and two skew subspaces (Examples 2.1.3 to 2.1.7); the pages that bring the Hodge algebra structure to Pfaffians, if any, were not opened, so no statement about Pfaffian ideals is made here.
- [I] Theorem 3.1.1 and Theorem 3.1.2 give conditions (finite homological dimension of `R/I`, local formal equidimensionality) under which `I^n = I^(n)`; the repository's tested claim that the Rees ideal is radical and prime through the origin is a different statement, and no comparison was made.
