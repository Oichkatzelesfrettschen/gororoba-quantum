## Exact lifting theorem

Let `R` be a Henselian discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal `(t)`,
fraction field `K`, completion `Rstar`, and completed fraction field
`Kstar`. Let `F` be finitely many polynomials in finitely many unknowns over
`R`. In positive characteristic the theorem additionally assumes that
`Kstar/K` is separable. The extra separability assumption is confined to
positive characteristic.

Theorem 1, printed p. 59 (PDF p. 2), gives integers `N>=1`, `c>=1`, `s>=0`
depending on the ideal generated by `F`, such that for `nu>=N`,

```text
F(x) = 0 modulo t^nu
```

implies the existence of an exact solution `y` over `R` satisfying

```text
F(y)=0,
y=x modulo t^(floor(nu/c)-s).
```

Corollary 2 states that a finite-type scheme over `R` has an `R`-point if
and only if it has a point modulo `t^nu` for every `nu>=1`. A compatible
inverse system of the chosen approximate points is not an additional
hypothesis in that statement. Section 1 warns that `c` can exceed one;
the cusp example explains why approximation order and retained jet order
cannot be identified. The positive-characteristic separability hypothesis
also has an explicit counterexample when omitted (p. 62).

To preserve coefficients through degree `m-1`, an application needs
`floor(nu/c)-s>=m`. The theorem establishes existence of constants for the
fixed ideal. It supplies no value for the repository's Schur ideal and
does not make an arbitrary finite jet sufficient.

## Application and remaining bridges

The repository can clear the Schur denominators near an invertible center
and combine the numerator equations with the constant-norm equation. The
pivot has nonzero constant term, so it is a unit along a sufficiently local
analytic solution; its nonvanishing must still be retained in the geometric
interpretation. Fixing a first jet can be expressed before lifting by
substituting

```text
x(t)=x0+t*h+t^2*z(t)
```

into that finite polynomial system. A lifting result for the substituted
system then preserves the prescribed center and velocity. There are two
different choices of coefficient ring:

- Over `R=real formal power series`, the theorem concerns formal solutions.
  A formal solution carries no automatic convergence claim.
- To conclude a real analytic arc, one must identify the intended ring of
  convergent one-variable real germs as a Henselian DVR and apply the
  theorem there, or provide a separate valid approximation/convergence
  theorem. This ring bridge is a mathematical prerequisite, rather than a
  consequence of finite rational calculations.

A candidate tangent can need a ramified analytic parameter. For example,
the real cusp `y^2=x^3` has the analytic path `(s^2,s^3)` and the asymptotic
direction `(1,0)` after division by `s^2`. Parameterizing by `x=t` gives
`(t,t^(3/2))`, which is differentiable at zero but not analytic there.
Demanding an analytic path with nonzero first derivative would therefore
exclude this valid tangent direction. A sufficient analytic-arc search for
general tangent directions may need

```text
x(s)=x0+s^q*h+s^(q+1)*z(s), q>=1,
```

and must prove the relevant ramification order and real branch conditions.

The [Schur acceleration certificate](../schur_acceleration_certificates.md)
checks compatibility through order two. Greenberg explains how a proved
lifting threshold or solvability at all orders could close a later bridge;
it does not supply that missing certificate. A proof of tangent-cone
equality still needs every candidate direction to be realized, as well as
the already established necessary inclusion. These are written-mathematics
and exact-computation tasks. A Rocq theorem needs a separate formalization;
QPU measurements cannot settle these polynomial lifting obligations.
