## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Definition (pp. 1-3): a Cayley-Dickson-like product on pairs satisfies `(1, 0)(b, c) = (b, c)`, `(a, b)(1, 0) = (a, b)` and
  `(a, b)(a*, -b) = (c*, -d)(c, d)`-type norm identities with `x x* = x* x = |x|^2` for the involution `(a, b)* = (a*, -b)`; the pair
  is the shuffle of the two sequences, so `i_(2k) = (i_k, 0)` and `i_(2k+1) = (0, i_k)`. The product can be defined in 32 ways;
  eight satisfy the quaternion properties, with `i_1 = i` and either `i_2 = j, i_3 = k` or `i_2 = k, i_3 = j`.
- [P] Section 3 (pp. 3-35): the 32 products `P0 .. P31` with their recursive 2 x 2 block tables and corner, edge, diagonal and
  interior blocks. The product `P31` is `(a, b)(c, d) = (ac - d* b, d a + b c*)` (p. 35). `P0 = (ca - b* d, d a* + b c)`,
  `P1 = (ca - d b*, d a* + b c)`, `P2 = (ca - b* d, a* d + c b)`, `P3 = (ca - d b*, a* d + c b)`, `P4 = (ac - b* d, d a* + b c)`, ... in the
  order of the formulas on pp. 4-35.
- [P] Section 4 (pp. 36-37): the twist `omega(p, q)` on the group `(W, xor)` with `i_p i_q = omega(p, q) i_(p xor q)`; `omega(p, p) = -1` for
  `p > 1` and `omega(q, p) + omega(p, q) = 0` for `0 != p != q != 0` (Eqs. 10 to 12), and the recursive equations (13) to (22) for the
  four block types.
- [P] Sections 5 to 7 (pp. 38-50): the 32 twists `omega_0 .. omega_31`, the five basic twist blocks `C, L, T, D, N` (Eqs. 27 to 31, p. 49)
  with constants `alpha', alpha, beta, gamma, gamma'`, and the generalized twist tree (Table 3, p. 50).
- [P] **Section 8 (pp. 50-53):** the two quaternion properties `omega(p, q) + omega(q, p) = 0` and
  `omega(p, q) = omega(q, pq) = omega(pq, p)`; the first holds for all 32 products; the second fails for `omega_8` to `omega_23`
  in the basis step and for eight more twists (`omega_1, omega_2, omega_5, omega_6, omega_25, omega_26, omega_29, omega_30`) in the
  inductive step, leaving `omega_0, omega_3, omega_4, omega_7, omega_24, omega_27, omega_28,
  omega_31`, exactly those with `alpha' = -alpha` and `gamma' = gamma` (Tables 5 and 6, pp. 53-54).
- [P] Section 9 and Tables 7 and 10 (pp. 54-56): the octonion twist table of the eight products, parameterized by `alpha, beta, gamma`
  (Table 7, with `alpha = 1` giving the traditional quaternion sub-table, Table 8), and the index cycle `pi_0 = (1 3 7 6 5 2 4)`
  with the multiplication table of `P0` (Table 10).
- [P] Sections 10 and 11 (pp. 56-60): recursive structure constants `(1, 2n, 2n + 1)` for `omega_0, omega_3, omega_4, omega_7` and
  `(2n, 1, 2n + 1)` for `omega_24, omega_27, omega_28, omega_31` (Eqs. 35 and 36), the successor rules (Eqs. 38 to 41), the sense
  `sigma(p, q, r)` with `alpha = sigma(1,2,3)`, `alpha beta = sigma(2,4,6)`, `alpha gamma = sigma(2,5,7)` (Eqs. 42 to 44), the permutation
  `pi = (1)(357)(246)` fixing `sigma`, and the Fano plane modes of Table 11 (p. 59), by which three signs determine all 42
  oriented triples of the octonions.

## Convention map and cd_core check

[I] Identification with `cd_core`. The product `P31` is `cd_core`'s doubling `(ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))` in the shuffle
basis, whose index is the bit reversal of `cd_core`'s index. The catalog's labels of the eight quaternion-property products
agree with those of [1707.07318](1707.07318.md) as follows: `P0, P3, P4, P7, P24, P27, P28, P31` there are `P0, P1, P2, P3, P0T, P1T,
P2T, P3T` here, in that order.

[I] Check results: no mismatch with the catalog's counts and constants. A scratch script (ignored `docs/local/`, run with `-I`):

| Paper statement | Result |
| --- | --- |
| eight of the 32 products satisfy the quaternion property | exactly `(f0,g0), (f1,g1), (f2,g0), (f3,g1), (f4,g2), (f5,g3), (f6,g2), (f7,g3)` pass at `D = 32`, i.e. the catalog's `P0, P3, P4, P7, P24, P27, P28, P31` |
| Table 5 constants `alpha, beta, gamma` of the eight | computed from the exact products as `alpha = sigma(1,2,3)`, `beta = alpha sigma(2,4,6)`, `gamma = alpha sigma(2,5,7)` in the shuffle basis at `D = 8`: `(+,-,+), (+,-,-), (+,+,+), (+,+,-)` for `P0, P3, P4, P7` and `(-,-,+), (-,-,-), (-,+,+), (-,+,-)` for `P24, P27, P28, P31`, all equal to the printed columns |
| Table 7 as "the octonion twist table for the eight products" | **not all octonions:** at `D = 8` only `P0, P7, P24, P31` (the products `P0, P3, P0T, P3T` of 1707.07318) are norm-multiplicative with a Moufang basis loop; `P3, P4, P27, P28` have `(e2 + e5)(e3 + e4) = 0`, a zero divisor at dimension 8 |

[I] The catalog does not state which of the eight give octonions; its Table 7 is titled the octonion twist table for all eight
and its Section 9 builds the octonion table from `P0` only. The statement that four of the eight have zero divisors at the
third doubling appears in [1707.07318](1707.07318.md) (p. 13); the exact check above confirms it for the products of this
paper's labels `P3, P4, P27, P28`. The twist blocks of Tables 1 and 2 (`omega_0` to `omega_31`) were not compared with exact
tables for the 24 products that fail the quaternion property.

## Repository bearing

[I] Convention. The catalog's `P31` is the product of `cd_core` and the product that [Wilmot](2512.07210.md) and
[Lemley](2609.11588.md) use; `P7` is its transpose, printed by [Saniga et al.](1405.6888.md). The catalog lists all 32
candidate doubling formulas with their recursive tables, so a claim written for another convention can be translated into
`cd_core` by this identification. The claims `cd-sign-cocycle-degree`, `cd-sign-row-degree`
and `cd-right-row-degree` concern `P31` only.

[I] Zero divisors, annihilator dimensions, Stiefel elements, automorphisms and box-kites: none. Dimensions 32 and above: the
recursive tables and twist trees define the sign table for every `N`; the check reaches `D = 32` for the quaternion
property and `D = 64` in [1707.07318](1707.07318.md). Moreno's Stiefel question (`math/0512517` Section 5): none.

## Boundaries

[I] Pages 36 to 49 (twist tables) were read for structure and not compared; the 24 products that fail the quaternion property
were checked only for the quaternion property, not for their tables. The paper is a catalog without proofs of the 32-way
claim beyond the elimination arguments of Section 8, which the exhaustive evaluation to `D = 32` supports.
