## Main claims and evidence

- [P] A detector is its POVM {pi_n}; the outcome probability is p_(n,rho) = tr[rho pi_n] (Eq. 1), and the interchangeability of rho and pi_n makes detector tomography the dual of state tomography: known probe states characterize an unknown detector.
- [P] The probe set must be tomographically complete, a basis for the operator space of the pi_n. Coherent states |alpha> serve, and their statistics are the detector's Q-function, Q_n(alpha) = p_(n,alpha) / pi (Eq. 2).
- [P] Phase-insensitive detectors have POVM elements diagonal in the number basis (Eq. 3), so the data take the linear form P = F Pi (Eq. 4) with F_(i,k) = |alpha_i|^(2k) exp(-|alpha_i|^2) / k!.
- [P] A plain fit can return unphysical elements, so the reconstruction solves min ||P - F Pi||_2 + g(Pi) subject to pi_n >= 0 and sum_n pi_n = I (Eq. 5), a convex quadratic program whose dual certifies optimality.
- [P] Two detectors are reconstructed: a two-outcome avalanche photodiode and a time-multiplexed detector with eight bins and nine outcomes; the TMD elements agree with a theoretical model at fidelity >= 98.7 % for every n.
