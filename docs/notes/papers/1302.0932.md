## Results needed by the project

1. [P] Common-state assumption (p. 1): tomography assigns one density matrix
   to a sequence of systems. The justification rests on permutation
   invariance (Renner): if the order of the measured systems is randomized
   and a subset is traced out, the remaining systems are approximately
   independently and identically distributed. Measuring each observable of
   the quorum in one long block, the standard practice, voids that argument.
2. [P] Drift example (p. 2): with a source that drifts across three
   sequential groups, `rho_bar_1 = |+><+|`, `rho_bar_2 = |+i><+i|` and
   `rho_bar_3 = |0><0|` give outcome `+1` in every measurement of `sigma_x`,
   `sigma_y` and `sigma_z`. Linear inversion returns a non-positive matrix
   (Eq. 1; eigenvalues `(1 +- sqrt 3)/2`), and maximum likelihood returns a
   projector; neither predicts future outcomes. Correction: Eq. 1 prints the
   off-diagonal entries as `(1+i)/2` above and `(1-i)/2` below the diagonal
   (p. 2), which under the standard `sigma_y` is the matrix of
   `<sigma_y> = -1`; the paper's convention is the conjugate one, and the
   eigenvalues and the conclusion are unaffected.
3. [P] Cause (p. 2): the failures share one mechanism, sample-apparatus
   correlation. Experimental drift is the simple case, in which the sample
   state and the apparatus setting both vary with time. Randomizing the order
   of measurements removes the drift case; other correlations stay.
4. [P] Detection (pp. 2-3, 5): the Akaike information criterion
   `Omega_k = ln L_k - K_k` ranks a single-density-matrix model against an
   alternative with one matrix per time block or per measurement setting. For
   an overcomplete measurement set, repeating each observable in two blocks
   lets the criterion compare the two estimates of each average; the
   alternative of the six-block case has two matrices that are not
   independent, with 4 or 5 parameters in total (p. 5). In the simulation of a
   random-walk angle over 3000 measurements in six blocks of 500 (Fig. 1,
   p. 5), the single-matrix model ranks `Omega_s - Omega_a = -5.07` against
   the five-parameter alternative, significantly worse, and tomography fails.
   A smaller drift leaves the single-matrix model ranked best: the text is on
   p. 5 and the value `Omega_s - Omega_a = 1.38` is in the Fig. 2 caption on
   p. 6. The `Omega` of Eq. 2 is minus one half of the usual Akaike
   criterion (footnote 21, p. 9), and weights follow
   `w_k / w_k' = exp(Omega_k - Omega_k')`.
5. [P] Consequence (p. 7): what fails under strong evidence of drift is the
   independent and identically distributed assumption, so the joint state
   lacks de Finetti form. A relaxed form with a distribution over several
   states (Eq. 17, p. 8) needs its own validation against a richer model.

## Section-by-section reading

1. Sec. I.A (p. 1). [P] An estimate `rho` is useful to predict unmeasured
   systems of the same source. Measurements of an observable yield averages
   of `Tr rho_k O`, linear in `rho_k`, so they fix the average density matrix
   `rho = mean(rho_k)`. Renner's result gives the rigorous form: if the
   systems are permuted at random and a subset is traced out, the remainder
   is approximately i.i.d. Assumption: the order of the measured observables
   is random.
2. Sec. I.B (pp. 1-2), Eq. 1. [P] Measuring one observable in a block voids
   the permutation argument. The drifting source `|+>, |+i>, |0>` returns
   `+1` for every `sigma_x`, `sigma_y`, `sigma_z` outcome; Eq. 1 is the
   resulting linear-inversion matrix. Negativity is a sufficient and not a
   necessary symptom: states mixed as `rho' = rho/2 + 1/4` give a positive
   estimate that is still useless. The unifying cause is sample-apparatus
   correlation, which randomized order removes only for the drift case.
3. Sec. I.C (pp. 2-3), Eq. 2. [P] The criterion `Omega_k = ln L_k - K_k` ranks
   models; only differences count; parameters must buy a likelihood gain of
   at least a factor `e`; the alternative of one matrix per group never
   fits worse than the standard model. Assumption: `3N >> K_k`, otherwise the
   corrected criterion of footnote 22 applies.
4. Sec. II.A (pp. 3-4), Eqs. 3 to 12. [P] For `R^2 = X^2 + Y^2 + Z^2 <= 1` the
   two models tie. For `R > 1`, `Omega_a` is Eq. 5, the standard model is the
   pure state of Eq. 8 (two parameters, footnote 24), and
   `Omega_s - Omega_a` is Eq. 9 with expansion Eq. 10. The standard model
   stays consistent only for `R - 1 <= C/sqrt(N)` (Eqs. 11 and 12).
   Assumptions: equal groups of `N`, single-qubit Pauli settings, the
   expansion valid for `(R-1)^2 << 1 - M^2`.
5. Sec. II.B (pp. 4-5), Eqs. 13 and 14, Figs. 1 and 2. [P] Repeating each
   setting in two blocks makes the set overcomplete, and the criterion then
   detects drift without relying on negativity. The simulation uses
   `p = 0.9`, a random-walk angle `phi`, six blocks of 500: the fast walk
   gives `Omega_s - Omega_a = -5.07`, the slow walk `1.38`.
6. Sec. II.C (pp. 5-6), Eqs. 15 and 16. [P] For two qubits with nine settings,
   9 quantities are measured once and 6 are measured three times, so ad-hoc
   pairwise comparisons become hard, while the criterion works unchanged with
   any one superior alternative.
7. Sec. III (pp. 6-7). [P] The criterion estimates a Kullback-Leibler
   divergence, is asymptotic, and has rank-deficient failure modes when an
   event of nonzero probability goes unobserved. In tomography the future
   observables differ from the quorum, which undermines Akaike's
   assumption, except when the future measurements are the Pauli quorum
   itself (the CSS-code example of p. 7).
8. Sec. IV (pp. 7-8), Eqs. 17 to 19. [P] Strong evidence of drift refutes the
   i.i.d. assumption, so the state lacks de Finetti form; a relaxed form
   `P_a(rho_1, rho_2, rho_3)` over per-block states (Eq. 17) predicts later
   systems as Eq. 18 and mixes with the standard model by weights
   `w = exp(Omega)/sum exp(Omega)` (Eq. 19). Open question stated on p. 8:
   whether a quantum version of the criterion exists for future measurements
   that differ from the quorum.

## Repository use

Each item below is [I] and names the design element it supports.

[I] Gate G4 (entanglement witness), claim level C2, and `bell_fidelity` and
`witness_lower_bound` in `scripts/qd_network_protocol.py`. The witness
`F = (1 + C_XX - C_YY + C_ZZ)/4` pools three correlators that an acquisition
measures in separate settings. If each setting runs in its own block and the
source drifts, the three correlators come from different states. The two-qubit
form of the paper's Eq. 1 example shows the consequence: the separable states
`|++>`, `|+i,-i>` and `|00>`, one per block, give `C_XX = 1`, `C_YY = -1`
and `C_ZZ = 1`, so `F = 1`. The Hoeffding bound of `witness_lower_bound`
assumes a common state and basis-independent selection; the grids of
`measure_and_prepare_grid` and `product_witness_grid` hold one state fixed
across all three axes, so the test
`test_separable_resources_do_not_pass_the_witness` covers the common-state
case and leaves this drift case outside. Interleave or randomize the order of
the three correlator settings, repeat each in at least two separated blocks,
and compare the two estimates of each correlator with the model comparison of
Sec. II.B before pooling.

[I] Gate G1 (observation) and the open setting-to-count association of the
Belenos imaginary-superposition Fourier setting. A count series whose
association with the applied setting is unresolved is a sample-apparatus
correlation in the paper's sense (Sec. I.B), and the paper's remedy is
randomized setting order plus a repeated-block overcomplete design.

[I] The design's inference conditions (units of events, settings,
compilations, jobs and calibration epochs). The paper's Renner argument needs
random order across the unit that drifts; many counts within a few
configurations supply few drift replicates, so the replicate count for a
drift test is the number of independent blocks, not the number of events.

[I] Teleportation statistic `F_succ` (gate G5) and the six Pauli inputs: Sec.
III accepts the criterion's conclusions when the future measurements are
the Pauli quorum, which matches the three output axes of the design's
benchmark; the criterion remains a screen for a single common state and
does not replace the type-I error bound. Pair this note with
[1810.08240](1810.08240.md) (time-uniform confidence sequences, which assume
a stated conditional-mean model) and with
[1907.13608](1907.13608.md) (Proctor et al., detecting and tracking drift),
which tests stationarity directly. Zero-count cells of a threshold histogram
are the rank-deficient case of Sec. III; keep them as data and never drop them
to fit one model.
