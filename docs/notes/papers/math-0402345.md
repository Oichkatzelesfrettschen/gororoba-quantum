## Statements read

- [P] Abstract and introduction (p. 1): twenty-five years after Conway and Norton's paper, the survey reviews the progress on the relationship between finite simple groups and modular functions. It opens with McKay's remark `196 884 = 196 883 + 1` (equation (1.1)) and states that "there is a vertex operator algebra, called the Moonshine module `V^natural`, which interpolates between the left and right sides of (1.1): its automorphism group is the Monster and its graded dimension is the `j`-function (`-744`)".
- [P] Proof of the conjectures (p. 17): verifying, for each conjugacy class `K_g` of the Monster, that the first, second, third, fourth and sixth coefficients of the McKay-Thompson series `T_g` and of the corresponding Hauptmodul `J_(G_g)` agree gives `T_g = J_(G_g)`; "that is what Borcherds then did: he compared finitely many coefficients, and as they all equalled what they should, this concluded the proof [11] of Monstrous Moonshine". The text adds that this case-by-case check gives little insight into why the `T_g` are of genus 0.
- [P] Theorem 6 (p. 17, attributed to the paper's reference [29]): a function `B(tau) = q^-1 + sum_(n>=1) b_n q^n` that obeys a modular equation for all `n = 1 (mod N)` is either `q^-1 + b_1 q` or a Hauptmodul for a modular group of moonshine type; the converse also holds. The page states the classical modular equation satisfied by `j`.
- [P] Norton series (pp. 17-18): about a third of the McKay-Thompson series have some negative coefficients. Norton extended `g -> T_g` to commuting pairs `(g, h)` of Monster elements, giving functions `N(g, h; tau)` with the `SL_2(Z)` transformation law (5.1). An element `g` is Fricke if `G_g` contains an element sending 0 to `i infinity`, "as are 120 of the 171 `G_g`" (p. 18). For `g, h, gh` all in class 2A, `N(g, h; tau) = sqrt(J(tau) - 984)`, and `N(1, h; tau) = T_h(tau)`.
- [P] Equation (5.2) (p. 18): for `g` in `Aut(V^natural)` the unique `g`-twisted module `V^natural(g)` and a commuting automorphism `h` give `Z(g, h; tau) = q^(-c/24) Tr_(V^natural(g)) h q^(L_0)`; for the Monster `Z(g, h) = N(g, h)`.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The survey is the standard secondary reference for statements about the moonshine module, and it settles three points that circulate in informal accounts: the Monster acts on `V^natural`, whose graded dimension is `j - 744`, and not on `j` itself; the McKay-Thompson series `T_g` are traces of `g` on `V^natural`, whereas a trace over a `g`-twisted module is Norton's `Z(g, 1)` (equation (5.2)); Borcherds' proof compared finitely many coefficients after the denominator-identity argument (p. 17).
- [I] The survey contains no Cayley-Dickson content, and no statement on the pages opened links the Monster or `V^natural` to sedenions or zero divisors. The repository has no moonshine computation; the survey serves as the citation against which such claims are checked ([Conway and Norton note](conway-norton-1979-monstrous-moonshine.md)).
