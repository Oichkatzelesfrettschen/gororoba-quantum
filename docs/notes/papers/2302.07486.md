## Setting and conventions

- [P] `K` a field of characteristic zero ("throughout the article", p. 3; the repository's `F_p` computations fall outside it), `X` a skew-symmetric matrix, `Pf(X)^2 = det X`, and `Pf_(n-1)(X)` the ideal of Pfaffians of the submatrices of order `n - 1` obtained by deleting one row and its column (p. 1). For a generic `X` of odd order `n = 2r + 1` the ideal `I = Pf_(n-1)(X)` has `n` generators and is of linear type (Section 2, p. 5).
- [P] The Rees algebra `R(I)` is the bigraded algebra `sum_(i>=0) I^i`; `I` is of linear type when `R(I)` equals the symmetric algebra (p. 1). Definition 1.2 (p. 3): for `c, e >= 0` not both zero, the `(c, e)`-diagonal is `Delta = {(cs, es)}` and the diagonal subalgebra of a bigraded algebra `R` is `R_Delta = sum_(i>=0) R_(ci, ei)`.
- [P] Computations in the paper use Macaulay2 (p. 2).

## Theorem statements

- [P] Lemma 2.1 (p. 5, citing Buchsbaum and Eisenbud): the minimal graded free resolution of `B/I`, `B = K[X]`, is `0 -> B(-2r-1) -> B^n(-r-1) -> B^n(-r) -> B -> 0` with `d_1 = [Pf_1 ... Pf_n]`, the middle differential `d_2` a skew matrix of signed variables, and `d_3` the transpose of `d_1`.
- [P] Remark 2.2 (p. 5): `reg(B/I) = 2r - 2`; the `c`-th Veronese `(B/I)^(c)` is Koszul for `c >= n/4`; because `I` is of linear type, the defining relations of `R(I)` in `S = K[X, Y]` are the entries of `d_2 [y_1 ... y_n]^T`.
- [P] Lemma 2.3 (pp. 5-6): for those relations `g_1, ..., g_n`, the elements `g_1, ..., g_(n-1)` form an `S`-regular sequence, `(<y_n, g_1, ..., g_(n-1)> :_S Pf_1(X)) = <y_1, ..., y_n>`, `(<g_1, ..., g_(n-1)> :_S g_n) = <g_1, ..., g_(n-1), y_n, Pf_1(X)>`, and `(<g_1, ..., g_(n-1)> :_S y_n) = J`.
- [P] Proposition 2.4 (p. 7): the defining relations of `R(I)` form a `d`-sequence and generate an almost complete intersection ideal.
- [P] Question 2.5 (p. 7) asks whether `R(I)` is Koszul for every generic odd order. Proposition 2.6 (p. 8) answers for `n = 3, 5`: `R(I_1)` is Koszul for `n = 3` and `R(I_2)` is not Koszul for `n = 5` (the Betti table of the Pfaffian ring `B_(X_2)/I_2` on p. 8 has a nonlinear second syzygy); `I^j` has a linear resolution for `j >= 2` and `reg_x^S(R(I^j)) = 0` for `j >= 2`.
- [P] Theorem 2.8 (p. 8): for `I = Pf_(n-1)(X)`, `X` generic of odd order `n = 2r + 1`, the diagonal subalgebra `R(I)_Delta` is Koszul for every `Delta = (c, e)` with `c > 0` and `e > 0`. Remark 2.9 (p. 9): `R(I)_Delta` is Cohen-Macaulay for `c >> 0` and `e >> 0`, and for `n = 3, 5` when `c >= 2e + 1`.
- [P] Section 3 opening (pp. 9-10): for non-maximal Pfaffians the Rees relations are generally not quadrics. For `I = Pf_4(X)` with `X` the displayed sparse `7 x 7` skew matrix in 10 indeterminates (`x_12, x_14, x_23, x_25, x_34, x_36, x_45, x_47, x_56, x_67`), `R(I)` has 52 generators of bidegree `(1,1)`, 14 of bidegree `(0,2)` and 3 of bidegree `(0,3)`, "hence ... not generated by quadrics" (p. 10).
- [P] Theorem 3.2 (p. 10): for the tridiagonal skew matrix `X_3` of odd order `n = 2r + 1`, `Pf_(n-1)(X_3)` is a monomial ideal of Groebner linear type. The introduction (p. 2) adds that this ideal is the vertex cover ideal of an unmixed bipartite graph and that `R(I)_Delta` is Koszul and Cohen-Macaulay for all `Delta` (items (ii) and (iii) of the summary).

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The repository's exceptional fiber is the fiber cone `F(I) = sum_d I^d / m I^d` of a kernel-coordinate ideal ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)). For an ideal of linear type, `F(I) = R(I)/m R(I)` is a polynomial ring in `mu(I)` variables, because every entry of `d_2` lies in `m`. The measured fibers have analytic spread at most 6 against `mu(I)` of 10, 15, 17, 21 and 24, and their degree-2 Hilbert function lies below `C(h_1 + 1, 2)`, so none of the five kernel-coordinate ideals is of linear type. Theorem 2.8 therefore does not describe any repository fiber.
- [I] The paper's corank is odd (`n = 2r + 1`, a kernel line). The repository's kernel coordinates come from skew matrices whose complex corank is 4, 6 or 8. The maximal order Pfaffians of the paper are the wrong object for those strata; the non-maximal case is the one the repository needs, and the paper treats it only through the `7 x 7` example, whose relations of `y`-degree 2 and 3 show that fiber cones of non-maximal Pfaffian ideals carry nonquadratic relations. The matrix is sparse, so the counts 52, 14 and 3 are the counts of that matrix, not of a generic one.
- [I] The characteristic-zero hypothesis differs from the repository's computations over `F_32009`; a transfer needs an argument the paper does not give.
