## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are PDF page indices.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): an impinging particle ionizes the substrate and radiates high-energy phonons that
  induce a burst of quasiparticles "destroying qubit coherence throughout the device". The paper observes
  "large bursts of quasiparticles that simultaneously and severely limit the energy coherence of all
  qubits, causing chip-wide failure".
- [P] Setup (p. 2): a 26-qubit subset (`N_Q = 26`) of a Google Sycamore processor on a
  `10 mm x 10 mm` qubit chip, each qubit about 1 mm from its nearest neighbors, with `T_1` near 15
  microseconds at the operating frequency. The Rapid Repetitive Correlated Sampling cycle prepares,
  idles for 1 microsecond and measures every 100 microseconds.
- [P] Event shape (p. 3): the number of simultaneous qubit decay errors jumps from a baseline of about
  4 to about 24, "effectively saturat[ing] the qubit patch", then decays exponentially to baseline with a
  time constant of about 25 ms, "much larger than the typical QEC round time of 1 us". Across events the
  decay timescale is tightly grouped in the 25 to 30 ms range, and the peak heights run from the minimum
  identifiable to the full number of qubits.
- [P] Rate (p. 3): 326 time periods between events fit an exponential distribution with an average event
  rate `lambda = 1/(10 s)`, so events are independent in time, "occurring on average every 10 s without
  significant bunching or anti-bunching". The paper notes that this interval is short against the several
  hours projected for an error-corrected algorithm.
- [P] Spatial and temporal evolution (p. 4, Fig. 3): at a 3 microsecond sampling interval the error
  count rises from about 4 to about 10 in around 10 microseconds, to about 15 in around 1 ms (a
  saturation regime at 1.5 ms), and then relaxes with the 25 ms time constant. High error rates are first localized to a
  small number of qubits and then spread through the device. The recombination of quasiparticles
  emitting further phonons has an estimated timescale of about 180 microseconds.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Timescale against IBM shots: with the default repetition delay of 250 microseconds
  ([ibm_heron_platform.md](../ibm_heron_platform.md)) a 25 ms recovery spans at most about 100 consecutive
  shots, and a burst saturates all qubits of a patch for the first millisecond, so a burst appears as a
  block of shots with correlated errors on every qubit. A `1/(10 s)` rate on the paper's 10 mm chip means
  one burst per ten seconds of continuous operation, so a retained job of `t` seconds on a chip of
  comparable area meets about `t / 10` bursts, each affecting a 25 ms stretch of shots.
- [I] Signature: chip-wide simultaneous errors on all qubits, a 25 to 30 ms exponential recovery,
  Poisson arrivals with no clustering, and independence from the sidereal angle and from the circuit
  content. The shot-ordered bit strings under `results/live/*/raw/<job_id>/` are the test data; the
  this note carries no such search.

## Relevance

- [I] Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: low (a burst of elevated error rate bounds the
  validity window of a calibration snapshot).
- [I] Cayley-Dickson structure: none.
- [I] Factoring resource estimates: low (correlated burst errors enter fault-tolerant overhead estimates).
