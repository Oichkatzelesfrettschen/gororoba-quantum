## Statements

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): a quantum walk encodes the combinatorial Laplacian and a unitary projects onto the Laplacian kernel (the harmonic cycles), at O(n^3 log(1/epsilon) / lambda_k) gates for n vertices, smallest nonzero Laplacian eigenvalue lambda_k and projection error epsilon.
- [P] Abstract: the speedup is superpolynomial provided the spectral gap is inverse-polynomially bounded and simplex sampling is efficient, and it does not rely on quantum oracles for large datasets.
- [P] Abstract: the walk acts on a space containing both positively and negatively oriented simplices (doubling the space relative to unoriented walks), and coherent interference of the oriented pairs encodes the Laplacian, which unoriented walks cannot.
- [P] Page 2: the normalized expectation process (the difference between the probabilities of a positive simplex and its negative partner) corresponds to the projector onto harmonic homology when the Laplacian gap is inverse-polynomial.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The oriented-pair doubling parallels the repo's XOR pairs {k, k XOR m}: a kernel direction e_k - s e_(k XOR m) is exactly an interference between a basis state and its sign-twisted partner. The kernel-map readout uses selector interference on those pairs to count aligned pairs; this paper uses oriented-pair interference to project onto a kernel. The structural parallel (kernel read through signed-pair interference) is the point of contact; the gate counts concern cliques of n vertices and do not transfer.
- [I] The kernel-projector construction would encode the nullity of a known operator only up to the gap condition lambda_k; on the repo's operators the Gram eigenvalues are 0, 2, 4, which supply an explicit gap of 2 (pathion_kernel_map.py docstring), so the gap promise holds trivially at N = 32.
