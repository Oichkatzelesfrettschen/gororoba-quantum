## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page
indices. All probabilities are conditional on postselected single-photon detection.

### Construction and error model

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): the filters realize "the classical [8, 7, 2] single-parity-check code (the
  zero-sum 'neutral' subspace N) and the [8, 4, 4] extended Hamming code (the parity-checked subspace
  S in N, with one DC and three face-parity syndrome channels)"; these "are first-quantized
  path/mode encodings of classical codes: nothing is corrected, and all reported probabilities are
  conditional on postselected single-photon detection -- photon loss, the platform's dominant error,
  is removed by the postselection and is not detected by the checks".
- [P] Section II.A (pp. 2-3): the error model is a single-mode additive amplitude perturbation,
  v_k -> v_k + delta, or a local phase rotation on an occupied mode; "this is not the standard
  multi-qubit Pauli channel", and coherent multi-mode phase errors, correlated insertion-loss
  patterns and source phase drift "fall outside this single-mode model and are not protected by the
  present checks".
- [P] Eq. 1 (p. 3): the BALANCE operator B = I_8 - (1/8) 1 1^dagger projects onto the zero-sum
  subspace N; Eq. 2 (p. 3) gives the 4 x 8 parity-check matrix C of one all-ones row and the three
  first-order Walsh rows; the minimum distances are d(N) = 2 and d(S) = 4 (pp. 3-4).
- [P] Section II.D and Table I (p. 4): the compiled core R_N is real orthogonal, fixes the uniform
  vector with eigenvalue +1 and has determinant -1; the projector B "is not a unitary mesh element:
  on hardware it is realized only as the BALANCE/parity separator followed by single-photon
  postselection".

### Hardware platform and methods

- [P] Section IV (pp. 5-7): all runs used Quandela Belenos through Perceval, with 8 logical modes of
  the 24-mode chip; "the platform handled the assignment of logical to physical modes", so "the
  specific physical mode indices (and any run-to-run reassignment) are not fixed in advance" and are
  recorded per job in the raw payloads; each circuit is the single composite U_sep U_prep submitted
  as one interferometer, with a single photon injected in mode 0.
- [P] Section IV (p. 6): the remote-processor metadata reports a clock of 4.94 MHz, g(2)(0) = 0.019 and
  a transmittance of 4.84 %; the Hong-Ou-Mandel visibility "exceeded 90%" on the calibrated chip;
  each configuration collected about 10 000 single-photon events; uncertainties are 95 % Wilson
  intervals. Of 350 000 recorded events 171 registered two photons and none three or more, a
  multi-photon fraction of 0.049 %.
- [P] Section IV (p. 7): the backend stack was mosaiq-belenos 2.7.12, hardware-core 3.7.4, pcvl-worker
  1.4.0, perceval-quandela 1.1.0, universalchipworker 1.7.0 and exqalibur 1.1.1, exported with pcvl
  1.0.1, in an April 2026 campaign; single-photon postselection retains 99.9 % of detected events;
  "absolute photon loss (zero-photon events) is not registered by the platform's event counter", so
  loss claims "should therefore be read as relative path-amplitude statements".

### Hardware results

- [P] Experiment 1, Table II (p. 7): a single photon in each of the 8 input modes gives a dump-port
  (port 0) probability from 0.140 to 0.237 against the ideal 0.125; the mean is 0.188 +- 0.030
  (across-mode standard deviation), the root-mean-square deviation from 0.125 is 0.070 and the mean
  upward bias is 50 %. The paper calls this "fixed-pattern bias" and "the dominant systematic of the
  BALANCE experiments".
- [P] Experiment 2, Table III (pp. 7-8): the non-neutral control reads 0.191 [0.183, 0.198], the
  neutral inputs 0.0073, 0.0002 and 0.0106, and the pure-DC input 0.980; the mean over the three
  neutral inputs is 0.6 % (95 % CI [0.5 %, 0.7 %]), a suppression of 0.125/0.006 ~ 21 x against the
  ideal baseline and 31.6 x (log-ratio 95 % CI [27.2, 36.7]) against the measured control. "The gap
  between them quantifies the BALANCE calibration bias, not additional physics." The input-to-input
  range 0.0002 to 0.0106 is "dominated by how much residual uniform component each preparation
  accumulates under the QR convention". The |0> - |1> baseline acquired in three independent jobs
  gave 0.0073, 0.0070 and 0.0075, "agreeing within their Wilson intervals".
- [P] Experiment 3, Eqs. 4 and 5, Table IV (pp. 8-9): the injected-contamination response follows the
  ideal c^2/(1 + c^2) only after a two-parameter fit with a floor p_floor = 0.010 and a gain
  g = 1.12; the fit residuals are "at the +-0.02 level (largest, +0.024, near c = 0.4)" and
  chi^2/dof is about 24 against counting statistics, so "they are dominated by systematic
  calibration offsets at the +-0.02 level"; the paper treats +-0.02 as a per-point systematic
  budget.
- [P] Experiment 4, Table V and Fig. 3 (pp. 9-10): dump probability for a neutral input at 0, 1, 2 and 3
  core applications is 0.0075, 0.0004, 0.0018 and 0.0002, against 0.196 and 0.190 for the controls.
  "Each depth was submitted as a different compiled unitary, so cross-depth comparisons also
  include depth-dependent compiler and calibration variance"; the ideal core preserves the DC
  component and "cannot by itself reduce the dump leakage", so the scatter "is therefore consistent
  with differing preparation and separator calibration for each separately compiled circuit, not any
  cleaning action of the core". The paper claims only that leakage stays below the non-neutral
  control and makes no claim of monotonic sector preservation; the Fig. 3 caption states the four
  points are "not a depth scan".
- [P] Experiment 5, Tables VI to VIII (pp. 10-11): the four code states leak 0.009, 0.062, 0.011 and
  0.012 into the syndrome ports (mean 2.32 %, CI [2.18 %, 2.48 %]); the b0 xor b2 state at 0.062 is
  an outlier that the paper attributes "tentatively" to state-dependent calibration, "a post hoc
  hypothesis that the present single run cannot confirm". Eq. 6 (p. 11) models the leakage as
  the squared Walsh-transform weight of the per-mode multiplicative error profile on the frequency of
  the code state and its three single-face-flip neighbors. The three face-parity inputs route to
  their predicted syndrome ports with selectivity 0.985, 0.944 and 0.942 (mean 95.1 %), with
  off-target rows at most 2.8 %. Against a single-mode control at 55.0 % (ideal 50 %) the suppression is
  21.6 x referred to the ideal and 23.7 x (CI [22.2, 25.3]) measured.
- [P] Experiment 6, Table IX (p. 12): across a Belenos recalibration window the Hong-Ou-Mandel
  visibility fell from 90.7 % to 21.2 % and was restored to about 91 %; the neutral dump probability
  moved from 0.0073 to 0.481 (95 % CI [0.467, 0.495], about 66 x) and back to 0.0058. The paper calls
  the degradation "opportunistic, not deliberately induced", states that "the maintenance-window
  change was not an isolated control parameter" and reads the comparison "as a diagnostic of the
  calibrated versus degraded photonic operating state, not as a standalone measurement of the
  microscopic mechanism"; the restored visibility was taken from the platform's calibration report.
- [P] June revision note (p. 13): five Belenos calibration sessions repeated the code-state
  acquisitions, the core was scanned to depth 15 and randomized orthogonality controls were run. These
  data "are not folded into the April event totals"; the depth scan "showed non-monotonic calibration
  scatter", "randomized dark-port controls showed that DC-port suppression is a generic
  orthogonality effect rather than a uniquely cube-specific signature", and the b0 xor b2 code-state
  leakage "ranged from 3.8% to 12.4% across the June sessions". The robust hardware claim is stated as
  "postselected leakage suppression and syndrome routing, with calibration-dependent leakage floors,
  not a session-independent suppression constant".
- [P] Section VII.B (p. 14): the experiments do not demonstrate closed-loop recovery, feed-forward
  control, a multi-block architecture or a fault-tolerance threshold; the hardware measures
  intensities only.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Belenos acceptance and routing controls
  ([bdsc-qualification.md](../../design/bdsc-qualification.md)). The 0.188 mean dump probability
  against the ideal 0.125 is a fixed-pattern bias of the separator readout on the same platform. A
  suppression ratio taken against a measured non-neutral control inherits that bias (31.6 x here
  against 21 x referred to the ideal), so a Belenos acceptance statement reports the leakage itself
  and names the baseline a ratio uses. The dependence of leakage on the compiled circuit (an order
  of magnitude at the 1e-3 level across separately compiled depths) matches the repository's
  practice of resubmitting one frozen request instead of comparing separately compiled circuits.
- [I] Drift series ([bdsc_drift_series.py](../../../scripts/bdsc_drift_series.py)). The paper's only
  repeat of one configuration is three jobs inside one calibration (0.0073, 0.0070, 0.0075), which
  bounds within-calibration scatter at counting statistics for n of about 10 000; the HOM episode and
  the June sessions (3.8 % to 12.4 % across five calibrations) show the between-calibration
  variation that a series spanning a calibration has to resolve. The paper reports no designed inter-job spacing,
  so it constrains the series' design only through these two scales.
- [I] The survey in [literature.md](../quandela/literature.md) (source 7) records this paper with
  its unrefereed status, and the calibration-episode paragraph of
  [strategy.md](../quandela/strategy.md) cites its HOM episode.
