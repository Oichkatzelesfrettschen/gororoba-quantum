# Ma et al. -- Six-input teleportation over a high-loss channel and its 2/3 comparison

[P] identifies a statement in the source. [I] identifies a repository
application or deduction. The paper supplies a six-state teleportation experiment with
a heralded-pair single-photon input, the explanation of the 2/3 limit for an input the
classical device does not know, a fixed-input attenuation study and a
coincidence-window study; this note implements no benchmark and no hardware case.

## Results needed by the project

The paper writes `f` for the fidelity `<phi_ideal|rho|phi_ideal>` of a teleported
state, `f_bar` for the mean over six inputs, `V = 2 f - 1` for the teleportation
visibility, `chi` for the process matrix, `eta` for the link efficiency, `n` for the
dark-count rate at Bob, `tau` for the coincidence-window width and `p_BSM` for the
probability of a successful Bell-state measurement.

1. [P] Protocol (Sec. 2, p. 4; Sec. 3, pp. 4-6). Alice and Bob share the polarization-entangled
   state `|Psi^->_12` (Eq. 1), and Alice performs a Bell-state measurement on photons 1
   and 3, the input `|phi>_3 = alpha|H> + beta|V>` (Eq. 2). Linear optics identify two of
   the four Bell states, here `|Psi^->` and `|Psi^+>`; in "the other 50 % of the cases" the
   measurement projects onto a product state and "quantum teleportation is impossible"
   (p. 4). Bob applies a pi phase shift for `|Psi^+>` by feed-forward with an
   electro-optical modulator (p. 6).
2. [P] Input source (p. 4; Fig. 1 caption, p. 5). Photon 3 comes from a collinear type-II
   spontaneous parametric down-conversion crystal (BBO2) as a heralded single-photon
   source, with photon 4 as the herald. The local detected rates without attenuation are
   about 90 kHz for the entangled pair and 110 kHz for the single-photon input; the
   pump is a mode-locked laser at 80 MHz with 140 fs pulses (p. 4).
3. [P] Six-input result (p. 6; Fig. 2, p. 7). The inputs are `|H>`, `|V>`, `|P>`, `|M>`, `|R>`
   and `|L>`, "six states of three mutually unbiased bases". At 31 dB attenuation the
   maximum-likelihood fidelities are 0.87(1), 0.83(1), 0.90(1), 0.74(1), 0.81(1) and
   0.80(1), mean `f_bar = 0.82(1)`, against "the average classical fidelity limit of 2/3"
   (references 1 and 31, Bennett et al. and Popescu). The data hold 9891 four-fold
   coincidence counts in about 50 hours summed over all inputs, with Poissonian errors
   (Fig. 2 caption). The text gives no coincidence-window width for these data.
4. [P] Process tomography (p. 7; Fig. 3, p. 8). From the four inputs `|H>`, `|V>`, `|P>` and
   `|R>` the process matrix `chi` (`rho = sum_(l,k) chi_(lk) sigma_l rho_in sigma_k`) gives
   `f_process = tr(chi_ideal chi) = 0.77(1)` with feed-forward and 0.24(2) without it.
   Without feed-forward the equatorial inputs come out completely mixed, and `|H>` and
   `|V>` are unaffected (pp. 8-9). The authors attribute the tilt of the output ellipsoids
   to polarization drift in the 50 m delay fiber over the long integration time (p. 9).
5. [P] Multiphoton emission (p. 7). The authors attribute the purity reduction of the
   teleported state mainly to higher-order emission in the down-conversion process. They add
   that the part stemming from the source of photon 3 could be removed by tomographically
   measuring the input state and reconstructing the process from input and output density
   matrices (pp. 7-8). The paper does not do this.
6. [P] The 2/3 explanation (p. 9). A classical Alice who "does not know the input state"
   picks a measurement basis at random and sends a corresponding photon to Bob, which gives
   a classical average fidelity `f_cl = 2/3`. The classical visibility is
   `V_cl = 2 f_cl - 1 = 1/3` (pp. 9-10), and a measured visibility above 1/3 is read as quantum
   teleportation. The paper gives no proof that the strategy is optimal and cites references
   1 and 31 for the limit (p. 6).
7. [P] Fixed-input attenuation study (Sec. 4, pp. 9-10; Fig. 4, p. 10). The input is always `|P>`.
   The text states "Based on the results obtained above, we assume that the quality of our
   teleportation device is the same for all bases." The state `|P>` is chosen as "a coherent
   superposition of the eigenstates of the BSM", which requires non-classical two-photon
   interference. The visibility stays near 0.75 up to roughly 35 dB and the model crosses the
   classical line near 43 dB; the paper concludes that teleportation is feasible up to 36 dB
   (p. 12). The Fig. 4 caption states that these measurements used a single-room setup
   "without feed-forward". The four-fold rate (right axis, Hz) levels off above 50 dB, where
   a three-fold coincidence at Alice with a Bob dark count dominates (caption).
8. [P] Model (p. 9, Eq. 3). The analytical model is built on the photon statistics of
   down-conversion; its background terms for a four-fold count are second-order emission,
   third-order emission and dark counts at Bob's detectors. The success probability is
   `p_BSM eta`, the noise probability is
   `p_BSM n tau`, and `SNR = (eta/n)(1/tau)` (Eq. 3). No rate equation for the multiphoton
   terms is printed.
9. [P] Coincidence window (p. 11; Fig. 5). At 31 dB over 2 hours, the visibility falls with the
   window width from about 0.83 at 1 ns to about 0.55 at 29 ns, with an abrupt drop where
   the window reaches the 12.5 ns spacing of consecutive pump pulses; the violation of the
   classical bound falls from 18-20 to about 10 standard deviations (Fig. 5).
10. [P] Attenuation definition (p. 11). The quoted attenuations count only the neutral
    density filters and the free-space link; the inefficiency of Bob's detectors and the
    loss in his optical elements are left out of the attenuation analysis.

## Reproduction and repository reading

[I] The 2/3 explanation is a premise about the input ensemble, and the paper's own
strategy only attains it. A classical device that measures a random Pauli basis and
prepares the outcome scores 1 with probability 1/3 and 1/2 otherwise, so
`1/3 + (2/3)(1/2) = 2/3` for the six states at equal priors. That is the value
the paper uses. [Chiribella and Xie](1301.1170.md), Eq. (4), gives it as the optimum over
all probabilistic measure-and-prepare operations with free post-selection (verified there
numerically against the repository solver), so the six-state value 2/3 is an optimum
for a classical device that does not know the label and receives one input copy.

[I] A fixed-input series does not carry the 2/3 bound by itself. The input is always `|P>`
in the attenuation study (result 7), and a classical device that prepares `|P>` returns
fidelity 1 on it. The repository's `six_state_benchmark` returns pooled free-post-selection
bounds of 0.857 for a prior of 1/2 on one input, 0.979 for 0.9 and 0.998 for 0.99 (the
other five inputs sharing the remainder), tending to 1 as the prior concentrates on one input. The paper's
comparison of the `|P>` visibility with 1/3 therefore holds through its stated assumption that device
quality is the same for all bases, as a proxy for the six-state mean, and not as a classical
bound on the fixed input. The same applies to any rehearsal series with one input or an unequal
attempt allocation: the threshold of
[`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`](../../../scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py)
(`attempts_benchmark`) depends on the allocation.

[I] Multiphoton input terms are named and left in the figure. The input is a heralded
down-conversion photon, so accepted four-fold events include two-pair emission, in which
the input arm carries extra photons. The paper calls this the main cause of the purity loss
(result 5), gives no share of such events among the 9891 counts, and proposes
a tomographic reference for the input instead of a classical bound for the contaminated
input. That reference changes the figure of merit to a process fidelity against the measured input, which is
not a comparison with 2/3. The design's open question on multiphoton sectors
([the design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md), review of
2026-10-26) matches this paper's unsolved part, and the sector values of
[Chiribella and Xie](1301.1170.md) bound the accepted identical-copy sectors from above.

[I] The attempt count is knowable here and is not in the Belenos record. An 80 MHz
pump over about 50 hours is about 1.4e13 pulses, so 9891 four-fold counts correspond to
about 7e-10 accepted events per pump pulse at 31 dB. The 50 hours is the paper's
approximation of the total, and the pulse count is an upper bound for the exposed
pulses, so the rate is an order-of-magnitude figure at medium confidence, to rise with
the logged exposure time. The paper does not use the count as a denominator: the classical
comparison is the unconstrained 2/3.

[I] Per-event dependence. The paper attributes a tilt of the process ellipsoids to
polarization drift in the delay fiber over the integration time (result 4) and quotes
Poissonian errors on pooled counts over 50 hours. Pooled counts from a drifting channel are
not independent draws from one process, and the 2/3 comparison is not tested against that
drift; Fig. 5 shows that the verdict margin depends on a post-processing choice, the
coincidence window, which the paper varies after acquisition and fixes for Fig. 2 without stating it.

[I] Arithmetic. The six printed fidelities average to 0.825 (printed 0.82(1)). For a
single-qubit process the average state fidelity is `(2 f_process + 1)/3`, which is 0.847 at
`f_process = 0.77`; the paper does not reconcile this with 0.82(1), and the process matrix
uses four of the six inputs.

  medium. The paper states the premise of the six-state value in words (the classical

  a classical bound.

  accepted counts, dual-rail photons, the optimality of its random-basis strategy
