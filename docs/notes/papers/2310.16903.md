## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are PDF page indices. The text layer drops overbars and
over-arrows, so Eq. 1 was read from the layout text and checked numerically below.

- [P] Eq. 1 (p. 2), the Sagnac phase of the rotating fiber loop: `phi_s = 8 pi Omega_E A cos(Theta) / (lambda c)`,
  with `Omega_E` the rotation rate of the Earth, `A` the effective interferometer area of 715 m^2
  (calibrated in Appendix A), `Theta` the angle between the area vector of the fiber loop and the
  angular velocity vector of the Earth, and `lambda` the photon wavelength of 1546 nm.
- [P] Section II (p. 3): an N-photon N00N state `(|N>_a |0>_b - |0>_a |N>_b)/sqrt(2)` acquires
  `exp(i N phi_s)`, so the output probabilities oscillate as `P_(N,a) = (1 + cos(N phi_s))/2`; the
  two-photon coincidence probability is `P_HV = [1 + cos(2 phi_0 + 2 phi_s)]/2` (Eq. 4, with `phi_0` the
  applied bias phase).
- [P] Section III (p. 4): the fiber is split into two 1 km segments joined by a four-port optical switch.
  Flipping the switch reverses the propagation direction in one segment, which cancels the Sagnac
  phase (two opposite area vectors, zero effective area) and so separates the Earth-rotation signal from
  any other phase shift. The frame rotates in pitch and yaw to set `Theta`.
- [P] Section IV (p. 5): at `Theta = 2.5` degrees the single-photon phase is `phi_s = 2.8(2)` mrad. The
  maximum Sagnac phase over the six frame angles of Fig. 3 follows the `cos(Theta)` dependence of
  Eq. 1: 5.5(4) mrad for the two-photon N00N state (`Omega_E = 7.1(5) x 10^-5` rad/s) against 2.8(1) mrad
  for one photon (`Omega_E = 7.2(3) x 10^-5` rad/s). The accepted rate is `7.3 x 10^-5` rad/s, and the
  enhancement factor is 1.97(16).
- [P] Appendix A (p. 8): a classical-light calibration at six frame angles spaced by 22.5 degrees fits
  `phi_E(Theta) = S Omega_E cos(Theta + Theta_0)` with `Omega_E = 7.29 x 10^-5` rad/s and returns the
  scale factor `S = 38.8(1)` s and the offset `Theta_0 = 0.03(33)` degrees.
- [P] Abstract and Section V (pp. 1, 5): the rotation sensitivity is 5 microradian per second. The scale
  factor instability limits the phase resolution, and the dominant contributions are mechanical
  vibration of the frame, thermal fluctuation and acoustic noise.
- [P] Section V (p. 6): the general-relativistic rotation-rate correction is `Omega_GR = 10^-9 Omega_E`,
  and reaching it needs a resolution below 0.1 prad/s.
- [P] Section V (p. 6), outlook: a loop of about 20 km^2 would reach that correction.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Eq. 1 with `A = 715 m^2`, `lambda = 1546 nm` and `Omega_E = 7.2921 x 10^-5` rad/s gives 2.83 mrad
  and `S = 38.8` s, which reproduces the paper's 2.8 mrad and its calibrated scale factor.
- [I] Size against the repository's photonic devices. At 925 nm the same formula gives
  `6.6 x 10^-12` rad for 1 mm^2 and `6.6 x 10^-10` rad for 1 cm^2 of enclosed area at `cos(Theta) = 1`
  (doubled for a two-photon state). The repository's Belenos and Ascella records move by order-one
  fractions between reads (observed transmittance 2.89 % to 6.06 %,
  [platform-specifications.md](../quandela/platform-specifications.md)), and a cited simulation study
  finds that a heater phase noise of 15 to 30 mrad moves a 4-of-24 embedding's total variation distance
  by 2.7e-2 to 5.2e-2 ([literature.md](../quandela/literature.md); a simulation parameter, not a
  measured instability). Earth rotation sits 7 orders of magnitude (1 cm^2) to 9 orders (1 mm^2) below
  that simulated phase scale.
- [I] Signature a real Sagnac coupling needs: a phase proportional to `A cos(Theta)` that reverses sign
  when the enclosed area vector reverses (the paper's switch or a physical flip of the chip). For an
  Earth-fixed device `Theta`, the angle between the area vector and Earth's rotation axis, stays
  constant, so the Earth-rotation phase is static and only reorientation or area reversal exposes it;
  a sidereal modulation instead tests a direction fixed among the stars, a different hypothesis
  (the preferred-frame searches of [Dreissen et al.](2206.00570.md)). The superconducting
  processors (IBM Heron r2, Tuna-17) enclose no optical loop, and their qubit phases carry no `A`.
