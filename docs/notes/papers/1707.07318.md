## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Setting (pp. 1-4). Doubling products `(a, b)(c, d) = (f(a, b, c, d), g(a, b, c, d))` with `f` among `f0 = ca - b*d`, `f1 = ca - d b*`,
  `f2 = ac - b*d`, `f3 = ac - d b*`, `f4 = ca - b d*`, `f5 = ca - d* b`, `f6 = ac - b d*`, `f7 = ac - d* b` and `g` among `g0 = d a* + b c`,
  `g1 = a* d + c b`, `g2 = a d + c* b`, `g3 = d a + b c*`; conjugation `(x, y)* = (x*, -y)`. The requirements are a unit, a norm with
  `x x* = |x|^2`, `(xy)* = y* x*`, and unit basis vectors `e_p` forming a sign group with the antisymmetric property
  `e_p e_q + e_q e_p = 0` and the quaternion property `e_p e_q = e_r` implies `e_q e_r = e_p`, for `0 != p != q != 0`. All 32 combinations satisfy
  the unit and antisymmetry requirements; the quaternion property leaves eight (Sections 7 and 8, Tables 7.1 and 8.1, pp. 5-9).
- [P] Shuffle basis (pp. 2-3): the pair `(x, y)` is the interleaved sequence `x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1, ...`, so `e_(2p) = (e_p, 0)` and
  `e_(2p+1) = (0, e_p)`. The index group is `(W, xor)`, `e_p e_q = omega(p, q) e_(p xor q)`, `omega(p, p) = -1` for `p > 1` and
  `omega(p, q) = -omega(q, p)` for `0 != p != q != 0` (pp. 4, 14).
- [P] The eight products (p. 8): `P0 = (f0, g0)`, `P1 = (f1, g1)`, `P2 = (f2, g0)`, `P3 = (f3, g1)`, `P0T = (f4, g2)`, `P1T = (f5, g3)`,
  `P2T = (f6, g2)`, `P3T = (f7, g3)`; the product `P3T`, `(a, b)(c, d) = (ac - d* b, d a + b c*)`, is "the one most commonly used" (p. 10) and
  `P3` is its transpose. The four of them `P0, P3, P0T, P3T` give the octonions at the third doubling (p. 8); the other four have
  zero divisors there, `(e_(2p) + e_(2q+1))(e_(2p+1) + e_(2q)) = 0` for `0 != p != q != 0`, for example `(e2 + e5)(e3 + e4) = 0`
  (p. 13), and are called pseudo Cayley-Dickson products.
- [P] Structure constants (pp. 9-11): in the shuffle basis `P0..P3` satisfy `(1, 2n, 2n + 1)` and the transposes `(1, 2n + 1, 2n)`;
  Eqs. 9.4 to 9.7 propagate a triple `(p, q, r)` to its four successors; the octonion triples are listed for `P0..P3` (pp. 10-11)
  and reversed for the transposes. The oriented Fano plane (Section 11, Figure 1, pp. 11-12) records the senses of the
  three sides, the circle and the three altitudes; `2^3 = 8` combinations give the eight products.
- [P] Twist maps (Sections 12 to 14, pp. 13-18): Table 12.1 gives the recursive block product `(e_(2r), e_(2s)) -> ...` for each of the
  eight; the twist `omega` has the block form `C, L, T, D, N` of Table 14.1 with the eight `omega_k`, and the table of `A_N` is
  the upper-left quadrant of the table of `A_(N+1)`.
- [P] Section 16 and the appendix (pp. 21-23): the twist tree of `omega_2` (the product `P2`, with `omega_2` stable) and a `bc`
  program that generates the twist figures from the recursive rules `sgn(p, q)` for `omega_0, ..., omega_3`.

## Convention map and cd_core check

[I] Identification with `cd_core`. `P3T` is `cd_core`'s doubling `(a,b)(c,d) = (ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))`, and `P3` is its
transpose. The paper's shuffle index `p` is the bit reversal of `cd_core`'s index over the `n` doubling bits (`e_(2p+1) = (0, e_p)`
puts the last doubling flag in the lowest bit, `cd_core` puts it in the highest).

[I] Check results: no mismatch. A scratch script (ignored `docs/local/`, run with `-I`) built all 32 products recursively and
compared them with the paper and with `cd_core`:

| Paper statement | Result |
| --- | --- |
| all 32 products satisfy the unit and antisymmetry requirements; eight satisfy the quaternion property | unit and antisymmetry hold for all 32 at `D = 16`; the quaternion property at `D = 32` holds for exactly `(f0,g0), (f1,g1), (f2,g0), (f3,g1), (f4,g2), (f5,g3), (f6,g2), (f7,g3)`, the eight of p. 8 |
| `P0, P3, P0T, P3T` give octonions and the other four do not | norm-multiplicativity holds on 40 of 40 random pairs and the basis loop is Moufang at `D = 8` for exactly `P0, P3, P0T, P3T`, and fails (37 to 40 of 40 pairs) for `P1, P2, P1T, P2T` |
| `(e2 + e5)(e3 + e4) = 0` for the four pseudo products (p. 13) | the product is zero for `P1, P2, P1T, P2T` and equals `+-2 e6` for the other four, in the shuffle basis at `D = 8` |
| `P3T` is the most common product (p. 10) | the sign table of `P3T` equals `cd_sign` (pair-ordered basis) at `D = 8, 16, 32, 64`; `P3` equals `cd_sign(q, p)` at `D = 16, 32` |
| octonion triples of `P3T` (p. 11) `(1,3,2), (1,5,4), (1,7,6), (2,6,4), (5,7,2), (7,3,4), (3,5,6)` | under bit reversal each is a positive `cd_core` product `e_p e_q = +e_r` at `D = 8` |
| appendix rules for `omega_0` to `omega_3` (p. 22) | the recursive rules reproduce the sign tables of `P0, P1, P2, P3` in all `D^2` cells at `D = 8, 16, 32, 64` |

[I] Naming hazard. The companion catalog [1107.1301](1107.1301.md) numbers the same 32 products `P0..P31` with different
labels: its eight true products are `P0, P3, P4, P7, P24, P27, P28, P31`, which are this paper's `P0, P1, P2, P3, P0T, P1T, P2T, P3T`
in that order, and its `P31` is `cd_core`'s product. A statement about "`P3`" or "`P2`" needs the paper named.

## Repository bearing

[I] Convention. Wilmot's sedenion table ([2512.07210](2512.07210.md)) and Lemley ([2609.11588](2609.11588.md)) use `P3T` (equal
to `cd_core`); Saniga et al. ([1405.6888](1405.6888.md)) print `P3`, the transpose, and Gazeau ([2512.22134](2512.22134.md)) prints
the opposite algebra; any cell-by-cell comparison with `cd_sign` needs this identification first. The repository's gates on
`cd_core` (for example the sign-cocycle claims `cd-sign-cocycle-degree`, `cd-sign-row-degree` and `cd-right-row-degree`)
fix `P3T`; the seven other quaternion-property products have different sign tables on the same XOR basis, so
algebraic-degree and associator-census statements are not asserted for them here.

[I] Zero divisors. The four pseudo products have zero divisors already at dimension 8, which no result for the true
Cayley-Dickson algebras covers. Dimensions 32 and above: the paper's recursive rules and the check at `D = 64` show the
twist structure of `P3T` is fixed by `omega_3`; no zero-divisor, annihilator, Stiefel or box-kite statement is made.
Moreno's Stiefel question (`math/0512517` Section 5): none.
