## Main claims and evidence

- [P] A graded view of the Cayley-Dickson process defines associativity in three classes and splits the non-associative parts into four types.
- [P] The graded notation derives the eight octonion and seven power-associative subalgebras of the sedenions; sedenion zero divisors occur in multiples of 84 and reduce to seven primary pairs.
- [P] Table 5 (page 13) lists, from the octonions O through U7 (D = 8 to 1024), the quaternion-like counts H 7, 35, 155, 651, 2667, 10795, 43435, 174251, the octonion counts O 1, 8, 50, 310, 2046, 14478, 107950, 831470, and the quasi-octonion counts P4, P12, P14 with totals 0, 7, 105, 1085, 9765, 82677, 680085, 5516245.
- [P] Theorem 8 states S_{m+1} = 7 (O_m + S_m) for the quasi-octonion total S; its proof observes the pattern in Table 5 and argues by induction from the triad identity C(N_m, 3) = H_m + 28 (O_m + S_m), N_m = 2^(m+3) - 1.
- [E] The closed forms O(D) = (D-2)(D-4)(D+48)/1344, Q(D) = (D-2)(D-4)(D-8)/192 and H(D) = (D-1)(D-2)/6 reproduce every Table 5 column, and Theorem 3 of [the census note](../topics/cd_associator_defect_census.md) gives a proof of Theorem 8 from the doubling lifting identity.

### What changed from version 3 to version 4

- [P] Structure. Version 4 is 32 pages against 25. The zero-divisor material, previously inside Section 3, is Section 4
  ("Zero Divisors") and the split algebras remain Section 5. Version 3 has 12 numbered theorems and version 4 has 13.
- [P] Abstract. Version 3 listed "3-cycles that distinguish between the Moufang identities", "three power-associative
  subalgebras" and a split-algebra statement that those algebras involve the same power-associative subalgebras as the sedenions.
  Version 4 names "octonion and three quasi-octonion, power-associative subalgebras", the statement that power-associativity
  "grows by multiples of seven that introduce zero divisors in factors of 84 for all Cayley-Dickson algebras", the 84 sedenion
  zero divisors reduced "via three cycles and four modes down to seven primary zero divisor pairs", and for the split algebras
  "mappings between three of these ... showing 180 zero divisors and the other split algebra is analogous to sedenions with 84
  zero divisors", with "quasi-octonion algebras with mixed signature".
- [P] Theorem renumbering (version 3 to version 4): Theorem 4 "Ultronion generation" to Theorem 4 "Ultracomplex algebra
  generation"; a new Lemma 5 ("The Malcev identity simplifies for Cayley-Dickson triads"); Theorem 6 (Moufang associativity) and
  Theorem 7 (Cycles) keep their numbers; version 3 Theorem 8 ("Ultronion subalgebras") is split into version 4 Theorem 8
  ("Ultracomplex algebra growth") and the new Theorem 9 ("Ultracomplex subalgebras"); version 3 Theorem 9 (Projection) to
  Theorem 10; Theorem 10 (Zero divisors) to Theorem 11; Theorem 11 (Modes) to Theorem 12; Theorem 12 (Trace) to Theorem 13.
  The term "Type 3 associativity" of version 3 reads "Class 3 associativity" in version 4.
- [P] Theorem 8 and Table 5. The statement is unchanged: `S_(m+1) = 7 (O_m + S_m)` for `m >= 0` (Eq. 19), with `N_k = 2^k - 1` and
  the triad identity `C(N_(m+3), 3) = H_(m+3) + 28 (O_m + S_m)` (Eq. 18). Table 5 (counts of `H`, `O`, `P4`, `P12`, `P14` and the total quasi-octonion
  count for `O` and `U1` to `U7`) is identical in both versions, row for row (checked by text comparison of the six rows). The
  proof changes. Version 3 observes that each `P_k` count is divisible by 7 and the pattern `S_m = 7 (S_(m-1) + O_(m-1))` in the
  table, then argues by induction on the ratio of triad counts. Version 4 derives `S_(m+1) = (1/4) C(2^(m+3), 3) - H_(m+3)` (Eq. 20) by
  counting four-fold products of triad elements ("quads") after removing those that involve the scalar, then substitutes
  `O_m = S_(m+1)/7 - S_m` into Eq. 18 to confirm Eq. 20, and cites Byrapuram et al., who derived Eq. 20 from the EvenQuads card game
  and gave `S_(m+1) = (1/24)(2^(m+3) - 1)(2^(m+3) - 2)(2^(m+3) - 4)` (Eq. 21; OEIS A006096), so that `O_m + S_m =
  (1/21)(2^(m+3) - 1)(2^(m+2) - 1)(2^(m+1) - 1)` (Eq. 22). A Corollary states `T_m = O_m + S_m = N_(m+1) N_(m+2) N_(m+3)/21`.
- [P] Theorem 9 (new, p. 18): `O_m = N_(m+1) N_(m+2) (N_(m+3) + 49)/168` and `S_m = N_(m+2) (N_(m+2) - 1)(N_(m+2) - 3)/24`. With `D = 2^(m+3)` these
  equal the closed forms `O(D) = (D-2)(D-4)(D+48)/1344` and `Q(D)` recorded in the check above.
- [P] Zero divisors. Version 3 Theorem 10 gave the count for `U_n` (`n = m + 3`, `N_m = 2^n - 1`) as `Z_m = (1/16)(N_m - 1)(N_m - 3)(N_m - 7)`;
  version 4 Theorem 11 gives `Z_m = (1/2) N_(m+2)(N_(m+2) - 1)(N_(m+2) - 3)` and calls `Z_m` the number of zero divisor pairs. Both
  equal 84 at the sedenions, 1260 at `n = 5` and 13020 at `n = 6`; [E] the two forms agree for every `n = 4` to `15` under `N_k = 2^k - 1`.
  Read literally, version 4's sentence "`N_m = 2^n - 1` with `n = m + 3`" conflicts with the argument `N_(m+2)`, while the definition
  `N_(m+3) = 2^(m+3) - 1` on p. 16 fixes `N_k = 2^k - 1`; the formula gives 84 at `m = 1` only under that reading.
- [E] Checks run for this note, against the printed Table 5 columns `U_m`, `m = 0` to `7`, with a throwaway integer script: the
  Theorem 9 closed forms give exactly the `O` row and the total `P_k` row; Eq. 18 holds in every column; `S_(m+1) = 7 (O_m + S_m)`
  (Theorem 8) holds for `m = 0` to `6`; and Eq. 20, `S_(m+1) = C(2^(m+3), 3)/4 - H`, with `H` read from the table column `U_m`, holds for `m = 0` to
  `6`. The pre-existing check that `O(D)`, `Q(D)` and `H(D)` reproduce every Table 5 column therefore carries to version 4's
  statements. Not run: the proofs of Theorem 8 and Theorem 9, and the split-algebra count of 180.
