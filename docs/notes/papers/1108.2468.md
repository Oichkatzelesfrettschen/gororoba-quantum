## Results needed by the project

1. [P] Validity (Sec. I, pp. 1-2). A protocol computes a p-value
   `p = b(T(x))` from a test statistic `T` of the data `x`. It is valid when
   `b(t) >= Prob(T(x) >= t)` for every local realistic (LR) model. For `n`
   independent identically distributed trials the p-value falls as
   `p ~ 2^(-G n)`; `G` is the asymptotic confidence-gain rate.
2. [P] Setting-randomization requirement (Sec. II, p. 2). The Bell expression
   `I(x)` has the setting probabilities `p_ij` built in (Eqs. 2 and 3,
   `I(x) = (1 - 2 delta_{i,2} delta_{j,2}) ab / p_ij`, `B = 2`). The paper
   states that the locality and memory loopholes cannot be closed unless the
   settings are chosen randomly and independently by each party, with no
   causal connection, according to a known probability distribution.
3. [P] Standard-deviation (SD) p-value (Eq. 4, Eq. 5, pp. 3-6). The Gaussian
   p-value `Q((I - B)/sigma)` has rate `G_SD = log2(e) (I - B)^2 / (2 sigma_1^2)`.
   It assumes independent identically distributed trials and a Gaussian tail
   and is not valid: Fig. 1 shows `G_SD` above the optimal rate for
   unbalanced Bell states with `theta` above 33.41 degrees.
4. [P] Martingale (Azuma-Hoeffding) p-value (Sec. III B, Eqs. 6, 7, 13 to 15,
   pp. 4, 6, 7). With `W_k` the information available before trial `k` and
   `M_k = sum_{l <= k} (I(x_l) - B)`, any LR model gives
   `<M_k | W_k> <= M_{k-1}` (Eq. 13), because the Bell inequality holds for
   every LR model whatever the prior information. After scaling `I` into
   `[-4, 4]`, `p_mart = exp(-2 n (I_hat - B)^2 / (b_u - b_l)^2)` (Eq. 15)
   with `b_u - b_l = 8`, which is `exp(-n (I_hat - B)^2 / 32)` (Eq. 6) and
   rate `log2(e) (I - B)^2 / 32` (Eq. 7). The protocol is valid under
   memory and suboptimal.
5. [P] PBR protocol (Sec. II C, Sec. III C, Eqs. 8, 9, 16, 17, pp. 4, 7).
   After `k` trials the protocol returns a nonnegative function `R_k(x)`
   with `<R_k(x)> <= 1` for every LR model that has the setting distribution
   `p_ij` (Eq. 8). `R_k` may depend on `x_1, ..., x_k` and on other
   information available before trial `k+1`. The p-value is
   `p = min(1 / prod_{k=1}^{n} R_{k-1}(x_k), 1)` (Eq. 9). The product
   `P_k = prod R_{l-1}(x_l)` obeys `<P_k | W_k> <= P_{k-1}` under any LR model
   with arbitrary memory (Eq. 16), so `<P_n> <= 1`, and Markov's inequality
   gives `Prob_LR(P_n >= P_hat) <= min(1/P_hat, 1)` (Eq. 17). The increments of
   `P_k` are unbounded, so the Azuma-Hoeffding bound does not apply to it.
6. [P] Optimal ratio (Eqs. 18 to 20, Eq. 10, pp. 7-8). With `q` a forecast of
   the next trial's setting and outcome distribution and `p_LR` the LR
   distribution that minimizes the Kullback-Leibler divergence
   `D_KL(q|p) = sum q_x log2(q_x/p_x)` (Eq. 18), the choice
   `R_n(x) = q_x / p_LR,x` satisfies `<R_n(x)>_p <= 1` for every LR
   distribution `p` (Eq. 20). The derivation (Eq. 19) takes the first-order
   optimality condition of `p_LR` along the segment toward `p`, and it needs
   the set of LR distributions to be convex and the setting distribution to
   be fixed. The statistical strength `S_q = D_KL(q|p_LR)` is the optimal
   confidence-gain rate (Bahadur, reference [24]), and `G_PBR = S_q` (Eq. 10).
7. [P] Suboptimal forecasts and numerical optima (Appendix 3, Eqs. A.3 to
   A.8, pp. 10-11). A forecast `q'` different from `q` keeps validity and
   costs at most `D_KL(q|q')` of rate (Eq. A.3). A numerically imperfect
   `p'_LR` can break `<R> <= 1`; dividing by `1 + epsilon`, the maximum of
   `<q'/p'_{e,LR}>` over the finite set of deterministic hidden-variable
   assignments (Eq. A.7), restores validity and costs at most
   `log2(1 + epsilon)` of rate (Eq. A.8).
8. [P] Estimating the next-trial distribution (Appendix 2, pp. 9-10). The
   forecast uses maximum likelihood subject to the setting distribution and
   the no-signaling constraints (Eqs. A.1, A.2), mixed with a distribution
   uniform given the settings, `q_1 = n/(n+1) q_0 + 1/(n+1) u`, because a
   zero forecast for an event that then occurs sets a factor to 0 and the
   p-value stays at 1 for good. Blocking sets `R_k = 1` for the first block of
   `h` trials, then updates `R` once per block, with
   `h = max(ceil(N/1000), ceil(ln(2d) d))` for `d` setting-outcome
   combinations; neither validity nor optimality needs a per-trial update.
   Priming the forecast with outside knowledge is allowed when the prior and
   its weight are fixed before the data are seen. The setting-distribution
   constraint is required for Eq. (20); the no-signaling constraint is
   "not critical" (p. 9).
9. [P] Stopping (Sec. II D, p. 5). Running p-values serve to monitor and tune
   an experiment; once the experiment is configured they must not serve as a
   stopping criterion.
10. [P] Non-detection (Fig. 2 caption, p. 3). Trials with no detected photon
    carry the outcome -1, so every trial enters the analysis.
11. [P] Simulation and data (Figs. 3 and 4, p. 5). For an ideal Bell state the
    running PBR log-p-value falls below its asymptotic line by a transient
    from learning the distribution, and it ends between the SD-based and the
    martingale-based values; for 3016 trials of the ytterbium-ion data
    (`I = 2.414 +- 0.058`, unprimed) it does not clearly exceed the
    martingale value.
12. [P] Interpretation of p-values (Appendix 1, pp. 8-9). Validity concerns the
    protocol before the experiment: if LR holds, `Prob(p <= alpha) <= alpha`
    for every fixed `alpha`. The p-value is not the probability that LR is
    true. The null set contains all sequences of LR models that depend on
    earlier trials, which makes tight p-values impractical, and Markov bounds
    are conservative, so better tests exist at finite `n`.

## Do the settings need to be randomized and unknown to the device?

Randomized in every trial, from a known distribution: [P] yes. Eq. (13) and
Eq. (16) condition on the past `W_k` and then invoke the LR bound
`<R_{k-1}(x_k)> <= 1`, which the paper asserts only for the fixed setting
distribution `p_ij` (p. 7); Eq. (20) holds only when the setting distribution
constraint is met (p. 9); and the settings must be chosen independently of
the hidden variables (p. 2).

[I] Known fixed order breaks the step. Given `W_k`, the setting of trial `k`
is then deterministic, the null set of conditional distributions is no longer
cut by the setting marginal `p_ij`, and an LR model that sees the schedule
satisfies `<R_k(x)> > 1` for the ratios built against the randomized null.
The six fixed blocks of the rehearsal fall in this class.

Unknown to the device: [P] each Bell party knows its own setting, and the
null models are distributions over (setting, outcome) with the setting marginal
fixed; the paper does not ask the parties to be ignorant of their own setting.

[I] The teleportation mapping changes the roles. The input label belongs to
the preparer, and the classical null must not hold it. The rehearsal sets the
input of each iteration in that iteration's compiled circuit parameters, which
is the input-label access that the design lists among the conditions voiding
the 2/3 bound itself
([design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md),
"Teleportation benchmark"). The PBR construction presumes a null set of
distributions over (input, outcome) with the input marginal fixed and the
input unread; it does not supply or test that premise for a compiled chip.

## Transfer to the teleportation benchmark

[I] The construction needs a convex null set of per-trial distributions and a
fixed marginal for the randomized input. For the rehearsal, take the trial
record `x = (input i, accepted, correct)`, the input drawn uniformly from the
six Pauli eigenstates, and the null set the distributions that classical
measure-and-prepare instruments induce (convex, because mixtures of
instruments are instruments). The ratio `R = q / p_LR` is then valid against
every classical strategy with memory and every acceptance profile. The null
set can be widened to instruments that hold several input copies, which is the
form the accepted multiphoton sectors need; Eq. (19) to (20) use convexity
and the fixed input marginal only.

[I] The smallest instance needs no optimization. With
`D = 1{accepted and correct} - (2/3) 1{accepted}`, the single-copy identities
of the design give `E[D | past] <= 0` under random input. Then
`R = 1 + lambda D` is nonnegative for `0 <= lambda <= 3/2` and has
`E[R | past] <= 1`, so `lambda` may be set from earlier trials, as Eq. (8)
allows, and Eq. (9) gives a valid p-value. This is a deduction; the paper
treats Bell expressions only.

[I] The martingale p-value of Eq. (15) with range `r` replaces `b_u - b_l`.
For the `D` sequence `r = 1`, which reproduces the repository's Hoeffding
radius `sqrt(ln(1/alpha)/(2n))` of `hoeffding_radius` in
`scripts/qd_teleportation_rehearsal.py` for a fixed `n`. The PBR product uses
the whole distribution of (accepted, correct) and gains rate over that
radius. For the ideal CHSH case Eq. (7) gives 0.031 bits per trial (`I = 2 sqrt(2)`,
`B = 2`), against an optimal rate near 0.046 read from Fig. 1 at `theta = 45` degrees.

[I] Eq. (17) is a fixed-`n` statement, and p. 5 forbids running p-values as a
stopping rule. The product `P_k` is a nonnegative supermartingale, so a
time-uniform version through Ville's maximal inequality also holds; the paper
does not state it, and a repository use of it needs its own derivation.

## Relevance

- [I] Memory in the classical null: high. The PBR p-value is the strongest
  memory-robust construction in this batch: it holds against arbitrary
  history dependence and a drifting state, and it adapts to the actual
  distribution. It needs the random-input premise above, which the six fixed
  blocks of `scripts/qd_teleportation_rehearsal.py` do not meet.
- [I] Multiphoton components under postselection: moderate. The construction
  is agnostic to the null set as long as it is convex and the input marginal
  is fixed, so it absorbs the multi-copy classical instruments once a
  certified bound for them exists in `scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`.
  The paper computes no such bound.
- [I] Unknown attempt profile: partial. Every trial enters the analysis,
  non-detections included (item 10), so `N_attempt` must be known; the service
  reports no optical attempts. `R_k` may use all information available
  before trial `k+1` (item 5), which covers an acceptance profile estimated
  from earlier trials.
- [I] Preregistration: `R_k` must be fixed from information available before
  trial `k`; priming "blindly" before the data (item 8) matches the
  preregistered decision rule of the
  [design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md).
- [I] Not settled: no teleportation or prepare-and-measure null appears in
  the paper; the finite-`n` power of PBR for roughly 300 accepted events is
  unassessed (Fig. 4 shows no gain over the martingale value at 3016 trials
  without priming); the ancillary software is unread; and the systematic
  allowance of the design is outside the null set.
