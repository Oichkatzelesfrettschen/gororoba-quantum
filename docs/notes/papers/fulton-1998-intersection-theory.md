## Projective cones (Section B.5, pp. 432-435)

- [P] Section B.5 names [EGA] II.8, Hartshorne II.7 and Lascu-Scott as its references (p. 432).
- [P] B.5.1 (pp. 432-433): for a graded sheaf of `O_X`-algebras `S = S^0 + S^1 + ...` on a scheme `X` with `O_X -> S^0` an isomorphism and `S` locally generated by `S^1`, the cone of `S` is `C = Spec(S)` and the projective cone is
  `P(C) = Proj(S)`, with a canonical line bundle `O(1)` and a proper projection to `X`. For affine `X` with coordinate ring `A` and `S = A[x_0, ..., x_n]/I` with `I` homogeneous, `C` is the subscheme of `X x A^(n+1)` and `P(C)` the subscheme of
  `X x P^n` defined by `I`. A surjective graded homomorphism `S -> S'` gives closed embeddings of `C' = Spec(S')` in `C` and of `P(C')` in `P(C)`. For a morphism `f : Z -> X` the pull-back `f^* C = C x_X Z` is the cone on `Z` defined by `f^* S`,
  written `C|_Z` for a subscheme `Z` of `X`.
- [P] B.5.2 (p. 433): the projective completion `P(C + 1)` of a cone, in which `P(C)` is the hyperplane at infinity with complement `C`. B.5.3 (pp. 433-434): the irreducible components of a cone, their supports, and their correspondence with those
  of `P(C + 1)`. B.5.4 (p. 434): a graded algebra generated by `S^1` with `O_X -> S^0` only surjective defines a cone over the closed subscheme cut out by the kernel, called the support of the cone.
- [P] B.5.5 (p. 434): a vector bundle `E` is the cone of `Sym` of the dual of its sheaf of sections, and `P(E) = Proj` of that algebra is the bundle of lines in `E`.

## Normal cones and blowing up (Section B.6, pp. 435-437)

- [P] Section B.6 names [EGA] II.8 as its reference (p. 435).
- [P] B.6.1 (p. 435): for a closed subscheme `X` of a scheme `Y` with ideal sheaf `I`, the normal cone `C_X Y` is `Spec` of the graded algebra `sum of I^n / I^(n+1)`. For a morphism `f : Y' -> Y` and `X' = f^(-1)(X)`, with `g : X' -> X` induced,
  `C_X' Y'` is a closed subcone of `g^* C_X Y`.
- [P] B.6.2 (p. 435): for a regular imbedding of codimension `d`, `C_X Y` is a vector bundle of rank `d`, the normal bundle `N_X Y`.
- [P] B.6.3 (pp. 435-436): the blow-up of `Y` along `X` is `Bl_X Y = Proj(sum of I^n)` with projection `pi`. The canonical line bundle `O(1)` is the ideal sheaf of `E = pi^(-1)(X)`, a Cartier divisor called the exceptional divisor, and `E` is the
  projective cone of `(sum of I^n) tensor O_X = sum of I^n / I^(n+1)`, that is `E = P(C_X Y)`, the projective normal cone; `pi` is an isomorphism from the complement of `E` onto `Y - X`.
- [P] B.6.4 (p. 436): if `Y` is a variety and `X` a closed subscheme, `Bl_X Y` is a variety, because the algebra `sum of I^n` of an ideal in a domain is a domain. B.6.5: if `X` is nowhere dense in `Y`, `pi` is birational. B.6.6: if `Y` has pure
  dimension `k`, so has `C_X Y`. B.6.7: if `Y` is flat over a nonsingular curve `T`, so is `Bl_X Y`.
- [P] B.6.8 (p. 436): if `X` is a Cartier divisor on `Y` the blow-up is `Y`; for the product of the ideal of `X` with that of an effective Cartier divisor `D`, the blow-up is canonically that of `X`, with exceptional divisor `E + pi^* D`.
- [P] B.6.9 (pp. 436-437): for a closed imbedding `X` in `Y`, a morphism `f : Y' -> Y` and `X' = f^(-1)(X)`, there is a closed imbedding of `Bl_X' Y'` in `Bl_X Y x_Y Y'`, and the induced morphism of blow-ups pulls the exceptional divisor `E` back
  to `E'`. B.6.10 (p. 437): the residual-scheme relation and the normal bundle of the proper transform for regular imbeddings `X` in `Y` in `Z`.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Exceptional fiber. The A_5 fiber-cone program (`docs/evidence/a5-exceptional-fibers/ledger.tsv`, rows A5-416 and A5-418) identifies the fiber of the blow-up of a local ring `(R, m)` along an ideal `I` inside `m`, over the closed point, with
  `Proj` of the fiber cone `F_I(R) = R[It]/m R[It]` ([Huneke and Swanson 2006](huneke-swanson-2006-integral-closure-ideals-rings-modules.md), Definition 5.1.5). B.6.3 gives the exceptional divisor as `Proj` of the associated graded ring
  `sum of I^n / I^(n+1)` over `X = V(I)`. The fiber over the closed point lies in the exceptional divisor because `I` is inside `m`, and the affine description of B.5.1 (the projective cone is the subscheme of `X x P^n` defined by the homogeneous
  ideal) restricts over the point to the subscheme of `P^n` over `R/m` defined by the image of that ideal, which is `Proj` of `gr_I(R) tensor R/m = F_I(R)`. The restriction step is this note's inference from B.5.1; the book states the pull-back for
  cones (`f^* C = C x_X Z`) and does not print the sentence for projective cones or for the fiber over a point.
- [I] Strict transforms and base change. B.6.9 is the form of [Eisenbud and Harris 2000](eisenbud-harris-2000-geometry-of-schemes.md) Proposition IV-21 and of [Hartshorne 1977](hartshorne-1977-algebraic-geometry.md) Corollary 7.15 used when a blow-up
  is computed inside a larger one; B.6.1 adds that the normal cone of the pulled-back center embeds in the pull-back of the normal cone, with no claim of equality.
- [I] Dimension. B.6.6 gives that the normal cone, and so the exceptional divisor of a pure-dimensional scheme, has pure dimension; it gives no bound on the dimension of a single fiber of the exceptional divisor, which is the analytic spread minus one
  (Huneke and Swanson, Definition 5.1.5 and Corollary 8.3.9).
- [I] What the pages read do not give. (a) The conventions of Appendix B on the words scheme and variety (Section B.1) were not read, so the note carries B.6.4 with the book's word variety and its stated reason. (b) No graph-closure description of the
  blow-up; see Eisenbud and Harris, Proposition IV-22. (c) No statement on integral closure, Rees valuations or the equations of a Rees algebra. (d) No comparison between characteristics.
