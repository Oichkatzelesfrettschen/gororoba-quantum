## Transcribed statements

- [P] Setting (p. 1, Eq. 1): a (split) octonion is `o = q + l p` with `q, p` in `H` and `l^2 = +-1`, with product
  `(q_1 + l p_1)(q_2 + l p_2) = q_1 q_2 + l^2 p_2 p_1* + l (q_2 p_1 + q_1* p_2)`.
- [P] Section 2 (pp. 1-2): the linear map `M(o) = [[q, l^2 p*], [p, q*]]` (Eq. 2); the reversal marker (Definition 2.1)
  writes `x y` with the arrow for `y x`; the interlaced product of `2 x 2` matrices (Definition 2.3, Eq. 3) expands the usual
  matrix product with reversed order in the off-diagonal contractions. Proposition 2.4 (p. 2): `M(o_1) (interlaced) M(o_2) = M(o_1 o_2)`.
- [P] Section 3 (p. 2): each block of the product is a sum of one right-ordered and one left-ordered product, in the pattern
  `[[R + L, L + R], [L + R, R + L]]`; the author states that the explicit "(R + L)" mnemonic does not appear in the classical
  literature.
- [P] Section 4 (pp. 2-3): for `A_(n+1) = A_n + eps A_n`, `eps^2 = +-1`, `M_(n+1)(a + eps b) = [[a, eps^2 b*], [b, a*]]`; Theorem 4.1
  (p. 3): the interlaced product on `M_(n+1)(.)` reproduces the Cayley-Dickson multiplication of `A_(n+1)`, so the pattern propagates
  through all `A_n`. Remark 4.2: for `n >= 4` the algebras cease to be alternative and zero divisors appear; the identity remains
  valid, but invertibility and determinant-like constructions need care.
- [P] Sections 5 and 6 (pp. 3-4): maps `M_n : A_n -> Mat_2(A_(n-1))`; speculative uses (Moufang-type identities on special
  subspaces, norm-preserving groups, determinant-like invariants); the interlaced product on `Mat_2(A)` for a unital
  non-associative algebra `A` (Eq. 6, `[[a, b], [c, d]] (interlaced) [[a', b'], [c', d']] = [[a a' + c' b, b' a + b d'], [a' c + d c', c b' + d' d]]`).
  Proposition 6.2: left and right inverses of `X` for the interlaced product exist when `Delta_L = d a - c b` is invertible in the
  left nucleus and `Delta_R = a d - c b` is invertible in the right nucleus.

## Convention map and cd_core check

[I] Product. The printed doubling (Eq. 1 and its tower form) differs from `cd_core`'s
`(a,b)(c,d) = (ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))`. The note's check shows that, for `l^2 = -1`, it generates the opposite
algebra of `cd_core` on the basis: the basis products satisfy `e_p e_q (Gazeau) = e_q e_p (cd_core)` in every cell.

[I] Check results: no mismatch with the paper's own statements. A scratch script (ignored `docs/local/`, run with `-I`) built
the paper's doubling recursively, compared it with `cd_core.cd_sign` and replayed the matrix identity with exact integers:

| Paper statement | Result |
| --- | --- |
| index of the product of basis elements equals the XOR of the indices | true at `D = 8, 16, 32` |
| the printed doubling with `l^2 = -1` against `cd_core` | the sign of `e_p e_q` equals `cd_sign(q, p)` in 64 of 64, 256 of 256 and 1,024 of 1,024 cells, and equals `cd_sign(p, q)` in only 22, 46 and 94 cells |
| Proposition 2.4 and Theorem 4.1, `M(x) (interlaced) M(y) = M(x y)` | holds on 5 random integer pairs at each `n = 2..6` (`D = 4..64`) for both `l^2 = -1` and `l^2 = +1` |

[I] The identity of Theorem 4.1 is tested for the paper's own product, whose blocks use the explicit form of Eq. 6. The note
did not replay the identity through `cd_core`'s product, which would need the exchange of factors implied by the
opposite-algebra relation. Sections 5 and 6 are programmatic and carry no testable claim; Proposition 6.2 was not replayed.

## Repository bearing

[I] Multiplication convention. The paper's product is the opposite of `cd_core` (and of [Lemley](2609.11588.md) and
[Wilmot](2512.07210.md), which agree with `cd_core`); a table taken from this paper needs its factors exchanged before it
is compared with `cd_sign`, as for [Saniga et al.](1405.6888.md).

[I] Zero divisors and Stiefel elements: none. Remark 4.2 notes the loss of alternativity and the appearance of zero divisors from
`n = 4` without a statement about their structure; the interlaced matrix form gives a block `2 x 2` presentation of left
multiplication that the repository's annihilator-dimension claims (`a5-stiefel-universal`, `a6-stiefel-witness`,
`dimension32-global-topology`) do not use. Moreno's Stiefel question (`math/0512517` Section 5): none. Dimensions 64 and above:
the identity is stated for all `n` and replayed to `n = 6` (dimension 64).
