## Setting and conventions

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): the paper relates the `j`-multiplicity of an ideal to the degree of its fiber cone under some constraints, computes the `j`-multiplicity of all ideals defining rational normal scrolls, and computes the `j`- and epsilon-multiplicity of ideals of determinantal varieties as integrals that "are central in random matrix theory".
- [P] Theorem 3.1 (p. 7) uses the Rees algebra `R(I)`, the associated graded ring `G(I)` and the fiber cone `F(I)`, with `l(I)` the analytic spread. Theorem 6.3 names `A_t(Z) = F(P_(2t)(Z))`, "the algebra of pfaffians" (p. 21).

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 3.1 (p. 7): let `A = sum_(k>=0) A_k` be a standard graded noetherian domain over a field `K = A_0` with `m = sum_(k>0) A_k`, and `I` an ideal generated in a single degree `t >= 1` with `l(I) = dim A`. Then (i) `j(I)` is a positive integer multiple of `e(F(I))`; (ii) `j(I) >= t e(F(I))`, with equality if and only if `R(I)` localized at `m R(I)` is a discrete valuation ring; (iii) if `[(I^s)^sat]_r = [I^s]_r` for all `s >> 0` and all `r >= st`, then `j(I) = t e(F(I))`.
- [P] Theorem 6.3 (pp. 20-21): let `Z` be a generic skew-symmetric `n x n` matrix, `m = floor(n/2)`, `0 < t < m`, `c = (C(n, 2) - 1)! / (m! (n - 1)! (n - 2)! ... 1!)` and `delta(n) = 0` for even `n`, `1` for odd `n`. Then (i) `j(P_(2t)(Z)) = c t` times the integral over `[0,1]^m` with `sum z_i = t` of `(z_1 ... z_m)^(2 delta(n)) prod_(i<j) (z_j - z_i)^4` against the measure `dv`; (ii) the epsilon-multiplicity is `c C(n, 2)` times the integral of the same integrand over the region `max_i{z_i} + t - 1 <= sum z_i <= t`; (iii) if `char(K) = 0` or `char(K) > min{2t, n - 2t}`, then `e(A_t(Z)) = c` times the integral of the integrand of (i) over the hypersimplex `sum z_i = t`. Theorems 6.1 and 6.2 give the determinantal and symmetric analogues (p. 20 shows Theorem 6.2).
- [P] Corollary 6.4 (p. 21): for the ideals of Theorems 6.1, 6.2 and 6.3 the epsilon-multiplicity is a rational number.
- [P] Remark 6.5 (p. 22): outside the ranges of Theorems 6.1 to 6.3 the analytic spread is not maximal and the `j`- and epsilon-multiplicities vanish; `A_m(Z)` is a polynomial ring in one variable if `n = 2m` and a polynomial ring in `n` variables if `n = 2m + 1`, by Huneke's result that these ideals are of linear type.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The fiber cone `F(I)` of the repository is the algebra of Pfaffians of a section of the skew family `a -> L_a` ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)). Theorem 6.3(iii) gives the degree of the generic algebra of Pfaffians `A_t(Z)` for `n` up to 32 under the characteristic condition, and the primes 32009 and 32029 satisfy `p > min{2t, n - 2t}` because that minimum is at most 16 for `n <= 32`. The theorem therefore fixes the ambient degree and nothing about a restriction to a linear section.
- [I] Theorem 3.1 requires a standard graded domain and an ideal generated in one degree with maximal analytic spread. The repository ideal is local with generators of mixed order at a point of a smaller orbit, so the theorem applies to a homogeneous model (the principal Pfaffians of `L_a` of one degree in `a` on the projective stratum) and then concerns the fiber cone of the global kernel image, not the local special fiber. Whether hypothesis (iii) of Theorem 3.1 holds for a non-maximal Pfaffian ideal restricted to a subvariety is not established by the pages opened.
- [I] Hypothesis (iii) is a statement about saturation degrees of the powers of `I`, not a bound on the degrees of the defining equations of `F(I)`, so no stopping degree for the repository fiber computations follows from it.
