## Setting and conventions

- [P] Section 3 (pp. 405-406): the paper works over a Noetherian coefficient ring `R` with a term order; `lp`, `lc` and `lt` are the leading power product, coefficient and term; a reduced Groebner basis over a field has monic leading coefficients and is unique.
- [P] Page 406: the Hilbert function method requires a homogeneous ideal; for an arbitrary ideal one homogenizes the generators, computes a Groebner basis of the homogeneous ideal and dehomogenizes, so that, in the paper's words, "all ideals are assumed to be homogeneous" without loss of generality.
- [P] Definition 5.1 (p. 407): for `I` generated by primitive integer polynomials `f_1, ..., f_r`, with `G` the reduced Groebner basis of `I` and `G_p` that of `I_p` (the ideal of `Z_p[X]` generated by the reductions of the `f_i`), a prime `p` is lucky for `I` if and only if `Lp(G) = Lp(G_p)`.
  The paper states that two primes cannot be compared by their leading power products alone, which motivates the Hilbert function.
- [P] Section 5.1 (p. 408): `I` is a homogeneous ideal of `Q[X]` and the Hilbert function `HF_I(n) = dim_Q (Q[X][n]/I[n])` is the counterpart of the degree of the GCD. Definition 5.2 (p. 408): a prime `p` is Hilbert lucky for `I` if and only if `HF_I = HF_(I_p)`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 5.3 (p. 408): for every degree `n`, `HF_I(n) <= HF_(I_p)(n)`. The proof compares `I` with the ideal `J` of `Z[X]` generated by the same `f_i`, shows `dim_Q I[n] = rank_Z J[n] >= dim_(Z_p) I_p[n]`, and concludes by counting dimensions of the free abelian groups of polynomials of degree `n`.
- [P] Corollary 5.4 and Theorem 5.5 (p. 409): the Hilbert unlucky primes are exactly the prime divisors of the first invariant factor `d_1^(n)` in the decomposition of `Z[X][n]/J[n]`, equivalently the primes `p` for which `Z[X]/J` has a `p`-torsion element. Theorem 5.6 (p. 409): for a Hilbert lucky prime, the leading power products of `G_p` do not exceed those of `G` in increasing order, so
  leading terms only "go down" modulo a prime. Example 5.7 (p. 410): for an ideal of `Q[y, x]` in the degree lexicographical ordering with `y > x`, the prime 5 is lucky and Hilbert lucky while the prime 2 is Hilbert unlucky, so comparing leading power products of `G_2` with those of `G_5` without checking Hilbert luckiness first would discard the lucky prime 5.
- [P] Theorem 7.1 (p. 416), the verification criterion, for a homogeneous ideal `I` of `Q[X]` as everywhere in the paper: let `G~` be a subset of `Q[X]` with `Lp(G~) = Lp(G_p)` for the reduction at a prime `p`, such that `G~` is a Groebner basis of the ideal `<G~>` it generates and `I` is contained in `<G~>`; then `I = <G~>`. The proof (p. 416) runs
  `HF_I <= HF_(I_p) = HF_(<G~>) <= HF_I`, using Theorem 5.3 and `I` contained in `<G~>`. The paper adds that `G~` is then the reduced Groebner basis of `I`, and that the check does not require `p` to be a lucky prime.
- [P] Section 7 (p. 416): the check has two parts, that `G~` is a Groebner basis of `<G~>` (all S-polynomials reduce to zero, with the Buchberger and Gebauer-Moeller criteria) and that `I` is contained in `<G~>` (the generators `f_i` reduce to zero by `G~`); the reverse inclusion would be as hard as computing a Groebner basis of `I`,
  and Theorem 7.1 avoids it by keeping the leading power products fixed through the lifting.
- [P] Sections 6.2 and 6.3 (pp. 415-416): the Farey rational map recovers rational coefficients from the `p`-adic lift when the coefficient bound `N` satisfies `N <= sqrt(p^i/2)` (p. 415); the Chinese remainder lifting forms `G_(kp)` from `G_k` and `G_p` for a product `k` of lucky primes, with Theorem 6.3 (p. 415) giving `G = G_n mod n` for a product `n` of lucky primes.
  Section 8 and Table 1 (pp. 416-417) report timings on three examples in `Q[x, y, z]` against CoCoA, Macaulay 2 and Maple.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 program computes special fibers of Rees algebras with Singular over `Q(i)` and modulo the primes 32009 and 32029, and its ledger separates results over `F_p` from characteristic 0 statements. Theorem 5.3 gives, for a homogeneous ideal with fixed integer generators, the one-sided
  bound of the Hilbert function over `Q` by the Hilbert function of each reduction, so a Hilbert function measured modulo a prime bounds the characteristic 0 one from above when the same generators define both ideals. Theorem 7.1 is the verification step that turns a modular candidate into a statement over `Q`
  without a lucky-prime hypothesis, for homogeneous ideals.
- [I] The paper works over `Q` and `Z`, and its setting is homogeneous (Section 3 reduces the general case by homogenization). The program's ground field is `Q(i)`, reduced at split primes with `i` sent to a square root of -1 modulo `p`, so a transfer of Theorem 5.3 or Theorem 7.1 needs the same argument over `Z[i]`, which this paper does not supply;
  the later paper on standard bases for non-homogeneous ideals ([1005.5663](1005.5663.md), whose Theorem 2.4 admits a counterexample as printed) and the paper on number fields ([1504.04564](1504.04564.md)) address the other two gaps.
