## Statements

- [P] Problem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 (p. 1): for a Laurent polynomial with `s` monomials in `n` variables over F_q and augmented support matrix `U` of rank rho, a quantum algorithm outputs an estimate within epsilon q^(n + s/2 - rho) of the torus point count N(f) with probability 1 - delta, in poly(n, s, log q, 1/epsilon, log(1/delta)) on the promise class rho <= r_0, ||U||_inf <= C_0.
- [P] Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 4.2 (pp. 2, 9): with `U` free in the input, the same approximation with 0 < epsilon < 1/4 and delta = 1/3 is #P-hard under randomized polynomial-time Turing reductions; the reduction uses s = n = rho and ||U||_inf = (q - 1)/2 (Remark 4.3, p. 10).
- [P] Proposition 3.1 and Eq. (3.5) (pp. 4-5): N(f) = (q - 1)^n / q + q^(s/2 - 1)(q - 1)^(n + 1 - rho) B_U(q) Xi_f, a normalized Gauss-sum average estimated by Hadamard tests on samples from the Smith-normal-form kernel K_U(q) (Algorithm 1, p. 6; Theorem 3.3, p. 7).
- [P] Section 5 (p. 10): no a-priori reduction relates torus counting of Laurent polynomials to ordinary affine counting in the approximation setting, and approximate counting for ordinary polynomial systems is #P-hard as well (Appendix D).
