## Setting and conventions

- [P] Data 3.1 (p. 7): `k` a field, `R = k[x_1, ..., x_d]`, `T = k[T_1, ..., T_n]`, `S = R[T_1, ..., T_n]`, `m = (x_1, ..., x_d)`,
  `I` a homogeneous ideal minimally generated by forms `g_1, ..., g_n` of one degree `delta`. These define the rational map
  `Psi = [g_1 : ... : g_n] : P^(d-1) --> P^(n-1)` with base locus `V(I)`, closed image `X` and homogeneous coordinate ring
  `A = T/I(X)`.
- [P] The Rees ring `R(I) = R[It]` is the image of `pi : S -> R(I)`, `T_i -> g_i t`, with kernel `J`; the grading is
  `deg x_i = (1, 0)`, `deg T_i = (0, 1)`, `deg t = (-delta, 1)`. `L` is the ideal of `Sym(I)` in `S`, generated by the entries of
  `[T_1, ..., T_n] phi = [x_1, ..., x_d] B`, where `phi` is a minimal homogeneous presentation matrix and `B` a Jacobian dual
  with `d` rows; for linear `phi` the entries of `B` lie in `T`. Cramer's rule gives `L + I_d(B) subset J`, and equality is the
  "expected form". `I` is of fiber type if `J = L + I(X) S`. The special fiber ring is `A`.
- [P] Condition `G_d` (Artin-Nagata): `mu(I_p) <= dim R_p` for every prime `p` in `V(I)` with `dim R_p <= d - 1`, equivalently
  `i < ht Fitt_i(I)` for `0 < i < d`. A height three Gorenstein ideal has an odd number `n` of minimal generators, and by the
  Buchsbaum-Eisenbud structure theorem its presentation matrix `phi` can be chosen alternating.
- [P] Definition 8.1 (p. 24): for an `n x n` alternating matrix `phi` of linear forms in `R`, with Jacobian dual `B`, form the
  `(n + d) x (n + d)` alternating matrix `[[phi, -B^t], [B, 0]]`; `F_i` is `(-1)^(n+d-i)` times the Pfaffian with row and column
  `i` removed, viewed in `T[x_1, ..., x_d]`, and `C(phi) = c_T(F_(n+d))` is the content ideal of `F_(n+d)` in `T`.
- [P] Invariants used (Section 2): `topdeg`, `indeg`, `b_0`, `a_i`, `b_i`, regularity `reg M = max{a_i(M) + i}`, the unmixed part
  `I^unm`, grade, perfect and Gorenstein ideals, and the largest generator degree `b_0(M)`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Theorem 9.1 (main theorem, p. 28; stated as Theorem 9.1 in the introduction, p. 3): let `I` be a linearly presented
  height three Gorenstein ideal in `R = k[x_1, ..., x_d]` satisfying `G_d`, minimally generated by `g_1, ..., g_n`. Then
  (a) `J = L + I_d(B) S + C(phi) S`, and `C(phi) = 0` when `d` is odd, so `J` has the expected form; (b) `I(X) = I_d(B) + C(phi)`;
  (c) `J = L :_S (x_1, ..., x_d)(T_1, ..., T_n)`; (d) `I(X) = I_d(B) :_T (T_1, ..., T_n) = I_d(B)^unm`. In particular `I` is of
  fiber type.
- [P] Remark 9.2: linear presentation can be weakened to the condition that the entries of `phi` generate a complete
  intersection ideal (flat descent from `k[y_1, ..., y_s]` with `I_1(phi) = (y_1, ..., y_s)`).
- [P] Corollary 9.3 (p. 29): `X` is Cohen-Macaulay; if `n <= d + 1` then `A` is Cohen-Macaulay, and otherwise `depth A = 1` for odd `d`
  and `2` for even `d`; for odd `d < n`, `I(X)` has a linear free resolution and `reg(A) = d - 1`; for `d < n` the Hilbert
  series of `A` is given by the formula of part (d).
- [P] Theorem 4.3 (p. 12): for a linearly presented height three Gorenstein `I`, a subideal `a` of `s >= 3` generators in
  degrees `indeg(I)` and `indeg(I) + 1`, `alpha` the number of generators of `I/a` and `J = a : I` an `s`-residual
  intersection, the ring `R/J` has multiplicity `e = sum_(i=0)^(floor((alpha - 1)/2)) C(s + alpha - 2 - 2i, s - 1)`.
  Theorem 4.4 (p. 12): for `d <= n`, linearly presented height three Gorenstein `I` with `G_d`, `A` has dimension `d` and
  multiplicity `e(A) = sum_(i=0)^(floor((n - d)/2)) C(n - 2 - 2i, d - 2)`, the number of monomials of even (odd) degree at most
  `n - d` in `d - 1` variables for odd (even) `d`. Proposition 4.1: the map `Psi` is birational onto its image for linear `phi`.
- [P] Theorem 5.6 and Corollary 5.4 (Section 5): bounds on the generator degrees of local cohomology `H^i_m(M)` through
  approximate resolutions `C_*`, for instance `H^0_m(M)` is concentrated in degrees `<= b_0(C_d) - d` and generated in
  degrees `<= b_0(C_(d-1)) - d + 1`.
- [P] Theorem 6.1 (pp. 15-16) and Corollary 6.4: for `I` satisfying `G_d`, the ideal `A = H^0_m(Sym(I))` of the symmetric algebra
  is annihilated by an explicit power of `m`, with bounds in terms of the column degrees (perfect height two) or the common
  entry degree `D` (Gorenstein height three; `m^(d(D-1)+1) A = 0` for odd `d`). For `D = 1` Corollary 6.4 gives that `I` is of
  fiber type and `A` is annihilated by `m^((n-d+1)/2)` for even `d`.
- [P] Theorem 7.2 (p. 21): under the hypotheses of Lemma 7.1 (linear type on the punctured spectrum and approximate linear
  resolutions of a high symmetric power), `sqrt(I(X)) = sqrt(I_d(B'))`, `ht I_d(B') = n - dim A` and `sqrt(J) = sqrt((L, I_d(B)))`.
- [P] Theorem 8.7 (p. 24 ff., restated from the authors' earlier work on matrices annihilated by a vector of indeterminates):
  for `3 <= d < n`, `n` odd and `n - d <= ht I_d(B)`, the unmixed part `I_d(B)^unm = I_d(B) + C(phi)`, `T/(I_d(B) + C(phi))` is
  Cohen-Macaulay on the punctured spectrum with depth 1, 2 or `n - 1` according to `d`, `I_d(B)` is unmixed with linear
  resolution for odd `d`, and the Hilbert series is explicit. Corollary 8.8: `I_d(B) + C(phi) subset I_d(B) :_T (T_1, ..., T_n)
  subset I(X)`.
- [P] Proof architecture (introduction, p. 4): `I` is shown to be of fiber type, then `I_d(B) + C(phi)` and `I(X)` are shown to
  be unmixed of equal height `n - d`, and the multiplicities are equated through `deg X` via residual intersections.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The repository's exceptional fiber is the fiber cone `F(I) = sum I^d / m I^d` of the kernel-coordinate ideal at a point
  of a smaller orbit ([A_5 exceptional fiber findings](../topics/a5-exceptional-fiber-findings.md)). For an ideal generated in
  one degree, `F(I)` equals the special fiber ring `A = T/I(X)` of this paper, and the repository's Hilbert function
  `h_d` is the Hilbert function of `A`. Theorem 9.1 would give `I(X)` as `I_d(B) + C(phi)` and Corollary 9.3 the full Hilbert
  series, in place of the degree-by-degree rank computation.
- [I] The hypotheses of Theorem 9.1 are not established for any repository ideal: the paper needs `R` a polynomial ring over a
  field, `I` of height three, Gorenstein, linearly presented and satisfying `G_d`. The repository works over the coordinate
  ring of an orbit closure `Y` at a singular point, so `R` is not a polynomial ring there. Whether a kernel-coordinate ideal is
  Gorenstein of height three is not computed.
- [I] Observed parity of the generator counts of the repository table: `n = 15` (pair X+ to N+ and X- to N-), `17` (Iso) and `21`
  (E8-bar to N+ and N-) are odd, while `24` (W8 to N-) and `10` (E8-bar to VB) are even. A height three Gorenstein ideal has an
  odd number of generators, so the even pairs cannot satisfy the hypotheses as stated.
- [I] If the hypotheses held at `(n, d) = (15, 3)`, Theorem 4.4 would give `e(A) = 13 + 11 + 9 + 7 + 5 + 3 + 1 = 49`; whether any repository
  fiber matches is untested. The statement is a falsifiable prediction, `dim A = d` and `deg X = 49`, and the dimension bound
  of 3 in the repository table is the same number as `d` only under that reading.
- [I] The paper's `G_d` condition and the "linear type on the punctured spectrum" hypothesis of Theorem 7.2 correspond to the
  repository's tested claim that the Rees ideal is radical and prime through the origin; no comparison was made.
