## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are PDF page indices.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): ionizing radiation from environmental radioactive materials and cosmic rays
  raises the quasiparticle density, which "would ultimately limit the coherence times of
  superconducting qubits of the type measured here to the millisecond regime"; a lead shield reduces the
  flux and correlates with longer energy-relaxation time. The model infers an energy-relaxation rate
  `Gamma_1 ~ 1/4 ms^-1` for the qubit if it were limited only by the measured cosmic-ray and
  laboratory background radiation.
- [P] Section on the measurement (p. 3): the qubits Q1 and Q2 have transition frequency
  `omega_q = 2 pi x 3.48 GHz`; a calibrated 64Cu source supplies the radiation used to fix the model
  parameter `a` that converts deposited power density into energy relaxation.
- [P] Power density (p. 4): a NaI detector and a Geant4 transport simulation give a total external
  power density `P_ext = (0.10 +- 0.02) keV mm^-3 s^-1` deposited in the aluminum; about 60 % is from decays in
  the concrete walls (`0.06 keV mm^-3 s^-1`) and about 40 % from cosmic rays (`0.04 keV mm^-3 s^-1`).
- [P] Limits (p. 4): the lower limit on the relaxation rate from external radiation alone is
  `Gamma_1 ~ 1/3950 us^-1` for Q1 and `1/3130 us^-1` for Q2, the point where laboratory radiation
  becomes the dominant limit; the radiation background gives a quasiparticle density
  `x_qp ~ 7 x 10^-9`, in line with the lowest reported excess densities.
- [P] Shielding (pp. 5-6): the expected effect of the lead shield with no internal radiation source is
  `delta Gamma_1 ~ 1/15.5 ms^-1`, 0.26 % of Q1's relaxation rate. Cycling the shield up and down
  every 15 minutes over many cycles gives a median shift `delta Gamma_1 = 1/22.7 ms^-1` with a 95 %
  confidence interval `[1/75.8, 1/12.4] ms^-1`.
- [P] Methods (p. 10): the cosmic-ray muon flux from the CRY generator is
  `1.24 x 10^-2 cm^-2 s^-1`, and a fit to the low-gain NaI data gives `(9.7 +- 0.1) x 10^-3 cm^-2 s^-1`,
  about 20 % lower; the muon-induced power density is `(0.042 +- 0.002) keV mm^-3 s^-1`.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Size: the radiation floor `Gamma_1 ~ 1/4 ms^-1` is a `T_1` limit of 4 ms, while the paper's qubits
  have baseline relaxation times near 40 and 32 microseconds, so the radiation term is about 1 % of the
  total relaxation rate. The repository's retained IBM Heron r2 calibration moves
  ([ibm_heron_platform.md](../ibm_heron_platform.md)) and Tuna-17 readout shifts
  ([tuna-17.md](../quantum_inspire/tuna-17.md)) are larger than that term.
- [I] Signature of a radiation origin: a time-independent deposited power density with
  Poisson-distributed bursts that carry no dependence on the sidereal angle or on the circuit content.
  The lead-shield contrast of the paper (a 15 minute up/down cycle) is the controlled test; this note carries no such test.
