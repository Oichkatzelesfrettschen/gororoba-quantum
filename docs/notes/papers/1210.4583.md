## Results needed by the project

1. [P] The class of separable quantum states is precisely the set generated
   exclusively by LOCC acting on pure product states, and any sensible
   entanglement measure has an expected value that does not increase under
   LOCC (Sec. 1, p. 2). Teleportation consumes one entangled bit plus two
   classical bits per transmitted qubit under LOCC (p. 2).
2. [P] A multipartite state is fully separable when it is a convex
   combination of product states (p. 6). An instrument `J = (E_1, E_2, ...)`
   belongs to SEP when each `E_j` is a separable map: the state
   `(I' (x) E_j)[rho]` of Eq. (2) is separable whenever the input `rho`,
   including its ancilla copies `A'_1 ... A'_N`, is separable (p. 7).
3. [P] Eq. (3) (p. 7): for `N >= 2` parties, a sufficiently large index set
   and any `r >= 2`, the classes obey
   `LOCC_1 < LOCC_r < LOCC_(r+1) < LOCC_fin < LOCC < closure(LOCC) < SEP < PPT`
   with every inclusion proper. `LOCC_r` is the class of `r`-round protocols
   and `LOCC_fin` (the paper's blackboard-N subscript) the class of
   finite-round protocols. The paper states that SEP admits a simpler
   mathematical characterization than LOCC through the Choi-Jamiolkowski
   isomorphism: separable operations are precisely the maps whose Choi
   matrices are separable (pp. 2-3).
4. [P] Every separable instrument is implementable by LOCC with a nonzero
   success probability, at least `1/(m d^2)` for `m` outcomes and total
   dimension `d`, with the depolarizing map `D` as the failure outcome
   (stochastic LOCC, Lemma 1, p. 8). Shared randomness at the first round
   makes LOCC convex (Proposition 1, p. 8).
5. [P] The paper restricts itself to a finite number of finite-dimensional
   systems (p. 4).

### Corrections to the earlier reading

The full reading changed three statements. The separable-state definition sits
on p. 6, and only Eq. (2) with the SEP and PPT bullets sits on p. 7; item 2
now carries both pages. The earlier introduction called the paper "a
structural survey"; the abstract and Secs. 3-4 present new results (a nonempty
interior of LOCC, compactness of finite-round LOCC with a bounded outcome
count, and a two-qubit instrument in the closure but not in LOCC), so the
sentence now says so. No [P] statement of the earlier note is contradicted by
the PDF.

## Repository use

The repository uses this source in
[the Ascella-Belenos design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md)
(sections "The partition constraint", "Claim levels" and "Gates") and in
[`scripts/qd_network_protocol.py`](../../../scripts/qd_network_protocol.py).

[I] LOCC maps separable states to separable states, including states shared
with ancillas held by the same parties. The statement follows from item 3:
LOCC is contained in `closure(LOCC)`, which is contained in SEP, and item 2
defines a separable map as one that preserves separability with ancillas. A
hardware or simulation claim that a classical channel plus local operations
created entanglement contradicts this inclusion, so a measured entanglement
witness on a state prepared with only local operations and classical
feed-forward signals a modeling error or a non-local resource.

[I] Conditioning on the full classical transcript. The authority is the
per-outcome definition on p. 7: an instrument is in SEP only if each `E_j` is
a separable map, so the unnormalized state attached to every individual
outcome string (the fine-grained transcript) is separable, and coarse-graining
sums separable maps. The design's partition-constraint sentence ("a separable
state across the Ascella|Belenos cut stays separable, including after
conditioning on the full classical transcript") therefore rests on p. 7 and
Eq. (3). It supports claim levels C2 to C6, each of which needs an entangled
resource across the cut, and gate G2 (physical access), which is the only
route to such a resource when the two QPUs are independent jobs.

[I] Row-by-row pairing of two independent terminal jobs. Two jobs plus a
workstation form a protocol whose only inter-site resource is classical data,
and Proposition 1 (p. 8) adds shared randomness at no cost. Lemma 1 (p. 8)
shows that selecting records by any rule both sites can compute is a
stochastic LOCC outcome of a separable instrument, so post-selected joint
records stay inside SEP. Pairing outputs by row index therefore establishes no
level C2 to C6 claim and no gate G4 to G8 pass, whatever the counts.

[I] The inclusion runs one way: a map in SEP need not be in LOCC. Use SEP
only to bound what LOCC cannot do, and never as an implementation. The strict
steps of Eq. (3) are not needed by any repository statement.

[I] Assumptions that limit the transfer. The paper treats finite-dimensional
parties with arbitrary local maps and error-free classical messages. The
design restricts itself to a fixed photon number per protocol model
(`scripts/qd_network_protocol.py`), which is finite-dimensional, and the
statement covers loss and multiphoton emission only after a fixed
finite-dimensional embedding that includes the lost and extra photons. The
paper does not address the statistical estimation of a witness from finite
counts; the separable bound of G4 (witness above 1/2) is derived in
[the Horodecki note](quant-ph-9605038.md), and the Hoeffding radius that
turns counts into a lower bound is not in this source or in any of the four
network sources; it belongs to [Hoeffding 1963](hoeffding-1963.md).

[I] Teleportation accounting. Item 1 states the ideal exchange rate (one
entangled bit and two classical bits per qubit). The design's partial
linear-optics Bell analyzer accepts each Pauli input with probability 1/2 and
spends a dual-rail pair of modes per qubit; the ideal rate gives the resource
count behind C3 and says nothing about the 1/2 acceptance, which comes from
[quant-ph/0007058](quant-ph-0007058.md).
