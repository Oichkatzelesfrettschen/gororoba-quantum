## Results needed by the project

The paper writes `mu` for the mean photon number of a weak coherent pulse,
`P(mu, N) = exp(-mu) mu^N / N!` for its photon-number law, `eta_M` for the
memory's storage-and-retrieval efficiency, `eta_C` for the effective
efficiency of the classical device, `N_min` for a photon-number threshold and
`gamma` for the probability of emitting at the threshold itself.

1. [P] The memory stores polarization qubits as weak coherent pulses with
   `mu` from 0.01 to 36 (p. 1), in two spatially separated ensembles of a
   praseodymium-doped crystal, for 500 ns with an average storage-and-retrieval
   efficiency `eta_M = (10.6 +- 2.3)` % (p. 3). The fidelity is conditional: it
   assumes that a photon was re-emitted (p. 1).
2. [P] `mu` is fixed by the detection probability per pulse with the atoms
   off resonance, back-propagated with detector efficiency `eta_D = 50` % and a
   transmission `eta_t = 40` % from the input to the detector (p. 2).
3. [P] Table I (p. 3) lists raw conditional fidelities for six inputs at
   `mu = 0.4`: 0.982, 0.983, 0.968, 0.938, 0.954 and 0.926 for H, V, D, A, R
   and L, with `eta_M` between 8 % and 10 %. The mean is quoted as
   `F_mean = (96 +- 2)` %, a lower bound because it refers to a target state
   (p. 4). Fig. 4 plots the average of three inputs (V, D, R) against `mu`
   without background subtraction, with dark-count-subtracted points beside it;
   the raw fidelity falls below `mu = 0.1` because of detector dark counts, and
   the measured fidelity is too low to prove a quantum memory at `mu >= 3.5`
   (p. 4).
4. [P] Main-text benchmark (p. 4): the best classical strategy for a state of
   `N` qubits reaches `F_c = (N+1)/(N+2)` (Massar and Popescu, reference 39).
   For a weak coherent state the classical fidelity is
   `F_class(mu) = sum_{N>=1} (N+1)/(N+2) * P(mu, N)/(1 - P(mu, 0))`
   (Eq. 1, p. 4), valid for a memory of unit efficiency. If `eta_M < 1` the
   classical memory can output only when the photon number is high, which
   improves its estimate (p. 4; the idea is credited to
   [Specht et al.](1103.1528.md), reference 20).
   [I] Reference 39 is S. Massar and S. Popescu, "Optimal extraction of
   information from finite quantum ensembles," Phys. Rev. Lett. 74, 1259 (1995),
   DOI https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.74.1259. The repository registers it
   as a citation only: the DOI resolver and the APS PDF route answered HTTP 403
   on 2026-10-26T22:30:18Z, so the formula rests on this paper's and Specht's
   statement of it.
5. [P] Appendix A (p. 5) states the strategy class: a measure-and-prepare
   device performs classical state estimation on the input, stores the result
   and prepares a new qubit from it. The weighted sum over photon number is
   Eq. (A1), the same sum as Eq. (1) (p. 5).
6. [P] The effective classical efficiency is the probability that the classical
   device outputs a qubit given at least one input photon,
   `eta_C = P_out/(1 - P(mu, 0))` with `P_out = sum_{N>=N_min+1} P(mu, N)`
   (p. 5). A device that outputs for `N >= N_min + 1` reaches only some
   `eta_C` for a given `mu`; a general output probability is
   `P_out = gamma + sum_{N>=N_min+1} P(mu, N)` with `gamma <= P(mu, N_min)`,
   the probability of emitting at the threshold photon number (p. 5).
7. [P] Eq. (A2): `eta_C = (gamma + sum_{N>=N_min+1} P(mu, N))/(1 - P(mu, 0))`.
   Eq. (A3), with the assumption `eta_M = eta_C`:
   `N_min = min i : sum_{N>=i+1} P(mu, N) <= (1 - P(mu, 0)) eta_M`. Eq. (A4):
   `F_class = [ (N_min+1)/(N_min+2) * gamma + sum_{N>=N_min+1} (N+1)/(N+2) P(mu, N) ]
   / [ gamma + sum_{N>=N_min+1} P(mu, N) ]`, with `gamma` and `N_min` from
   Eqs. (A2) and (A3) (p. 5). The output photon-number statistics do not enter
   because the detectors do not resolve photon number (p. 5).
8. [P] Fig. 5 (p. 5): at `mu = 3` and a target efficiency of 50 %, Eq. (A3)
   gives `N_min = 3`; the grey part of the `N = 3` bar plus the bars `N >= 4`
   make up the 50 %. Fig. 6 (p. 6) plots Eq. (A4) for `eta` in
   {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1}; at `eta = 1` it reduces to Eq. (A1).
9. [P] Which efficiency enters (p. 6): the classical memory could also exploit
   the optical loss and the finite detection efficiency of the experiment, so
   that `eta_C = eta_M eta_t eta_D`; with `eta_M = 0.1`, `eta_t = 0.4` and
   `eta_D = 0.5` this is 0.02. Fig. 4 draws both thresholds: a solid curve at
   `eta = (10 +- 2)` % and a dashed curve at `eta = eta_M eta_t eta_D = 2` %,
   beside the Poisson-only curve and the 2/3 line (Fig. 4 caption, p. 4). The
   data lie above all of them for most of the `mu` range tested (p. 4).
10. [P] The measured `eta_M` is between 8 % and 10 % for all points of Fig. 4,
    and an additional neutral-density filter is placed before the detector for
    `mu > 1` to avoid saturation (Fig. 4 caption, p. 4).

## Reproduction and repository reading

[I] Eqs. (A2) to (A4) implemented from the printed statements give, with
`N_min = min i` and `gamma = eta_M (1 - P(mu, 0)) - sum_{N>=N_min+1} P(mu, N)`:

| `mu` | `eta` | `N_min` | `gamma` | `F_class` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.5 | 3 | 0.1223 | 0.8385 |
| 0.4 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0.6835 |
| 0.4 | 0.1 | 2 | 0.0250 | 0.7629 |
| 0.4 | 0.02 | 3 | 0.0058 | 0.8042 |
| 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0.7090 |

The first row reproduces Fig. 5 (grey part near 0.12), the `eta = 1` rows
reproduce Eq. (A1) and Specht's 70.9 % at `mu = 1`, and the 0.7629 and 0.8042
rows give the two finite-efficiency thresholds of Fig. 4 at `mu = 0.4`, against
the measured mean fidelity of 0.96. On the grid `mu` in {0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3,
1, 3, 10} by `eta` in {1, 0.7, 0.5, 0.3, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001}
(63 values) `F_class` is non-increasing in `eta`.

[I] Eq. (A4) is the optimum within the strategy the appendix describes: output
only for the highest photon numbers, with a randomized boundary at `N_min`,
and the best classical estimate `(N+1)/(N+2)` at each emitted `N`. Because
`(N+1)/(N+2)` increases with `N`, filling the top of the tail first is the
right order inside that class. The appendix prints no proof that the class is
optimal over all classical strategies, and it assumes the device resolves
photon number and acts on one pulse at a time.

[I] Two limits follow from Eq. (A4). When the acceptance `eta (1 - P(mu, 0))`
falls to or below the weight of the components with `N >= 2`, the device emits
only for `N >= 2` and `F_class >= 3/4`; at `mu = 0.4` that holds for
`eta <= 0.187`, consistent with the 0.7629 row. When the acceptance tends to
zero, `F_class` tends to `(N_max+1)/(N_max+2)` for the largest `N` with
non-zero weight, so a pooled benchmark with free selection on photon number
has no finite value without a floor on acceptance.

[I] Normalization. The paper's `eta_C` counts emitted outputs per non-empty
input pulse. [Specht et al.](1103.1528.md) count retrieved energy per
incident energy, which divides by `mu` instead of `1 - exp(-mu)`. At `mu = 1`
and efficiency 0.093 the two give 0.8125 and 0.8010. Both are accepted events
per optical attempt up to this factor, and a repository benchmark states which
denominator its acceptance probability uses.

[I] Which efficiency the classical comparator carries is a choice the paper
exposes and does not settle. Result 9 gives the device-only value 0.1 and the
detector-inclusive value 0.02; the lower value yields the higher threshold, and
the paper draws both without stating when each applies. [Specht et
al.](1103.1528.md) cancel the detection chain instead (their result 6), so the
two papers bracket the choice: device-only acceptance, or acceptance counted
from the optical input through the detector.

[I] Per-event dependence. Eqs. (1) and (A1) to (A4) treat every pulse as an
independent draw from `P(mu, N)` at one `mu`, with one fixed acceptance. The
paper adds a technical error for slow drifts to its Table I uncertainties
(Table I caption, p. 3) and analyzes no dependence between events, no
drifting `mu` and no input-dependent efficiency.

## Relevance

- [I] Rehearsal benchmark (`scripts/qd_classical_benchmark.py`,
  `scripts/qd_teleportation_rehearsal.py`,
  [the design](../../design/ascella-belenos-entanglement-teleportation.md)):
  high as the construction for the design's open question that accepted
  multiphoton sectors leave the single-copy benchmark. The fill-the-tail rule
  needs a non-decreasing fidelity per photon number, a photon-number law (the
  quantum-dot input's law is constrained by its `g2`) and the acceptance per attempt,
  and returns a benchmark that tightens as acceptance falls. The accepted
  multiphoton events of the rehearsal include failed CNOT branches and are not
  identical copies of the input, so `(N+1)/(N+2)` supplies the form and not the
  value.
- [I] Attempt profile. The threshold is non-increasing in acceptance per
  attempt. For a fixed accepted count, more optical attempts mean lower
  acceptance and a higher classical value, so a sound benchmark needs an upper
  bound on the attempt count. `attempts_benchmark` weights inputs by matching
  attempts and `model_benchmark` takes attempts as samples over a modeled
  probability; the service reports no attempt count, so the modeled attempts
  remain a forecast, and this paper does not turn the forecast into a bound.
- [I] What the paper does not settle: the six-state ensemble (Eq. (A4) uses
  the uniform-ensemble `(N+1)/(N+2)`), dual-rail photons, input-dependent
  acceptance, dependence between events, and strategies outside the
  photon-number-threshold class.
- [I] Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware, Cayley-Dickson structure
