## Main claims and evidence

[P] The method compares repeated executions of the same circuits in two or
more contexts, such as neighboring activity or collection period. Contexts
label separate pools of outcome counts (Secs. I-II).

- [P] The individual-circuit test uses a log-likelihood ratio between a shared outcome distribution and distinct distributions per context. Wilks' theorem supplies an asymptotic chi-squared reference with `(contexts - 1) * (outcomes - 1)` degrees of freedom (Eqs. 3-6).
- [P] Multi-circuit analysis combines an aggregate likelihood-ratio test with individual tests and multiple-testing corrections to control family-wise error (Sec. II B). The paper distinguishes detecting a change from measuring its magnitude.
- [P] Jensen-Shannon divergence and total variation distance quantify differences in outcome distributions. Finite-sample estimates can be positive even when the underlying distributions agree (Sec. III).
- [P] The statistically significant TVD is left undefined when context dependence is undetected. The paper explicitly rejects interpreting a failed rejection as evidence that a circuit is probably context independent (p. 6, Eq. 20 discussion).
- [P] The simulated example applies deliberate time-dependent over-rotations to gate-set-tomography circuits. Some adjacent collection-period comparisons remain below the detection threshold despite injected drift (Secs. IV and V opening).
- [P] The hardware experiment uses the 16-qubit superconducting `ibmqx3`. A fixed target qubit executes short circuits while different neighboring pairs perform CNOTs (Sec. V).
- [P] Each pair is studied with three time-ordered settings: other qubits idle, CNOT activity on the chosen pair, other qubits idle again. The two idle contexts test whether temporal variation plausibly explains the activity comparison (p. 8).
- [P] The target circuits take longer in the CNOT context. The paper identifies extra decoherence from that duration change as a nuisance context and recommends fixing each layer's duration across contexts to isolate crosstalk (pp. 8-9).
- [P] Context testing applies to arbitrary circuit families. Direct application to ordinary simultaneous-RB data needs a modification: each random target sequence must occur in both contexts (Sec. VI).
- [P] The authors integrated these techniques into pyGSTi and state that archived data can support the analysis when context-identifying information survives (Sec. VII).

## Mapping to this repository

[I] [adjudicate.py](../../../gororoba_quantum/adjudicate.py) reads shot-ordered
registers, hardware scheduling tables, and retained circuits. Its
`schedule_offsets` and `schedule_aware` functions address timing differences,
while `block_means` exposes variation along shot order. These are relevant
inputs to a context test; they do not reproduce the paper's complete inference
procedure.

[I] A folded-QPE comparison with a fixed companion workload estimates the arm
effect in that companion context. A separate idle-versus-active companion
comparison estimates companion sensitivity. The two designs answer different
questions and should retain separate identifiers and analysis endpoints.

[I] Holding the target's physical patch fixed removes one deliberate source of
variation. Duration matching, measurement alignment, and time blocking address
other sources. Balanced rotation of arms across patches is a repository design
proposal; the paper's IBM experiment does not validate that proposed crossover.

[I] Label: known statistical method available for adaptation. Detecting context
dependence narrows the hardware explanation; attributing the change to a
particular physical interaction additionally requires controls and a model.
The paper's own scheduling caveat is directly relevant to the model-hardware
gap described by the repository's retained QPE adjudication.

## Relevance

- [I] Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: high. The source provides an IBM experiment and direct tests of context dependence with a concrete scheduling confound.
- [I] Cayley-Dickson structure: none. The statistical tests supply neither the finite census nor a general algebraic classification theorem.
- [I] Factoring resource estimates: low. The source establishes neither a factoring resource advantage nor a complete cost model.

[I] Diagnostic applicability to the repository's QPE experiments is high:
context tests can examine endpoint changes, while timing controls constrain
physical explanations. Repeated Walsh circuits across controlled contexts can
also test preservation of a classically predicted distinction. Those uses are
hardware diagnostics, separate from the structural and resource ratings.

## Action items and acceptance gates

1. Specify the same target circuit in every comparison context. Acceptance:
   records bind target circuit digest, physical patch, outcome decoder,
   companion setting, period, and schedule to every result pool.
2. Match target timing or make residual timing an explicit experimental factor.
   Acceptance: compiled and returned schedules establish per-qubit delays and
   measurement times; changed durations remain visible in the analysis.
3. Select a context test and practical effect tolerance before viewing results.
   Acceptance: simulated fixtures validate false-positive behavior, detection
   power, and sparse-count handling for the actual outcome alphabet.
4. Allocate multiplicity across arms, patches, endpoints, and contexts.
   Acceptance: the plan states a global error criterion and distinguishes
   exploratory follow-ups from preregistered tests.
5. Preserve nondetection as a sensitivity-limited result. Acceptance: an
   equivalence claim requires a separate tolerance and interval or test that
   supports that claim; a large p-value alone cannot supply it.
6. Consult [Proctor et al.](1907.13608.md) for time-series drift analysis and
   [Sarovar et al.](1908.09855.md) for conditional-dependence designs. Acceptance:
   the combined plan records the role of each technique and its assumptions.
