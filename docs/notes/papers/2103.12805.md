## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are the printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page
indices. The text layer drops overbars, so Eq. 1.1 was read from the rendered page 2.

- [P] Eq. 1.1 (p. 2), the Cayley-Dickson process on an algebra `E` with scalar involution and a fixed
  non-zero `gamma` in `K`: `(a1, a2)(b1, b2) = (a1 b1 + gamma conj(b2) a2, a2 conj(b1) + b2 a1)`,
  with `conj(x) = (conj(a1), -a2)` for `x = (a1, a2)`.
- [P] Definition 2.1 (p. 3), after [Re; 71]: a twisted group algebra for a finite group `G` over a field
  `K` is an algebra with a basis `{a_g, g in G}` such that "`a_g a_h = f(g, h) a_(g h)`, where `g, h` in `G`,
  `f(g, h)` in `K`, `f(g, h) != 0`".
- [P] Basis (p. 4): the basis of `E_(t+1)` is `f_0 = 1 = (1, 0)`, `f_i = (f_i, 0)` for `i < 2^t` and
  `f_(2^t + i) = (0, f_i)`.
- [P] Proposition 2.3 (p. 4), with `pq` the exclusive or of the binary representations: `(2p)(2q) = 2pq`,
  `(2p)(2q+1) = 2pq+1`, `(2p+1)(2q) = 2pq+1`, `(2p+1)(2q+1) = 2pq`.
- [P] Proposition 2.6 (p. 5): for all `t` there is a twist map `alpha_t : Z_2^n x Z_2^n -> K`, `n = 2^t`, with
  `f_p f_q = alpha_t(p, q) f_(pq)` and, for `p, q` in `{0, ..., 2^t - 1}`:
  1. `alpha_(t+1)(p, q) = alpha_t(p, q)`;
  2. for `q = 2^t + r`: `alpha_(t+1)(p, q) = -alpha_t(p, r)` for `r != p`, `p != 0`, `r != 0`; `alpha_(t+1)(p, q) = alpha_t(p, 0) = 1`
     for `r != p`, `r = 0`; `alpha_(t+1)(p, q) = alpha_t(p, p)` for `r = p != 0`;
  3. for `p = 2^t + r`: `alpha_(t+1)(p, q) = -alpha_t(r, q)` for `r != q`, and `-alpha_t(r, r)` for `r = q`;
  4. for `p = 2^t + r`, `q = 2^t + s`: `alpha_(t+1)(p, q) = gamma_(t+1) alpha_t(r, s)` for `r != s`, `r != 0`, `s != 0`;
     `alpha_(t+1)(p, p) = -gamma_(t+1) alpha_t(r, r)` for `r = s != 0`; `-gamma_(t+1) alpha_t(0, s) = -gamma_(t+1)` for `r = 0`,
     `s != 0`; `gamma_(t+1) alpha_t(r, 0) = gamma_(t+1)` for `s = 0`.
- [P] Proposition 2.7 (p. 6): the same recursion for the sign map `theta_t : Z_2^n x Z_2^n -> {-1, 1}`, obtained by
  taking every `gamma_i = 1` ("It results from the above Proposition, by taking `gamma_1 = ... = gamma_(t+1) = 1`").
- [P] Theorem 2.8 (p. 7): "The algebra `E_t` is a twisted group algebra for the group `G = Z_2^n`, `n = 2^t`,
  `t` in `N`, over a field `K`, with char `K` = 0." The proof reads, in full, "It results from Proposition 2.6
  and Proposition 2.7."
- [P] The algorithm (pp. 7 to 8): for indices `p, q` with binary digits `i_t ... i_1` and `j_t ... j_1`, let
  `k_1, ..., k_z` be the positions where `i_k = j_k = 1`; then `f_p f_q = theta_t(p, q) gamma_(k_1) ... gamma_(k_z) f_(pq)`.

## Main claims

- [P] An algebra obtained by the Cayley-Dickson process over a field of characteristic zero is a twisted
  group algebra for `Z_2^n` (Theorem 2.8), with a recursive twist map (Proposition 2.6) and an algorithm
  that computes a basis product from the sign map and the generator parameters.
- [P] The construction starts from "ideas given by Bales" (abstract); Section 2 (p. 3) says Bales presented
  the twist tree "in the real case and in the situation when `gamma_i = -1`" ([1107.1375.md](1107.1375.md)).
- [P] No degree, polynomial, algebraic normal form or Walsh statement about the twist appears (full-text
  search of the PDF).

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Flaut and Boboescu build the basis products of the Cayley-Dickson algebras by an algorithm on the
  twist tree and prove the twisted group algebra structure (Theorem 2.8). The paper states no degree,
  ANF or Walsh property of the sign, within the scope read.
- [I] Proposition 2.6 indexes the basis as `f_(2^t + i) = (0, f_i)`, which places the newest doubling on
  the highest index bit, as [Ren and Zhao](2205.07986.md) do, and its case 2 reads
  `alpha_(t+1)(p, 2^t + r) = -alpha_t(p, r)`, the rule `gamma(2r, 2s+1) = -gamma(r, s)` of Bales
  Corollary 8.2 in the other index order ([1107.1375.md](1107.1375.md)). The proof of Theorem 2.8 cites
  the two propositions without a separate argument.
- [I] Printed slips in the PDF to read around: Proposition 2.6 case 4 gives the range of `q` as
  `{2^t, ..., 2^t - 1}`, Proposition 2.7 case 3 gives the range of `q` up to `2^(t+1) - 1` where the
  recursion needs `2^t - 1`, and the proof of case 3 of Proposition 2.6 writes `gamma_r` for a twist value.
  The transcription above repeats the printed statements and corrects none of them.
