## Main claims

The review's hardware section (Sec. V) is the part that matters here: it states
that circuit quality, not qubit count, limits optimization on superconducting
hardware and lists the transpilation, suppression, and mitigation choices that
move quality.

- Scope: complexity theory (Sec. II), algorithmic building blocks (Sec. III),
  problem classes (Sec. IV), execution on noisy hardware (Sec. V), benchmarks
  (Sec. VI), finance and sustainability applications (Sec. VII), conclusion
  (Sec. VIII). [from-paper, contents]
- Hardware context: superconducting processors up to 433 qubits; median T1 and
  T2 approaching 300 us and 150 us on 127-qubit IBM Eagle devices; qubits in
  planar lattices with nearest-neighbor coupling. [from-paper, Sec. V
  introduction]
- Quality, not scale, is typically the limiting factor for optimization.
  [from-paper, Sec. V A 1]
- Transpiler duties: (i) select qubits, (ii) decompose to native instructions,
  (iii) route. Qubit selection should use a metric matched to the circuit: gate
  fidelity for two-qubit-dominated circuits [403], cross-talk [415], layer
  fidelity [416]; measurement quality enters when the circuit uses mid-circuit
  measurement [417]; the variable-to-qubit mapping changes depth [418]. The
  qubit-selection citation is Nation and Treinish, "Suppressing quantum circuit
  errors due to system variability," PRX Quantum 4, 010327 (2023) [414].
  [from-paper, Sec. V A 2]
- Approximate compilation can trade accuracy for depth under noise. [from-paper,
  Sec. V A 2]
- Dynamic circuits can replace SWAP networks with constant-depth circuits at a
  quadratic auxiliary-qubit cost on grid-like coupling maps. [from-paper,
  Sec. V A 2]
- Error suppression: dynamical decoupling adds pulses in idle windows without
  extra shots; it has little room in dense SWAP-network circuits and a large
  effect on heavy-hex native circuits with many idle windows [438]. [from-paper,
  Sec. V A 3]
- PEC learns the Pauli-twirled layer noise and inverts it by quasi-probability
  sampling, an unbiased estimate at a variance factor gamma^2; for n qubits and
  d layers with average per-qubit gamma_bar the cost scales as
  gamma_bar^(2nd). The factor gamma^2 for 60 CNOT layers on 127 qubits was
  10^128 in the cited utility experiment, so PEC is infeasible at that scale;
  the probabilistic variant PEA learns noise like PEC, amplifies it, and
  extrapolates. [from-paper, Sec. V A 3; the value 10^128 verified on the
  rendered page]
- ZNE runs logically equivalent circuits at several noise levels and
  extrapolates, a biased estimate; circuit depth limits the stretch factors and
  can make the extrapolation unusable; partial-gate folding and stretching
  cross-resonance pulses relieve this. [from-paper, Sec. V A 3]
- Regular SWAP networks alternate two identical CNOT layers, which minimizes the
  noise-learning cost of PEC and PEA. [from-paper, Sec. V A 3]
- Mitigation targets expectation values; optimization needs mitigated samples,
  which remain an open problem. A noiseless simulation of the mean of samples
  drawn at optimized parameters showed a large gap to hardware samples.
  [from-paper, Sec. V A 3 and V C]
- Pulse level: pulse-efficient transpilation encodes variational parameters in
  cross-resonance durations and shortens schedules; it is not always compatible
  with error mitigation [452]. [from-paper, Sec. V A 4]
- Metrics: Quantum Volume, Layer Fidelity, Error Per Layered Gate (EPLG), the
  PEC gamma, and CLOPS; EPLG 1.7 percent and CLOPS 2,700 on ibm_sherbrooke.
  These proxies can fail to predict which device performs best on a given task;
  application-like benchmarks are the best measure. [from-paper, Sec. V B]
- Worked example: depth-2 QAOA on random 3-regular graphs up to 40 nodes on
  127-qubit processors, run on the best qubits by two-qubit gate fidelity, with
  a predetermined SWAP network, a SAT-derived variable-to-qubit mapping, and
  machine-learning mitigation of expectation values. [from-paper, Sec. V C,
  after Sack and Egger]
- Factoring and Shor: factoring is not believed classically efficient, Shor's
  algorithm is efficient, and the paper expects this to be demonstrated once
  fault-tolerant hardware exists, citing Gidney and Ekera, "How to factor 2048
  bit RSA integers in 8 hours using 20 million noisy qubits," Quantum 5, 433
  (2021) [18]. The 2021 estimate appears only in that reference title; the text
  gives no resource numbers. [from-paper, Sec. II and reference list]
- Factoring can be cast as a QUBO [129-131], giving an exponential speedup on
  the QUBO subset that encodes factoring; the authors do not expect such
  instances to arise naturally. QPE underlies Shor's algorithm. [from-paper,
  Sec. III C]
- Pirnay et al. [66] prove a quantum separation for a combinatorial
  optimization problem on distributions that are Karp reductions of factoring
  instances [67]; the paper judges it of little practical value. [from-paper,
  Sec. II C]
- Benchmark frameworks cited: Lubinski et al. [469] (width and depth of Shor,
  Grover, Hamiltonian-simulation circuits) and Tomesh et al. [470] (connectivity,
  depth, two-qubit gate count, parallelism, qubit reuse, mid-circuit measurement
  count). [from-paper, Sec. VI A]

## Methods

Reuse the Sec. V pipeline as a checklist; the paper does not give a runnable
procedure.

1. Model and encode with hardware in view: fewer, denser-connected variables
   versus more, sparser ones; QRAC encodings trade retrieval probability for
   qubits. [from-paper, Sec. V A 1]
2. Choose qubits by a circuit-matched metric, then map decision variables to
   minimize SWAPs, then route; exploit commuting-gate structure. [from-paper,
   Sec. V A 2]
3. Add suppression (DD) after scheduling, then mitigation (ZNE, PEA, PEC) sized
   by gamma_bar^(2nd). [from-paper, Sec. V A 3]
4. Optionally lower to pulses for shorter schedules. [from-paper, Sec. V A 4]
5. Evaluate on application-like benchmarks, report time-to-solution split into
   pre-processing, transpilation, compilation, execution, and post-processing.
   [from-paper, Sec. V B and VI C]

## Relevance

(a) Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: high. Sec. V A 2-3 and V C
state which calibration quantities drive qubit selection (two-qubit fidelity,
layer fidelity, readout for mid-circuit measurement), when DD helps (heavy-hex
idle windows), and why PEC fails at scale.

(b) Cayley-Dickson, octonion, zero-divisor structure: none. The review does not
treat algebraic structure beyond QUBO and group-agnostic optimization.

(c) Shor and factoring resource estimates: low. It only cites the 2021
Gidney-Ekera estimate by title and points to factoring-as-QUBO and
factoring-reduction papers [129-131, 66-67, 132]; it produces no estimate.

(d) Action items for gororoba-quantum:

1. Rank candidate qubit sets for each compiled circuit from live backend data
   with a score matched to the circuit: sum of -log(1 - error) over used
   two-qubit edges plus readout terms for measured qubits when the circuit
   measures mid-circuit, and prefer layer-fidelity data where available.
   Qiskit 2.5.2 ships `VF2PostLayout` in the installed environment; `mapomatic`
   is not installed. [inference from Sec. V A 2; package presence verified]
2. Measure the idle fraction of each scheduled circuit before enabling DD and
   run each Cayley-Dickson circuit with DD on and off; the paper predicts a
   large gain on idle-rich heavy-hex circuits and little on dense ones. Runtime
   0.50.0 option: `dynamical_decoupling.enable`, `sequence_type` in {XX, XpXm,
   XY4}. [from-paper, Sec. V A 3; option names verified in the installed
   package]
3. Compute gamma_bar^(2nd) from learned noise before choosing PEC. Runtime 0.50.0
   exposes `resilience.pec_mitigation` with `pec.max_overhead` and `pec.noise_gain`,
   and a `pea` amplifier under `resilience.zne`; use PEC only for blocks small
   enough that gamma^2 stays bounded (small 3- to 4-qubit multiplication
   blocks) and ZNE or PEA beyond. [from-paper, Sec. V A 3; the small-block
   threshold is inference; option names verified]
4. Select among the three reachable backends with a project probe (a small
   gororoba circuit run on each) and record EPLG, layer fidelity, and CLOPS only
   as context; the paper states these proxies can mis-rank devices for a given
   task. [from-paper, Sec. V B]
5. Follow the Sack-Egger recipe for any swap-heavy circuit: best qubits by
   two-qubit fidelity, a fixed alternating two-layer SWAP network, and a
   mapping that minimizes SWAP count; keep repeated layers identical so noise
   learning stays cheap. [from-paper, Sec. V A 3 and V C]
6. Report raw sample gaps explicitly: the paper states mitigation of samples is
   unsolved, so a gororoba result that is a bitstring distribution should carry
   the noisy-versus-ideal gap and a readout-mitigated variant, not only a
   mitigated expectation. [inference from Sec. V A 3 and V C]
7. Record per-circuit metrics in the style of Tomesh et al. [470]: connectivity,
   depth, two-qubit count, parallelism ratio, mid-circuit measurement count.
   [from-paper, Sec. VI A; adoption is inference]
8. For factoring context, read references [129-131] (factoring as QUBO) and [18]
   directly before quoting any resource number; this review is not a source for
   one. [inference]

## Open points and discrepancies

- The quoted gamma^2 of 10^128 comes from a cited experiment, not from this
  review's own measurements.
- The paper's device numbers describe IBM Eagle processors; Heron figures are
  not given.
- Sec. V C is a summary of Sack and Egger [403]; the underlying preprint was not
  fetched.
