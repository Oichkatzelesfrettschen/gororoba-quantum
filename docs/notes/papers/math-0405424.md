## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] Setting (p. 2): `A_n = R^(2^n)` for `n >= 0`, `(a, b)(x, y) = (a x - conj(y) b, y a + b conj(x))`. Flexible and
  power-associative for all `n`, normed only for `n <= 3`, and `Re0` is the center for `n >= 1`. For pure `a`,
  `a^2 = -|a|^2 e0`.
- [P] Section I (pp. 3-8). `exp(x) = sum x^m / m!` converges for every `n` (Theorem 1.3, p. 4) and, for `x = r e0 + a` with `a` pure,
  `exp(x) = e^r (cos|a| e0 + sin|a| a/|a|)` (Lemma 1.1, p. 3; Theorem 1.3); `|exp(x)| = e^r` (Corollary 1.4). `exp(pi a) = -e0`
  and `exp(pi a/2) = a` for every unit pure `a`.
- [P] C-dependence (p. 5): two elements are C-dependent when their pure parts are linearly dependent. Lemma 1.5 (p. 5):
  C-dependent elements commute; for `n = 2` and `n = 3` commuting is equivalent to C-dependence. Remark 1 (p. 6) notes that
  zero divisors give nonzero pure `a, b` with `a b = b a = 0` and `a` orthogonal to `b` for `n >= 4`. Remark 2 (p. 6), stated
  without proof: for `n >= 4` and pure `a`, the centralizer `C_a = {b pure : a b = b a}` equals `R a + Ker L_a`.
- [P] Theorem 1.7 (p. 7): for C-dependent `x, y`, `exp(x + y) = exp(x) exp(y)`; the remark after it states that the identity
  forces `x y = y x` and `x(x y) = x^2 y`, so the theorem is best possible. Proposition 1.8: `exp(conj(x)) = conj(exp(x))` and
  `exp(-x) = exp(x)^(-1)`. Corollary 1.9: De Moivre, `exp(k x) = exp(x)^k` for integers `k`.
- [P] Section II (pp. 8-12). Theorem 2.1 (p. 8): `exp : A_n -> A_n \ {0}` and its restriction `Im(A_n) -> S(A_n)` are onto. Lemma 2.2
  (pp. 9-10): `|x^k| = |x|^k`, `conj(x^k) = conj(x)^k` and `(x^(-1))^k = (x^k)^(-1)` for powers of a single element. Theorem 2.3
  (p. 11): the `k`-power map `rho_k` on the unit sphere `S^(2^n - 1)` has topological degree `k`. Lemma 2.4 (pp. 11-12) and
  Corollary 2.5 (p. 12): `A_n x A_0` inside `A_(n+1)` is closed under `k`-th powers, so the restriction of `rho_k` to the sphere
  `S^(2^n)` also has degree `k`.
- [P] Section III (pp. 13-16), the fundamental theorem of algebra. Lemma 3.1 (p. 13): non-real C-dependent elements
  associate and their powers are C-dependent. Theorem 3.2 (p. 14): every polynomial `x^k + xi_(k-1) x^(k-1) + ... + xi_0` whose coefficients
  are C-dependent with each other and with `x` has a root. Proposition 3.3 (p. 15): such polynomials are generalized
  polynomials `x^k (e0 + g(x))` with `|g| -> 0` at infinity. Theorem 3.4 (p. 16): every generalized polynomial has a
  root in `A_n`, by homotopy to `rho_k` on the one-point compactification. The example on p. 15 shows `a x - x a + e0` has no
  root for pure `a` and `n >= 2`, because commutators have zero real part.

## Convention map and cd_core check

[I] Product. With bars restored the printed doubling is `cd_core`'s `(a,b)(c,d) = (ac - conj(d) b, d a + b conj(c))`; the paper
prints no multiplication table, and its statements are convention-independent for the single-element algebra generated
by one `x`.

[I] Check results: no mismatch. A scratch script (ignored `docs/local/`, run with `-I`) tested the algebraic statements with
exact rational arithmetic:

| Paper statement | Result under `cd_core` |
| --- | --- |
| `a^2 = -\|a\|^2 e0` for pure `a` | holds on 6 random pure `a` at each of `D = 16, 32, 64` |
| `\|x^k\| = \|x\|^k` (Lemma 2.2) | `\|x^k\|^2 = (\|x\|^2)^k` for `k = 2..6` on 6 random `x` at each of `D = 16, 32, 64` |
| power associativity | `x^2 x^3 = x^5` and `(x x^2) x^2 = x^2 (x x^2)` at `D = 32` |
| Remark 1 (p. 6): zero divisors commute without C-dependence | `(e1 + e10)(e4 - e15) = (e4 - e15)(e1 + e10) = 0` at `D = 16`, with disjoint supports |
| Remark 2 (p. 6): `C_a = R a + Ker L_a` | holds for 12 elements at `D = 16` and `D = 32`: `e1 + e10` (centralizer dimension 5 at `D = 16`, 9 at `D = 32`), `e1 + e18` (13 at `D = 32`), `e1 + e10 + e20 - e31` (9) and random pure elements (1) |
| `[a, x]` has zero real part (p. 15 example) | holds on 10 random pairs at `D = 32` |

[I] Remark 2 is the same statement as Lemma 4.2 of [Zhilina](../2608.26893.md) for doubly alternative elements with nonzero
norm, `C(a) = F + F a + O(a)`; the checks above include non-alternative zero divisors (`e1 + e10`) and show equality there
as well, which the paper asserts without proof.

## Repository bearing

[I] The paper's content concerns single-element subalgebras, powers and topology, which are convention-independent, so no
repository claim in `docs/state/research_state.json` depends on it: `cd-sign-cocycle-degree`, `cd-associator-census`,
`a5-stiefel-universal` and `dimension32-global-topology`: none. Zero divisors enter only through Remarks 1 and 2 and the
no-root example. Remark 2 gives the dimension of the centralizer of a zero divisor as `1 + dim Ker L_a`; the repository's
annihilator dimensions (4, 8, 12 at dimension 32, in the [sparse census](../topics/cayley_dickson_sparse_census.md)) then give
centralizer dimensions 5, 9 and 13 in `Im(A_5)`, which the check above reproduced for the nullity-8 element `e1 + e10`
and the nullity-12 element `e1 + e18`.

[I] Moreno's Stiefel question (`math/0512517` Section 5): none. Dimensions 32 and above: the theorems hold for all `n`; the
topological degree statements are about the unit spheres `S^31`, `S^63` and beyond. Dimension-specific counts: none.

## Boundaries
