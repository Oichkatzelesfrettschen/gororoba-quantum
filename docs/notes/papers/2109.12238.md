## Transcribed statements

Read from the PDF above; page numbers are PDF page indices.

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): atomic clocks have tested the gravitational redshift at distance scales from 30
  centimeters to thousands of kilometers. This work measures "a linear frequency gradient consistent with
  the gravitational redshift within a single millimeter scale sample of ultracold strontium", enabled by
  a fractional frequency measurement uncertainty improved by more than a factor of 10, to
  `7.6 x 10^-21`.
- [P] Eq. 1 (p. 5): two identical clocks on a planet's surface separated vertically by `h` differ in
  frequency by `delta f / f = a h / c^2`, with `f` the clock frequency, `c` the speed of light and `a` the
  gravitational acceleration. At Earth's surface this is a fractional frequency gradient of
  `-1.09 x 10^-19` per mm in the coordinates of the paper's Fig. 1a.
- [P] Section on the gradient (pp. 5-6): the weighted mean of the measured frequency gradient is
  `-1.00(12) x 10^-19` per mm, and after the differential systematics the final gradient is
  `-9.8(2.3) x 10^-20` per mm, consistent with the predicted redshift.
- [P] Section on synchronous comparison (p. 6): two uncorrelated regions of the cloud, about 100 pixels
  each, over 92 hours of data give a fractional frequency instability of `4.4 x 10^-18 / sqrt(tau)`
  (`tau` in seconds) and an uncertainty of `7.6 x 10^-21` for the full measurement time; the inferred single-region
  instability is `3.1 x 10^-18 / sqrt(tau)`. Dividing the difference by the separation of the region
  centers gives `-1.30(18) x 10^-19` per mm, and `-1.28(27) x 10^-19` per mm after the same systematic
  corrections, "again fully consistent with the predicted redshift".
- [P] Fig. 1 caption (p. 8) and Methods (p. 16): the sample is a millimeter-length one-dimensional
  optical lattice clock of strontium; the clock wavelength is 698 nm and the lattice wavelength 813 nm.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Size: the surface gradient `g / c^2 = 1.09 x 10^-16` per m is `1.1 x 10^-18` per cm. A 5 GHz
  qubit transition shifts by `5.5 x 10^-9` Hz per cm of height difference, and a 0.35 mm chip thickness
  gives `3.8 x 10^-20`. The repository's instabilities (Tuna-17 q9 idle `P(1|0)` of 0.026 to 0.27,
  [tuna-17.md](../quantum_inspire/tuna-17.md); Belenos transmittance 2.89 % to 6.06 %,
  [platform-specifications.md](../quandela/platform-specifications.md)) sit more than 16 orders above it.
- [I] Signature: a redshift coupling is a static offset set by the height difference between two clocks
  and flips sign when their order swaps; it cannot produce a drift, a sidereal line or a burst. A
  gradient measurement needs the roughly `10^-20` resolution of this paper, not the repository's.
