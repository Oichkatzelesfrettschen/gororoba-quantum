## Results needed by the project

The paper writes `f` for the average fidelity of a teleportation scheme, `F`
for the singlet fraction (overlap with the maximally entangled state `P+`;
the entanglement fidelity of a channel), and `d` for the local dimension.

1. [P] Noisy singlet (singlet mixed with the maximally mixed state, weight
   `p` on the singlet) has `f = p + (1-p)/d` (Eq. 14) and
   `F = p + (1-p)/d^2` (Eq. 15), related by `f = (F d + 1)/(d + 1)`
   (Eq. 16, p. 5). The same state is separable if and only if
   `0 <= p <= 1/(d+1)`, equivalent to `1/d^2 <= F <= 1/d` and
   `1/d <= f <= 2/(d+1)` (p. 5); the paper credits the separability range to
   its reference 18, a reduction-criterion preprint, and the claim that every
   state with `F > 1/d` is entangled (and distillable) to the same reference
   (p. 8).
2. [P] Proposition 1 (p. 7, Eq. 29): for any channel `Lambda`,
   `f(Lambda) = (F(Lambda) d + 1)/(d + 1)`. The proof shows that `f` and `F`
   are invariants of twirling and that the relation holds for the
   depolarizing channel.
3. [P] Teleportation theorem (p. 7, Eq. 30): for a shared state `rho`, the
   maximal teleportation fidelity reachable with trace-preserving local
   quantum operations and classical communication (LQCC) is
   `f_max = (F_max d + 1)/(d + 1)`, where `F_max` is the maximal singlet
   fraction reachable from `rho` by trace-preserving LQCC.
4. [P] Classical teleportation fidelity (p. 8, Sec. IV): separable states
   have `F <= 1/d`, and the noisy singlet with `F = 1/d` is separable, so the
   best fidelity through a classical channel is `f_cl = 2/(d + 1)`. Every
   bound entangled state gives at most `f_cl`.

## Section-by-section reading

1. Secs. I and II (pp. 1-4), Eqs. 1 to 10. [P] The paper works with `d x d`
   states `rho` whose first marginal is `I/d` and with channels (completely
   positive, trace-preserving maps). Eq. 2, `rho_Lambda = (I (x) Lambda)P_+`,
   is an affine isomorphism between such states and channels (Eqs. 3 to 9).
   Two parameters follow: the channel fidelity `f(Lambda)`, the average over a
   uniform distribution of input pure states (Eq. 10), and the singlet
   fraction `F`, the overlap `<Psi_+|rho|Psi_+>` of the Choi state. Assumption:
   finite dimension, uniform input ensemble over all pure states.
2. Sec. III.A (pp. 4-5), Eqs. 11 and 12. [P] The most general teleportation
   scheme is a trace-preserving LQCC operation on the pair and the input
   particle; it defines a channel `Lambda_{T, rho}`. Trace preservation means
   no selection of an ensemble. A restricted input of dimension `m < d` is
   handled by an embedding (p. 5).
3. Sec. III.B and C (pp. 5-6), Eqs. 13 to 20. [P] Noisy singlets (Eq. 13) have
   `f` and `F` of Eqs. 14 to 16, separability range `p <= 1/(d+1)`, and
   twirling (Eq. 17) turns any state into a noisy singlet of equal `F`. The
   depolarizing channel of Eq. 18 is the channel of the noisy singlet (Eq.
   19), and standard teleportation through the noisy singlet reproduces that
   channel (Eq. 20).
4. Sec. III.D and E (pp. 6-8), Eqs. 21 to 30. [P] Twirling a channel (a random
   unitary before the channel and its inverse after, with the unitary sent to
   the receiver) yields the depolarizing channel of equal `F` (Lemma 1) and
   keeps `f` (Lemma 2). Proposition 1 (Eq. 29): `f = (F d + 1)/(d + 1)`.
   Theorem (Eq. 30): `f_max = (F_max d + 1)/(d + 1)`. Assumption: LQCC means
   trace-preserving local operations and classical communication.
5. Sec. IV (p. 8). [P] The best fidelity through a classical channel is
   `f_cl = 2/(d + 1)`; every bound entangled state gives at most that.
6. Secs. V to VII (pp. 8-12), Eqs. 31 to 46. [P] Conclusive (post-selected)
   teleportation: with success probability `p` the best fidelity is
   `f_p = (F_p d + 1)/(d + 1)` where `F_p` is the best singlet fraction with
   success probability `p`; a pure nearly product state reaches perfect
   fidelity with a small probability (Eqs. 31 and 32); quasi-distillation
   gives `F` arbitrarily close to 1 with vanishing probability for the state
   of Eq. 38 and a threshold for the state of Eq. 41.
7. Sec. VIII (pp. 12-13). [P] To teleport only when the transfer fidelity is
   known to exceed `f_0`, the state must admit an LQCC operation (possibly
   with some probability) to a state of singlet fraction above
   `F_0 = (1 + 1/d) f_0 - 1/d`.

## Repository use

Each item below is [I] and names the design element it supports.

[I] Qubit values and gate G4. Setting `d = 2` in item 4 gives `f_cl = 2/3`,
the average fidelity beyond which a teleportation experiment certifies shared
entanglement. The paper states the formula for general `d`; the number 2/3 is
the repository's substitution. Eq. 16 maps a measured average teleportation
fidelity `f` to a singlet fraction `F = ((d + 1) f - 1)/d`; for `d = 2`,
`F = (3f - 1)/2`, and `f = 2/3` corresponds to `F = 1/2`. The threshold 1/2
of gate G4 for `F = (1 + C_XX - C_YY + C_ZZ)/4` is the statement that every
state with `F > 1/d` is entangled (p. 8) at `d = 2`.

[I] Scope. Eq. 29 relates the average fidelity and the singlet fraction of a
channel, and Eq. 30 optimizes over trace-preserving LQCC; the two relations
differ. A measured teleportation fidelity applies Eq. 29 or Eq. 16 to the
channel the experiment realized; it carries no statement about the optimum
over LQCC postprocessing. Eq. 29 and the channel of Eq. 12 describe the whole
trace-preserving scheme with failed branches included, and a heralded
success-weighted fidelity is the conclusive-teleportation quantity of Sec. V.

[I] Input ensemble (replaces the earlier reading of the remark on restricted
input). The paper's `f` averages over the uniform distribution of all input
pure states (Eq. 10). The restriction it treats (p. 5 and the remark of p. 8)
confines the input to an `m`-dimensional subspace and replaces `d` by `m`; it
makes no statement about a finite input ensemble. The design's six Pauli
eigenstates with weight 1/6 have first moment `I/2` and second moment
`(I + SWAP)/6`, which equal the uniform-distribution moments at `d = 2`, and
`f` of Eq. 10 depends on the input only through these two moments. The design
ensemble therefore reproduces Eq. 29. An ensemble that is not a 2-design
(for example the eigenstates of two axes only) has a different second moment
and a different classical threshold; rederive the threshold for it.

[I] Gate G5, claim level C3, `success_weighted_fidelity`, and the bound 2/3 of
`measure_and_prepare_grid`. For a completely positive, trace-nonincreasing
accepted instrument `E` with Choi state `J = (I (x) E)(P_+)` of trace `s` (the
mean success probability, `s = tr E(I/d)`) and `F_acc = <Psi_+|J|Psi_+>/s`,
the six-state moments give the numerator
`sum_i p_i tr[rho_i E(rho_i)] = s (d F_acc + 1)/(d + 1)` and the denominator
`sum_i p_i tr E(rho_i) = s`, so
`F_succ = (d F_acc + 1)/(d + 1) = (2 F_acc + 1)/3` at `d = 2`;
Eq. 29 is the case `s = 1`. If the accepted branch is a measure-and-prepare
map, its Choi state is separable (Theorem 4 of
[quant-ph-0302031](quant-ph-0302031.md), condition C), so `F_acc <= 1/2`
(Sec. IV, p. 8) and `F_succ <= 2/3`. This gives the bound of the design by a second
route from the direct six-state computation in the design. The step from
Eq. 29 to a trace-nonincreasing map is a deduction by hand and not a statement
of the paper, which proves Eq. 29 for channels.

[I] Selection. Sec. VIII says a certified fidelity above `f_0` needs an LQCC
operation that raises the singlet fraction above `F_0 = (1 + 1/d) f_0 - 1/d`,
which is `1/2` at `f_0 = 2/3`. LQCC with selection maps separable states to
separable states ([1210.4583](1210.4583.md)), so a separable resource stays
at `F <= 1/2` under post-selection, which is the design's statement that
post-selection alone leaves the bound intact.

[I] Gate G8 and `two_hop_depolarizing`, `TWO_HOP_CLASSICAL_THRESHOLD`. At
`d = 2` Eq. 14 gives `f = (1 + p)/2` for the depolarizing channel of Eq. 18,
and two hops compose as `Lambda_p o Lambda_q = Lambda_{pq}`, which follows
from Eq. 18. With `p = 2f - 1` the composite fidelity is `(1 + p^2)/2`,
`0.625` at `f = 0.75`, and exceeds `2/3` only when `p^2 > 1/3`, that is
`f > (1 + 1/sqrt 3)/2 = 0.7887`. The assumption is that each hop is exactly
the depolarizing channel of Eq. 18. A hop of another form composes as a
product of non-depolarizing maps, and Lemma 1 turns it into a depolarizing one
only under the twirl of Sec. III.D, whose random unitary must be communicated
to the receiver. The design's use is therefore a benchmark and no property of
an untwirled link.

[I] The paper's own Eq. 40 prints prefactors `1/n` and `(1 - F)/n` for the
operators of Eq. 39; the operators give `1/n^2` and `(1 - F)/n^2`. Sec. VII's
conclusion (singlet fraction tending to 1 as the success probability tends
to 0) holds either way and the repository does not use Eq. 40.
