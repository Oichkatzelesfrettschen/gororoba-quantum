## Result needed by the project

[P] Abstract and Sec. IV (pp. 1, 5): with passive linear optical elements and
auxiliary modes in the vacuum state, no apparatus discriminates the four
equiprobable Bell states of two polarization qubits unambiguously with
probability above 50 percent (`P_s = 1/2`, Eq. 24). If the four Bell states
carry unequal prior probabilities `p_1 >= ... >= p_4 > 0`, the optimum is
`p_1 + p_2` (footnote 3, p. 3).

Assumptions of the bound, each stated in the paper:

1. [P] Linear elements only: output creation operators are unitary linear
   combinations of the input creation operators (Eq. 1, p. 2). Active
   elements that mix creation and annihilation operators are excluded
   (footnote 1, p. 1).
2. [P] Auxiliary modes start in the vacuum state, and the apparatus is a
   fixed array with no conditional dynamics: no measurement outcome selects a
   later optical element (p. 3).
3. [P] Detectors are ideal and photon-number resolving; each outcome projects
   onto photon-number states (p. 2).
4. [P] The qubits are the polarization states of two single photons; an
   unambiguous result means a POVM element that only one Bell state can
   trigger (pp. 2-3).
5. [P] The bound is achieved by a 50/50 beam splitter followed by two
   polarizing beam splitters, which separates two of the four Bell states
   (p. 5).

Outside the bound, the paper reports that relaxing assumption 2 changes the
result: the scheme of Knill, Laflamme and Milburn (footnote 2, p. 1;
conclusion, p. 5) reaches a failure probability that falls as `1/(n+1)` with
`n` highly entangled auxiliary photons, and the paper calls that scheme
impracticable with the technology of its date.

## Repository use

[I] The 50 percent figure applies to the class above and to nothing wider.
Entangled or non-vacuum auxiliary photons, feed-forward between detection
events, and encodings other than two-photon polarization qubits (for example a
dual-rail photonic qubit read through a different mode structure) fall
outside the stated assumptions; the paper's proof does not cover them. Name
the assumption that a design relaxes before using or discarding the bound.

[I] A Bell-state measurement that succeeds in half of the events halves the
rate of teleportation events and sets a heralded, post-selected success
fraction. Pair this note with [2411.12387](2411.12387.md), whose
teleportation model assumes a 50 percent Bell-state measurement.
