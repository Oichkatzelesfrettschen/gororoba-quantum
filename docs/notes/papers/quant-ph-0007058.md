## Result needed by the project

[P] Abstract and Sec. IV (pp. 1, 5): with passive linear optical elements and
auxiliary modes in the vacuum state, no apparatus discriminates the four
equiprobable Bell states of two polarization qubits unambiguously with
probability above 50 percent. Eq. 24 is a chain `P^s <= (1/2) sum_i p_i^s =
(1/8) sum_i |alpha_i|^2 = 1/2` whose first relation is an inequality, and p. 5
supplies the scheme that attains it. If the four Bell states
carry unequal prior probabilities `p_1 >= ... >= p_4 > 0`, the optimum is
`p_1 + p_2` (footnote 3, p. 3).

Assumptions of the bound, each stated in the paper:

1. [P] Linear elements only: output creation operators are unitary linear
   combinations of the input creation operators (Eq. 1, p. 2). Active
   elements that mix creation and annihilation operators are excluded
   (footnote 1, p. 1).
2. [P] Auxiliary modes start in the vacuum state, and the apparatus is a
   fixed array with no conditional dynamics: no measurement outcome selects a
   later optical element (p. 3).
3. [P] Detectors are ideal and photon-number resolving; each outcome projects
   onto photon-number states (p. 2).
4. [P] The qubits are the polarization states of two single photons; an
   unambiguous result means a POVM element that only one Bell state can
   trigger (pp. 2-3).
5. [P] The bound is achieved by a 50/50 beam splitter followed by two
   polarizing beam splitters, which separates two of the four Bell states
   (p. 5).

Outside the bound, the paper reports that relaxing assumption 2 changes the
result: the scheme of Knill, Laflamme and Milburn (footnote 2, p. 1;
conclusion, p. 5) reaches a failure probability that falls as `1/(n+1)` with
`n` highly entangled auxiliary photons, and the paper calls that scheme
impracticable with the technology of its date.

## Section-by-section reading

1. Sec. I (pp. 1-2). [P] The paper defines a class of photonic measurements:
   passive linear mixing of modes, auxiliary modes in any state, conditional
   switching on photon counts, and photon-counting detection; it studies the
   subclass with vacuum auxiliary modes and no conditional switching.
   Assumption: qubits are carried by indistinguishable photons.
2. Sec. II (pp. 2-3), Eq. 1 and Fig. 1. [P] Output creation operators are a
   unitary combination of input creation operators,
   `c_i^dag = sum_j U^dag_ij a_j^dag`. The paper states that such a unitary
   acts on the creation operators and translates into the same map on states
   only for one-photon states (p. 2).
   Detectors are ideal and resolve photon number. Assumptions: passive
   elements only (footnote 1) and a unitary `U` of any size, realized by
   beam splitters and phase shifters (Reck et al., p. 2).
   [I] Supports `scripts/qd_network_protocol.py`: `propagate` and
   `propagate_by_permanents` carry the creation-operator map onto multiphoton
   Fock states, and `analyzer_unitary` is a `U` of this class.
3. Sec. III (pp. 2-5), Eqs. 2 to 12. [P] An unambiguous analyzer is a POVM
   with elements that only one Bell state can trigger. The four Bell states
   of two photons on modes `(a_1, a_2)` and `(a_3, a_4)` are written as
   bilinear forms `a . N . a |0>` (Eqs. 2 to 6), propagate as
   `M^mu = U^T N^mu U` (Eqs. 7 to 9), and reduce to `W^mu`, a unitary 4x4
   block (Eqs. 10 to 12). Assumption: the two input photons occupy four
   distinct modes, so the input is exactly two photons.
4. Sec. III (pp. 4-5), Eqs. 13 to 25. [P] Two photons in one output mode never
   identify a Bell state (Eqs. 13 and 14); a single detected photon leaves a
   conditional state, the four conditional states are linearly dependent, and
   at most two Bell states are linearly independent of the others (Eqs. 15 to
   22). Each output mode carries at most `|alpha_i|^2/4` of success
   probability, each success event uses two modes, and the unitarity of `U`
   gives `sum_i |alpha_i|^2 = 4`, so `P^s <= 1/2` (Eqs. 23 to 25).
   Assumption: equal prior `1/4` per Bell state and a success probability of
   one for the two discriminated states; footnote 3 replaces the priors and
   gives `p_1 + p_2`.
5. Sec. III (p. 5), achievability. [P] A 50/50 beam splitter and two
   polarizing beam splitters discriminate one pair of Bell states and merge
   the other pair, which gives `P^s = 1/2`; a half-wave plate before one input
   mode selects the other pair.
6. Sec. IV (p. 5). [P] The conclusion restates `P^s = 1/2` for a fixed array of
   linear elements and equiprobable Bell states, gives no quantitative result
   for conditional measurements or non-vacuum auxiliary modes, and reports
   that Knill, Laflamme and Milburn reach a failure probability that falls
   as `1/(n+1)` with `n` entangled auxiliary photons.

## Repository use

Each item below is [I] and names the design element it supports.

[I] Claim levels C3 and C5, gate G5, and `bell_pattern_table` in
`scripts/qd_network_protocol.py`. The 50 percent figure applies to the class
above. The paper writes the Bell states over four mode creation operators and
admits any passive unitary mixing of those modes (Eqs. 1 to 5), so relabeling
the two polarization modes of each photon as two spatial rails changes
nothing: an ordinary single-photon dual-rail Bell measurement with vacuum
auxiliary modes stays inside the bound. Entangled or non-vacuum auxiliary
photons and feed-forward between detection events fall outside the stated
assumptions, and the paper's proof does not cover them. Name the assumption
that a design relaxes before using or discarding the bound.

[I] Label mapping. The paper's `Psi^1, Psi^2` are the same-polarization pair
(`HH` plus or minus `VV`) under its mode labels, and its text names that pair
as the one the beam-splitter scheme discriminates; the repository analyzer
accepts the opposite-rail pair, `psi+` and `psi-`, and rejects `phi+` and
`phi-`. The paper's half-wave-plate remark makes the two choices local-unitary
relabelings of one another (p. 5), and the paper gives no polarizing-beam-
splitter orientation, so the bound `P^s = 1/2` holds for both and the pair
names carry no claim. Quote the bound, never the pair names.

[I] The success fraction in the design. With resource `Phi+`, each of the four
Bell outcomes occurs with probability `1/4` for every input (the standard
teleportation identity, not a statement of this paper), so the equal-prior
hypothesis of Eqs. 23 to 25 holds for the teleportation use, and the
design's accepted fraction 1/2 for each of the six Pauli inputs
(`teleportation_frames`, success probability) attains the bound. Any
physical analyzer of the same class sits at or below 1/2, and the ratio of
accepted trials to attempts needs the attempt denominator `N_attempt` of the
design's denominator rule.

[I] Threshold detectors. The paper assumes photon-number-resolving detectors.
A threshold detector is a coarse-graining of that measurement, and an
outcome of a coarse-grained measurement identifies a Bell state only if
every photon-number event it contains does; the union of such events is
bounded by the same optimum. The bound therefore also covers the design's
threshold patterns (`click`, `bell_pattern_table`). This is a deduction, not
a statement of the paper.

[I] Heralded post-selected success. A Bell-state measurement that succeeds in
half of the events halves the rate of teleportation events and sets a
heralded, post-selected success fraction. Pair this note with
[2411.12387](2411.12387.md), whose teleportation model assumes a 50 percent
Bell-state measurement.
