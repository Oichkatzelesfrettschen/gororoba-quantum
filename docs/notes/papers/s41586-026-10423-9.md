## Main claims

Two electron spins in a six-dot 28Si/SiGe array are carried toward each other
in separate travelling potential minima (conveyor-mode shuttling); the overlap
turns on a tunable exchange interaction that implements a CZ gate, and the same
gate prepares a Bell pair used for conditional post-selected teleportation.

- Interaction strength is tunable by spatial separation: shuttling both spins
  toward the center by 120 nm each (240 nm total) gives an average two-qubit
  gate fidelity of about 99 percent. [from-paper, abstract, PDF p. 1]
- Teleportation between qubits 320 nm apart reaches an average gate fidelity of
  87 percent, conditional and post-selected. [from-paper, abstract]
- The device is a linear array; the architecture of Fig. 1a (storage zones,
  pairs of conveyor channels meeting at interaction zones, readout zones with
  sensing dots and ancilla qubits) is a proposal, and only the interaction
  between two mobile qubits in one channel is demonstrated. [from-paper,
  Fig. 1a, PDF p. 2-3]

## Methods

- Device: six quantum dots on an isotopically purified 28Si/SiGe heterostructure
  with a 7 nm quantum well; three Ti:Pd gate layers separated by Al2O3
  (screening, six plungers P1-P6, seven barriers B0-B6); sensing dots SD1 and
  SD2 at both ends; cobalt micromagnet for EDSR single-qubit control.
  [from-paper, Fig. 1c and main text, PDF p. 2-3]
- Operating conditions: in-plane field 260 mT, temperature 200 mK.
  [from-paper, PDF p. 3]
- Qubit roles: Q2 and Q5 are the mobile qubits (dots 2 and 5); Q1 and Q6 are
  stationary ancillas; readout is a Pauli-spin-blockade variant that returns the
  parity of Q1-Q2 and Q5-Q6. Measurement time is 40 us. [from-paper, PDF p. 3
  and Methods "Detailed teleportation protocol"]
- Conveyor shuttling: two sine tones at f and f/2 with spatial periods of four
  and eight gate electrodes, phase offsets increasing symmetrically from P2 and
  P5 toward the central barrier B3. One conveyor cycle is a nominal displacement
  of 180 nm, twice the plunger pitch. [from-paper, PDF p. 3, Methods]
- Initialization: parity check, conditional X, post-selection on odd parity,
  adiabatic (4,0) to (3,1) and (0,4) to (1,3) transitions over 50 ns, then an X
  on Q5 to reach |1000> on (Q1,Q2,Q5,Q6). [from-paper, Methods "Parity readout
  and initialization sequence"]
- Exchange characterization: a decoupled controlled-phase (DCPhase) sequence
  (Rx(pi/2) on Q2, exchange for t/2, Rx(pi) on both, exchange for t/2, Rx(pi/2)
  on Q2, parity readout) versus conveyor cycles and a pulsed offset on B3
  between 65 and 115 mV. [from-paper, Fig. 2a-b]
- CZ gate: loading 2 ns to c = 0.4, approach 2 ns at 125 MHz conveyor frequency
  (22.5 m/s) to c = 0.65, interaction 25 ns at 10 MHz (1.8 m/s) with adiabatic
  approach, symmetric return; total 58 ns; spins start 270 nm apart across four
  dots; target maximum exchange 33 MHz, chosen far below the Zeeman difference
  of Q2 and Q5 so that flip-flop terms are suppressed and ZZ remains.
  [from-paper, Fig. 3a-b, PDF p. 4]
- CZ calibration: one spin in Rx(pi/2) superposition, the other in |0> or |1>,
  shuttle, virtual Rz(theta) by microwave phase, Rx(pi/2); the two curves are
  out of phase for a calibrated gate, and the same data calibrate single-qubit
  phase accumulated during shuttling. [from-paper, Fig. 3c]
- Benchmarking: interleaved randomized benchmarking, 120 random sequences per
  reference or interleaved measurement, 800 single shots per sequence, about 250
  shots per sequence kept after post-selection; sequence fidelity measured as
  the probability that both qubits return to spin down. [from-paper, Fig. 3d,
  Methods "Interleaved randomized benchmarking"]
- Teleportation circuit: Q6 holds the state to teleport; Q2 and Q5 form a Bell
  pair through the shuttled CZ with single-qubit gates and then are separated;
  Bell-state measurement on Q5 and Q6 is a basis change followed by two
  sequential parity readouts separated by a CNOT (Ry(pi/2) on Q6, a CZ by
  pulsing a barrier between static dots, Ry(-pi/2) on Q6). Tomography pulses on
  Q2 precede the Bell measurement, which is order-independent for the teleported
  state and shortens the delay between preparation and projection because the
  40 us readout exceeds the coherence times. [from-paper, Fig. 4a, Methods]

## Quantitative results

- Single-qubit randomized benchmarking in the shuttling device: F1Q =
  99.65(0.02) percent for Q2 and 99.69(0.02) percent for Q5 (Rabi chevrons at
  reference frequencies 11.657 GHz and 11.999 GHz). [from-paper, Fig. 1b,d]
- Coherence times and control fidelities of the static qubits are summarized in
  Supplementary Figs. 5 and 6, which are not retained. [from-paper, PDF p. 3]
- Exchange coupling tunable up to 90 MHz through conveyor cycles and B3 offset;
  higher values were not measurable because of fast decay. At the zero-cycle
  start J is too small to measure; J increases exponentially with the B3 offset.
  [from-paper, Fig. 2a-b, PDF p. 4]
- Operating point for the high-fidelity CZ: 0.9 conveyor cycles and J = 33 MHz.
  T2* of both qubits decreases as J grows, and T2* depends on the state of the
  other spin through the magnetic field gradient. T2* during shuttling exceeds
  the static T2* because of motional averaging. [from-paper, Fig. 2d, PDF p. 4]
- Elongated-dot regime (one sine wave, period of eight electrodes, B3 offset
  -10 mV): J grows exponentially up to about 0.86 conveyor cycles and then
  saturates as the two potentials merge, consistent with strong electron-
  electron interaction (possible Wigner-molecule formation); a short-T2* region
  precedes the saturated regime. [from-paper, Fig. 2e-f]
- Interleaved RB: reference Clifford fidelity FC = 85.18 +- 0.14 percent;
  interleaved CZ fidelity FCZ = 98.86 +- 0.29 percent. [from-paper, Fig. 3d]
- Teleportation: for the Psi+ Bell-measurement outcome (effective X on the
  teleported state, even-even parity outcome |00> between Q5 and Q6) the
  quantum process tomography of the channel gives an average gate fidelity of
  86.7 +- 0.9 percent after correcting for state preparation and measurement
  errors; the uncertainty is the bootstrap standard deviation; the fidelity
  exceeds the classical bound of 2/3. For the Phi- outcome the fidelity is
  lower. Process matrices come from projected least-squares with complete
  positivity and trace preservation constraints. [from-paper, Fig. 4b, PDF p. 5]
- The Bell-state measurement distinguishes |00> from |11> but not |10> from
  |01>, because singlet-T0 mixing in spin-blockade parity readout hides that
  difference; the protocol is therefore conditional and succeeds
  probabilistically through post-selection. [from-paper, Methods]
- Stated next step: deterministic teleportation with fast non-demolition
  readout and real-time feed-forward on four distinguishable Bell outcomes.
  [from-paper, Conclusion, PDF p. 6]

## Relation to this repository

Retained run: `results/quantum_inspire/runs/2026-10-16T211635Z-tuna_17/`
(`analysis.json`, `cases.json`, `teleport.cq`, produced by
`scripts/qi_crosstalk_teleport_cases.py`). Everything below is inference.

- [inference] The script's transported-pair teleportation case is a gate-based
  counterpart of this paper's protocol. On a path I - P1 - P2 - D, P1 and P2 form
  a Bell pair, a SWAP carries P2's half to D (standing in for conveyor
  shuttling of Q5 away from Q2), and I and P1 take a Bell-state measurement.
  The mapping of roles is Q6 to I, Q5 to P1 and Q2, the remote half of the
  Bell pair that receives the teleported state, to D; it is a structural analogy of the circuit, not a physical equivalence, since a SWAP
  on a transmon coupling map and an exchange-based CZ between moving spins
  share only the logical Bell-pair-plus-BSM structure.
- [inference] Tuna-17 has no feed-forward, so the repository applies the Pauli
  frame X^m(P1) Z^m(I) classically to the recorded outcome of D, where each
  input is a Pauli eigenstate measured in its own basis and every correction
  keeps or flips the outcome. The paper's hardware likewise lacks real-time
  feed-forward and resolves only two of four Bell outcomes; it reports the
  Psi+ post-selected channel. The repository's post-selected subset
  m(I) = m(P1) = 0 is the analog of the paper's conditional protocol, and its
  classically corrected result covers all four BSM outcomes, which the paper's
  parity readout cannot resolve.
- [inference] Retained Tuna-17 values (`analysis.json`, `teleport`): corrected
  fidelities 0.746 (input 0), 0.827 (input 1), 0.844 (input +), 0.862 (input
  +i); post-selected fidelities 0.768, 0.782, 0.846, 0.864 with post-selected
  fractions 0.268, 0.230, 0.253, 0.236; the cross-axis mean is 0.831 under an
  assumption of path independence. These numbers carry no SPAM correction and
  each input ran on a different path of the coupling map, so path-dependent
  gate and readout errors enter each input differently.
- [inference] The values are not directly comparable to the paper's 86.7 +- 0.9
  percent. That number is an average process (gate) fidelity of one
  post-selected channel after SPAM correction by process tomography. The Tuna
  values are single-input state fidelities for a Pauli eigenstate, uncorrected
  for SPAM. A known eigenstate input also admits a classical strategy that
  scores unit fidelity on that input, so the paper's 2/3 bound applies to an
  average over input states and cannot be applied to one input or to the
  four-input mean above. What the comparison supports is only that a
  transported Bell pair with classical Pauli-frame correction reproduces the
  protocol's logic on a transmon device.
- Relevance (inference): (a) calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: low,
  the result concerns spin-qubit transport and has no IBM backend content;
  (b) Cayley-Dickson structure: none; (c) factoring resource estimates: none.

## Open points and discrepancies

- The publisher page gives pages 391-397 and the retained PDF has 8 pages; a
  pre-retrieval summary of this paper that stated page 392 and 11 pages does not
  match the file or the page metadata, which this note follows.
- The abstract states "about 99%" for the two-qubit gate; the benchmarked value
  is 98.86 +- 0.29 percent, extracted from the interleaved and reference decay
  rates (reference Clifford fidelity 85.18 percent). [from-paper, abstract,
  Fig. 3d]
- The 33 MHz figure is the exchange at the operating point; the paper tunes J up
  to 90 MHz. [from-paper, Fig. 2b, PDF p. 4]
- The Supplementary Information, including the error budget (section G) and
  the separate polarization and phase teleportation tests (section F), is not
  retained; those values stay unread.
