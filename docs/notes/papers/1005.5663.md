## Setting and conventions

- [P] Section 1 (pp. 1-3): the task of computing a Groebner basis `G` of an ideal `I` of `Q[x_1, ..., x_n]` by modular methods has three steps: a Groebner basis modulo
  `p` for sufficiently many primes `p`, a lift to `Q` by the Chinese remainder theorem and rational reconstruction, and a verification that `I = <G>` and that `G` is a Groebner
  basis of `<G>`; omitting the third step yields a basis only with high probability. The implementation is in the Singular library `modstd.lib`, shipped with Singular 3-1-2.
- [P] Page 3: for an ideal `I = <f_1, ..., f_r>` and a prime `p` that divides no denominator of the coefficients of the `f_i`, `I_p` is the ideal of `F_p[X]` generated by the reductions of the `f_i` modulo `p`.
  Section 2 (p. 3) works with a monomial ordering that is either global or local, and with no homogeneity hypothesis on `I`.
- [P] Definition 2.1 (p. 4): for `G` a standard basis of `I`, a prime `p` is lucky for `I` if and only if the leading monomials satisfy `LM(G) = LM(G_p)` with `G_p` a standard basis of `I_p`, and unlucky
  otherwise; a set `P` of lucky primes is sufficiently large for `I` when the product of its primes is at least the maximum of `2 |c|^2` over the coefficients `c` occurring in `G`.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Page 2: the paper restates Arnold's result for a homogeneous ideal `I` of `Q[X]` and a global ordering: if `G` generates an ideal containing `I`, is a Groebner basis of `<G>`, and satisfies `LM(G) = LM(I F_p[X])` for some prime `p`,
  then `G` is a Groebner basis of `I`. The sentence before Theorem 2.4 (p. 5) attributes the homogeneous case to the paper cited as [A03] (Arnold; see [the note on that paper](arnold-2003-modular-groebner.md)), the local case to the paper cited as [Pf07] (Pfister 2007; the bibliography entry was not opened) and the global case to weighted homogenization (Theorem 7.5.1 of Greuel and Pfister).
- [P] Theorem 2.4 (p. 5): let `G` be a set of polynomials of `Q[X]` such that `LM(G) = LM(G_p)` for a standard basis `G_p` of `I_p` for some prime `p`, `G` is a standard basis of `<G>`, and `I` lies in `<G>`; then `I = <G>`. The paper notes that the first condition follows from a positive `pTestSB` and that the other two are checked
  explicitly at the end of the algorithm.
- [P] Proof of Theorem 2.4 (pp. 5-6), for a global ordering: a positive weight vector `w` is chosen so that `G` is a standard basis of `<G>` for the weighted ordering and the weighted degrees of the leading terms and tails satisfy strict inequalities; the weighted homogenizations `I-bar`, `G-bar`
  in `Q[X, t]` are compared through their Hilbert functions, and the chain `HF_(I-bar)(n) <= HF_(I-bar_p)(n) = HF_(<G-bar>)(n) <= HF_(I-bar)(n)` (the first inequality because `dim_Q I-bar[n] >= dim_(F_p) I-bar_p[n]`, the second because `I-bar` lies in `<G-bar>`) gives `I-bar = <G-bar>`, hence `I = <G>`.
- [P] Procedures and Algorithm 1 (pp. 4-5): `deleteUnluckyPrimesSB` keeps the largest class of primes whose standard bases have equal leading monomials, `pTestSB` tests with a random fresh prime whether the reduction of the candidate `G` is a standard basis of `I_p`, and Algorithm 1 (`modStd`) loops over a list `P` of
  random primes, deletes the unlucky ones, lifts, applies `pTestSB`, then checks `I` in `<G>` and that `G` is a standard basis of `<G>`, enlarging `P` otherwise. Remark 2.3 (p. 5) parallelizes the per-prime computations and the final tests.
- [P] Remark 2.5 (pp. 6-7): Algorithm 1 without the final tests is probabilistic, its output is a standard basis only with high probability, and it works for every ordering including mixed orderings; for a mixed ordering the exact variant would homogenize first, which the authors report to be usually inefficient.
- [P] Section 3 (pp. 7-9): for a zero-dimensional ideal, Proposition 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 (p. 7) give the associated primes through one polynomial `F` in a generic linear form, Proposition 3.3 (p. 8) the radical through the squarefree parts of the elimination polynomials `f_i`, Definition 3.4 (p. 8) a notion of lucky prime by agreement of degrees of the modular `f_i^(p)`, and Algorithm 2 (`zeroRadical`, p. 9) the modular computation of the radical.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 program computes standard bases of ideals defined over `Q(i)` modulo the primes 32009 and 32029, and its findings state that a result over `F_p` certifies that reduction only. Theorem 2.4 is the lifting criterion for
  the characteristic 0 direction: a candidate `G` whose leading monomials equal those of a standard basis of one reduction `I_p`, that is a standard basis of the ideal it generates, and whose ideal contains `I`, generates `I`. The theorem needs
  no homogeneity and holds for global and local orderings (the global case through the weighted homogenization of the proof), so no homogeneity check is required before it applies.
- [I] The theorem is stated for ideals of `Q[X]`. The program's ideals live over `Q(i)`, so the statement does not apply to them unchanged; the method of Boku, Decker, Fieker and Steenpass ([1504.04564](1504.04564.md)) treats number fields and keeps an explicit verification step. The two conditions that carry the work, `I` in `<G>`
  and `G` a standard basis of `<G>`, are checks over the characteristic 0 field and are not supplied by a computation modulo 32009 or 32029.
- [I] The paper's `pTestSB` is the modular check that a candidate is likely correct, which matches the repository's practice of agreement at two primes as a consistency check; as Remark 2.5 states, passing it without the final tests gives a basis only with high probability.
