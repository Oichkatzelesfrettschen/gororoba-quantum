## Transcribed statements

Notation (pp. 1-4): `p_k` is the `k`th prime with `p_1 = 2`; `p_k#` is the `k`th primorial; `D(k)` is the sequence of
differences between consecutive integers coprime to `p_k#` (Definition 1.1); `h(k)` is the primorial Jacobsthal
function, the least `m` such that every `m` consecutive integers contain one coprime to `p_k#` (Definition 1.5);
`N_min(k)` is the even number such that every even number up to it is in `D(k)` (Lemma 1.2), and
`N_max(k) = h(k)` is the largest element of `D(k)` (Lemma 1.3 and p. 3).

- [P] Abstract (p. 1): the Jacobsthal function of the primorial "represent[s] the greatest of these differences";
  "exhaustive computational results on non-existent differences for primes `p_k` up to `k = 44`"; "the data
  suggest the assumption that all even natural numbers up to `h(k - 1)` occur as differences of coprimes to
  `p_k#`".
- [P] Restricted-covering reduction (Definition 2.4 and Corollary 2.5, pp. 6-7). A covering `{a_i mod pi_i}`
  of a run `<a>_m` of `m` consecutive integers assigns every position a residue class. It is restricted when no
  class also covers the position before the run (`a - 1`) or after it (`a + m`). Corollary 2.5 states that two
  consecutive numbers `x < y` coprime to a product `Pi` of distinct primes exist if and only if there is a
  restricted covering with all residues in `{1, ..., pi_i - 1}` of `<1>_(y-x-1)`. For `pi_i = p_i` this gives
  `m in D(k)` if and only if a restricted covering `{a_i mod p_i}`, `i = 1..k`, of `<1>_(m-1)` exists (p. 7).
  Corollary 2.3 (p. 6) relocates any covering to start at 1 with no zero residue (Lemma 2.1, p. 5).
- [P] Odd-prime reduction (Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.2, pp. 9-10): with the prime 2 split off,
  `m in D(k)` if and only if `{a_i mod p_i}`, `i = 2..k`, is a restricted covering of `<1>_(m/2 - 1)`. The search
  in Section 3 therefore runs over odd primes only, with the Greedy Permutation Algorithm of Ziller and Morack
  (reference [10], arXiv:1611.03310) extended to exclude the residue class `m + 1 mod p` per prime
  (Algorithm 1, p. 11).
- [P] Proposition 1.6 (p. 3): `h(k) >= 2 p_(k-1)` for `k > 1`, and `2 p_(k-1)` is in `D(k)`.
- [P] Proposition 2.6 (p. 7): `2k` is in `D(k)` for every `k`. Proposition 2.7 (p. 7): `m in D(k)` implies
  `m in D(k + 1)`. Corollary 2.8 (p. 8): `2k <= N_min(k)`.
- [P] Disproof of de Polignac (p. 4 and p. 13): de Polignac presumed `N_min(k) >= 2 p_(k-1)` for `k > 1`;
  Ziller's Table 1 violates it "for `k=6` and `k=8`", and the text says "Perhaps these are the only exceptions".
- [P] Conjecture 4.1 (p. 13): for `k > 1`, `h(k - 1) <= N_min(k)`. Proposition 4.2 (p. 13) restates it as: an even
  `2n < h(k - 1)` outside `D(k - 1)` lies in `D(k)`. The text (p. 13) adds "All computed data satisfy this
  assumption" and that the general question of lacking differences "is open", with the data favoring neither
  `N_min(k) = h(k)` for all `k` nor a gap for infinitely many `k`.

- [P] At `k = 6` (primorial 30030, primes up to 13) the missing difference 20 is the only entry and the smallest

- [P] At `k = 8` (primes up to 19) the missing difference 32 is the only entry; the smallest missing difference is 32.
- [P] At `k = 14` (primes up to 43) the missing differences are 86 and 88; the smallest missing difference is 86.
- [P] In each of the three rows `N_min(k) = (smallest missing difference) - 2` and `h(k - 1) < N_min(k)`, as

  the paper.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The reduction of Corollary 2.5 is the covering formulation of a defect: a gap of length `m` between
  consecutive integers coprime to `p_k#` is a restricted covering of `m - 1` positions by one residue class per
  prime up to `p_k`. A benchmark of cover defects over primorials tests its search against Table 1 (and
  against `h(k)` values from reference [10]).
- [I] The paper's literature link to Erdos Problem 854 is not stated in its text; the
  [problem record](erdos-854-consecutive-coprime-gaps.md) asks the same question, and Table 1 is the computation
  it concerns for `k <= 44`.
