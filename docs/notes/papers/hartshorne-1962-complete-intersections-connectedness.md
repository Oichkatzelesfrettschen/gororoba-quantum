## Connectedness in codimension k (Section 1, pp. 497-500)

- [P] Setting (p. 497): the paper works with Grothendieck's preschemes, so its results hold over arbitrary (Noetherian) ground rings. A closed subset `V` of a non-singular ambient prescheme `X` is a complete intersection if it is the intersection of `s = codim(V, X)` hypersurfaces, a hypersurface being a closed subset of pure codimension 1. The paper gives a necessary topological condition for `V` to be a complete intersection in some ambient prescheme: `V` is locally connected in codimension one.
- [P] Definitions (pp. 497-498): for a Noetherian topological space `X` and an irreducible closed subspace `Y`, `codim(Y, X)` is the supremum of the integers `r` for which a chain of closed irreducible subspaces `Z_0 < Z_1 < ... < Z_r` of `X` exists with `Y` contained in `Z_0`; for a closed subset `Y` that is no longer irreducible, `codim(Y, X)` is the infimum of `codim(Y', X)` over its closed irreducible subspaces `Y'`, so the empty set has codimension `+infinity`.
- [P] Proposition 1.1 (p. 498): `X` is a Noetherian topological space and `k >= 0` an integer. The following are equivalent: (i) whenever `Y` is a closed subset of `X` with `codim(Y, X) > k`, `X - Y` is connected; (ii) for any two irreducible components `X'` and `X''` of `X` there is a finite sequence `X' = X_1, X_2, ..., X_r = X''` of irreducible components such that for each `i` the intersection `X_i intersect X_(i+1)` has codimension at most `k` in `X`.
  Such an `X` is called connected in codimension `k`. Remarks 1.1.1 to 1.1.3 (p. 498): connected in codimension `k` implies connected in codimension `k'` for `k' >= k`; it implies connected; and for `k >= dim X` it is equivalent to connected. Example 1.1.4 (p. 498): a prescheme formed by two planes meeting in a point is connected in codimension 2 and not in codimension 1.
- [P] Standing assumption (p. 498): from here on every space has the generic point property, that every closed irreducible subset has a unique generic point (every prescheme has it).
- [P] Local spaces (p. 499): a local topological space has a single closed point, and the local space `X_y` of `X` at `y` is the set of generalizations of `y`. Lemma 1.2 (p. 499): for a connected topological space `X` and a closed subspace `Y` such that `X_y - y` is non-empty and connected for every `y` in `Y`, `X - Y` is connected.
  Proposition 1.3 (p. 499), for a Noetherian topological space `X` and `k >= 0`: the following are equivalent: (i) each local space `X_y` is connected in codimension `k`; (ii) whenever `dim X_y > k`, the space `X_y - y` is connected. A space with these properties is locally connected in codimension `k`, abbreviated `C_k` (p. 500).
- [P] Remarks 1.3.1 to 1.3.3 and Example 1.3.4 (p. 500): `C_k` implies `C_(k')` for `k' >= k`; `C_k` is a purely local property, and a connected space satisfying `C_k` is connected in codimension `k`, while the converse fails; for `k >= dim X`, `C_k` is an empty condition. The example takes three planes `A`, `B`, `C` in `P^4` with `A` and `B` meeting in a line, `B` and `C` meeting in a line and `A`, `C` meeting only in a point `x_0`, and the local space `X` of the affine cone over their union at the origin:
  `X` is connected in codimension 1 (its projectivization is) but fails `C_1`, because at a generic point `y` of the line over `x_0` the local space `X_y` looks like two planes meeting in a point.

## Depth and connectedness (Section 2, pp. 500-503)

- [P] Definitions (pp. 500-501): the depth of a Noetherian local ring `A` is the number of elements in a maximal `A`-sequence, and `A` is Cohen-Macaulay if its depth equals its dimension. A Noetherian ring `A` has Serre's property `S_k` (for `k > 0`) if for every prime `p`: 1) `dim A_p <= k` implies `A_p` Cohen-Macaulay; 2) `dim A_p > k` implies `depth A_p >= k`.
  Remarks 2.0.1 to 2.0.4 (p. 501): `S_(k')` implies `S_k` for `k' >= k`; `S_k` is stable under localization; for `k >= dim A` all the properties `S_k` are equivalent; for a Noetherian local ring of dimension `n`, `S_n` is equivalent to Cohen-Macaulay (a Cohen-Macaulay local ring has Cohen-Macaulay localizations, Zariski and Samuel, vol. II, p. 399, Corollary 4).
- [P] Proposition 2.1 (p. 501): for a Noetherian local ring `A` with `X = Spec A` and closed point `y = {m}`, if `X - y` is disconnected then `depth A <= 1`. The proof (pp. 501-502) writes `X` as the union of two closed subsets meeting in `y`, splits an irredundant primary decomposition of `(0)` accordingly into ideals `a` and `b`, finds elements `x` in `a` and `y` in `b` with `x b = 0 = y a`, and shows that `x + y` is a maximal `A`-sequence of length one.
- [P] Theorem 2.2 (p. 502): for a connected, locally Noetherian prescheme `X` and a closed subset `Y` such that the local ring `O_(y,X)` has depth at least 2 for each `y` in `Y`, `X - Y` is connected (the proof applies Lemma 1.2 and Proposition 2.1).
  Corollary 2.3 (p. 503): for a locally Noetherian prescheme `X` and `k >= 0`, if `depth O_(y,X) >= 2` for every `y` with `dim O_(y,X) > k`, then `X` is locally connected in codimension `k`. Corollary 2.4 (p. 503): a locally Noetherian prescheme all of whose local rings have Serre's property `S_2` is locally connected in codimension 1.
- [P] Remark 2.4.1 (p. 503; attributed to Grothendieck): a Noetherian local ring with `S_2` that satisfies Nagata's first chain condition (all maximal chains of primes between two primes `p < p'` have the same length) is equidimensional; the proof applies Corollary 2.4 and the chain condition along a chain of components.

## Application to complete intersections (Section 3, pp. 503-508)

- [P] Setting and definitions (pp. 503-505): `X` is a non-singular prescheme of dimension `n`; a closed subset `V` of codimension `s` is a complete intersection in `X` if it is the intersection of `s` hypersurfaces, and locally a complete intersection if every point of `V` has a neighborhood in `X` in which it is one. A local ring `A` of dimension `r` is a complete intersection if it is the quotient of a regular local ring `B` by an ideal generated by `dim B - r` elements; such a ring is Cohen-Macaulay (by the Cohen-Macaulay theorem cited on p. 504).
  A closed subprescheme `(V, O_V)` of `X` is a complete intersection if its underlying space is one and `O_V` is the structure induced by some representation with some multiplicities (pp. 504-505).
- [P] Proposition 3.1 (p. 504): for a complete intersection `V = H_1 intersect ... intersect H_s` in a non-singular prescheme `X`, with `s = codim(V, X)`, positive integers `n_1, ..., n_s`, `I_i` the ideal sheaf of `H_i` and `J = I_1^(n_1) + ... + I_s^(n_s)`, the subprescheme `(V, O_X/J)` has all its local rings complete intersections; Remark 3.1.1 notes that this structure is in general not the reduced one. Proposition 3.2 (p. 505): the completion of a complete intersection local ring is a complete intersection.
  Lemma 3.3 (p. 505): for a closed subset `V` of a Noetherian prescheme `X`, the underlying space `V_x^*` of `Spec(O_(x,V))^*` (completion of the local ring at `x` for a subprescheme structure on `V`) does not depend on the structure chosen; it is the formal local space of `V` at `x`, and a local topological property holds formally if it holds for every formal local space.
- [P] Theorem 3.4 (pp. 505-506): a closed subset `V` of a non-singular prescheme `X` that is locally a complete intersection is locally connected in codimension one, also formally. The proof reduces to `V` globally a complete intersection, applies Proposition 3.1 to give `V` a structure with complete intersection local rings, which are Cohen-Macaulay and so have `S_2` (Remarks 2.0.3 and 2.0.4), and then Corollary 2.4; the formal statement follows from Proposition 3.2 in the same way.
- [P] Remarks and examples (pp. 506-508).
  3.4.1: two planes in `C^4` meeting in a single point (`z_1 = z_2 = 0` and `z_3 = z_4 = 0`) are not connected in codimension 1 at the origin, hence not a complete intersection, and applying the theorem to the local ring of convergent power series shows they are not one in the sense of several complex variables either.
  3.4.2: the surface in `C^4` parametrized by `z_1 = t`, `z_2 = t u`, `z_3 = u(u - 1)`, `z_4 = u^2 (u - 1)` is irreducible, hence locally connected in codimension one, but formally at the origin it looks like the two planes of 3.4.1, so it is not formally connected in codimension one and is not a complete intersection.
  3.4.3: Kneser showed that three hypersurfaces suffice for curves in 3-space; whether two suffice for irreducible curves is open, and the example of 3.4.2 shows that two do not suffice for irreducible surfaces in 4-space. The paper states Grothendieck's question whether a complete normal local ring of dimension 2 can have a spectrum that is a complete intersection in no non-singular prescheme, and gives, in dimension at least 3, the ring of invariants of `k[[x, y, z]]` (characteristic of `k` different from 2) under `(x, y, z) -> (-x, -y, -z)`.
  3.4.4: the stronger formulation that any two components of a complete intersection through a point meet in codimension one with respect to each fails: in `C^4` the union of the planes `z_1 = z_2 = 0`, `z_2 = z_3 = 0`, `z_3 = z_4 = 0` is the complete intersection of `z_1 z_3 + z_2 z_4 = 0` and `z_2 z_3 = 0`, while the first and third planes meet only in a point.
  3.4.5: a subprescheme whose underlying space is a complete intersection need not be one even locally: the curve `x = t^3, y = t^4, z = t^5` with its reduced structure is not a complete intersection in the local space of affine 3-space at the origin (its ideal needs at least 3 generators, since the residues of `y^2 - xz`, `x^3 - yz`, `z^2 - x^2 y` modulo `m^3` are independent), although `V` is the set-theoretic complete intersection of `z^2 - x^2 y = 0` and `x^4 + y^3 - 2xyz = 0`.
  3.4.6: a complete intersection of dimension at least 1 in projective space is connected (by `H^0(V, O_V) = k`, via Serre, Faisceaux algebriques coherents, nos. 77 and 78), so with Remark 1.3.2 it is connected in codimension one; a remark of Serre (p. 508) gives a direct argument by cutting `V` with a generic intersection `W` of `n - r + 1` hyperplanes: if `V` were not connected in codimension one, `W intersect V` would split into two disjoint pieces of dimension 1, yet it is a connected complete intersection of dimension 1.
