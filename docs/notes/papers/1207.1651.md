## Setting and conventions

- [P] Section 2 (pp. 2-4): the value of `x = a/b` modulo `N` is `x_N = (a + N Z)(b + N Z)^(-1)`; the Farey map `phi_(B,N)` from `F_B` intersected with `Q_N` to `Z/NZ` is well defined and injective for `B <= sqrt((N-1)/2)`; Algorithm 2 computes the Farey preimage by the Euclidean algorithm, and Algorithm 3 (Chinese remaindering plus Algorithm 2) reconstructs a rational number
  under the assumption that the modular black box (Algorithm 1) either returns `false` or a correct residue.
- [P] Section 3 (pp. 4-5): a prime `p` is good for an algorithm with rational input data if the algorithm applied modulo `p` returns the reduction of the characteristic 0 result and bad otherwise. Type-1 bad primes make the reduced input undefined and are rejected at no cost; type-2 primes make the computation fail in the course of the construction;
  type-3 primes change an invariant whose characteristic 0 value is known a priori; type-4 primes change an invariant with a priori unknown value, for instance the leading ideal in a modular Groebner basis computation, and are eliminated by a majority vote when finitely many; type-5 primes are those no known test detects without the characteristic 0 result, and they enter the Chinese remainder step.

## Theorem statements

- [P] Lemma 4.2 and Lemma 4.3 (pp. 6-7): in the lattice `Lambda` generated by `(N, 0)` and `(r, 1)`, all vectors of squared length less than `N` are collinear (Lemma 4.2); if `N = N' M` with `a/b` reduced and `a = b s mod N'`, `r = s mod N'`, `r = t mod M` and `(a^2 + b^2) M < N'`, then every such vector represents `a/b`, and a shortest nonzero vector `(x, y)` with `gcd(a, b) = 1`
  has `gcd(x, y)` dividing `M` (Lemma 4.3). Algorithm 5 (p. 8), error tolerant lifting, computes a shortest vector of the lattice by Gaussian reduction and returns `u/v` when its squared length is below `N`; Example 4.5 (pp. 7-9) reconstructs 13/12 from `N = 38885 = 5 . 7 . 11 . 101` with a bad congruence at the prime 101 (failure) and at 7 (success).
- [P] Definition 5.2 (pp. 10-11): for an assigned ideal `U(0)` in `Q[X]` and its characteristic `p` counterparts `U(p)`, with the assumption `U(0)_p = U(p)` for all but finitely many `p`, a prime `p` is lucky if (a) `U(0)_p = U(p)` (violation is type-5) and (b) `LM(G(0)) = LM(G(p))` (violation is type-4), unlucky otherwise;
  a finite set of primes is sufficiently large when `N' > (a^2 + b^2) M` for all coefficients `a/b` of `G(0)`, with `N'` the product of the lucky and `M` the product of the unlucky primes.
- [P] Remark 5.3 and Example 5.4 (p. 11): the paper calls a prime Arnold-lucky for a given ideal `J` when `LM(H(0)) = LM(H(p))` for the reduced bases `H(0)` of `J` and `H(p)` of `J_p`; for homogeneous `J` it cites Arnold's Theorems 5.12 and 6.2 for `H(0)_p = H(p)` at an Arnold-lucky prime and Arnold's Corollary 5.4 and Theorem 5.13 for the finiteness of the unlucky primes, and states that, by weighted homogenization as in the proof of Theorem 2.4 of Idrees, Pfister and Steidel,
  these results also hold for non-homogeneous ideals. The proof of Lemma 5.6 (p. 12) uses that a prime satisfying condition (1b) is Arnold-lucky for `U(0)`. Example 5.4 lists the Arnold-unlucky primes 3, 5, 11, 809, 65179, 531264751 and a 27-digit prime for the ideal generated by the partial derivatives of `f = x^5 + y^11 + x y^9 + x^3 y^9` in the lexicographical ordering.
- [P] Lemma 5.5 (p. 11) and Lemma 5.6 (p. 12): the set of unlucky primes is finite, and a sufficiently large set of primes that satisfy (1b) lifts through Algorithm 5 to `G(0)`. Algorithm 6 (p. 13) reconstructs an ideal (the procedures `deleteByMajorityVote` and `pTest` are described on p. 12): it computes `G(p)` for a list of random primes, removes the minority leading ideals by `deleteByMajorityVote`, lifts, applies `pTest` (a random fresh prime), and returns only after a
  verification that the lifted basis equals `G(0)`, enlarging the list otherwise; Remark 5.7 (pp. 12-13) requires a weighted cardinality count when the loop runs more than once.
- [P] Example 5.12 (pp. 14-15): a sextic curve over `Q` for which the prime 5 is bad of type 5 for the algorithm `I -> sqrt(I + Jac(I))`: the leading ideals of `U(0)` and `U(5)` agree, so a majority vote cannot remove 5, while `U(0)_5` differs from `U(5)`; the paper states (pp. 11 and 14) that condition (1a) cannot be checked a priori, since `U(0)_p` is known only after `G(0)` and `U(0)` are found, and that a prime of this kind cannot be eliminated by a majority vote on the leading ideal.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] The A_5 program lifts computations from `F_p` to characteristic 0 and records a result modulo 32009 or 32029 as a statement about that reduction only. The paper supplies the vocabulary and the guarantees for the lifting: type-4 and type-5 bad primes (Definition 5.2), the statement that finitely many bad primes do not defeat error tolerant reconstruction
  (Lemma 5.6), and Example 5.12, a concrete case where a type-5 prime agrees with the characteristic 0 leading ideal but yields a different ideal. The example is the reason a match of leading ideals at two primes is a consistency check and not a proof of the characteristic 0 ideal.
- [I] The setting is `Q[X]` with a global ordering and a rational reconstruction by Chinese remaindering; the program's ground field is `Q(i)`, handled in [1504.04564](1504.04564.md), and its ideals are generated, as in Arnold's setting ([arnold-2003-modular-groebner](arnold-2003-modular-groebner.md)), by given generators, for which Remark 5.3 identifies Arnold luckiness with condition (1b).
- [I] The Arnold-unlucky primes of Example 5.4 include small primes (3, 5, 11, 809) beside large ones, so the size of 32009 or 32029 does not exempt either from being unlucky for a given ideal; the paper makes no statement about the program's ideals, and whether 32009 or 32029 is lucky for them is not established here.
