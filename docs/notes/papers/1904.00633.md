## Main claims

[P] A CNOT circuit mapped to a coupling graph is re-synthesized from its parity map by Gaussian elimination restricted to row operations along Steiner trees, which uses fewer two-qubit gates than general-purpose routers on random CNOT circuits.

- [P] Parity map (Sec. 2.1): a reversible linear map on bit strings, an N x N matrix over GF(2); post-composing a CNOT with control j and target k adds row j to row k. Gauss-Jordan elimination that records the row operations gives C P = 1, and the circuit for P is the recorded operations in reverse order.
- [P] Steiner trees (Defs. 2.1 and 2.2): a minimal subtree of the coupling graph that spans a vertex set S (NP-hard in general; the paper uses a heuristic from Floyd-Warshall paths and minimal spanning trees), and a decreasing variant in which every vertex exceeds its children in a fixed total order.
- [P] steiner-gauss (Sec. 2.2): steiner-down builds an upper-triangular matrix column by column, filling a Steiner tree with ones and then emptying it from the leaves to the root; steiner-up clears the entries above the diagonal with decreasing row operations so that the triangular form survives. The algorithm assumes a Hamiltonian path in the coupling graph, which supplies the order, and every row operation travels along an edge of the graph.
- [P] General graphs (Sec. 4.1): a recursive version, steiner-gauss-rec, removes the Hamiltonian-path assumption by numbering a spanning tree by depth-first post-order.
- [P] Placement: a genetic algorithm permutes the rows and columns of the parity map to shrink the Steiner trees (population 30 and 15 iterations on 9 qubits, 50 and 100 on 16, 100 and 100 on 20; crossover 0.8, mutation 0.2); running time a few seconds to about a minute.
- [P] Benchmark (Sec. 3): 380 random CNOT circuits (20 per size, sizes 3 to 30 on 9 qubits and 4 to 256 on 16 and 20 qubits) on a 3 x 3 grid, a 4 x 4 grid, IBM QX5, Rigetti 16Q Aspen, and IBM Q20 Tokyo, against Rigetti QuilC and the Cambridge Quantum Computing tket compiler. The paper reports average savings of 48% over QuilC and 36% over tket (Table 1). Overheads turn negative at 256 gates because re-synthesis from the parity map beats the input circuit.
- [P] Scaling (Fig. 1): on 9 qubits the unconstrained Patel-Markov-Hayes bound 9^2 / log2(9) is about 25.6 CNOTs; the constrained method levels off near 42; the naive routing baseline of Nash, Gheorghiu, and Mosca would level off near 102.
- [P] Extensions (Sec. 4.2): CNOT plus Rz circuits through phase polynomials, where the same Gaussian elimination produces the CNOTs and the phase gates land on unit-vector parities; a ZX-calculus extraction routine, described and not implemented.
- [P] Limits (Sec. 5): the evaluation counts CNOT gates only; depth and gate-fidelity effects are future work, as is exploiting unequal two-qubit fidelities. The paper notes that parallel gates on neighboring qubits can interfere.
- [P] Related work (Sec. 1): Nash, Gheorghiu, and Mosca (ref. 22) developed a very similar technique independently, within days of this preprint.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Gauss-Jordan CNOT synthesis. Repository claim: MODULAR_SYNTHESIS.md, "Affine-residual challenger", and affine_residual.py `linear_cnot_network` -- swap-free Gauss-Jordan elimination synthesizes the linear part A in place with 10, 11, and 12 CNOTs for multipliers 2, 4, and 16. Source item: Sec. 2.1 (a CNOT adds row j to row k; record the row operations of the elimination and reverse them). Model map: `reduced[row] ^= reduced[column]` with the recorded pair (column, row) is the paper's row operation, and `cnots = tuple(reversed(operations))` is its reversal. The repository fills a missing pivot by adding a lower donor row, one CNOT where a swap costs three; the paper does not address pivoting without topology. Label: "known theorem reproduced". Not established by the source: any topology constraint inside the repository's synthesis, which treats the five data qubits as fully connected.
- [I] Exact minimum linear networks. Repository claim: `minimum_cnot_count` -- bidirectional breadth-first search over GL(width, 2) returns minima 10, 9, and 9 for multipliers 2, 4, and 16, below the Gauss-Jordan 10, 11, 12. Source item: Fig. 1 and the Patel-Markov-Hayes asymptotic bound it cites; the paper gives no exact minimum for any matrix. Model map: the search distance from the identity to A in the Cayley graph of CNOT generators is the optimal count over an all-to-all graph. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: exact optima, any width-5 value, or any search over a constrained graph.
- [I] Why a CNOT-count gain does not carry through the phase control. Repository claim: MODULAR_SYNTHESIS.md -- the residual alone costs 2,346 phase-controlled CX equivalents over ten rounds, 210 below the 2,556 target-flip baseline; the promoted linear Toffolis add 678 for 3,024; minimum-CNOT networks bound the composition at 2,892; and on FakeFez the challenger routes worse on every seed (median 6,130.5 routed CZ against 5,422, +13.1%, at least 565 CZ per seed). Source item: Sec. 2.1 (parity maps) and Sec. 4.2 (CNOT plus Rz). Model map: a CNOT under the phase control is a Toffoli, and the controlled network is no longer a parity map, since its action on the pair (phase bit, data) includes the product of the phase bit with a data bit. The surrogate weights price one data control at 6 CX, so the 113 Gauss-Jordan CNOTs over ten rounds (10 + 5 x 11 + 4 x 12) cost 678 and the 91 minimum CNOTs (10 + 5 x 9 + 4 x 9) cost 546. The 210 of headroom is 35 promoted CNOTs, so any linear part that needs 36 or more controlled CNOTs over the ten rounds loses at the surrogate level whatever synthesis produces them; the minimum is 91. Label: "new implementation or measurement". Not established by the source: controlled linear reversible circuits, Toffoli networks, cost under a control, or any benchmark on a heavy-hex graph or a CZ-native device.
- [I] Topology-aware reuse. Repository claim: MODULAR_SYNTHESIS.md, "Affine-residual challenger" -- the rejected challenger synthesizes A with no coupling graph and Qiskit routes the result on FakeFez; this bullet extends that claim. Source item: Defs. 2.1-2.2, steiner-down and steiner-up (Sec. 2.2), steiner-gauss-rec (Sec. 4.1), the genetic placement search (Sec. 2.2 end). Model map: whichever linear maps stay uncontrolled can be synthesized on the layout subgraph with every CNOT on a coupler, using steiner-gauss-rec where the seven-qubit layout has no Hamiltonian path (a property of the chosen layout, not checked here), and the genetic search chooses which logical qubit sits where. A topology-aware affine challenger would reuse exactly this for the uncontrolled pieces and would need a separate answer for the controlled ones. Label: "candidate new result". Not established by the source: Steiner synthesis under a control, depth or fidelity effects, results on a degree-three graph, or a comparison with a Qiskit-routed Toffoli network.
- [I] Conjugated form with uncontrolled linear parts. Repository claim: MODULAR_SYNTHESIS.md, which notes that conjugation (uncontrolled A^-1, controlled r o A, uncontrolled A) keeps the CNOTs uncontrolled and synthesizes the different residual r o A. Source item: Secs. 2.1-2.2 (the uncontrolled pieces). Model map: the controlled block for multiplier f is B C(B^-1 f B) B^-1 for any invertible linear B. An uncontrolled CNOT costs 1 CX and a controlled one costs 6 in the repository's surrogate. The operations between rounds (Hadamard, measurement, reset, conditional phase) touch only the control qubit, so work-register gates commute with them and the trailing B of one round merges with the leading B^-1 of the next into one GL(5,2) element per round boundary (with one B for every multiplier the boundary map is B^-1 B = identity, and the construction is a linear re-encoding of the work register); the leading B^-1 acts on the fixed input |1> and becomes X gates, and the trailing B of the last round acts on a register that nothing reads. With the multiplier sequence 4, 16, 4, 16, 4, 16, 4, 16, 4, 2 of the ten-round N = 21, a = 2 circuit this leaves nine boundary maps. Whether any B makes the surrogate of B^-1 f B plus the boundary CNOTs fall below the 2,556 baseline is not computed. Label: "candidate new result". Not established by the source: this decomposition, any cost for B^-1 f B, or any search over conjugators.
- [I] Scope of the benchmark. Repository claim: the N = 21 routing results are Qiskit preset-pass-manager results on FakeFez at optimization level 3. Source item: Sec. 3 and Table 1, random CNOT circuits on five architectures, gate counts only, against QuilC and tket. Model map: none; the paper compares different compilers on different circuits and metrics. Background; no label. The 48% and 36% figures do not predict savings for the repository's circuits.
