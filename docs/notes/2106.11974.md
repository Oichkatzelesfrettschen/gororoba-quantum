## What makes the basic model memoryless

[P] Sections 4.1-4.5, printed pages 11-16, assume a product initial state
`rho_S tensor eta_1 tensor eta_2 ...`, without initial system-bath or
interancilla correlations. Section 4.1.1 identifies three relevant conditions:
ancillas do not interact with each other, ancillas start uncorrelated, and each
ancilla meets the system once. Consecutive repeated interactions with one
ancilla can be regrouped as one collision; returning to that ancilla after
intervening collisions changes the model.

[P] Equation 4.7 defines the reduced collision map

```text
E(rho) = Tr_A[U (rho tensor eta) U^dagger].
```

For identical collision maps, the reduced state after `n` collisions is
`E^n(rho_0)`. Section 4.5 allows time-varying ancilla states or unitaries and
obtains stepwise completely positive trace-preserving maps. The homogeneous
semigroup and the inhomogeneous CP-divisible case are distinct assumptions.

[P] Section 4.3 explains that spent ancillas may remain correlated with the
system and each other. The factorization required for the next collision
concerns the unused incoming ancilla. Requiring every spent ancilla to become
uncorrelated would misstate the model.

[P] Chapter 8 explains mechanisms that alter those conditions: ancilla-ancilla
interactions in section 8.1, initial ancilla correlations in section 8.3, and
repeated nonconsecutive collisions with the same ancilla in section 8.4.
Section 8.3 includes classically correlated initial baths. A shared classical
random seed can therefore matter even when each ancilla marginal is correct.

[P] Section 5.8, printed pages 37-38, derives a continuous-time dissipator using
the singular scaling `g^2 Delta_t = gamma` as `Delta_t` tends to zero. The
first-order Hamiltonian term may diverge and requires centering or a
counterterm. A finite discrete circuit does not establish that limit by
choosing a small time step.

Reading scope: sections 4.1-4.5, 5.8, 8.1, 8.3, and 8.4, plus the title,
contents, and rights page. The complete 159-page review, its full trajectory
theory, and its cited derivations were not exhaustively audited.

## Circuit construction and model tests

[I] A fresh-ancilla implementation and a reset-and-reuse implementation can
realize the same ideal system law if reset is the replacement channel

```text
R_A(rho_SA) = Tr_A(rho_SA) tensor eta.
```

An actual reset instruction supplies a physical operation whose errors and
correlations require measurement. Resetting one accessible qubit does not
refresh the device's entire environment. Equality of system marginals under a
replacement channel is narrower than equality of joint records when bath
measurements, feedforward, or postselection are retained.

[I] `RY(theta)|0>` prepares a coherent pure state with off-diagonal density
matrix entries. A diagonal thermal mixture with the same populations requires
independently randomized state preparation, purification followed by discard,
or another validated mixed-state preparation. Reusing one randomized branch
across collisions creates a correlated bath unless a separate proof shows
that the chosen output task is insensitive to those correlations.

[I] A small fresh-versus-recycled-ancilla study can test reset, schedule, and
channel-model conformance. The experiment compares deliberately engineered
baths. It does not by itself establish that the uncontrolled processor bath
obeys the same collision model. Small qubit collision models also have direct
exact classical baselines.

## Candidate Rocq theorem stack

1. Define density matrices, partial trace, the collision unitary, and the
   replacement channel with explicit dimensions and tensor ordering.
2. Prove that the collision map is CPTP under density-state and unitary
   premises. Separate homogeneous `E^n` from time-varying compositions.
3. Prove fresh/reset-reused system-marginal equivalence by induction. State
   which registers are discarded. An outcome-indexed instrument is needed
   when the theorem concerns joint recorded bits.
4. Prove the density matrix produced by the selected mixed-state preparation.
   For randomized preparation, expose branch independence as a premise or
   prove it from the classical sampling mechanism.
5. Prove an error propagation inequality for imperfect reset and synthesis,
   using a norm with the required compositional property. Keep numerical
   values as externally certified enclosures until their bounds are proved.
6. If a continuous-time claim is required, formalize the scaling, centering,
   convergence, and remainder estimates separately. The finite circuit proof
   remains useful without claiming the limit.

[I] A hardware falsifier should compare a preregistered endpoint across
matched schedules and preparations, with uncertainty and layout effects
accounted for. A discrepancy rejects the specified model-plus-error envelope;
the discrepancy alone does not identify environmental memory as its cause.
