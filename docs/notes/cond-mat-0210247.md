## Results and a source inconsistency

[P] For transverse coupling `V=A*F*sigma_x`, equations (11), (13) and (14)
define the unsymmetrized spectrum and rates as

```text
S_F(omega) = integral exp(+i*omega*t) <F(t)F(0)> dt
Gamma_up   = (A/hbar)^2 S_F(-omega01)
Gamma_down = (A/hbar)^2 S_F(+omega01).
```

Negative frequency describes energy emitted by the noise source into the
qubit; positive frequency describes energy absorbed by the source from the
qubit. The sign belongs to the declared Fourier and operator-order convention.
Equation (15) adds `Gamma_down/Gamma_up=exp(beta*hbar*omega01)` only in
thermal equilibrium. Section 2 permits stationary nonequilibrium noise and
explicitly removes the general detailed-balance relation in that case.

[P] The first row of Table I, p. 15, reverses the spectral signs compared with
equations (13)-(14). Equation (43), on the same page as the table, again uses
positive frequency for downward relaxation. The implementation convention
must follow the explicit definition and derivation, also independently
confirmed by [Clerk et al.](0810.4729.md), equations (2.6a)-(2.6b), rather
than copying the inconsistent table row.

[P] The derivation assumes weak coupling, a stationary bath correlation
function and a short correlation time. Footnote 3, p. 4, identifies the
window `tau_c << t << 1/Gamma` for first-order probabilities. Its rates then
enter a master equation for longer-time relaxation. A narrow resonant bath,
strong coupling, history dependence or drift can invalidate the constant-rate
description even when an exponential fit looks plausible over a short window.

[P] Equations (38)-(41), pp. 13-14, give
`dp_e/dt=Gamma_up*(1-p_e)-Gamma_down*p_e` and
`1/T1=Gamma_up+Gamma_down`. The identification `1/T1=Gamma_down` uses the
zero-temperature limit `Gamma_up=0`. The passive zero-temperature reservoir
can absorb energy while being unable to supply excitation energy. Relaxation
therefore does not establish harvestable zero-point energy.

## Repository mapping and qualification

[I] A paired ground/excited preparation and delay family can fit the solution

```text
p_e(t) = p_inf + [p_e(0)-p_inf] exp(-Gamma_sum*t)
Gamma_sum = Gamma_up + Gamma_down
p_inf = Gamma_up/Gamma_sum.
```

The paired family needs preparation and readout constraints. Under a fixed
binary assignment model with `epsilon0=P(report 1|g)` and
`epsilon1=P(report 0|e)`, the observed probability is
`q(t)=epsilon0+(1-epsilon0-epsilon1)*p_e(t)`.
Unknown assignment errors and imperfect initial populations leave the
asymptotic physical population nonidentifiable from unconstrained curves.
Additional shots reduce sampling error while leaving that ambiguity intact.
