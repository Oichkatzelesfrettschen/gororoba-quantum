# open_gororoba survey for factoring and higher-dimensional circuit design

Scope: read-only survey of the open_gororoba repository at commit fe668020
(working tree: `.gitignore` modified and `.ignore` untracked before the survey
began; the survey changed nothing in the repository). Companion file with the
replayable scripts and the full evidence log:
`open_gororoba_survey_deep_notes.md` (same directory).

## 0. Findings first

1. No Shor, RSA, QFT-over-Z_Q, modular exponentiation, period finding, or
   quantum-circuit IR exists in the repository. Searches for Shor, RSA, order
   finding, modular exponentiation, phase estimation, discrete log, QASM,
   Toffoli, and qiskit return nothing relevant; "QFT" in the registry means
   quantum field theory. The quantum code is a tensor-network state-vector
   simulator, a Grover state-vector search, a hardware-profile model, and
   several modules that borrow the words "stabilizer" or "compiler" without
   the mathematics (section 6).
2. The repository's mathematical core is the one the sister project already
   ported: `cd_basis_mul_sign(dim, p, q)` at
   `crates/cd_kernel/src/cayley_dickson/signs.rs:30`. Its structure is a
   non-associative twisted group algebra of F_2^n. Section 7 reports
   independent computations: the twist fails the 2-cocycle identity exactly
   on the associator support; every non-identity `L_{e_p}` is an XOR
   translation times a diagonal sign whose Boolean degree is n-1; the group
   generated by all `L_{e_p}` has order 2^(n + 2^(n-1)) for every n >= 3
   (proof in left_mult_sign_rank.md); the
   sign function on two registers has (3^n + 1)/2 - 2^n + n ANF monomials for
   n = 1..11, yet the repository's own 4-case recursion unrolls into a phase
   circuit of n(n-1) controlled-swap gates plus 3n-2 multi-controlled-Z gates
   (gate-level simulation exhaustive for n <= 6; the recursion matches the
   sign table through n = 11).
3. Words in basis-unit multiplications permute indices by XOR translation only.
   `x -> a x mod N` fixes 0 and is not a translation unless a = 1. Section 9
   gives the proof and the list of what a modular-exponentiation circuit would
   need.
4. The only fast-factorization of the CD product that is proven in Rocq is the
   Cariow 2013 sedenion schedule (dim 16): 16-point Walsh-Hadamard
   diagonalization plus a 106-term sparse correction, 16 + 106 = 122 real
   multiplications (`proofs/verified/C1636_Cariow2013SedenionSchedule.v:183`,
   `crates/cd_kernel/src/cayley_dickson/cariow_factorization.rs:50-53`). The
   dim-32 count 498 and the dim-64 count 1494 are targets; no schedule exists.
5. Work at dimensions 32 through 1024 is substantial and mostly Rust-tested
   in an `#[ignore]`d heavy lane. Dimension 2048 has exact alternator counts,
   an ignored imbalance test with no recorded value, and Rocq scale-homogeneity
   lemmas. Dimension 4096 has the alternator count and Rocq scale lemmas.
   Neither has a zero-divisor census or box-kite count (section 3). A closed
   form that matches the repository's measured edge totals at dims 16..256
   predicts the totals at 512 through 4096 (section 3.1; conditional).
6. The phrase "Rocq at 512/1024/2048" is a name collision. The only Rocq files
   with those numbers in their names, `curated/01_theory_frameworks/confine_theorems_{512,1024,2048}*.v`,
   state capability-delegation facts about `Thread_0 ... Thread_N` and are not
   about Cayley-Dickson algebras (section 2.5).
7. The registry label `kernel_checked` (`docs/THEOREMS.md`, 162 of 162 files)
   covers finite arithmetic, counts of hand-written lists, and files with
   global axioms. Section 2 re-grades every CD-relevant file.
8. All 313 `.v` files contain zero `Admitted` and zero `admit`
   (comment-and-string-aware scan). Global axioms exist in three CD-adjacent
   files (C958, CDFidelity, SStructuralGaps; confirmed with `rocqchk -o`).

Closed form (C). The number of ordered basis triples (a,b,c) in [0,N)^3 at
which the sign 2-cocycle identity fails, equivalently the basis triples with a
non-zero associator, is v(N) = N^3/2 - 14N + 24; all values for N = 4..2048
were computed and match (168, 1848, 15960, 130200, 1046808, 8385048,
67101720, 536856600, 4294938648 at N = 8..2048). Brown's criterion
(`ZD_Criterion.v:375`, condition iii: the antiassociator vanishes) motivates a
correspondence between cross-pair zero products at dimension 2N and such
triples at dimension N, and in every dimension the repository measured, the total cross-pair edge count is
exactly v(D/2)/2:

    E(D) = D^3/32 - 7D/2 + 12  =  84, 924, 7980, 65100, 523404  at D = 16..256.

These five values equal the repository's totals (`tests.rs:518-860`; CSV row
counts through 128). Independent of the Rust census, the formula therefore
reproduces the dim-256 total that only an `#[ignore]`d test asserts. The
predicted totals for 512 through 4096 in the table are extrapolations of the
same identity, not measurements, and the identity is verified at five
dimensions, not proved. Per component, the emanation-table DMZ count of each
regime equals twice the edge count of the matching motif class at dims 16..128
(for example dim 64: 840, 456, 168, 552 against 420, 228, 84, 276; dim 128:
360, 1032, ... , 3720 against 180, 516, ... , 1860), so the two Rust test
families describe the same objects.

Scaling laws asserted by tests over 16..128 (`tests.rs:892-947`, Ti): components
dim/2 - 1; nodes per component dim/2 - 2; K_{2,...,2} components dim/4 - 1 parts
at most; motif classes dim/16; K_2-type component count 3 + log2(dim).
`test_no_octahedra_beyond_16d` (T, `tests.rs:968`) shows no octahedron at 32, 64, 128.
Note e_max(512) = 32004 = C958's `zd_edges_at_256`. The largest component at
dim D has C(D/2 - 2, 2) - (D/4 - 1) edges, which equals the full
basis-participation formula (d^2 - 6d + 8)/2 at d = D/2 for D = 32..512
(84, 420, 1860, 7812, 32004; C, section 7).

## 9. Candidate connections to factoring

### 9.1 What the repository's structures are

Let G = F_2^n and A_n = R[G] with basis `e_p` and product
`e_p e_q = s(p,q) e_{p XOR q}`, where `s` is the recursion at `signs.rs:30`.

- Group-algebra part. With s identically 1 the algebra is the group algebra
  R[F_2^n]; its multiplication is XOR convolution, and the Walsh-Hadamard
  transform (the Fourier transform over F_2^n, all twiddles +-1) diagonalizes it.
  Cariow's schedule exploits exactly this and corrects the sign twist with a
  sparse term.
- Twist. `s` is not a group 2-cocycle for n >= 3 (section 7, item 3); the
  twisted algebra is a quasialgebra: associativity fails exactly where the
  cocycle defect is non-zero. Albuquerque and Majid, "Quasialgebra structure of
  the octonions" (arXiv:math/9802116, DAMTP 97-138), obtain the octonions as a
  twisting of the group algebra of Z_2 x Z_2 x Z_2 with a 3-cocycle and list
  higher 2^n-onion Cayley algebras among their examples; the abstract does not
  state the Z_2^n result for n > 3, and the journal reference was not
  verified here. The repository cites the preprint at
  `registry/external_sources.toml:2276`.
- Gates. `L_{e_p} = P_p D_p`, with `P_p` the permutation `q -> p XOR q` and `D_p`
  the diagonal `diag(s(p,.))`. `L_{e_p}` is orthogonal at every n tested. On n
  qubits `P_p` is a layer of X gates on the bits of p, and `D_p` is a phase
  polynomial of degree n-1, i.e. a multi-controlled-Z with up to n-2
  controls. Octonion rows (n = 3, degree 2) are Clifford (CZ-type); every
  non-identity sedenion row has degree 3 (CCZ-type, non-Clifford). A
  bilinear twist, as in Pauli strings and Clifford algebras, has degree 1 per
  row; the repository's own C-1511 (`registry/claims.toml:4386`) reports that the
  bilinear sign `(-1)^{popcount(p AND q)}` matches `cd_sign` on 21 of 42 octonion
  products.
- Two-register form. `U |p>|q> = s(p,q) |p>|p XOR q>` is the joint phase
  followed by a CNOT fan from the p register into the q register. The phase
  has ANF degree n and `(3^n + 1)/2 - 2^n + n` monomials (Theta(dim^1.585),
  section 7 item 5), but the fold recursion (section 7 item 10) implements it
  with n(n-1) controlled-swaps and 3n-2 multi-controlled-Z gates, so the
  circuit is polynomial in n = log2(dim). `U` computes products of basis
  elements and leaves the p register as garbage. The algebra product of two
  superposed vectors `sum_r (sum_p psi_p phi_{p XOR r} s(p, p XOR r)) |r>` is a
  bilinear contraction, not a unitary; it needs a linear-combination-of-
  unitaries step with post-selection or a norm-dependent success probability.
- Closure. Products of basis-unit left multiplications have index action in
  the translation group F_2^n and generate a group of order 2^(n + 2^(n-1))
  (every n >= 3, left_mult_sign_rank.md), a 2-group of monomial matrices; the
  right multiplications
  generate the analogous group. Words mixing left and right multiplications
  keep the F_2^n index action but generate a larger group: 32 at dim 4, 1024 at
  dim 8, 524288 at dim 16 by closure. The map
  `p -> L_{e_p}` is not a representation for n >= 3 (cocycle defect), so the
  non-associativity of the algebra cannot be carried by gate composition:
  a circuit multiplies matrices, which is associative.
- General elements. `L_a = sum_p a_p L_{e_p}` has entries
  `a_{i XOR j} s(i XOR j, j)` (`gpu/tensor_avt/mod.rs:6`). For unit a it is
  orthogonal when n <= 3 (norm is multiplicative there). At n >= 4 the norm is
  not multiplicative and zero divisors give singular `L_a`, so `L_a` is not a
  gate in general; use as a gate needs block encoding with a normalization
  factor (`sum |a_p|`) or post-selection.

### 9.2 What modular exponentiation requires

Shor's algorithm needs (R1) a unitary `U_a |x> = |a x mod N>` for x < N,
identity otherwise, controlled on exponent bits with the constants
`a^(2^j) mod N`; (R2) the family must compose as exponents add, i.e. be a
homomorphism from Z into permutations of Z_N; (R3) a quantum Fourier transform
over Z_Q with controlled-phase angles 2 pi / 2^k, or its semiclassical
equivalent, then classical continued fractions and gcd; (R4) a register of
about log2 N qubits for the target plus work space, so about 2048 plus
ancillas for RSA-2048. The reversible multiplier mod N is carry arithmetic
(Toffoli-class). The permutation `x -> a x mod N` has order ord_N(a), which
divides lambda(N) and is in general not a power of 2.

### 9.3 Plausible candidates

1. Circuit synthesis for the CD product itself (the sister project's object).
   The fold recursion gives an exact, polynomial-size circuit for the basis
   product at every dimension: n(n-1) controlled-swaps, 3n-2 multi-controlled-Z
   gates of width at most n, and n CNOTs, on 2n qubits (dim 4096: n = 12, 132
   controlled-swaps and 34 multi-controlled-Z gates). `U` is the SELECT
   operation of a linear-combination-of-unitaries block encoding of `L_a`. For
   a classical p, `L_{e_p} = X^p D_p` has a phase polynomial of degree n-1 with
   the row monomial counts of section 7 item 4. The product is non-Clifford from
   n = 4 (CCZ-type rows). Value: an exact resource table up to dimension
   2^2048 for the basis product, and a concrete use of the repository's
   "doubling" recursion as a circuit generator. Relation to Shor: none.
2. Cariow-style diagonalization as a circuit pattern. The dense XOR-convolution
   part of `L_a` is `H^{(x)n} diag(WHT(a)) H^{(x)n}`; the classical butterfly
   with n stages costs depth 1 on n qubits. The sign correction is the sparse
   term (106 products at n = 4; no schedule beyond n = 4). Relation to Shor:
   the same Hadamard layer prepares the uniform superposition over the exponent
   register; nothing more.
3. Hidden-subgroup experiments over F_2^n (Simon-type problems) using the WHT
   and the repository's MPS simulator as a harness. These are genuine uses of
   the Walsh-Hadamard transform; they do not solve order finding.
4. Compiler-test material: the 16 to 2048 families of monomial unitaries with
   known gate counts (section 7) are a benchmark for phase-polynomial and
   multi-controlled-Z synthesis. This concerns compiler quality, not factoring.
5. Structured error-detection ideas from the strut-space projective geometry
   (PG(n-1,2) point sets, Hjelmslev nets, C881 separating degree 3). The
   existing `qec_boxkite.rs` is not an instance; a real code would need
   commuting Pauli generators and a proven distance.

### 9.4 Category errors

1. Realizing `x -> a x mod N` with basis-unit multiplications. Every word in
   `L_{e_p}` or `R_{e_p}` maps index q to q XOR v for a fixed v. The map
   `x -> a x mod N` fixes the encoded residue 0 (and, for N < 2^m, every unused
   index), while an XOR translation by v != 0 has no fixed point; so v = 0, the
   index action is the identity, and the map is the identity only for a = 1.
   The argument does not depend on how residues are encoded as indices.
   Order gives a second obstruction: the generated group is a 2-group, while
   ord_N(a) is in general not a power of 2. A single algebra element does not
   repair this: column j of `L_a = sum_p a_p L_{e_p}` has |supp(a)| non-zero
   entries at rows j XOR p, so a permutation matrix needs |supp(a)| = 1.
   Linear combinations of products of basis multiplications are a different
   matter: their real span is every matrix at n = 3, 4, 5
   (docs/design/factoring-notes.md), so a linear combination of unitaries
   can represent a multiplier there. Any nonzero exact scaled representation
   over signed XOR translations has effective normalization
   `lambda/|beta| >= T`, where T counts the distinct index displacements.
   Standard LCU post-selection is therefore at most `1/T^2`. Base two has
   `T >= (N+1)/2` for every odd N and exactly `2^(n-1)` for `N = 2^n-1`; the
   base-two bound proves an exponential dictionary mismatch for the base-two
   family. The family `N=3(2^m+1)`, `a=2(2^m+1)+1` has only linear T, so no
   universal exponential-displacement statement covers every base. Its even-`m`
   subfamily has order two and yields the factors `2^m+1` and 3 through standard
   Shor post-processing; its odd-`m` subfamily has order three and does not.
   Section 8.12 of the deep notes gives both derivations.
2. Replacing the Z_Q quantum Fourier transform by the Walsh-Hadamard transform
   or by a "butterfly fold". Both have the same butterfly topology and
   different groups (F_2^m versus Z_{2^m}); the Walsh-Hadamard transform has
   no twiddle phases and encodes Simon-type structure. Period finding for
   a^x mod N needs the cyclic group.
3. Equating CD dimension with RSA bit length. Dimension 2048 means an index
   register of 11 qubits. A register holding a 2048-bit modulus would be
   n = 2048 index qubits, i.e. dimension 2^2048; the basis-product circuit
   would still be polynomial (n(n-1), about 4.2 million controlled-swaps), but
   it implements signed XOR translation, not arithmetic mod N. No dimension in
   the 32..4096 range encodes a 2048-bit integer.
4. Chunking modular multiplication by zero-divisor structure. The graph is fixed
   by dyadic XOR structure and is independent of N. The ring-theoretic remark
   that Z_6 has zero divisors (`DE_MARRAIS_PRESTO_DIGITIZATION.md`) is
   rhetoric and no map exists from divisors of N to CD zero divisors.
5. Using non-associativity as a compilation resource. A gate sequence is an
   ordinary matrix product, which is associative; `sedenionic_braid_compiler.rs`
   changes bracketing of a vector product, which is not a circuit.
6. Treating `padic_cd_crypto.rs`, `qec_boxkite.rs`, and
   `sedenionic_braid_compiler.rs` as implementations. They are stubs or
   analogies (section 6).
7. Treating the WHT "preserves the CD associator" axiom (`CDFidelity.v:105`) as
   a theorem. It is not even a statement about the algebra.

### 9.5 What would be needed for a genuine bridge

An explicit embedding of Z_N (or of the exponent group) into algebra data in
which multiplication mod N becomes a CD product, with a homomorphism property.
The cocycle defect shows that the full index-basis map p -> L_{e_p} is no
homomorphism at n >= 3. It does not rule out an embedding whose image lies in
an associative subalgebra: every CD algebra contains a complex subalgebra, into
which a cyclic group embeds by roots of unity. Such an embedding supplies no
efficient modular arithmetic, and the XOR-translation argument of 9.4 item 1
is what rules out basis multiplications as multipliers. A
repository-independent route to a "maximally decomposed" Shor circuit is
arithmetic circuit engineering (adders, windowed lookups, QFT over Z_Q); the
CD material above supplies none of those components.

## 10. Top ten findings by relevance

1. The repository's sign recursion unrolls into a polynomial-size circuit:
   the basis product `|p>|q> -> s(p,q) |p>|p XOR q>` costs n(n-1)
   controlled-swaps, 3n-2 multi-controlled-Z gates, and n CNOTs, verified
   exhaustively to n = 6 (computed). The ANF monomial count
   `(3^n + 1)/2 - 2^n + n` is the expanded size, not the circuit size.
2. `L_{e_p} = X^p D_p` with `D_p` of Boolean degree n-1 for every non-identity
   p and every n tested (n = 3..11); non-Clifford from n = 4 (computed).
3. Words in basis products have index action in F_2^n; the generated group has
   order 2^(n + 2^(n-1)) for every n >= 3 (proved in left_mult_sign_rank.md,
   computed through n = 11); `x -> a x mod N` is out of
   reach of any such word, in any residue encoding.
4. The one proven fast factorization is Cariow 2013 at dim 16: 122 multiplies,
   Walsh-Hadamard diagonalization plus a sparse correction (`C1636`,
   `cariow_factorization.rs`); 498 and 1494 are unachieved targets.
5. Closed forms (computed): ordered non-associative basis triples
   v(N) = N^3/2 - 14N + 24 for N = 4..2048, and the cross-pair zero-divisor edge
   total E(D) = v(D/2)/2 = D^3/32 - 7D/2 + 12, which reproduces the repository's
   totals at D = 16..256 (including the dim-256 value that only an ignored test
   asserts) and predicts 4192524, 33550860, 268428300, 2147469324 at
   D = 512, 1024, 2048, 4096 -- the only numbers available for 2048 and 4096
   zero-divisor structure.
6. The formal recursion: `signs.rs:30`, `CDSignHalfStep.v:18-70` (the only
   dimension-generic sign lemmas), `CompressedSignTable`, Theorem 11 table
   embedding, and the GF(2) fold of item 1.
7. The cocycle defect is the associator support (168, 1848, 15960, 130200 at
   dims 8..64), agreeing with `M3IsAssociator.v` and `C1467`; Brown's criterion
   condition (iii) is the bridge to zero-divisor edges.
8. Zero-divisor chunk counts by dimension: dim/2 - 1 components of dim/2 - 2
   nodes (cross-pair), PG(n-1,2) strut spaces, regime counts 1, 2, 4, 8, edge
   totals 84, 924, 7980, 65100, 523404 (Ti above 32; CSV through 128).
9. Rocq calibration: zero admits, three global-axiom files, `kernel_checked`
