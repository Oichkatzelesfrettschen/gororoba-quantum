## Main claims and evidence

- [P] Theorem 1.4 (matrix Bernstein). For independent random self-adjoint d x d matrices X_k with E X_k = 0 and lambda_max(X_k) <= R almost surely, and sigma^2 = ||sum_k E X_k^2||, P{lambda_max(sum_k X_k) >= t} <= d exp(-(t^2/2) / (sigma^2 + R t / 3)) for all t >= 0.
- [P] Theorem 6.1 states the chain: a Bennett form d exp(-(sigma^2/R^2) h(R t / sigma^2)) with h(u) = (1 + u) log(1 + u) - u, the Bernstein form above, and a split form exp(-3t^2/(8 sigma^2)) for t <= sigma^2/R and exp(-3t/(8R)) beyond. It places no assumption on the minimum eigenvalues of the summands.
- [P] Theorem 1.6 extends Bernstein to rectangular d1 x d2 summands with ||Z_k|| <= R and the factor d1 + d2.
- [P] The companion bounds are matrix Chernoff (1.1), Gaussian and Rademacher series (1.2) and Hoeffding (1.3); Section 7 treats matrix martingales.

## Mapping to this repository

Relevance: (a) medium, (b) none, (c) none.

- [I] `bdsc.bernstein_radius` is Theorem 1.4 solved for t. Each trial of setting s contributes X = D_s (B - p_s) / n_s with B a Bernoulli outcome, so ||X|| <= ||D_s|| / n_s =: R and E X^2 = p_s (1 - p_s) D_s^2 / n_s^2 <= D_s^2 / (4 n_s^2); n_s trials give sigma^2 = ||sum_s D_s^2 / (4 n_s)||. Applying the bound to X and -X doubles the dimension factor, and setting 2m exp(-(t^2/2)/(sigma^2 + R t/3)) = alpha gives t = R h/3 + sqrt((R h/3)^2 + 2 sigma^2 h) with h = log(2m/alpha), the function's docstring.
- [I] The handoff's complement radii (0.409805 to 0.015689 at 1500 to 10^6 trials) reproduce at alpha = 0.0125 with m = 12 ([dark-subspace certificate](quandela/bdsc.md), Preparation bank and estimator).
- [I] The bound needs independent bounded summands with known mean zero, the `bernoulli_sampling` premise of an acquisition record; converted counts from Perceval's probs_to_sample_count violate it (`docs/evidence/bdsc/packages/packages.json`).
