## Mechanism and evidence

- [P] Figure 1 compares adaptive iterative phase estimation (IPE) with
  nonadaptive Kitaev QPE. IPE reuses a pointer qubit, measures and resets it
  between iterations, and chooses each later Z rotation from earlier measured
  bits. Kitaev's method measures separate sine and cosine circuits for each
  power of the unitary, then reconstructs the phase through classical
  post-processing (main pages 2-3; supplement, "Kitaev Estimator").
- [P] The experiments use two transmon qubits from a 14-qubit processor,
  with FPGA control implementing conditional reset and algorithmic
  feed-forward. Both protocols run up to ten phase bits for 600 phases
  chosen randomly from `[-pi, pi)` (main pages 3 and 5).
- [P] The paper defines one sampling resource as a physical qubit measurement.
  For resource allowance `R` and bit count `m`, each Kitaev circuit receives
  `floor(R / (2*m))` samples and the IPE circuit receives `floor(R / m)`
  samples (main page 5). Figure 4(b) compares allowances 50, 70 and 200.
- [P] Figure 4(c) selects each protocol's lowest error over its available bit
  counts for a given resource allowance. The authors report consistently lower
  IPE errors below approximately 100 resources and comparable performance as
  the allowance increases (main pages 5-6). At fixed allowance, increasing the
  bit count can degrade performance as samples per bit fall; IPE also becomes
  deeper while the individual Kitaev circuits retain constant depth.
- [P] The controlled powers are implemented directly as parameterized
  single-qubit rotations and two CNOTs per controlled power, instead of by
  repeating the base controlled unitary. The paper assumes efficient access
  to those powers within the comparison (main pages 3 and 5; Figure 4(a)).
- [P] Two measurement-and-reset cycles give a total reset latency of 1.4 us.
  The phase-branch determination occurs within that interval. Figure 3(b)
  varies coherence and reset latency in simulations at six bits and
  allowances 50 and 100, using fixed pointer measurement and reset errors
  0.01 and 0.03 (main pages 4 and 6). The reported advantage is conditional
  on the noise, latency and resource regime.
- [P] The selected IPE estimator averages the two most likely consecutive
  output bitstrings with their observed weights. The authors compare four
  estimator choices; supplement Figure S5(a) shows a five-bit noiseless
  comparison. The Kitaev estimator produces two additional bits from its
  terminal octant estimate, which Figure 4's caption explicitly notes
  (main page 5; supplement pages 9-11).
- [P] The supplementary device characterization interleaves relaxation and
  coherence probes with phase-estimation experiments to track drift. The
  supplement also separately characterizes readout discrimination, resonator
  population, controller latency and repeated-reset error. Those measurements
  support specific hardware mechanisms beyond an algorithm-output histogram.

## Relevance and limits

| Axis | Relevance | Bounded use |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptive/nonadaptive superconducting QPE | high | Direct experimental prior art for the broad comparison and its noise/latency tradeoff. |
| Folded-QPE control design | high | Motivate explicit phase laws, reset/feedback controls, physical timing and estimator definitions. |
| Charged QPU seconds | indirect | The paper's measurement allowance differs from provider billing and shared-shot acquisition cost. |
| Cayley-Dickson algebra and ripple visuals | indirect | Supply experimental-control principles; the paper establishes no Cayley-Dickson identity or zero-divisor construction. |
| General factoring advantage | limited | Directly synthesized unitary powers and small known-phase instances leave generic modular arithmetic and end-to-end factoring cost outside the experiment. |

[I] A claim that comparing adaptive and nonadaptive QPE on superconducting
hardware is new would conflict with this primary source. The repository's
folded-QPE ladder can contribute specific reproducible observations and tests;
the existence of an adaptive/nonadaptive comparison supplies no general novelty
claim. A bounded reading of one paper also leaves exhaustive priority research
open.

[I] The paper's nonadaptive comparator is Kitaev's sine/cosine sampling method.
It is neither a fully coherent inverse-QFT circuit nor an IPE circuit with its
feed-forward removed. Keep those three controls distinct when interpreting the
repository's coherent and no-feed-forward arms. A result against one comparator
does not establish the same ordering against another.

[I] A sampling-resource advantage also leaves charged-time savings unmeasured.
Provider repetition delay, runtime overhead, dynamic scheduling, compiled
duration and spatial concurrency can change seconds per useful estimate.
The repository's raw job metrics and circuit manifests must supply that evidence.

[I] The [folded-job audit](https://github.com/Oichkatzelesfrettschen/gororoba-quantum/blob/ed7fefc1ddae89e3778351868b9b2278b714240c/results/ripple/folded-job-review.md) compares
specific routed arms and tests a restricted common-binary-channel explanation
of model residuals. Its different readout qubits, entangling-gate counts and
execution windows bound causal attribution. The paper's latency analysis
motivates controller hypotheses; citing it does not identify the cause of the
repository's observed residuals.

## Action gates for the repository

1. Cite this paper when presenting folded-QPE results. State the phase labels,
   exact comparator, estimator, routed circuits, physical qubits and execution
   periods beside any reported advantage or loss.
2. Preserve original raw measurements, the preregistered analysis and later
   reanalyses with their own provenance. Report a change in estimator separately
   from a change in hardware performance.
3. Compare measured seconds at a declared useful-output precision. Keep circuit
   count, measurement count, physical duration and billed usage as separate
   quantities; shared-shot packing requires its own dependence and quality checks.
4. Test proposed reset, latency, readout and drift mechanisms through explicit
   interventions or matched controls. A hardware/model discrepancy establishes
   a failed model prediction within its assumptions before it establishes a
   particular physical explanation.

[I] The defensible contribution is an auditable workload-specific comparison,
including controls, source-bound raw evidence, noise-model falsifiers and
measured timing. Any stronger novelty or causal claim requires additional
evidence beyond this note and the retained folded-QPE run.
