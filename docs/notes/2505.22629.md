## Main claims

[P] Learning SPAM and gate noise jointly makes error mitigation unbiased whatever gauge is chosen, and choosing the gauge well cuts the sampling cost by an order of magnitude.

- [P] Pauli noise models learned from SPAM-robust experiments contain gauge degrees of freedom: combinations of gate and SPAM parameters no experiment can fix. Earlier mitigation removed them by two assumptions, equal Pauli eigenvalues for conjugate pairs (for example lambda_XI = lambda_XX for CNOT) and ideal state preparation. The paper calls the resulting model "inconsistent" and shows it biases mitigated values.
- [P] Theorem 1: probabilistic error cancellation (PEC) run with any learned model gauge-equivalent to the true noise gives an unbiased estimator for every circuit and observable. The gauge does not change observables. It does change the sampling overhead.
- [P] PEC overhead: gamma = exp(sum over generators of max(0, tau_a)) per layer (Eq. 15); the shot cost is the product of gamma^2 over noise channels. Overheads below are gamma; Fig. 8b plots gamma^2.
- [P] For quasi-local (sparse Pauli-Lindblad) models, the gauge reduces to n single-qubit depolarizing maps: 28 n parameters for a ring with two gate layers, of which 27 n are learnable and n are gauge.
- [P] Gauge optimization is a second-order cone program, solved with CVXPY: minimize the sum of max(tau, 0) subject to ||F' r - b||_2 <= epsilon (Eq. 16), with epsilon set from bootstrapped shot noise of the learning data (Appendix D).
- [P] Experiments (Clifford targets, so noisy predictions are exact):
  - Two qubits on ibm_auckland: predicted bias at odd CNOT depths falls from 3.2% +/- 0.4% (inconsistent) to 0.5% +/- 0.3% (self-consistent), averaged over 16 odd depths to 31 and reproduced on six qubit pairs. Even depths show no difference.
  - GHZ states up to 21 qubits on ibm_strasbourg (127 qubits): bias of the full-weight stabilizer X^n reaches 35.2% +/- 6.5% under the inconsistent model and -1.2% +/- 4.1% under the self-consistent model.
  - A ring of 92 qubits on ibm_strasbourg, weight-1 Z observables: median mitigation error 4.9% (inconsistent) to 3.1% (self-consistent); one observable 12% +/- 0.5% against 0.3% +/- 0.5%.
- [P] PEC on ibm_fez, 20 qubits, non-Clifford single-qubit angles (-pi/3 and 2 pi/3), open chain with 10 layers and 190 CNOTs:

  | Model | gamma | Median error |
  |-------|-------|--------------|
  | unmitigated | -- | 21.8% |
  | inconsistent | 6.74 | 5.6% |
  | self-consistent, default gauge | 48.68 | large error bars |
  | self-consistent, gauge-optimized | 3.7 | 2.4% |

  Each model used 100,000 randomized circuit instances with one shot each, quasi-probabilistic sampling plus Pauli twirling. Readout error was corrected by division by readout fidelities in the manner of TREX. A leakage-detecting post-selection kept about 18% of shots.
- [I] In sampling cost the three gammas are 45.4, 2370 and 13.7 (squares).
- [P] The remaining gauge-optimized error is consistent with shot noise; the inconsistent model sits above the shot-noise floor (Fig. 8c).
- [P] Limits: temporal drift between learning and target circuits, two-level-system fluctuations, leakage, non-nearest-neighbor correlated noise and coherent crosstalk lie outside the model. Mitigation bias from such terms scales roughly as 4 N lambda for N terms of strength lambda (ref. [47]). Self-consistent learning relies on depth-1 circuits and is the more exposed to TLS outliers. Mid-circuit measurements are left to future work.

## Methods

[P] The method learns one linear system for all log Pauli fidelities, then reads off the model in a gauge of choice.

- [P] Gate set: state preparation (generalized depolarizing channel), computational-basis measurement (generalized depolarizing channel), Clifford entangling layers (Pauli channels), and single-qubit gates assumed noiseless. Randomized compiling (Pauli twirling) enforces the Pauli form.
- [P] Each experiment prepares a Pauli eigenstate, applies d entangling layers and measures a Pauli observable. Then -log of the expectation value is a sum of log Pauli fidelities along a cycle of the pattern transfer graph, giving b = F x (Eq. 9) with design matrix F.
- [P] F reaches maximal rank, null space equal to the gauge dimension, once depth-0 (SPAM), depth-1 and multi-layer even-depth experiments are all included. Even depths alone fix only the products of conjugate eigenvalues.
- [P] Quasi-local ansatz: noise generators supported on nearest-neighbor Paulis (Eq. 10-12); only local expectation values are needed, so the number of measurement settings does not grow with system size.
- [P] Circuit counts for a ring (Appendix B 2): 1 SPAM circuit, 17 depth-1 circuits per template layer, 9 circuits per template layer per even-depth value. For 92 qubits with two layers and depths 4, 12, 24 the design matrix is 9108 x 2576.
- [P] Shot settings (Table III):

  | Experiment | Qubits | Twirls | Shots per twirl | Even depths | Design matrix |
  |------------|--------|--------|-----------------|-------------|---------------|
  | single CNOT | 2 | 250 | 200 | 2 to 32 | 9 x 9 |
  | GHZ preparation | 21 | 100 | 256 | 2, 4, 8 | 56 x 46 |
  | ring circuit | 92 | 100 | 150 | 4, 12, 24 | 9108 x 2576 |
  | non-Clifford PEC | 20 | 128 learning, 100,000 PEC | 64 learning, 1 PEC | 4, 10, 20, 40 | 2349 x 521 |

- [P] Each shot lasts approximately 1 ms.
- [P] GHZ preparation used only two dense CNOT layers ("a" and "b"), synthesized with a SAT solver and weighted Steiner trees, so that only two layers needed learning. The 21-qubit case needed 29 learning experiments for 56 observables and 46 fidelity parameters, interleaved with target runs seven times over 18 hours immediately after calibration.
- [P] Self-consistent zero-noise extrapolation follows from the same model (Appendix A 1).
- [P] Design-matrix code is provided in the companion paper (ref. [21]).

## Relevance

(a) Calibration-aware or noise-adaptive compilation on IBM hardware: high. [I]
- [P] The PEC demonstration ran on ibm_fez, a backend the project already uses. It supplies a worked noise-learning, gauge-optimization, PEC pipeline with measured shot costs and a measured accuracy gain, 21.8% to 2.4%.
- [I] The pipeline learns a model of the specific CZ layers executed, which is noise-adaptive compilation input in the strict sense: a compiler could score candidate layouts and layer structures by their learned gamma.

(b) Cayley-Dickson, octonion, zero-divisor, nonassociative structure: none. [I]
- [P] No occurrence of the terms in the text (grep; the only hit is the surname Jordan in a reference).
- [I] Pauli labels form F_2^(2n) with a symplectic form, and the project's Cayley-Dickson products index basis units by XOR (e_p e_q = sign e_(p xor q), per DESIGN_NOTES). The paper builds nothing on that resemblance. It matters only practically: Cayley-Dickson unitaries transpile to CZ layers with single-qubit rotations, which is exactly the gate-set form the paper models.

(c) Shor's algorithm and factoring resource estimates: low. [I]
- [I] PEC cost compounds exponentially in gate count. At the paper's measured rate, ln(gamma^2) = ln(13.7) = 2.6 per 190 CNOTs, 1000 CNOTs cost about 1e6 times more shots. This puts any 1e9-Toffoli factoring run (note 2505.15917) far beyond error mitigation; error correction is the only route.

(d) Concrete action items for gororoba-quantum:
1. Count the distinct CZ layers (as edge sets) in the routed Design A (16 CZ) and Design B (224 CZ) circuits. The constant-circuit-count result holds for two template layers; learning cost grows with the number of distinct layers. Acceptance: three or fewer distinct layers means learning is affordable; more means recompile into alternating dense layers first. Source: Sec. IV B, Appendix B 2.
2. Check the learning budget against the 10-minute monthly QPU allowance in DESIGN_NOTES before any run. At about 1 ms per shot, the paper's own settings cost: two-qubit learning about 18 circuits x 50,000 shots = 900 s; 21-qubit GHZ learning 29 x 25,600 = 742 s; 92-qubit ring 89 x 15,000 = 1335 s; PEC itself 100,000 shots, 100 s per model (all [I], from Table III and Appendix B 2). Scale twirls x shots down (for example 50 x 100 = 5000 shots per circuit) and compute the resulting error with the paper's bootstrap (Appendix D 2) first. Source: Table III.
3. Include a depth-1 learning circuit whenever a CZ layer is calibrated. Omitting it reproduces the 3.2% odd-depth bias of the inconsistent model. Source: Sec. IV A, Fig. 5.
4. Compute gamma = exp(sum max(0, tau)) from the learned model before committing PEC shots to Design B. The paper's 190-CNOT, 20-qubit circuit needed gamma = 3.7 after gauge optimization and 100,000 instances; if Design B's gamma is comparable, note 2507.08088's ODR is the cheaper alternative. Source: Eq. 15, Fig. 8b.
5. Solve the gauge-optimization SOCP with CVXPY and epsilon from bootstrapped shot noise whenever PEC is used; the default gauge raised gamma 13x (48.68 against 3.7) with identical predicted observables. Source: Sec. III B, Appendix D.
6. Use the project's known ideal outcomes as the bias test, as the paper uses Clifford-exact targets: Design A ideal P1 = 1 and Design B ideal P_zd = 0, P_control = 1. Report bias as measured over model-predicted noisy value, with learning and target runs interleaved inside one calibration window. Source: Sec. IV B, Appendix C 2.
7. Treat the leakage post-selection (about 18% of shots retained) as unavailable: the PDF defers its definition to a manuscript in preparation (ref. [51]) and a keynote. Do not budget shots on that retention rate. Source: Sec. IV D.

## Gaps and cautions

- [P] The learning protocol targets Clifford entangling layers, and the hardware runs describe CNOT layers.
- [I] Heron's native two-qubit gate is CZ (Table II of note 2507.08088) and is Clifford. The paper's text does not discuss CZ-native transpilation, so the mapping of its layers to CZ is untested here.
- [P] Non-Markovian noise, including negative generator rates after twirling, is modeled but not diagnosed beyond the shot-noise-floor comparison.
