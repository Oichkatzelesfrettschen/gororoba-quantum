## Statements and hypotheses

- [P] The graph `Gamma_e(A_(n+1))` restricts vertices to projective classes
  represented by `(e_i,+/-e_j)` with `i!=0`, among the zero divisors of
  that form. The algebra has dimension `2^(n+1)`. Its support restriction
  belongs to the definition, even when the ambient algebra has arbitrary
  real coefficients.
- [P] Theorem 4.6 constructs component graphs recursively, using altered
  lower-stage doubling parameters described in Notation 3.18. Its figure
  distinguishes adjacency tests in `A_n^circle` and `A_n^bullet`.
- [P] Corollary 4.9 gives `2^n` connected components when the final parameter
  is `+1`, and `2^n-1` when it is `-1`, for `n>=3`. Each component has
  diameter three. A component has `2^(n+1)-2` vertices when its `chi` is
  `+1`, and `2^(n+1)-4` when `chi=-1`.
- [P] Theorem 5.12 reconstructs the two lower-stage graphs from an unlabeled
  graph for `n>=4`. Theorem 5.15 recovers doubling parameters indexed 3
  through `n`; the first three are recovered up to the isomorphism class
  of the dimension-eight algebra. Corollary 5.16 and Theorem 5.17 give the
  equivalence of graph isomorphism and algebra isomorphism for `n>=3`.
- [P] Section 6 expands the support class to all `e_i+/-e_j` with distinct
  imaginary basis indices. Same-half supports add recursively inherited
  graph parts. The section explains why a directed zero-product graph is
  also needed for certain split-algebra reductions.

## Census reconciliation and further use

[I] In the repository's positive-definite dimension-32 algebra, the final
parameter is `-1`, `n=4`, and `chi=-1`. Corollary 4.9 therefore gives 15
components of 28 projective vertices, totaling 420 vertices, for the
cross-half graph `Gamma_e`. The result counts a support-defined graph.

[I] The repository's [930-vector census](cayley_dickson_sparse_census.md)
uses all two-term imaginary supports and both relative signs. Its 588
zero-divisor representatives include same-half supports. Consequently, its
588 count and the paper's 420 cross-half count refer to different sets.
An exact bridge checks the cross-half restriction, quotient convention, and
adjacency relation before comparing these numbers. The numerical deduction
above follows from the source's formula; this note adds no new executed
adjacency classification.

[I] The recursion suggests a reusable corpus reduction: retain a representative
with an explicit witness map for each certified support-graph equivalence,
then replay the exact multiplication relation after expanding the map. Such
a reduction can shorten classical circuit-candidate generation. A graph
isomorphism alone need not induce a unitary circuit equivalence, preserve
physical routing cost, or preserve a kernel bundle transition map; those
additional identities require separate certificates.
