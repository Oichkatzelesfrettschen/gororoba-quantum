## Main claims

[P] Polynomial function graph problems over a finite field are hidden subgroup problems in metabelian function graph groups, solvable in quantum polynomial time for constant degree and constant number of variables over every finite field, and the general hidden symmetry subgroup problem contains Grover search.

- [P] Sec. 2.1: an oracle reveals only the partition of its domain into level sets; the information obtained is whether two elements share an image. For the hidden polynomial problems, adding a constant to the polynomial or scaling all its coefficients by a common nonzero constant leaves the partition unchanged, so the polynomial is determined only up to those operations.
- [P] Definitions: HPP(F_q, n, d) has an oracle f on F_q^n with f(x) = f(y) iff P(x) = P(y). HPGP(F_q, n, d) has an oracle f on F_q^n x F_q with f(x_1, y_1) = f(x_2, y_2) iff y_1 - Q(x_1) = y_2 - Q(x_2). Restricting an HPGP oracle to y = 0 gives an HPP oracle.
- [P] Definition 2 (HSSP): a finite group G acts faithfully on a finite set M; f: M -> S satisfies f(x) = f(y) iff H.x = H.y for a closed subgroup H in a given family; the output is H. The hidden subgroup problem is the case M = G with the action g.h = gh.
- [P] Proposition 1: HSSP(Aff_q, F_q, ., H) has query complexity Omega(q^(1/2)), by a reduction of Grover search with the oracles f_c(x) = delta_(c,x). Sec. 4.1 adds Omega(|G|^(1/4)) for sharply 2-transitive Frobenius groups.
- [P] Lemma 1, Proposition 2, Theorem 1: an efficiently computable strong base reduces HSSP to HSP; for a Frobenius group G = K x| H with |H| < |K| - 1 a random set of Theta(log|K| log(1/epsilon)) points is a strong base.
- [P] Proposition 4 and Theorem 2: HQPP(F_q), HSSP(Aff_q({+1, -1}), F_q, ., FC), and HSP(Aff_q({+1, -1}), FC) are polynomially equivalent; HQPP is solvable in quantum polynomial time over fields of constant characteristic, and appears difficult over prime fields, where it is equivalent to the dihedral hidden subgroup problem. Proposition 5 and Theorem 3: HPP(F_q, n, 2) reduces to O(n^2) HQPP instances (with a separate case q = 2, where x^2 = x), giving polynomial quantum time in constant characteristic.
- [P] Definition 4 and Propositions 6 and 7: for a function group K closed under shifts, the function graph group is Fg(K) = K x| A; HPGP(F_q, 1, d) is the HSSP over Fg(F_q^(d)[x]) with the standard complements as hidden subgroups. Lemma 4: a set D is a strong base iff every Q in K vanishing on the x-coordinates of D is zero.
- [P] Theorem 4 and Corollary 2: HPGP(F_q, 1, d) reduces to HSP(Fg(F_q^(d)[x]), SC) in time polynomial in d and log q. The group is metabelian with exponent the characteristic and nilpotency class d + 1 (reducible to class d), and for q a power of a fixed prime the problem is solvable in quantum time polynomial in d and log q. This removes the finite exceptional set E_d of characteristics left open by the earlier work of Decker, Draisma, and Wocjan.
- [P] Theorem 5: HPGP(F_q, n, d) reduces classically, in time polynomial in C(d + n, n), to O(C(d + n, n)) univariate instances, via a generalized Vandermonde matrix; for constant d it is solvable in quantum polynomial time over every finite field. The method learns d |I^(n)| log2(q) bits against the information bound |I^(n)| log2(q), optimal up to the factor d. Proposition 8 and the closing remark: for n >= 2 no polynomial-size strong base exists, since multivariate polynomials have many zeros.

## Mapping to this repository

- [I] Repository claim: "The higher-degree forms are phase-oracle benchmarks whose nonuniform Walsh spectra fall outside standard HSP coset sampling." (gororoba_quantum/DESIGN_NOTES.md, Design C; the same claim, with "HSP promise", in gororoba_quantum/FACTORING_NOTES.md).
  - Source: Proposition 6, Proposition 7, Theorem 4, Corollary 2 (graph-oracle polynomial problems as hidden subgroup problems in Fg(K)); Theorem 5 and Proposition 8 (multivariate case).
  - Model map: the oracle of this paper is a partition (equality) oracle on F_q^n x F_q, not a phase oracle. Over F_2 the HPGP oracle is f(x, y) = pi(y + Q(x)) for an unknown relabeling pi; with Q(0) = 0 the comparison of f(x, 0) with f(0, 0) reveals whether Q(x) = 0. The note author derives from this that a Boolean Q of degree at most d is learned classically from the sum_(k=1..d) C(n, k) values at the points of weight 1 to d (Mobius transform), the F_2 monomial count; Theorem 5's quantum cost is polynomial in C(d + n, n); for constant d both costs are polynomial in n.
  - Label: new implementation or measurement (the measured non-flat spectra). The claim holds only relative to abelian coset sampling of the phase block over Z_2^n; Theorem 4 places the graph-oracle formulation of the same polynomials inside a nonabelian hidden subgroup problem.
  - Not established by the source: any statement for degree growing with the number of variables. At d = n - 2 the monomial count sum_(k=1..n-2) C(n, k) = 2^n - n - 2 is exponential in n, so Theorem 5 yields no polynomial-time algorithm there; the paper neither refutes nor supports hardness at that degree. Over F_2 the classical learner matches the quantum cost for constant d, so no separation follows from this paper.
- [I] Repository claim: "quotient by observational equivalence (uncontrolled phase access identifies f and f + 1; controlled access is an extra resource)" (literature/notes/open_gororoba_handoff.md, hidden oracle row).
  - Source: Sec. 2.1 (the oracle reveals only level sets; constants and common scalars leave the partition unchanged).
  - Model map: observational equivalence for an equality oracle is equality of the level-set partitions, and f and f + 1 have the same partition; for a phase oracle (-1)^f the equivalence is equality up to a global phase, which also identifies f and f + 1. The two quotients coincide on Boolean functions.
  - Label: known theorem reproduced (the level-set principle of Sec. 2.1, as a definition of the recoverable object).
  - Not established by the source: the role of controlled access, which this paper does not model.
- [I] No repository claim: Scope of the refutation of "cubic and higher is unsolved". Theorem 5 and Corollary 2 give an efficient quantum algorithm for polynomial function graph problems of constant degree and constant number of variables over every finite field, including characteristic 2. The hypotheses do not cover degree n - 2 on n Boolean variables, and the problem stays a graph-oracle problem, not a phase-oracle problem.
