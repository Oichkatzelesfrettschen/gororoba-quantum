## Observations and assumptions

[P] The studied 3D transmon had transitions `f_ge=4.97 GHz` and
`f_ef=4.70 GHz`. Its inferred excited-state population followed the thermal
model over 35-150 mK and saturated near 0.1% below 35 mK, with an equivalent
effective temperature around 35 mK. These are measurements of the paper's
device and measurement chain, rather than calibration values for an IBM QPU.

[P] Figure 1 and equation (1), pp. 2-3, use driven `e-f` Rabi oscillations
with and without a `g-e` pi pulse. The signal and reference amplitudes are
proportional to `P_e-P_f` and `P_g-P_f`. Under negligible `P_f` and a common
readout conversion factor, `P_e=A_sig/(A_sig+A_ref)`. Finite higher-level
population changes that interpretation. The protocol uses higher-level
control and state-sensitive resonator readout; a computational-basis bit
count alone does not implement the protocol.

[P] The calibration experiment, equation (2), uses controlled population
pumping with `P_e_pumped=k*P_g+(1-k)*P_e` and extrapolation to zero pumping.
The two-point acquisition replaces full Rabi traces only after checking the
common signal/reference frequency and phase. Those reference checks and
population assumptions carry the identifiability burden.

[P] The supplement cycles reference and signal points to reduce slow drift,
examines finite repetition-time contamination, and estimates uncertainty from
cycle-to-cycle samples under an independent, identically distributed model.
The supplement reports `0.055 +/- 0.039%` at 15 mK for one dataset, while the
main text summarizes the saturation near 0.1%. Finite measurement noise and
the precise estimator matter at this scale. The source does not justify
treating the reported saturation as an exact universal floor.

[P] Equations (4)-(5), p. 4, infer a quasiparticle density and a 108 us
lifetime **assuming all residual excitation is due to hot quasiparticles**.
The measured lifetime was 80 us. The agreement is a conditional consistency
check; the paper calls hot quasiparticles a potential mechanism and explicitly
leaves the residual excitation mechanism unresolved.

## Repository mapping and qualification

[I] The source motivates a measured excitation-floor control for long-delay,
reset and mid-circuit-measurement experiments. It also supplies a useful
warning: a small terminal `1` fraction can reflect assignment error,
preparation/history, leakage or a physical steady-state population. A
population and its apparent temperature require an identifiable calibration
model. The [paired-rate note](cond-mat-0210247.md) gives the simpler binary
assignment model and its nonidentifiability boundary.

[I] Compare a targeted gate-level population contrast with the paper's
higher-level method and with self-consistent instrument characterization.
The targeted contrast can test a specific intervention using available
controls. A physical residual-population estimate requires stronger SPAM
constraints. Higher-level spectroscopy requires separately verified control
and readout access; abstract unitary synthesis does not grant that access.

Retain preparation, repetition-delay and reset details; distinguish observed
counts from inferred populations; propagate reference uncertainty; test
drift/history and leakage alternatives. A future quasiparticle or vacuum
attribution needs measurements that distinguish the candidate mechanisms.
Additional shots can narrow statistical uncertainty but cannot supply the
missing control or separate an unidentified mechanism.
