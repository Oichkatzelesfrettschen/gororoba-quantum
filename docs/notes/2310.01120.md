## Main claims and evidence

- [P] The study runs component, system and application metrics on the Starmon-5 backend of Quantum Inspire in November and December 2022: five qubits, four couplings, qubit 2 coupled to the other four by CZ.
- [P] Readout fidelity per qubit comes from preparing |00000> and |11111> and measuring: F_R/O = 1 - (M10 + M01)/2 from the 2x2 assignment matrix; the five values are 96.4-98.4 percent (Table 1).
- [P] The paper states that this approach cannot separate preparation error from measurement error, so the reported readout error is a combined number.
- [P] Repeating the readout-fidelity measurement gave a higher variance than expected for a stable system.
- [P] A full 25x25 readout assignment matrix (16384 shots per basis state) quantifies readout crosstalk; an ideal device gives a diagonal matrix, and the figure shows the difference from the diagonal.
- [P] The T2* experiments show outlier points at long waiting times, which the paper uses for its stability metric; it notes that the device drifts and is retuned or upgraded, so metric values change over time.

## Limits

- [P/I] Starmon-5, not Tuna-17; no two-qubit gate repeatability study and no time-ordered shot analysis.
- [P/I] The all-excited calibration (|11111>) is a single preparation context; the paper does not compare it with the assignment error inside an algorithm circuit.
- [P/I] Cloud-access measurements carry no per-shot timestamps, so no frequency-domain statement about shot order is possible from this source.

## Mapping to this repository

[I] The only retained paper that measures a Quantum Inspire device through the cloud API; its |00000> and |11111> readout calibration and 25x25 assignment matrix are the closest published analogue of the all-qubits-excited calibration, and it reports the readout fidelity as less stable than expected.
