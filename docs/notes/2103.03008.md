[P] A quantum instrument consists of outcome-indexed completely positive
maps `Q_i` whose sum is trace preserving. It produces both a classical
outcome and a conditional continuing state:
`p_i=tr Q_i(rho)`, `rho_i=Q_i(rho)/p_i` when `p_i>0`.
QILGST characterizes these maps together with gate, preparation and terminal
measurement models. The abstract reports a dispersive transmon experiment
that varies delay after measurement to investigate residual cavity photons.

[I] A terminal confusion matrix cannot represent the conditional-state
disturbance. The repository therefore retains both bits, delays and physical
writers when testing a folded-QPE model. The source supplies a model class,
not a measurement of residual photons on Heron. A small matched pilot can
discriminate selected predictions without performing complete QILGST.

## Informational completeness, gauge and estimator constraints

Additional bounded reading of the same v1 source covers the full QILGST
construction, gauge discussion, experimental design and model-check results
on pp. 2-4; p. 3 was also rendered. This extends the earlier opening-page
reading without claiming a new full review of the supplement.

[P] Page 2's construction uses informationally complete preparation and
measurement fiducials, plus null-operation circuits. Adding `F_p Q F_m`
circuits records both the intermediate and terminal outcomes. Page 3 stacks
the outcome-conditioned probability matrices into an `m*d^2` by `d^2`
object. Retaining only the intermediate outcome measures a POVM; retaining
only the terminal marginal loses information needed for the instrument.

[P] The QILGST estimate retains common GST gauge freedom. Page 3 fixes a
gauge by minimizing discrepancy between estimated gates and their targets
before reporting instrument metrics. Thus a decomposition of errors across
SPAM, gates and instrument entries depends on the stated frame. The
[GST foundations source](2009.07301.md), equations (15) and (38)-(40),
provides the corresponding probability-preserving transformations and Gram
matrix reconstruction.

[P] The experiment used 128 circuits, including 36 with a mid-circuit
measurement, and 1024 repetitions per circuit for each selected delay.
Those counts describe its single-qubit gate set and fiducials, not a universal
budget for a larger instrument. Page 3, footnote 2, says the fitted gates
were constrained CPTP while the estimated instrument was constrained TP
but not CP. A repository implementation should declare its own estimator
constraints; it should not cite this experiment as evidence that every fitted
instrument was constrained completely positive.

[P] The fitted Markovian gate-set model failed strongly at short delays in
that device (Fig. 3 and p. 4). The authors used likelihood-ratio diagnostics,
maximum circuit TVD and a physically motivated decaying Stark-shift model
for subsequent gates. Those observations support that device's model study;
they do not attribute the retained Heron gap to cavity photons. The reported
mid-circuit readout fidelity also differs from instrument error because it
omits conditional-state disturbance and can include preparation error.

[I] A future identifiable repository design should retain the fiducial
rank/conditioning audit, outcome-resolved joint counts, explicit common
gauge, CP/TP and leakage policy, and a predictive model-check protocol.
Compare targeted contrasts with full instrument estimation before spending
shots. [Optimal design](quant-ph-0411093.md) can prioritize identifiable
parameter directions, but Fisher information in a chosen model cannot
validate that model or prove a finite-shot mechanism attribution.
