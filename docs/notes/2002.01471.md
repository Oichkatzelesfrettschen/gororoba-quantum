## Main claims and evidence

- [P] Within a biased measurement model, nonideal n-qubit measurement equals ideal projective measurement followed by a classical Markov process Gamma on the outcome distribution (abstract, Theorem 1).
- [P] Gamma equals the measured transition matrix T only if the classical basis states are prepared with negligible error; otherwise Gamma must come from another route, which the paper takes from gate set tomography.
- [P] The biased model includes multiqubit crosstalk errors and excludes measurement-frame errors such as a rotated measurement basis; the paper expects it to describe dispersive readout of superconducting qubits, where energy relaxation during measurement dominates.
- [P] The paper applies the correction to single IBM Q superconducting qubits.

## Limits

- [P/I] A calibration that prepares a basis state and measures it returns T, which equals Gamma only under perfect preparation; preparation error in the all-excited state therefore enters the apparent |1> misassignment (inference from the paper's statement).
- [P/I] Demonstrated on single qubits; the n-qubit context dependence is covered by the model class, not measured.
