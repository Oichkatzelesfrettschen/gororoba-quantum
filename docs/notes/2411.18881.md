## Main claims

[P] The sedenion zero divisors form two homogeneous Riemannian manifolds whose isometry types the paper identifies: pairs Z(S) with a naturally reductive left-invariant metric on G_2, and elements ZD(S) with a G_2-invariant metric on the Stiefel manifold V_2(R^7).

- [P] Setup (Sec. 2.1): A_n = A_{n-1} x A_{n-1} with (a,b)(c,d) = (ac - d*b, da + bc*), (a,b)* = (a*, -b), <x,y> = Re(x y*), ||x||^2 = x x*. Indices count from 0. xy = 0 implies yx = 0, so left and right zero divisors coincide, and u != 0 is a zero divisor iff ann u = ker L_u is nonzero.
- [P] Cited facts (Sec. 2.1): a zero divisor is imaginary and dim ann u is 0 mod 4 (Moreno 1998); dim ann u <= 2^n - 4n + 4 for nonzero u (Biss, Dugger, Isaksen 2008), which is 4 at n = 4. Aut(A_n) = Aut(A_{n-1}) x S_3 = G_2 x (S_3)^(n-3) for n >= 4, and Der(A_n) = g_2.
- [P] Definitions (Sec. 2.1): Z(A_n) = {(u,v) : ||u|| = ||v|| = sqrt2, uv = 0} and ZD(A_n) = {u : (u,v) in Z(A_n) for some v}. For n >= 5 the strata ZD_k(A_n) = {u : dim ann u = k} "are also of interest"; the paper computes none of them.
- [P] Proposition 2.1 (attributed to Biss, Dugger, Isaksen): (a,b) in S is a zero divisor iff a and b are imaginary octonions with ||a|| = ||b|| != 0 and a orthogonal to b.
- [P] Table 1 (Appendix A.1): the 84 "standard zero divisors", the pairs (e_i + e_j, e_k +- e_l) in Z(S) with 1 <= i <= 6, 9 <= j <= 15, i < k <= 7, 9 <= l <= 15.
- [P] Theorem 2.2 (attributed to Moreno 1998): Z(S) is diffeomorphic to G_2. The identity component of Aut(S) acts simply and transitively on Z(S); G_2 . u0 = ZD(S) with isotropy SU(2); ZD(S) = G_2/SU(2) = V_2(R^7); the topology is encoded by the principal bundle SU(2) -> G_2 -> V_2(R^7), and the fiber over u in ZD(S) is the sphere of the annihilator subspace of u (Sec. 1 and 2.2).
- [P] Theorem 3.1: the metric induced on Z(S) from S x S = R^32 is naturally reductive, and Z(S) is the total space of a Riemannian submersion over the symmetric space G_2/SO(4) of quaternion subalgebras of the octonions, with totally geodesic fibers locally isometric to a product of two round 3-spheres of different radii. At the origin (e4 + e13, e6 + e15) the metric is g_bi|k0 + (1/3) g_bi|m0 + (1/2) g_bi|(m1 + m2), with g_bi(X,Y) = -tr(XY) on g_2 inside so(8) (Eq. 3.1 and the display after it).
- [P] Proposition 3.3: Z(S) has positive Ricci curvature (Eq. 3.2).
- [P] On ZD(S), at u0 = e1 + e10, the induced metric is (1/3)(X^3 X^3 + X^4 X^4) + (2/3) X^5 X^5 + (1/2) sum_{6..9} + (1/6) sum_{10..13}. Shrinking along the circle direction gives the family g_r (Eq. 4.1), with g_{2/3} the induced metric. Theorem 4.1: I_0(V_2(R^7), g_r) = G_2 x S^1. Theorem 4.2: g_r is Einstein iff r = 5/9, and has positive scalar curvature iff r < 20/3. Theorem 4.4: g_r has nonnegative sectional curvature iff r <= 4/9, proved by exhibiting F_0 and F_{4/9} as sums of squares (semidefinite programs solved numerically with CVXOPT in SageMath, then rounded to the exact positive semidefinite matrices printed in the proof). The plane pi_34 has sectional curvature 1 - (9/4) r.
- [P] Introduction: none of the g_r is normal homogeneous or naturally reductive, and g_{2/3} lacks nonnegative sectional curvature. Remark 4.7: g_r restricted to m0 is a Berger sphere, with positive sectional curvature iff 0 < r < 9/4.

The description "finite assessor graph with S^3 fibers".

- [I] The repository discusses the phrase "finite assessor graph with S^3 fibers" as an interpretation to audit. The mathematical claim under examination identifies the whole sedenion zero-divisor structure with a finite graph carrying S^3 fibers; literal occurrences in quotations or corrective prose supply neither a proof nor a defect. The fiber half is Reggiani's: over each u in ZD(S) the pairs (u,v) form the sphere {v in ann u : ||v|| = sqrt2}, a round S^3 of radius sqrt2 under the normalization of Z(A_n) (Sec. 2.2, principal bundle SU(2) -> G_2 -> V_2(R^7)). Convention map: Reggiani's normalization ||u|| = ||v|| = sqrt2 is the norm of the corpus operands e_i + t e_j, so corpus partners b are fiber points without rescaling. Label: known theorem reproduced.
- [I] The finite-graph half does not survive as a description of Z(S) or ZD(S). By Theorem 2.2 the total space is the connected 14-manifold G_2 and the base is the connected 11-manifold V_2(R^7) (G_2 is connected and ZD(S) = G_2 . u0), so a bundle with S^3 fibers over a finite set, a disjoint union of copies of S^3, has dimension 3 and cannot be Z(S). The finite objects of the repository are samples: 84 points of the base V_2(R^7), each with an orthogonal frame of 4 partner axes that meets the fiber sphere in the 8 points +-b (the corpus second operands and their negatives, norm sqrt2), the vertices of a 16-cell in S^3, and the 336 corpus rows are a finite subset of the 14-manifold Z(S) (up to the sign of the second operand). Convention map: as in the previous bullet. Label: new implementation or measurement. The source identifies the continuous homogeneous spaces and their bundle. A combinatorial graph requires an additional theorem connecting its realization to that continuous topology; the finite sample and the fiber dimension alone supply no such equivalence.
- [I] A statement that survives: the graph vertices and its ordered zero-product incidences give finite samples of the base and total space of the principal SU(2)-bundle Z(S) -> ZD(S). Calling a graph realization a topological skeleton requires an additional construction and equivalence theorem. The handoff table in literature/notes/topics/open_gororoba_handoff.md names that graph-topology obligation.
