## Main claims and evidence

[P] Time-ordered outcomes retain drift information discarded by aggregated
counts. The method transforms circuit outcome sequences into spectra, tests for
significant variation, and optionally estimates time-dependent probabilities
or model parameters (pp. 1-3).

- [P] The paper calls a time-ordered outcome sequence a clickstream. Rastering repeatedly runs each circuit in a sequence once, spreading each circuit's samples over the collection period (p. 2).
- [P] The inference assumes physically relevant probability trajectories admit a sparse frequency representation. Fitting an arbitrary probability separately to every observed bit would overfit (p. 3).
- [P] The analysis uses a normalized type-II discrete cosine transform, significance thresholds, and multiple-testing control. Time-varying parameter estimation then uses a circuit-specific physical model (pp. 3 and 8).
- [P] Both simulations and trapped-ion experiments demonstrate the methods. The hardware demonstrations use a single ytterbium-171 ion with microwave control, Ramsey spectroscopy, and gate-set tomography (pp. 1, 3, 7-8).
- [P] An initial tomography experiment identified drift associated with erroneous phase rotations in an active idle gate. The follow-up added periodic calibration of microwave frequency, pulse duration, and detection-laser position; the setup also changed the Rabi frequency to reduce a separate pulse-switching error (pp. 6-8).
- [P] Individual-circuit tests in the second tomography experiment detected no instability, while an aggregate spectrum still detected residual variation. The source reports substantial stabilization with an explicit residual (pp. 6-7).
- [P] Interleaving dedicated drift probes and application circuits can test whether parameter variation tracks changes in application outcomes. Interleaving lowers each circuit's acquisition rate and reduces sensitivity to faster changes (p. 7).
- [P] The sampling rate limits the highest resolvable frequency; total observation duration limits the lowest. Strong irregularity in sampling can degrade sensitivity, motivating methods that explicitly incorporate sample times (pp. 7, 11-12).
- [P] Appendix A distinguishes transform indices from physical frequencies. Conversion to hertz requires actual sample timing; a generic transform index is only a basis-function index (p. 11).
- [P] The core model treats clicks as independent Bernoulli observations conditioned on their time-varying probabilities. That assumption is part of the inference model, rather than a consequence of storing bit strings (p. 3).

## Mapping to this repository

[I] [adjudicate.py](../../gororoba_quantum/adjudicate.py) consumes shot-ordered
raw registers and computes `block_means`. That retained order supports drift
screening at the shot-index level. A physical drift-frequency claim needs
additional timing evidence or a justified sampling model.

[I] The same module's `pub_chunks` recognizes that a PUB can span multiple
execution windows while individual shots lack chunk tags. That boundary limits
calibration attribution and any reconstruction of individual sample times.
Replacing missing times with uniform spacing would add a model assumption that
must be identified and justified.

[I] A companion probe and a target stream collected in the same shot can reveal
shared variation. Shared variation alone cannot distinguish neighboring control
effects, common environmental drift, or readout dependence. [Sarovar et al.](1908.09855.md)
provide complementary varied-context experiments; [Rudinger et al.](1810.05651.md)
provide context-pool comparisons and timing controls.

[I] Label: known analysis method available for adaptation. Spectral analysis
could extend the existing block summaries, after validating assumptions and
multiplicity. A repository result would concern its measured QPE or Walsh
endpoint and the supported mechanism, rather than a reproduction of the ion
hardware intervention or a new drift-detection theorem.

## Relevance

- [I] Calibration-aware compilation on IBM hardware: high for analysis and acquisition design. Hardware demonstrations in this source are trapped-ion experiments; transfer to IBM runtime data requires a timing and execution-order audit.
- [I] Cayley-Dickson structure: none. The paper develops drift analysis rather than algebraic structure or classification.
- [I] Factoring resource estimates: low. The source supplies neither a factoring resource advantage nor a full cost model.

[I] Diagnostic applicability to a measured QPE gap is high, while an explanation
specific to the repository's QPU record still requires evidence. Drift can also
obscure a predicted Walsh distinction without changing its classical algebraic
classification. Those applications remain separate from the structural and
resource ratings.

## Action items and acceptance gates

1. Inventory timing evidence before applying a spectral estimator. Acceptance:
   each record states whether order, per-shot time, execution-window membership,
   and calibration attribution are observed, inferred, or unavailable.
2. Keep drift detection, drift magnitude, and mechanism attribution distinct.
   Acceptance: tests report a defined family-wise criterion, effect estimates,
   observation duration, and the assumptions needed for a physical explanation.
3. Compare block summaries with a validated spectral analysis offline.
   Acceptance: fixtures cover constant probability, slow variation, abrupt
   change, irregular sampling, and correlated shots; limitations remain visible.
4. Evaluate a fixed companion probe as a candidate diagnostic. Acceptance:
   the proposed probe targets a named physical parameter and the plan compares
   its explanatory value against its added schedule duration and interference.
5. If physical patch rotation is proposed, balance collection periods as well.
   Acceptance: arm, patch, and time remain separately identifiable; rotation is
   recorded as the repository's design proposal rather than this paper's result.
6. Require a submit-free dry run and a separate QPU approval case for new data.
   Acceptance: the proposed result names the targeted residual, predicted
   intervention effect, and expected charged seconds.

## Boundaries

[I] A stable aggregate count can hide changing probabilities; a detected spectral
feature does not identify its physical cause. A short collection also leaves
slow drift weakly constrained. The source's residual after stabilization is a
useful reporting precedent for the [parallel-experiment synthesis](quantum_experiment_parallelism.md).
