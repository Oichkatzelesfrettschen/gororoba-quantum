## Main claims and evidence

- [P] The sudden Net Zero (SNZ) pulse realizes CZ at the speed limit of the |11>-|02> coupling by maximizing intermediate leakage; the abstract reports 99.93 +/- 0.24 percent fidelity and 0.10 +/- 0.02 percent leakage in a multi-transmon processor.
- [P] Conditional phase and leakage vary with two control parameters, the pulse amplitudes A and B, in a regular landscape; tuneup follows contours of constant conditional phase in the (A, B) plane (Fig. 2).
- [P] Phase increments are quantized by the AWG time resolution t_s (about 1 ns) times the |11>-|02> detuning (Delta_02/2pi of 0.5-1 GHz), so fine phase control uses the amplitude B of the first and last sampling points.
- [P] The introduction names dephasing from flux noise, with one qubit displaced 0.5-1 GHz below its sweetspot, as the main limit of flux-based conditional-phase gates, and cites Net Zero as the built-in echo against low-frequency flux noise.
- [P] The experiment uses a 7-transmon processor in which every transmon has its own flux line, microwave line and Purcell-filtered readout resonator, with all qubits read through one multiplexed feedline.

## Limits

- [P/I] The gate is specified at a fixed amplitude point on a regular landscape; the paper gives no sensitivity of the phase to a periodic amplitude modulation at a given frequency.
- [P/I] Noise discussion is limited to flux noise as a dephasing source; shot-to-shot or job-to-job drift of the optimum is not characterized.
- [P/I] Whether Quantum Inspire's Tuna devices use the SNZ pulse is not stated in this source.
