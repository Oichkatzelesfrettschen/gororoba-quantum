# Hilbert-function certificates by Macaulay-matrix ranks

Status (2026-11-13): design proposal. No checker exists, no ledger row cites one, and the open questions at the
end decide whether one is built.

## What a certificate would prove

For an ideal K of a polynomial ring S = k[y_1..y_n] generated by quadrics q_1..q_m, the degree-d piece K_d is
the span of the products u q_j with u a monomial of degree d - 2. The Macaulay matrix M_d has one row per pair
(u, q_j), one column per monomial of degree d, and the coefficients of u q_j as its entries, so

    HF_(S/K)(d) = binomial(n + d - 1, d) - rank M_d.

A rank certificate for M_d therefore certifies one value of the Hilbert function. It certifies nothing about
primality, radicality, saturation or the Hilbert polynomial beyond the degrees checked: a Hilbert series is fixed
by finitely many values only together with a bound on the regularity, and that bound is a Groebner fact that this
format does not carry.

## Sizes for the Iso fiber cone (17 generators, 68 quadrics)

Recorded data (`docs/evidence/a5-exceptional-fibers/ledger.tsv`): row A5-294 gives the 68 quadrics and
h_2 = 85 over Q(i), and row A5-422 gives dimension 5 and degree 23 with the Hilbert function of the modular
ideal in every degree. The h-vector (1, 12, 10) is the working value of the A_5 program: it has the recorded
degree 1 + 12 + 10 = 23 and gives HF(d) = sum_i h_i binomial(d - i + 4, 4) with HF(1) = 17 and HF(2) = 85. A
ledger row that states the h-vector is to be named before a certificate cites it.

| d | monomials of degree d | HF(d) | rank M_d | rows of M_d (68 x monomials of degree d - 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Computed with `math.comb`; the HF column is the prediction of the recorded h-vector, and a certificate at degree

Over Q(i) the route of gororoba-open certifies the real block form [[Re, -Im], [Im, Re]], which doubles both
dimensions and the rank: at d = 3 a 2,312 x 1,938 rational matrix of rank 1,408.

## What the existing checker can take

`ExactRankCertificate.rank_certificate_check_sound` (gororoba-open) concludes `mxrank A = r` from A = U_L U_R and
L A R = I_r over a MathComp field. The retained ingestor turns a JSON certificate into a Rocq file whose proof
enumerates the entries of each matrix equation. Its fixtures are at most 2 x 2, and the nullity-twelve table of
that repository is 58 x 231. That proof style grows with the number of entries times the row length of each
product: at d = 3 the equation A = U_L U_R alone has 1,156 x 969 = 1.1 million entries, each a sum of 704
products. hypothesis: the enumerating proof is infeasible from d = 3 on; falsifier: a d = 3 certificate that
compiles in under an hour.

So the Macaulay route needs a different checker, not a larger input to the present one.

## Proposed checker, in order of cost

1. Computational reflection over a prime field. Represent a sparse matrix as a list of rows of (column, value)
   pairs with machine integers (Rocq `PrimInt63`, values reduced modulo a prime below 2^31), implement sparse
   row reduction or a sparse product check as a Gallina function, prove once that a `true` answer implies the
   rank statement, and run it with `vm_compute`. A Macaulay row has at most as many entries as its quadric has
   terms, so M_3 holds at most 1,156 times the largest term count.
2. Certificate shape for rank M_d = r, both bounds:
   - rank at least r: r row indices and r column indices with a permuted triangular factorization of that
     minor (pivots nonzero), checked by forward substitution;
   - rank at most r: for every other row, its expression as a combination of the r chosen rows, checked by one
     sparse product; equivalently a matrix C with (rows - r) rows, each a relation.
   The cost of the check is the number of nonzeros of the relation matrix times the row length, which the
   producer can report before any proof is attempted.
3. Characteristic zero. For a matrix with integral entries the rank modulo p is at most the rank over the
   fraction field, so HF_(Q(i))(d) <= HF_(F_p)(d): a modular certificate bounds the characteristic-0 value from
   above only. Equality at degree d needs an upper bound on the rank over Q(i), that is the relations
   themselves over Q(i), checked in the real block form. The dimension and the degree over Q(i) do not give
   it: they fix the leading term of the Hilbert polynomial and say nothing about one low degree. A note that
   cites a certificate states which field its rank was certified over.
4. The link from the certified matrix to the ideal is the weakest joint: the checker sees numbers, and the
   statement "this matrix is M_d of the 68 quadrics of row A5-294" is the producer's claim. Bind it as the rank
   certificates of gororoba-open do: the certificate names the sha256 of the retained quadric file, and a
   small independent script rebuilds M_d from that file and compares hashes of the sparse rows.

## Recommended first step

Degree 3 for Iso modulo 32009, by the reflection checker of item 1: 1,156 x 969, rank 704, the smallest case
that is beyond the enumerating proof and small enough to finish. Measure the `vm_compute` time and the
certificate size there before promising degree 4 (10,404 x 4,845, rank 4,205) or any Q(i) statement. If degree 3
takes more than minutes, the route stops at a documented negative.

## A smaller first input

Ledger row A5-421 records that the quadric ideal K of W8 over Q(i) has two minimal primes, of degrees 20 and 30;
the retained presentation of the degree-20 prime (`docs/evidence/a5-exceptional-fibers/structure/w8-kp/`, at
p = 32009) has 9 linear forms and 36 quadrics, so it lives in 15 coordinates. Whether that prime is a component
of the fiber cone over Q(i) is a separate question, which row A5-418 states with status open; a rank certificate
would concern the ideal, not that identification. Issue 863 tested the lead that the prime defines the cone over the
adjoint variety of SL_4: that form is refuted, and the working hypothesis is a rank-3 hyperplane section of the
Segre variety P^3 x P^3. Every hyperplane section of that Segre variety has Hilbert function 1, 15, 84, 300,
825, so the Macaulay matrices would be 540 x 680 of rank 380 in degree 3 and 4,320 x 3,060 of rank 2,235 in
degree 4, smaller than the Iso matrices above. hypothesis: the degree-20 prime has that Hilbert function
(reported from scratch computations on 2026-11-13, modulo 32009 and over Q(i), not yet a ledger row). A
certified HF(2) = 84 and HF(3) = 300 would be a replayable test of it.

## Out of scope

Primality and radicality of K (ledger rows A5-421 and A5-422), the regularity bound that turns finitely many
values into a Hilbert series, and Groebner-basis certificates. No route for these is proposed here.
