# Ascella A5 two-dimensional kernel-slice probe

This case tests whether Ascella reproduces a specified single-photon encoding of two A5 multiplication columns. The `cd_core` convention gives `(e1+e10)(e4-e15)=0` and `(e1+e10)(e4+e15)=2(e5-e14)`. Replacing `e10` with `e9` supplies a control; changing the sign of `e10` exchanges the dark and bright inputs. The probe encodes the success and failure branches of these two-column operators in eight optical modes and uses modes 8–11 as sentinels. The single photon carries a classical encoding of selected amplitudes. The experiment does not establish the nullity of the full 32-dimensional operator or physical nonassociativity.

The [builder](../../scripts/ascella_a5_kernel_slice.py) verifies the 8-by-2 isometry exactly, completes it to an 8-mode unitary, and checks every parameterized 12-mode operator against the request. The [direct multiplication test](../../tests/scripts/test_ascella_a5_kernel_slice.py) independently compares the success amplitudes with `cd_core` for all six settings. The ideal detected-branch success fractions in requested order are `1/2, 0, 1, 0, 1, 1/2`. The requested order supplies descriptive comparisons; it is not evidence of physical execution order or a causal drift correction.

One `sim:ascella` job precedes one `qpu:ascella` job. Each contains six settings, each requests 10,000 samples and at most 100,000 shots, and each has a 300-second duration limit. The written tariff model uses one credit per job and, on the QPU, `1e-6` credits per shot applied to the maximum per-iteration shot budget: simulator ceiling 1.0 credit and QPU ceiling 1.1 credits, total 2.1 credits. The plan uses a 60-second expected duration per job as a conservative planning scenario, not a provider guarantee. Provider pricing, exact shot estimation, platform status and token capacity are checked afresh before each submission. The case has no automatic retry.

The [ANU draw](../evidence/anu_qrng/draw-2026-10-30T185614Z.json) supplies six distinct compilation seed values, one per setting. The hardware repeats the simulator's setting and seed identifiers for a matched request, while returned circuit identities and output behavior are checked independently on each platform. A simulator pass establishes client and simulator conformance, not a guarantee of identical QPU compilation or device behavior.

For each setting, report counts in modes 0–3, modes 4–7, sentinel modes 8–11, and invalid multi-photon or vacuum events. The detected-branch success fraction uses the single-click counts in modes 0–7 as its denominator; the report also carries the full returned-event and `nb_shots_used` values. Detection is postselected, so this fraction is not an unconditional photon-source success rate. The six contrasts are descriptive and carry no causal or simultaneous-confidence verdict. QPU admission follows a completed simulator result and matching requested settings; a failed, incomplete or malformed simulator record stops the sequence.
